# osoe 0.2.0

*A draft of 2026-10-10, against `main` at `5748c97`, with every change of v0.2
merged but step 4's last part. 0.2 is not tagged: the author holds the tag while
more hardware - a link above a gigabit, for that last part - may be had
(`docs/decisions/2026-10-10-step4-merge-point.md`). Every number marked "at the
tag" waits for the checks run on the tree that is tagged.*

The second release of osoe, an implementation of OpenSHMEM 1.6 over TCP and
io_uring on Linux, for clusters of ordinary Ethernet. The README says what it is
and how to use it; this note says what changed since 0.1.0, what was checked
before the tag, and what it leaves for later.

## What changed

What a conforming program computes has not changed, but for a floating-point
reduction's result, which now depends on the collectives' shape - each block of
Träff's combined at its own lead, a tree's whole vector at its root - so two
runs whose measured links fall either side of a crossover may differ there, as
the specification allows: in the last bits, and more where a sum overflows on
the way, a NaN is among the inputs or zeros of both signs meet. Over 4,096
doubles on five nodes, each shape forced by a link set by hand, a sum that
overflows on the way was infinite in 2,458 elements at 0.1.0's fan-out and
pipeline, 1,639 under a tree the chooser takes and 819 under Träff's, and a
maximum with a NaN among its inputs fell below the largest number in 819 under
that tree, 656 under Träff's and none at 0.1.0's fan-out and pipeline (the
ninth review's conformance C1). `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE=fixed`, or the link's
three numbers set (`OSOE_LINK_*`), makes a run repeat its results - under
`fixed` the same bits from every run of this version, at any number of
progress helpers and under load (separate launches at none, two and three,
idle and beside a busy loop a core, give one hash), and nothing promised
across versions. Every installed header is 0.1.0's byte for byte, and the
library exports the same names, under the same soname,
`libosoe.so.0`: a program built against 0.1.0 runs on 0.2 without a rebuild -
the README's ring, built against a 0.1.0 install and run once 0.2 was installed
over it, printed its four lines on one node and on two (the ninth review). The
full build's requirements are 0.1.0's - Linux 6.7
or newer, liburing 2.6 or newer, CMake 3.25 or newer, GCC or Clang - and so is
the licence.

- **A single-node build** (`-DOSOE_SINGLE_NODE=ON`): the library and its tools
  for jobs of one node, with no liburing, no pkg-config and no io_uring, waiting
  on the classic futex calls - so on a distribution with Linux 4.17 and CMake
  3.25: Red Hat 9 and its rebuilds above all, Debian 12 or Ubuntu 24.04, and
  Ubuntu 22.04 with a newer CMake than its own. It has run on Rocky Linux 9 and
  Debian 13. A job of more than one node is refused at `shmem_init`, by name,
  before anything is made in `/dev/shm`.
- **Large collectives shaped from the link.** The root's lead measures its link
  at the end of `shmem_init` - a hop, a message's cost, the bandwidth - and every
  node computes from those three numbers the fan-out and the pieces in flight
  each large reduction or broadcast takes. On the Raspberry Pi cluster (four
  nodes, gigabit), in step 2's second part, a 1 MiB broadcast ran 1.43 to 1.47
  times as fast as 0.1.0's one tree, a 1 MiB reduction 1.20 to 1.26 times; the
  measurement costs a start about 27 ms there. `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE=fixed`, with
  `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_FANOUT=4 OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PIPELINE=2`, takes 0.1.0's fan-out
  and pipeline.
- **Träff's circulant reduce-scatter and allgather** for large reductions, where
  the cost model says they cost less than the best tree: a 1 MiB reduction on the
  Pi cluster 1.32 to 1.41 times as fast as the trees above, at step 3's merge
  point. `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE=tree` keeps to the trees. These ratios were
  timed before step 4 changed the engine under them: step 4's merge point's Pi
  run held the 64 KiB lines within 1.6% of step 3's, before Träff's grant became
  a field a receiver, which nothing has timed since; the tag's runs time them
  again.
- **One progress thread does more.** A put fills the room a connection's buffer
  has left; the kernel's unsent queue is held to about 128 µs of the link (at
  least 16 KiB); the buffer is 256 KiB by default; a receive is parsed where the
  kernel put it. A put stream over the development VM's loopback rose from a fifth
  of a bare io_uring stream to about three quarters; on the Pi cluster a
  fetch-add behind a 1 MiB put stream waited 3.3 to 3.9 ms where, with the
  kernel's queue unbounded as in 0.1.0, it waited 17 to 20, and a barrier behind
  the stream about 4 ms where it took 18 to 20.
- **Two connections between every pair of nodes**: the barrier's and the
  deadlock detector's messages go by a control connection of their own, so a
  barrier behind a 1 MiB put stream on the Pi cluster took 1.67 ms where, with
  the unsent limit alone, it took 4.1 to 4.4.
- **The mesh's start-up by a schedule** (`docs/design/mesh-schedule-proposal.md`):
  each node's lead calls about half its peers - those one to (n - 1)/2 places
  after it, round the circle - and answers the rest, one place a slot, at most
  eight calls in flight, where 0.1.0's leads called every higher node one after
  another once every lower one had called them. Modelled over a Pi-like link,
  a CPU a lead, 64 nodes connect in 4.6 ms where that order took 64 ms, and
  4,097 in 0.26 s where it took 4.1 s; no system at hand is large enough to
  measure it, which the author waived. A setting, `OSOE_MESH_SLOT`, and a test
  hook, `OSOE_TEST_MESH_HOLD`.
- **A large blocking get from another node sends its pieces together**, as a
  nonblocking get does, as far as the PE's landing zone holds them - four
  pieces at the default 256 KiB - where 0.1.0 waited a round trip for each: a
  get of a mebibyte on the Pi cluster took a fifth less time, as long as a
  nonblocking get and its quiet.
- **Progress helpers, off by default**: `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS` threads beside
  each node's progress thread, each holding the connections to a share of the
  peers, so that what they add is traffic to several peers at once. What they
  buy on a link faster than a gigabit has not been measured. On the Pi cluster's
  four nodes two of them made a 64 KiB broadcast and a 4 KiB alltoall 6 to 35%
  slower under Slurm's default binding, and 2 to 9% slower with
  `--cpu-bind=none`, which the README asks for; a 64 KiB reduction took 15 to
  37% less time under the default binding, and about the same unbound. The README's
  Limits say where they cost.
- **The zero-copy send, off by default** (`OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY`): a connection's
  buffer lent to the kernel rather than copied, one of its two at a time. Where
  the user's locked-memory limit refuses a send, the send goes again by copy and
  its connection copies from then on, which a notice naming the limit says,
  rather than ending the job.
- **TCP keepalive off where every node of a job has a loopback address**, as
  `osoe-launch`'s logical nodes have, unless `OSOE_KEEPALIVE` is set, and the
  check for a silent peer with it: on one machine there is no link to cut, and
  under load the loopback's backlog had ended healthy jobs.
- **Settings:** eleven new - `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS`, `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE`,
  the three `OSOE_LINK_*`, `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY` and its threshold,
  `OSOE_WIRE_UNSENT_BYTES`, and three test hooks; five defaults changed - the
  collectives' fan-out from 4 to 8, now a ceiling, their pieces in flight from 2
  to 4, a connection's buffer from 128 to 256 KiB, the buffers' budget from
  256 MiB to 1 GiB, which takes a job to 4,097 nodes where 0.1.0 stopped at
  1,025, and `OSOE_KEEPALIVE` off on loopback addresses (above). None removed.
- **The costs**: a PE's internal region is 15.6 MiB of address space at four
  nodes where it was 6.0, written only as collectives use it, though the
  start-up check of `/dev/shm`'s capacity counts it whole; a node's lead holds
  two sockets for every other node, so that at 256 PEs a node a hard limit of
  1,024 open files runs out at about 385 nodes, where 0.1.0's one socket ran out
  short of 800 - `shmem_init` raises the soft limit to the hard one, as it now
  does the locked-memory limit (the README's "What `shmem_init` changes in the
  process").
- **Nodes of 0.1.0 and 0.2 do not form one job**: their connections' hello
  differs, and a node of one takes the other's for a stranger's and does not
  answer it, so the job's start waits - its notice after 30 s and then every
  minute naming the node it waits for - until the launcher's timeout or
  `OSOE_NO_PROGRESS_TIMEOUT` ends it.
  Every node of a job must run the same osoe.
- **`osoe-launch` is installed**, with `oshcc`, `osoe-stat` and `osoe-pacer`;
  `osoe-stat` shows the link, the collectives' shape and a line for each thread
  that holds connections.

**Defects of 0.1.0 that 0.2 fixes:**

- `osoe-pacer mix --seconds` could hang near its deadline: each PE decided by
  its own clock, at the top of each period, whether its time was up, and a run
  whose periods ended near the deadline could split - about one run in forty at
  60 s on the default seed. The pacer's own defect, not the library's; the time
  is now up when the PE that has run longest says so (`bc0751d`).
- A small message behind a stream on the same connection waited for every byte
  the kernel had queued ahead of it - 17 to 20 ms for a fetch-add on the Pi
  cluster, measured on v0.2 step 3's tree, which queued as 0.1.0 does - where the
  design bounded it to a couple of segments; the unsent limit above is the fix,
  which leaves it 3.3 to 3.9 ms, and the control connection takes the barrier's
  messages off the stream's connection altogether.
- A signal handler without `SA_RESTART` ended a timed futex wait early, so a
  program's own signals could hold off a PE's checks.
- The start-up check of the futex calls tried only the private kind, where a
  node's PEs wait on the shared one: under a sandbox that allowed the private
  kind alone, a job passed the check and died at its first shared wait.
- A `SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE` that was not a multiple of 16 bytes gave a little less
  than it asked for.
- `osoe-launch`, piped into a reader that left early, died of SIGPIPE before it
  removed a refused start's leftovers from `/dev/shm`.
- `osoe-stat --summary` on a job of one node printed tree epochs that do not
  exist there.

## Known limits

*At the tag: the Pi cluster's round trips against the floor's pins, as 0.1.0's
note opened with them.* The README's Limits list every limit a program can meet;
in short:

- **Tested on aarch64 only.** x86-64 built and ran under emulation, without an
  x86-64 kernel; every node of a job must have the same architecture, and run
  the same osoe.
- **A link above a gigabit has never been measured**, and with it what progress
  helpers buy; `docs/fast-link.md` is the runbook for when one is had.
- **TCP only**, no RDMA backend. By default one thread a node moves every
  message between nodes; a pair of nodes' puts and gets go by one of its two
  connections, so a network that limits each flow gives them one flow's share.
- **A large get from another node is held by its PE's landing zone**: four
  pieces of up to 64 KiB in flight at the default 256 KiB, which the zone's
  threads and contexts share.
- **Teams**: a PE in at most `OSOE_MAX_TEAMS` teams at once (256 by default), a
  node holding at most `OSOE_TEAM_BLOCKS` (1,024).
- **Scale**: at most 4,097 nodes at the default connection budget, every pair of
  nodes connected twice at start-up; 65,535 PEs a node. Träff's reduction runs on
  at most 512 nodes at the default fan-out ceiling of 8.
- **Memory**: one symmetric heap a PE, its size fixed at start-up, in
  `/dev/shm`; global and static variables symmetric only in a program linked by
  `oshcc`.
- **Threads**: `shmem_init` provides `SHMEM_THREAD_SERIALIZED`;
  `SHMEM_THREAD_MULTIPLE` comes through `shmem_init_thread`.
- **Failures**: a PE or a node that dies ends the whole job, as does a process
  that leaves without `shmem_finalize`; a collective the PEs of one node call
  differently is not detected; PEs of several nodes calling `shmem_global_exit`
  at once, under `srun -K1` or `osoe-launch` without `-k`, can lose one caller's
  output.
- **C only**: no Fortran bindings; MPI in the same program has not been tried;
  `shmemx.h` is empty.
- **Slurm**: run with `--cpu-bind=none`; a login that ends on a node just before
  a job starts there can stall its start (systemd-logind's `RemoveIPC`).

What the reviews left for after this release is on `docs/release-checklist.md`'s
"After the release".
