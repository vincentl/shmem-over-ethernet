# osoe 0.2.0

*A draft, first written on 2026-10-10 against `main` at `5748c97` and kept up
as v0.2's later work merged, last against `main` at `5cefc7fb` (2026-10-30).
Step 4's last part, a link above a gigabit, has run since, in three sessions on
eight Google Cloud nodes (`docs/fast-link-results.md`). 0.2 is not tagged: the
tag waits for every item open before it (`docs/prompts/v0.2-open-items.md`; the
author, `docs/decisions/2026-10-11.md`). Every number marked "at the tag" waits
for the checks run on the tree that is tagged.*

The second release of osoe, an implementation of OpenSHMEM 1.6 over TCP and
io_uring on Linux, for clusters of ordinary Ethernet. The README says what it is
and how to use it; this note says what changed since 0.1.0, what was checked
before the tag, and what it leaves for later.

## What changed

What a conforming program computes has not changed, but for a floating-point
reduction's result, which now depends on the collectives' shape - each block of
Träff's combined at its own lead, a tree's whole vector at its root - so two
runs whose measured links fall either side of a crossover may differ there, as
the specification allows: in the last bits, and more where a sum overflows on
the way, a NaN is among the inputs or zeros of both signs meet. Over 4,096
doubles on five nodes, each shape forced by a link set by hand, a sum that
overflows on the way was infinite in 2,458 elements at 0.1.0's fan-out and
pipeline, 1,639 under a tree the chooser takes and 819 under Träff's, and a
maximum with a NaN among its inputs fell below the largest number in 819 under
that tree, 656 under Träff's and none at 0.1.0's fan-out and pipeline (the
ninth review's conformance C1). `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE=fixed`, or the link's
three numbers set (`OSOE_LINK_*`), makes a run repeat its results - under
`fixed` the same bits from every run of this version, at any number of
progress helpers and under load (separate launches at none, two and three,
idle and beside a busy loop a core, give one hash), and nothing promised
across versions. Two of the five installed headers changed, though no call a
program makes changed with them: in `shmem.h` the macros of the three-argument
`shmem_signal_set` and `shmem_signal_add` and of the four-argument
`shmem_sync` pass their arguments as written, so that a profiler can define
those routines by their plain names (B3), and comments there and in
`shmemx.h` say where the cache routines are declared (B4). The ABI is
unchanged: the library exports the same names, under the same soname,
`libosoe.so.0`, and a program built against 0.1.0 runs on 0.2 without a rebuild -
the README's ring, built against a 0.1.0 install and run once 0.2 was installed
over it, printed its four lines on one node and on two (the ninth review). The
full build's requirements are 0.1.0's - Linux 6.7
or newer, liburing 2.6 or newer, CMake 3.25 or newer, GCC or Clang - and so is
the licence.

- **A single-node build** (`-DOSOE_SINGLE_NODE=ON`): the library and its tools
  for jobs of one node, with no liburing, no pkg-config and no io_uring, waiting
  on the classic futex calls - so on a distribution with Linux 4.17 and CMake
  3.25: Red Hat 9 and its rebuilds above all, Debian 12 or Ubuntu 24.04, and
  Ubuntu 22.04 with a newer CMake than its own. It has run on Rocky Linux 9 and
  Debian 13. A job of more than one node is refused at `shmem_init`, by name,
  before anything is made in `/dev/shm`.
- **Large collectives shaped from the link.** The root's lead measures its link
  at the end of `shmem_init` - a hop, a message's cost, the bandwidth - and every
  node computes from those three numbers the fan-out each large reduction or
  broadcast takes, up to `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_FANOUT`, and for a reduction whether
  it takes Träff's (below); a tree's pieces in flight are always the capacity,
  `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PIPELINE`. On the Raspberry Pi cluster (four nodes,
  gigabit), in step 2's second part, a 1 MiB broadcast ran 1.43 to 1.47 times
  as fast as 0.1.0's one tree, a 1 MiB reduction 1.20 to 1.26 times; the
  measurement cost a start about 27 ms there then, and has not been timed there
  since P38 gave each of its message cost's fifteen tries a round trip more on
  three nodes or more (the README's `OSOE_LINK_*` row).
  `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE=fixed`, with
  `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_FANOUT=4 OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PIPELINE=2`, takes 0.1.0's fan-out
  and pipeline.
- **Träff's circulant reduce-scatter and allgather** for large reductions, where
  the cost model says they cost less than the best tree: a 1 MiB reduction on the
  Pi cluster 1.32 to 1.41 times as fast as the trees above, at step 3's merge
  point. `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE=tree` keeps to the trees. These ratios were
  timed before step 4 changed the engine under them: step 4's merge point's Pi
  run held the 64 KiB lines within 1.6% of step 3's, before Träff's grant became
  a field a receiver, which nothing has timed since; the tag's runs time them
  again. On two nodes, where a tree and Träff's send the same bytes, the cost
  model charges a tree's partial last piece its own bytes, so that the choice
  no longer turns on how a size divides into 16 KiB pieces: two nodes of the
  Pi's link take the tree at every size, measured there the faster to 16 KiB
  and within 12% of Träff's above it (P4, 2026-10-29).
- **One progress thread does more.** A put fills the room a connection's buffer
  has left; the kernel's unsent queue is held to about 128 µs of the link (at
  least 16 KiB); the buffer is 256 KiB by default; a receive is parsed where the
  kernel put it. A put stream over the development VM's loopback rose from a fifth
  of a bare io_uring stream to about three quarters; on the Pi cluster a
  fetch-add behind a 1 MiB put stream waited 3.3 to 3.9 ms where, with the
  kernel's queue unbounded as in 0.1.0, it waited 17 to 20, and a barrier behind
  the stream about 4 ms where it took 18 to 20.
- **Two connections between every pair of nodes**: the barrier's and the
  deadlock detector's messages go by a control connection of their own, so a
  barrier behind a 1 MiB put stream on the Pi cluster took 1.67 ms where, with
  the unsent limit alone, it took 4.1 to 4.4.
- **The mesh's start-up by a schedule** (`docs/design/mesh-schedule-proposal.md`):
  each node's lead calls about half its peers - those one to (n - 1)/2 places
  after it, round the circle - and answers the rest, one place a slot, at most
  eight calls in flight, where 0.1.0's leads called every higher node one after
  another once every lower one had called them. Modelled over a Pi-like link,
  a CPU a lead, 64 nodes connect in 4.6 ms where that order took 64 ms, and
  4,097 in 0.26 s where it took 4.1 s; no system at hand is large enough to
  measure it, which the author waived. A setting, `OSOE_MESH_SLOT`, and a test
  hook, `OSOE_TEST_MESH_HOLD`.
- **A pair's data wire on demand**, behind a setting, eager by default
  (`OSOE_MESH_DATA_WIRES=on-demand`, H23): the control wires still made at
  start-up to every node, and a pair's data wire, which carries its puts, gets
  and atomics, at the first traffic between the two nodes - for a program whose
  nodes each talk to few others, saving those wires' sockets and their part of
  start-up; a node then holds at most 4,096 data wires, and a job may have up
  to 12,288 nodes with no progress helpers and 16,384 with them, a lead's
  io_uring's ceiling. Measured on no large cluster yet. And **the mesh's ports
  from a range** (`OSOE_MESH_PORT_RANGE`), for a site whose firewall opens the
  nodes' ports by range; unset, the kernel chooses each lead's port.
- **A large blocking get from another node sends its pieces together**, as a
  nonblocking get does, as far as the PE's landing zone holds them - by default
  sixteen pieces of 64 KiB at one to four PEs a node, eight at eight and four
  from sixteen (below) - where 0.1.0 waited a round trip for each: a get of a
  mebibyte on the Pi cluster, at the zone of 256 KiB that 0.1.0 gave, took a
  fifth less time, as long as a nonblocking get and its quiet.
- **A PE's landing zone from its node's budget** (P32): unset,
  `OSOE_GET_LANDING_BYTES` is the node's 4 MiB split among its PEs, no less
  than 256 KiB nor more than 1 MiB a PE - 1 MiB to each of one to four PEs a
  node, 512 KiB to eight, 256 KiB from sixteen - where 0.1.0 gave every PE
  256 KiB. On eight of Google Cloud's nodes, in one round, the budget's zone
  was the best of three or within 1% of it at one to sixteen PEs a node, where
  a fixed 256 KiB cost one PE a node 37% of its gets' rate (H28).
- **`collect`'s places from the nodes' totals** (A11; P29 to P31, P33): where
  every node's PEs are one run of the team's ranks, as under a block
  distribution, a `collect` of differing blocks finds each PE's place from each
  node's total - through the leads' tree, by a scan of the totals among the
  leads, or, on up to 16 nodes by default, put by each lead straight to every
  other - the way the link makes cheapest (`OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PLACES`), where
  0.1.0 summed a vector of every PE's length, 8 bytes a PE, through the tree,
  as 0.2 still does where a node's PEs are not one run. On eight of Google
  Cloud's nodes the exchange was the fastest of the four ways, and the link's
  choice took it (H25).
- **A collective that the PEs of one node call differently is named** (P24;
  `OSOE_CHECK_COLLECTIVES`, on by default since 2026-10-28, P39): each PE says
  its call where the node's PEs meet, and the PE that meets them all ends the
  job naming two PEs and their calls before any result is given out, where
  0.1.0, which compared only the nodes' leads, gave or got wrong data with
  nothing said. Timed at 16 to 256 PEs a node on two of Google Cloud's nodes,
  it cost nothing one round could see but, at 256, a barrier's start-up, each
  PE's first check of a team's sync (the README's Limits).
- **Some programs 0.1.0 ran, wrong or by luck, now end by name**: a heap call
  whose PEs pass different arguments (P23), a team split whose PEs pass
  different triplets or `xrange`s (P6), an `iput` or `iget` with a stride below
  1 (P22), and a collective the PEs of one node call differently (P39, above).
  Each is undefined behaviour, which no conforming program meets.
- **Progress helpers, two by default on a job of three nodes or more where a
  node runs no more PEs than it has CPUs** (`OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=auto`, the
  author's word of 2026-10-28; `0` keeps one thread a node): threads beside
  each node's progress thread, each holding the connections to a share of the
  peers, so that what they add is traffic to several peers at once. On eight
  nodes of Google Cloud's 23 Gbit/s link, unbound, two of them took every stream
  to the provider's egress ceiling and the alltoalls a quarter faster; under
  Slurm's binding, the streams a fifth faster with a core a PE and little either
  way with one CPU a PE, and the mailbox halved at a core a PE. On the Pi
  cluster's four nodes two of them made a 64 KiB broadcast and a 4 KiB alltoall
  6 to 35% slower under Slurm's default binding, and 2 to 9% slower with
  `--cpu-bind=none`, which the README asks for; a 64 KiB reduction took 15 to
  37% less time under the default binding, and about the same unbound; the
  rule takes two there too, four nodes of four PEs, so `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=0`
  keeps one thread. The README's Limits say where they cost.
- **The zero-copy send, off by default** (`OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY`): a connection's
  buffer lent to the kernel rather than copied, one of its two at a time. Where
  the user's locked-memory limit refuses a send, the send goes again by copy and
  its connection copies from then on, which a notice naming the limit says,
  rather than ending the job.
- **TCP keepalive off where every node of a job has a loopback address**, as
  `osoe-launch`'s logical nodes have, unless `OSOE_KEEPALIVE` is set, and the
  check for a silent peer with it: on one machine there is no link to cut, and
  under load the loopback's backlog had ended healthy jobs.
- **A reopened connection buffer wakes as many waiting writers as its room
  holds messages of a chunk** (`OSOE_TURNOVER_WAKE=room`; P2), where 0.1.0
  woke every one - which had every writer of a node putting to one other node
  asleep at every turnover - with `all` and a chain of wakes, `hand-on`, as
  settings: in the development VM, 0.91 to 1.18 times the old rule's rate below
  64 writers and 1.13 to 1.94 at 64, its sleeps flat; timed on no cluster.
- **One PE carries a global exit for the whole job** (P1), the lowest-ranked of
  the nodes' first callers, where 0.1.0 had each node's first caller carry its
  node: PEs of several nodes calling `shmem_global_exit` at once - every PE
  that detects the same failure - under `srun -K1` or `osoe-launch` without
  `-k` could have the launcher kill another node's callers inside their
  handlers, their output lost and `srun` returning 143. Now a node that learns
  of a lower caller leaves with 0 before the job's carrier leaves - its callers
  after their handlers, but for a caller whose call another node's end
  outruns, which runs none - so the job has one bad exit, the last. Modelled
  first, each of its rules shown needed; the control segment's layout is 43.
- **Settings:** fourteen new - `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS`, `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE`,
  `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PLACES`, the three `OSOE_LINK_*`, `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY` and
  its threshold, `OSOE_WIRE_UNSENT_BYTES`, `OSOE_CHECK_COLLECTIVES`,
  `OSOE_TURNOVER_WAKE`, `OSOE_MESH_SLOT`, `OSOE_MESH_DATA_WIRES` and
  `OSOE_MESH_PORT_RANGE` - with `OSOE_MACHINE_PES`, which `osoe-launch` sets -
  and fourteen test hooks; six defaults changed - the collectives' fan-out from
  4 to 8, now a ceiling, their pieces in flight from 2 to 8 (4 at the step, 8
  since 2026-10-28), a connection's buffer from 128 to 256 KiB, the buffers'
  budget from 256 MiB to 1 GiB, which takes a job to 4,097 nodes where 0.1.0
  stopped at 1,025, a PE's landing zone from 256 KiB to its node's 4 MiB split
  among the node's PEs (above), and `OSOE_KEEPALIVE` off on loopback addresses
  (above). None removed.
- **The costs**: a PE's internal region is 25.3 MiB of address space at four
  nodes where it was 6.0, written only as collectives use it, though the
  start-up check of `/dev/shm`'s capacity counts it whole; a node's lead holds
  two sockets for every other node, so that at 256 PEs a node a hard limit of
  1,024 open files runs out at about 385 nodes, where 0.1.0's one socket ran out
  short of 800 - `shmem_init` raises the soft limit to the hard one, as it now
  does the locked-memory limit (the README's "What `shmem_init` changes in the
  process").
- **Nodes of 0.1.0 and 0.2 do not form one job**: their connections' hello
  differs, and a node of one takes the other's for a stranger's and does not
  answer it, so the job's start waits - its notice after 30 s and then every
  minute naming the node it waits for - until the launcher's timeout or
  `OSOE_NO_PROGRESS_TIMEOUT` ends it.
  Every node of a job must run the same osoe.
- **`osoe-launch` is installed**, with `oshcc`, `osoe-stat` and `osoe-pacer`;
  `osoe-stat` shows the link, the collectives' shape and a line for each thread
  that holds connections.
- **`osoe-stat --summary` walks a hung reduction, broadcast or gather** as it
  walks a barrier: each node's lead says what it waits for and the node to look
  at next, or, where it waits on a count several nodes add to, how many are
  missing.
- **A node whose PEs are in more than one PID namespace is refused** as it
  starts, naming two of them, where a death could go unseen or a live PE be
  taken for dead; and a leftover another namespace made is named as such.
- **A team of one PE takes no block of its node's pool**, where four
  `shmem_team_split_2d` grids of an `xrange` of 1 emptied the default pool at
  256 PEs a node.

**Defects of 0.1.0 that 0.2 fixes:**

- `osoe-pacer mix --seconds` could hang near its deadline: each PE decided by
  its own clock, at the top of each period, whether its time was up, and a run
  whose periods ended near the deadline could split - about one run in forty at
  60 s on the default seed. The pacer's own defect, not the library's; the time
  is now up when the PE that has run longest says so (`bc0751d`).
- A small message behind a stream on the same connection waited for every byte
  the kernel had queued ahead of it - 17 to 20 ms for a fetch-add on the Pi
  cluster, measured on v0.2 step 3's tree, which queued as 0.1.0 does - where the
  design bounded it to a couple of segments; the unsent limit above is the fix,
  which leaves it 3.3 to 3.9 ms, and the control connection takes the barrier's
  messages off the stream's connection altogether.
- A signal handler without `SA_RESTART` ended a timed futex wait early, so a
  program's own signals could hold off a PE's checks.
- The start-up check of the futex calls tried only the private kind, where a
  node's PEs wait on the shared one: under a sandbox that allowed the private
  kind alone, a job passed the check and died at its first shared wait.
- A `SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE` that was not a multiple of 16 bytes gave a little less
  than it asked for.
- `osoe-launch`, piped into a reader that left early, died of SIGPIPE before it
  removed a refused start's leftovers from `/dev/shm`.
- `osoe-stat --summary` on a job of one node printed tree epochs that do not
  exist there.

## Known limits

*At the tag: the Pi cluster's round trips against the floor's pins, as 0.1.0's
note opened with them.* The README's Limits list every limit a program can meet;
in short:

- **Tested on aarch64, and natively on x86-64 since 2026-10-12**: three
  sessions on eight Google Cloud `c4-standard-16` (Debian 13, Linux 6.12), the
  last on the tree of 2026-10-28 - the four presets, tier 3, the single-node
  build and the stress tester each on a node by itself in the first, and jobs
  of up to 512 PEs across the nodes, Sandia OpenSHMEM's test suite among them;
  no suite that checks results has run across separate x86-64 machines (the
  README's Limits). Every node of a job must have the same architecture, and
  run the same osoe.
- **A link above a gigabit has been measured on Google Cloud's only** (eight
  nodes at 23 Gbit/s, `docs/fast-link-results.md`), where progress helpers were
  found to pay; `docs/fast-link.md` is the runbook for another.
- **TCP only**, no RDMA backend. Without progress helpers - on a job of one or
  two nodes, or where a node runs more PEs than it has CPUs - one thread a node
  moves every message between nodes; a pair of nodes' puts and gets go by one
  of its two connections, so a network that limits each flow gives them one
  flow's share.
- **A large get from another node is held by its PE's landing zone**: by
  default its node's 4 MiB split among the node's PEs, so sixteen pieces of up
  to 64 KiB in flight at one to four PEs a node, eight at eight and four from
  sixteen, which the zone's threads and contexts share.
- **Teams**: a PE in at most `OSOE_MAX_TEAMS` teams at once (256 by default), a
  node holding at most `OSOE_TEAM_BLOCKS` (1,024).
- **Scale**: at most 4,097 nodes at the default connection budget, every pair of
  nodes connected twice at start-up; 65,535 PEs a node. Träff's reduction runs on
  a team of at most 65,536 nodes, whatever the fan-out ceiling; past 512 nodes at
  the default ceiling of 8 a lane is a row longer for each doubling of the nodes,
  128 KiB on each of a PE's 17 at the defaults (64 KiB at the 4 pieces in
  flight it had until 2026-10-28), and its gain there is the cost model's, not a
  measurement's.
- **Memory**: one symmetric heap a PE, its size fixed at start-up, in
  `/dev/shm`; global and static variables symmetric only in a program linked by
  `oshcc`.
- **Threads**: `shmem_init` provides `SHMEM_THREAD_SERIALIZED`;
  `SHMEM_THREAD_MULTIPLE` comes through `shmem_init_thread`.
- **Failures**: a PE or a node that dies ends the whole job, as does a process
  that leaves without `shmem_finalize`; a collective the PEs of one node call
  differently is named by the check that is on by default since 2026-10-28
  (`OSOE_CHECK_COLLECTIVES`) and goes unnamed only with it set to 0; and
  `shmem_global_exit` runs `atexit` handlers only on the callers it sees in
  time - on the library's own thread, for at most 10 s - and none on a caller
  whose call another node's end, or another caller's on its node, outruns.
- **C only**: no Fortran bindings; `shmemx.h` is empty.
- **MPI in the same program** works with Open MPI 5.0.7, started by
  `srun --mpi=pmix_v5` or by `mpirun` through a wrapper; beside it the deadlock
  detector proves nothing, and `shmem_global_exit`'s status does not reach
  `mpirun` (the README's limits).
- **Slurm**: run with `--cpu-bind=none`; a login that ends on a node just before
  a job starts there can stall its start (systemd-logind's `RemoveIPC`).

What the reviews left for after this release is on `docs/release-checklist.md`'s
"After the release".

## Where to start

The README's quick start builds osoe - about thirty seconds on a development
virtual machine's four cores - and
runs a first program, and its desktop section builds the single-node build
where liburing is not to be had; `docs/README.md` says what the rest of the
repository holds.
