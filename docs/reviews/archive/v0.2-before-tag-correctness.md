# v0.2 before the tag, question 2: is the concurrency of what changed since round nine right?

Round ten of the adversarial review (T13), the correctness investigator. The tree is `main` at
`5cefc7fb` (2026-10-30), read in the worktree `<repo>/build/scratch/wt/review-ten` and built and
run in the development VM `osoe` under `/var/tmp/review-ten-correctness/`; the models run on
the Mac. Round nine reviewed `3b45472`; its correctness deliverable is
`v0.2-before-part4-correctness.md`. Evidence for this round is under
`<repo>/build/scratch/review-ten/correctness/` (scripts in `vm/`, logs in `logs/`).

Labels: *documented* (a record or the README says it), *observed in source*, *measured* (run
here), *recalled*.

## Verdict

**Correct as far as I could run and read it; nothing blocks the tag, and nothing needs fixing
before it on correctness.** The newest work, which had no adversarial read of its own, holds:

- **P1, the single job-wide carrier**, is right on three to eight nodes, where its model has two:
  with every order of three nodes' goodbyes forced by holds - the lowest's held a second, a higher
  carrier outranked through a third node's adopted word before the lowest's own goodbye could
  reach it, the lowest caller on the middle node - at no helpers, at two, at counts that differ by
  node, eager and on demand, while every PE streams to another node, and with every PE calling at
  once unheld, on up to eight nodes, the job ended with one rank nonzero, its own status the
  launcher's, nobody killed and every handler that began finished, in every one of 108 runs.
  The three of the model's six rule breakages the record had not made in the C, I made: each
  fails P1's own tests.
- **P2's room count** loses no writer and starves none: the count bounds only the sleepers woken,
  at least one an open, the hand-on and the pass cover the rest, the kernel wakes sleepers in
  order, and the sweep bounds anything the count gets wrong to 100 ms - time, never a result;
  which is also why no test sees the count (C10-1).
- **H23's data wires on demand**: one thread owns every slot's state, the schedule rules out
  crossing calls, the hand-over and its closing at the abort are ordered on that one thread; an
  abort while wires are held mid-connect ended right with callers on two to four nodes.
- **Layout 42 and the defaults**: P7, D26, D1 and T38 are sound; **the collectives' check now on
  by default cannot fail a conforming program** - it compares only what §9.10 requires equal,
  and `collect`'s differing counts not at all; the per-call choices (P4, P31) are computed from
  what every node holds alike.

Every model the build registers passes, 52 runs, each breakage failing for the property it
names; the internode suite passed whole beside four busy loops, 633 of 633, and the newest tests
five times over beside them. Round nine's five findings are settled as recorded. Two findings,
neither a defect in what runs: C10-1 (after release), no test of the C fails when the room
count's open wakes too few writers, or none - a mutant that wakes none passes every turnover
test, two hundred times slower; and C10-2 (minor), a README row that still says a buffer's room
wakes every writer.

## Findings, ranked

### C10-1. No test fails when the room count's open wakes too few writers, or none (after release; high confidence; *measured*)

- **Location**: `connection.c:156-160` (the open under `room`: `osoe_wait_word_wake_up_to`
  with `ceil(room / chunk)`, at least one); the tests `shmem.turnover-wake.*`
  (`test-turnover-wake.c:18-24`: "Checked by counts, not by time"); the invariant that would
  name a lost wake, `invariant.missed_wake` (`count_if_lost`, `transport-internode.c:597-604`),
  which counts only a sweep that found room with the word unmoved.
- **Failure scenario**: a later change to the open - the count taken from the full buffer it
  reopens, as the model's `room_count_before_open` has it, a count of zero, or the futex wake
  dropped while the word is still bumped - leaves every writer asleep at a reopening until its
  sweep. Nothing is lost - each wakes within 100 ms and finds the room - so every check of
  the suite still passes, and the default that P2 made for its speed is two hundred times slower
  on its own test without a test saying so.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `vm/mutants/p2.sh`, `logs/mutants/test-m8-*`, `test-m9-*`, in a
  private copy, restored by `cp` and `touch` and passing after): `m8`, the open's word bumped and
  no sleeper woken, passed all eight turnover tests - `turnover-wake.default` and `.room` in
  19.95 and 19.77 s where the restored tree takes 0.10, `small.room` in 3.04 s against 0.09;
  `m9`, the open waking one, as the hand-on does, passed all eight in the restored tree's time.
  The design is held by `turnover_wake_model.py`, which fails `room_count_before_open` with
  LOST WAKE; the C's count is held by nothing.
- **Why not more**: a wrong count costs time, never a result or a hang - the sweep bounds it -
  and the hand-on half is held (`small.room` requires hand-ons). But the room count is the
  default since 2026-10-29 and exists for its speed (H12), so its regression would be silent.
- **Remedy**: one assertion that tells a wake from a sweep - in `test-turnover-wake.c`, that
  under `room` the writers' sleeps ended by a sweep with room waiting are few, from a counter of
  them beside `missed_wake` (a sweep that found room after a word that moved since the writer
  slept); or a bound on the test's time against `all`'s, which measures the same bytes.

### C10-2. The README's cost table still says a buffer's room wakes every writer (minor; high confidence; *observed in source*)

- **Location**: `README.md:837`, "Scaling with nodes, not PEs", the table "Time", row
  "waiting": "O(1) a waiter - but room made in a connection's buffer wakes every writer waiting
  for it (Limits)". Against it: `README.md:1487-1501` (the Limits entry it points to, rewritten
  for P2: "A reopening now wakes as many as its room holds messages of a chunk, four at the
  defaults (`OSOE_TURNOVER_WAKE=room`)"), the settings row `README.md:1936`, and the code,
  `connection.c:156-160` (`osoe_wait_word_wake_up_to` with `ceil(room / chunk)`, at least one).
- **What**: since `676fcca2` (P2, 2026-10-29) the default wakes up to the room's count of writers
  and hands on from there; `all`, which wakes every one, is a setting. The row dates from
  `375973ae` (2026-10-04) and was not touched by P2, so the README says two things about the
  default at once - against the author's rule that the README is true at every commit
  (`git blame`: the line is `375973ae`'s; `676fcca2` is an ancestor of `5cefc7fb`).
- **Failure scenario**: none at run time. A reader sizing a node for many writers to one peer
  takes from the cost table the cost P2 removed - every writer woken and most asleep again at
  each reopening, H12's quarter of a node's PEs a 64 KiB - where the Limits entry two screens on
  says the opposite.
- **Remedy**: the row's clause to "room made in a connection's buffer wakes as many writers as
  it holds messages of a chunk (Limits)", or the clause dropped.

No other finding: nothing I ran or read in the concurrency of the changes since round nine is
wrong, and nothing is rated *fix before release* or worse. C10-1 is a gap in what the tests can
see, not a defect in what they test.

## What is sound, and what convinced me

### P1, the global exit's single job-wide carrier (layout 43)

**Sound as built, on three to eight nodes as on two.** *Observed in source*: the three rules are
built as the record says - `peer_outranked_here` (`progress.c:1223-1226`) keeps a carrier's
goodbye going to a peer whose own named a higher caller, in both `flush_aborting` and
`flush_control_aborting`; `osoe_progress_outranked` (`progress.c:2212-2221`) is any wire's
goodbye naming a lower rank; the lead's wait (`progress-host.c:1712-1720`) ends on that or on
every peer's end, none excluded, and marks the word on the agent thread before that thread's own
`osoe_abort_end`, which reads it (`abort.c:143-176`), so the lead's two reads of the word need no
ordering beyond program order; the carrier's wait (`liveness.c:169`, `:187`, `:189`) asks `stop`
before each 100 ms poll and after the batch, and its status is reread after the wait. The
argument the model leaves to the C, for any number of nodes: every goodbye names a node's word,
and a word names either its own winning caller or, adopted, a word some other node sent - so
every rank a goodbye names is some node's carrier (or a fatal's declarer), the lowest carrier is
never outranked by a global exit's goodbye, and it tells every other node directly on the control
wire, which owner 0 holds at every helper count and which `flush_control_aborting` can skip only
for a wire that failed or ended. A higher carrier cannot end its wait by "every peer ended"
first, since the lowest does not end until it has (or its 20 s bound has run out).

*Measured*, with a private copy of the library in the VM (`vm/patch-holds.py`: the test hold
`OSOE_TEST_HOLD_GOODBYE` taking several `FROM:TO:MS` pairs, so that the order of three nodes'
goodbyes can be forced; `vm/patch-trace.py`: each carrier's lead saying, at the end of its wait,
which wire's goodbye named whom), a program of my own (`vm/callers.c`: callers each with a delay,
a status and a second-long handler) under `osoe-launch` without `-k`, which ends the job at the
first bad exit as `srun -K1` does (`vm/callers.sh` requires one rank nonzero, the expected one
with its status, the launcher's status the same, nobody killed, every handler that began
finished):

- **Three carriers, the lowest's goodbyes held a second** (`0,0,1,1,2,2`, PEs 1, 3 and 5 at 200,
  300 and 400 ms): PE 1 carries every time; the trace shows node 2 and node 1 each outranked by
  node 0's goodbye on its control wire once the hold ends, node 1 having told node 2 meanwhile
  since node 2's goodbye named a higher caller (`control-slot-1-named-5` at node 1).
- **Outranked through a third node's adopted word** (callers PE 1 and PE 5, node 1 none; node 0's
  goodbye to node 2 held 1.5 s, node 1's 300 ms, node 2's to node 1 500 ms): node 2 is outranked
  300 ms after PE 1's call by `control-slot-1-named-1` - node 1's goodbye carrying the word it
  adopted from node 0 - 1.2 s before node 0's own goodbye could reach it; PE 1 carries. The same
  with the callers as their nodes' leads (PEs 0 and 4); and with the first call on the middle
  node and the lowest caller's 100 ms later (PEs 2, 0 and 5 at 200, 300 and 400 ms, node 1's
  goodbyes held a second): PE 0 carries.
- Each of those four at no helpers and at two, eager and on demand (16 of 16), and at helper
  counts that differ by node (`-e RANK:OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=`, 0/2/1 and 2/0/1 by node; 12 of
  12) - the README allows nodes to differ, and no test of the global exit had them differ.
- **Every PE calling at once, unheld**, on three nodes (`0,0,1,1,2,2`) and four
  (`0,1,2,3,0,1,2,3`), at no helpers and two, eager and on demand, three runs each (24 of 24):
  one rank nonzero with its own status every time - ranks 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 among the runs, as
  the nodes' words fell - nobody killed, every handler that began finished, 2.0 to 4.2 s from the
  first call to the end.
- **While every PE streams** 1 MiB puts to the PE half the job away - on demand, the data wires
  being made as the calls come - the held three-carrier case and every PE of four nodes calling,
  the calls 1, 50 and 200 ms into the stream, at no helpers and two, on demand and eager (24 of

- **An abort mid-connect** (`OSOE_TEST_HOLD_DATA_WIRES=0:20000`, on demand, the link set by hand
  as `data-wires.sh` does; `callers.c`'s `early`: the callers call before any collective while the
  others' first `shmem_malloc` waits for node 0's held wires), callers on two, three and four

So the case the model could not reach is right in the C: no run of 108 left two ranks nonzero,
killed a rank, or lost a handler that began. Logs: `logs/p1/e1.txt` to `e6.txt`.

**Each rule's test, broken on purpose.** The record's four C mutants
(`build/scratch/carrier/mutants/`) broke three of the model's six breakages - the old send, the
carrier not rereading the word, the outranked lead not waiting for its carrier - and one of their
own, the word never marked; the other three of the six I made in a private copy
(`vm/mutants/make.py`, restored by `cp` and `touch`, the restored tree 9 of 9) and ran P1's nine
tests of two callers and of every PE calling against each (`logs/mutants/`):

| Mutant | The model's breakage | Caught by |
|---|---|---|
| `m5-lowest-excludes`: the lowest node excludes a peer whose goodbye named another caller, as before P1 | `single_lowest_excludes`, NO_KILL | 4 of 9: `kills.two-nodes` on all three maps and `kills.all-callers.0-0-1-1-2-2`, a rank killed each time |
| `m6-status-kept`: the demoted carrier keeps its status | `single_status_kept`, NO_KILL | 2 of 9: `kills.two-nodes.0-1-0-1`, `kills.all-callers.1-1-0-0` |

So every rule of the three is held by a test in the C, not only by the model.

What the bounds leave, as before P1 and as `abort.h` says: an outranked node marks its word only
when its lead's wait ends, at most `OSOE_ABORT_LEAD_WAIT_SECONDS` (20) after its goodbyes, which
take at most the grace (10); its carrier's own wait is `OSOE_ABORT_CARRIER_WAIT_SECONDS` (30)
from about the same moment. A lower node whose goodbye is held past those - a node stopped, or
goodbyes acknowledged only at the grace - leaves two carriers, each with its status, and under
`srun -K1` the later one's PEs killed. That is `abort.h`'s "a bound can run out first, and the
order with it", and the README's processes held or stopped that "can outlast the waits' bounds"
(`README.md:2212-2215`), not a new departure; a held goodbye of 500 ms to 1.5 s, the case P1
is for, is far inside it.
*Measured* to be just that (`vm/e5.sh`, `logs/p1/e5.txt`, twice, with and without `-k`): node
0's goodbye to node 1 held 25 s, callers PE 1 and PE 3 - node 1's lead's wait expired at 20.0 s,
not outranked (`expired=1`), its lead ended at once, which ended node 0's wait by "every peer
ended" 6 ms later, and both carriers ran their handlers and left nonzero, 13 and 11, the
launcher's status 13, at 21.3 s; nothing hung, and nothing outlasted the README's 50 s. Under
`-K1` the second carrier would have been killed had its handler been slower than the first's.
Nothing short of a node held or stopped delays the lowest node's goodbye that long: it is sent
on the control wire, which carries no bulk, at the start of its lead's abort. One
interaction I checked and do not count: a fatal's goodbye naming a rank below a carrier also
marks that carrier's node outranked (`osoe_progress_outranked` reads any disposition), so its PEs
leave with 0 - but the fatal's own node leaves with the fatal's status at once, which is the
job's either way, so nothing a program sees changes from before P1.

### P2, the turnover wake's room count

**Sound; no lost writer and no starved one, by reading and by the model.** *Observed in source*:
the open stores the head seq_cst, then `osoe_wait_word_wake_up_to` loads the waiter count
seq_cst, bumps the word and wakes `ceil((capacity - extent) / chunk)`, at least one
(`connection.c:151-160`) - the two-sided protocol of `wait_word_model.py` with a count; a
registered writer not yet asleep sees the bump and does not sleep, so the count only bounds the
sleepers woken, never the writers that proceed. A woken writer that took less than a chunk
hands on one more (`transport-internode.c:654-664`); one that found too little but room worth a
part passes once a turnover (`:748-757`); every writer that finds too little marks the
connection and wakes the lead, which seals and reopens, so every turnover wakes at least one,
and the kernel wakes a futex's sleepers in the order they slept, among threads of one
scheduling class (*recalled*), so a writer re-queued after a pass is behind the one it passed
to. `turnover_wake_model.py` holds the room design live in its five cases and fails
`room_count_before_open` with LOST WAKE (re-run below; I read its writer and agent,
`:60-145`). Two things the model leaves out and I checked by hand: the
pre-open drain's replies, which make the C's room smaller than the model's full buffer - the
count then smaller with it, still at least one; and a put's part taking all the room it finds
(`reserve_up_to`), which leaves the other woken writers nothing - they mark and sleep, and the
next open wakes them first. Whatever the count gets wrong costs time, not a result: a writer
left asleep ends its sleep at the sweep, 100 ms. *Measured*: the `turnover-wake` tests among
the 633 under load (below), with `CHECK_INVARIANTS`.

The limit of that evidence is C10-1: `invariant.missed_wake` cannot see a writer the open did
not wake, since the word moved, and the tests are checked by counts, not by time - so the
open's count is held by the model alone, as two mutants showed.

### H23, data wires on demand, and the mesh schedule

**Sound by reading, and the abort mid-connect right with several callers.** *Observed in
source*, `data-wires.c`: every slot's state is the agent's alone; a need is acted on once
(`need`, `:207-224`, guarded by the slot's state), the schedule's rule makes crossing calls
impossible (`caller_made` refuses a call from the node the schedule has this one call,
`:304-313`; `osoe_data_wires_requested` refuses a request where it does not, `:226-232`), and a
request that arrives after the peer has dialled for its own need finds the slot made and does
nothing; the hand-over is a release store of the socket then the owner's mark (`made`,
`:297-300`), taken by an exchange (`:555-560`); the abort sets `aborting` and then the
release store that closes the hand-over, on the one thread that hands over (`:567-586`), so a
socket made after it is told on the spot (`tell`) and never handed, and a helper's pass after
its acquire load of the close attaches every socket handed before it - the record's argument,
which holds. The blocked kind (`OSOE_BLOCKED_DETAIL_CONNECTING`, `transport-internode.c:719-727`
and `:762-766`) is a description for `osoe-stat` and the detector's report, and changes no wait.
`data_wire_model.py` and `mesh_model.py` re-run, every case as expected (below).

### Layout 42, and the defaults of 2026-10-28

- **P7**: every use of a team's `block_index` is on a path a team of one never reaches - its
  sync and every collective return before (`barrier.c:56-60`, `collectives.c`), and `team.c:556`
  gives it `UINT32_MAX`; `pool_dry_model.py`'s `one` re-run below. **D26**: the lead is judged
  by pid only where the namespaces agree, a degradation to waiting rather than a wrong verdict.
  **D1** and **T38**: published words and a counter, each written by one thread, read by
  `osoe-stat`; D1's waits on a count deliberately name no node rather than read fields the
  count does not yet cover.
- **P39, the collectives' check on**: *cannot fail a conforming program*, by reading every call
  record it compares (`collectives.c:174-178`, `:516`, `:815`, `:976-978`, `:1035-1036`,
  `barrier.c:47`) against §9.10's rules. Each compares only what the specification requires to
  be equal across a team's PEs - kind, operation and element, a broadcast's root, an fcollect's
  or alltoall's block (§9.10.6: "the same ... values for values of arguments dst, sst,
  nelems" for `alltoalls`), a scan's kind - and `collect`, whose counts may differ, says 0 for
  its bytes. Its words are per team number and per PE, written before the arrival's seq_cst RMW
  (`base/epoch.c:17-20`) and read by the one that sees every arrival, so a fast PE cannot
  overwrite them before they are compared: none passes the release of the collective it said
  them for. Barrier and sync say the same record,
  so a program mixing them is not named either - no false positive, only a mismatch it misses.
- **P36, helpers `auto`**: each PE computes the count from the job's nodes, its node's (or,
  under `osoe-launch`, its machine's) PEs and the CPUs online, so a node's PEs agree, as the
  rendezvous checks; nodes may differ, and P1's runs above at counts that differ by node held.
- **P37, the window of 8**: a parameter of protocols whose models are parameterised by it and
  the layout sized for it; nothing new to the order of anything.
- **The choices made per call** - P4's two-node cost, P31's way of `collect`'s places - are
  functions of the job's link, which every PE holds alike after `shmem_init`, of settings the
  rendezvous checks, and of the call's arguments: every node of a team computes the same
  choice, so none can take a different protocol from its peers (`internode-generic.c:760-790`,
  `collectives.c:680-700`; `exchange_positions` of the job's node count).
