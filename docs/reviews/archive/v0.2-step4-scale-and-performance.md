# v0.2 step 4, question 3: does osoe scale, and perform, as the README says?

Review of `main` at `c897948`, run on 2026-10-09 against
`docs/reviews/adversarial-review-prompt.md`, Question 3 only: v0.2's second full
review, at the merge point of its step 4. It covers everything since the first
review point (`7053819`): step 2, the tree's shape chosen from the link; step 3,
Träff's circulant reduce-scatter and allgather; and step 4 - Part 0's
one-thread ladder (a put filling the room a buffer has left, the kernel's unsent
queue limited by the link, the 256 KiB buffer, provided buffers parsed in place)
and the zero-copy send behind `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY`, Part 1's owners, Part 2's
control wire (two wires a node pair), Part 3's helper threads
(`OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS`, none by default). The first review point's report in
this area (`v0.2-step1-scale-and-performance.md`) is the baseline and its
findings are checked as claims; so are the answers to the per-part adversarial
reads. Labels: *documented*, *observed in source*, *measured*, *recalled*,
*inferred*. Severity: *blocks release*, *fix before release*, *after release*,
*minor*, where release means 0.2's tag. Known and not reported as discoveries,
as the lead asked: design §3.12 is owed a rewrite; ThreadSanitizer's blindness
through another process; question 4 (a blocking get a piece at a time); Part 4,
a fast link, has not run; the test that every `OSOE_` setting is in the README

## Verdict

**In this question's area the merge point can go to 0.2 as the README describes
it: nothing blocks, and nothing must change first.** The scaling table's memory
rows are the code's to within a few per cent at every size I could build, and
nothing step 4 added grows with a node's PEs: a lead's private state grows by
8,118 bytes a peer node (the README: 8.1 KB), and by 544 bytes in all when its
node's PEs double; the connection area by 513.4 KiB a peer (512 KiB of buffers
and 1.25 KiB of state); the control segment by 288 KiB a PE; sockets are
exactly 2(n - 1); each helper costs its lead about a mebibyte, a ring and a
thread, whatever the job's size; the owner of each wire is arithmetic, with no
table (*measured*, *observed in source*). Idle helpers sleep: 0.3 to 0.8 ms of
CPU each in four seconds of a waiting job, at two, three and eight helpers
(*measured*). On the Pi the step's two headline numbers held at the merge point
against step 3's: a barrier behind a 1 MiB put stream 1,665 to 1,669 µs at the
median where it was 18,322 to 19,917, a fetch-add behind it 3,871 to 3,883
where it was 17,155 to 19,931, the stream at 112.1 MiB/s and its sending
agent's CPU a gibibyte 23% lower, every remote line of `osoe-bench` within 0.7%
(*measured*, the lead's runs). And over loopback, on a quiet VM this
morning, the tip still moves what the README says it does: the 64 KiB put
stream 10,335 to 10,952 MiB/s, `shmem.floor`'s library stream 0.71 to 0.76 of
the bare one beside it, where the README quotes 10.7 to 10.9 GiB/s and 0.73 to
0.75 (*measured*, six runs, three of them under `fixed`, S5).

What should change, none of it first. The first review point's Q3-1 is half
closed: a helper that spins 30 or 200 µs before it sleeps fails the step's own
`shmem.helper-wake`, its wake count collapsing, while 854 other tests pass at
two helpers and the job burns 7 to 20 times the CPU; an agent that spins passes
854 of the 855 at the default of no helpers, and halves the barrier's time for
six times the CPU, which a benchmark would reward (S1). Träff's reduction runs
on at most 512 nodes at the default ceiling, where the scaling section says
65,536 - and past 512 the project's own cost model has the default forgo a
1 MiB reduction 3.2 to 4.3 times faster (S2). The shared receive pool's
re-arms grow with the peers sending at once (S3). Runs at two helpers on two
days, and mine on the cluster at 23:38, agree that helpers under Slurm's
binding make a 64 KiB broadcast 6 to 30% slower and a 4 KiB alltoall 20 to
35%, and a 64 KiB reduction a sixth to a third faster, and unbound cost the two
2 to 9% and buy the reduction nothing - which the record's summary leaves out
and the README calls unmeasured (S4). `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE=fixed` sets every
wire's unsent limit to its 16 KiB floor on any link, which neither the README
nor the design notes say; on the quiet VM it cost the loopback stream nothing
(S5). Several quoted numbers are older or narrower than their sentences (S6),
and a few small things are listed last (S7).

## Findings, ranked

### S1. "Nothing may spin, helpers included": a helper's idle wait is guarded, by a wake count; the agent's and the PEs' waits are not - the first review point's Q3-1, half open

- **Location**: `docs/prompts/v0.2-step4-progress-helpers-prompt.md:89`
  ("Nothing may spin (§3.11), helpers included"); `docs/decisions/README.md:40`
  ("no spin before blocking", a rule in force, whose named check counts memory
  orders); `docs/release-checklist.md:227-235`, where the test sits on the
  after-release list "best before v0.2 step 4", unedited since step 3; a
  helper's idle wait (`progress-host.c:1173`, `osoe_ring_wait_wake`), the
  agent's (the same call, `osoe_progress_host_wait`, `progress-host.c:1530-1536`),
  a PE's futex waits (`region-wait.c`, `base/futex-*.c`);
  `shmem/tests/test-helper-wake.c`
  (*documented*, *observed in source*).
- **Scenario**: a change that polls before it sleeps - the ordinary "spin a
  little for latency" change - in the agent or in a PE's wait goes in green, as
  at the first review point; every PE of a node, and under
  `OSOE_AGENT_CPUS=task` the lead PE's core with its agent and helpers
  (README:1416), pays for it.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `out-mutant/`): a mutant whose helpers spin up to
  200 µs on their ring's completions and their wake word before the idle wait
  (`mutant/mutate.py`). The release suite at two helpers: **854 of 855 passed**;
  the one failure, `shmem.helper-wake.0-1-2-3`, counts the wakes each put
  causes, and saw helper 2 woken 3 times in 100 puts where it asks at least 10 -
  a spinning helper is not asleep to be woken. At 30 µs the same test failed
  five runs of five (woken 1 to 2 times). So the step's own test catches a
  helper's spin, as a side effect of counting wakes - the guard the first
  review asked for, for the helpers alone. What the spin costs, osoe-bench's
  barrier and collectives at four logical nodes of two PEs and two helpers,
  alternating with the tip: at 200 µs the job's wall time 22.1 to 24.0 s
  against 7.9 to 8.7 and its user CPU 52.8 to 53.4 s against 2.6 to 2.9 (three
  rounds), an 8-byte alltoall 4.4 to 4.6 times slower, a 4 KiB one 3.6 to 4.3;
  at 30 µs 4.6 s against 2.8 and 9.3 CPU-seconds against 1.3 (one round). And
  a CPU threshold would have missed it: the idle probe (PE 0 asleep four
  seconds, the rest in a barrier) saw the 200 µs mutant's helpers use 7.5 to
  9.2 ms each against the tip's 0.3 to 0.6 - fifteen times as much, and 0.2% of
  a core, far under the "few percent" the checklist's test would fail at.
  The agent has no such guard (*measured*, `mutant/mutate-agent.py`): a lead's
  agent that spins up to 200 µs before its idle wait passed 854 of the 855
  tests at no helpers, the default; the one failure, `shmem.helper-wake` - a
  test at two helpers - failed once in the suite's parallel run and passed five
  runs of five alone, as the tip did (`wake-ab.txt`): the load, not the spin.
  No test counts the agent's wakes as `helper-wake` counts a helper's
  (`test-agent-wake.c` times a one-node finalize; `test-work-discovery.c`
  reports the writer's skipped wakes, which a spinning lead would raise). And
  the benchmarks would reward it: osoe-bench at four logical nodes of two PEs,
  alternating with the tip, two rounds - the barrier 32 µs against 73 to 74,
  an 8-byte reduction 57 to 63 against 115 to 116, a 4 KiB alltoall 91 to 111
  against 176 to 186, while the job's user CPU was 7.1 to 8.7 s against 1.2
  to 1.3 (`agent-bench.txt`) - a spin buys latency with a core, as the first
  review's mutant in the PEs' futex wait did, which passed both whole suites
  (`v0.2-step1-scale-and-performance.md`, Q3-1). Today the code keeps the rule
  (*measured*, `out-idle/`): the agents 0.5 to 1.5 ms of CPU in four seconds of
  a waiting job, the helpers 0.3 to 0.8 ms each at two, three and eight
  helpers (eight being more than the three peers, so five idle), the waiting
  PEs' main threads 0.1 to 1.7 ms.
- **Severity**: **after release**, as before, and narrower: the code is right by
  measurement and the helpers have a guard. The checklist's item should say
  what the mutant shows - count wakes, or CPU per idle transition, rather than
  a few per cent of the machine - and cover the agent at no helpers, the
  default.
- **Confidence**: high.

### S2. Träff's reduction takes at most 512 nodes at the default ceiling; the scaling section says 65,536

- **Location**: README:739-740 ("a team of more than 65,536 nodes takes the tree
  at every size"); README:1390 (`OSOE_COLLECTIVE_FANOUT`: "it runs only where
  ⌈log₂ n⌉ is at most the ceiling + 1 - on up to 512 nodes at the default");
  `shmem/src/collective-shape.c:231-232` (`rows <= ceiling + 1`);
  `shmem/src/internode-generic.c:745-760` and `internal-region.h:304`
  (`OSOE_INTERNAL_CIRCULANT_ROWS_MOST = 16`, the 65,536) (*observed in source*).
- **Scenario**: a reader of the scaling section - the one written for hundreds
  of nodes and the 4,000-node seam - takes Träff's for every team up to 65,536
  nodes. At the defaults a team of 513 nodes or more takes the tree at every
  size; at 4,000 nodes Träff's needs `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_FANOUT` of 11 or more,
  which also widens the barrier's tree and the small class's (they take the
  ceiling whole) and costs every PE about 3.6 MiB more of its region for each
  step of the ceiling (17 lanes of 64 KiB, 1,024 team numbers of 2.5 KiB;
  README:793).
- **Evidence**: the two conditions above, both enforced. What the default
  leaves off past 512 nodes, by the project's own model (*inferred*: the closed
  forms of `tools/modelcheck/shape_timing.py`, which `collective-shape.c`
  follows line for line, at the Pi's fitted link - a hop of 86 µs, 7 µs a
  message, 116 bytes a µs - run from a copy in the scratchpad): a 1 MiB
  reduction by Träff's takes 1.34 times less time than the best tree at 4
  nodes (the Pi measured 1.32 to 1.41), 1.74 at 7, 2.32 at 64, 3.23 at 500 and
  512 - where the default takes it - and 3.20 at 513, 3.58 at 1,024 and 4.26
  at 4,000, where the default takes the tree.
- **Severity**: **after release** - a design note for hundreds of nodes and the
  seam: Träff's rounds are coupled to a lane's rows, which the fan-out ceiling
  sizes, so the reduction the design measured as its best leaves the default
  at 513 nodes. **Minor** for the sentence at README:739-740 (the settings
  table is right), which should say 512 at the default and what to set past it.
- **Confidence**: high.

### S3. The receive pool is one per owner whatever its peers, and its re-arms grow with the peers sending at once

- **Location**: `shmem/src/ring.h:16-26` and `wire-settings.h:19-26` (64
  provided buffers of 16 KiB, shared by every wire of the ring, so that
  "the memory a node holds for receiving does not grow with its peers");
  `ring.c`'s `received` (every `-ENOBUFS` terminates the wire's multishot
  receive, re-armed at the end of the pass); README:1427 ("1 MiB at the
  defaults, however many peers it has") (*observed in source*, *documented*).
- **Scenario**: a node that many peers send to at once - an alltoall, a
  gather to one PE, a hot spot - empties the 64 buffers in each pass; every
  other wire with bytes waiting then completes with `-ENOBUFS` and is re-armed,
  pass after pass, so each pass costs a submission and a completion for each
  waiting peer and moves at most 1 MiB. At 32 peers that is cheap; at the
  hundreds of peers the README designs for, on a link where 1 MiB lasts 330 µs,
  it is not known to be.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `out-fanin/`; node 0's owner 0, 64 KiB puts, 16
  each a peer, 20 rounds): one peer, 4 `-ENOBUFS` in 1,479 completions; eight
  peers, 832 in 12,233; 32 peers, 14,845 terminations and as many re-arms
  against 42,085 buffers received, about ten a pass over 1,498 passes. With
  1,024 buffers, 0, 0 and 618. The memory claim holds; the rate in this loaded
  VM did not suffer (2,654 MiB/s at 64 buffers against 2,123 at 1,024, the
  larger pool no faster), so this is a cost that grows, not one shown to bind.
- **Severity**: **after release** - a Part 4 item: the fan-in at many peers on a
  fast link, with `transport.am.receive_enobufs` beside the rate, and the
  README's buffers row to say what a job of many peers might set.
- **Confidence**: medium (the counts are exact; the cost at scale is inferred).

### S4. Helpers' cost on the Pi is in runs that agree, and neither the record's summary nor the README says it

- **Location**: README:1121-1126 ("what helpers add is the traffic to several
  peers at once, and nothing has measured it"), :808 ("what they buy has not
  been measured"), :1416 ("nothing has measured what that buys");
  `docs/decisions/2026-10-08-helpers.md:311-317` ("At two helpers, once, as
  information ... the bench's 8-byte lines ... within the spread of the runs at
  none") (*documented*).
- **Scenario**: a user who reads that helpers spread a node's traffic tries
  `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=2` on a small cluster under Slurm's default binding,
  and a program heavy in broadcasts or alltoalls slows by a quarter; the README
  warns only that helpers then "add threads but no CPU".
- **Evidence** (*measured*: the raw `osoe-bench` outputs of the lead's run
  today and Part 3's run of 2026-10-08, the collective lines that the
  comparisons leave out; medians in µs):

  | Line | No helpers, six runs on the two days | Two helpers, 2026-10-08 | Two helpers, 2026-10-09 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | broadcast 64 KiB, 4 × 4 | 1,503 to 1,540 | 1,883 | 1,891 |
  | broadcast 64 KiB, 4 × 1 | 1,509 to 1,515 | 1,954 | 1,957 |
  | alltoall 4 KiB blocks, 4 × 4 | 2,573 to 2,608 | 3,483 | 3,434 |
  | alltoall 4 KiB blocks, 4 × 1 | 409 to 412 | 496 | 496 |
  | reduce 64 KiB, 4 × 4 | 2,262 to 2,280 | 1,452 | 1,440 |
  | reduce 64 KiB, 4 × 1 | 1,880 to 1,913 | 1,595 | 1,589 |

  A 64 KiB broadcast 22 to 30% slower, a 4 KiB alltoall 20 to 35% slower, a
  64 KiB reduction taking 15 to 37% less time, and the mailbox about a fifth faster
  (45,789 messages a second today against 35,205 to 38,173; the record's
  46,570 to 48,101) - each run of two helpers within 3% of the other. Those
  runs were under Slurm's default binding, where each lead's main thread, agent
  and helpers share one core (README:1416). So I ran the tip's
  `osoe-bench collectives` at 4 × 4 on the cluster from 23:38 to 23:41, no
  helpers against two, under the default binding and `--cpu-bind=none`,
  alternating, two rounds each (*measured*, `out-pi/`): bound, the same picture
  - the broadcast 1,522 to 1,525 µs against 1,623 to 1,857, the 4 KiB alltoall
  2,576 to 2,588 against 3,473 to 3,484, the reduction 2,275 against 1,436 to
  1,439; unbound, the broadcast 1,490 to 1,498 against 1,618 to 1,632 (8 to 9%
  slower), the alltoall 2,507 to 2,525 against 2,567 to 2,582 (2 to 3%), and the
  reduction 1,434 to 1,445 against 1,412 to 1,419 - no gain, since unbound it
  was already as fast: what helpers buy the 64 KiB reduction under binding is
  what the binding costs it at none, though they share the same core there;
  why, I did not find. The 8-byte reduction and broadcast
  were 10 to 16% faster with helpers under both. A third thing the lead's run
  shows, for whoever reads it next: the stream probe's "agent CPU" at two
  helpers reads 0.004 s a GiB because the helpers carry the stream and the probe
  times the agent alone (`stream-tip-helpers2.txt`).
- **Severity**: **minor**. It supports the default of none, and the README's
  "add threads but no CPU" is its mechanism under binding; but "nothing has
  measured it" is no longer so, and the record's sentence picked the lines that
  did not move. One README sentence with the numbers - on four nodes, helpers
  cost a broadcast or an alltoall 6 to 35% under Slurm's binding and 2 to 9%
  unbound - and the record's paragraph completed.
- **Confidence**: high (four runs on two days agreeing; at four nodes only).

### S5. `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE=fixed` sets every wire's unsent limit to its 16 KiB floor on any link, and neither the README nor the design notes say so

- **Location**: `shmem/src/link-measure.c:229` (under `fixed` no link is
  established); `shmem/src/init.c:444-446` (the transport is handed the
  hand-set bandwidth, 0 when there is none); `shmem/src/wire-settings.c:90-102`
  (`osoe_wire_unsent_bytes`: 0 bytes a second gives the floor,
  `OSOE_WIRE_UNSENT_FLOOR`, 16 KiB); README:1441 ("Unset, the link's bandwidth
  times 128 µs, at least 16 KiB ... set once the link is known at the end of
  `shmem_init`") and README:1393 (`fixed` "measures nothing - the setting that
  makes a run reproducible"); design §3.3 (`shmem-ethernet-design.md:395-408`,
  "`TCP_NOTSENT_LOWAT`, once the link is known"), silent on `fixed` as the
  README is; `docs/decisions/2026-10-07.md` item 11 ("and at 16 KiB where the
  shape is `fixed`") and `2026-10-07-one-thread-profile.md:334-336`, the only
  places it is said (*observed in source*, *documented*).
- **Scenario**: a reader sets `fixed` to make runs reproducible, as the README
  offers and as step 2's comparisons did for their base, and changes the
  transport without knowing it: every wire held to 16 KiB unsent whatever the
  link, where the default gives the link's bandwidth times 128 µs. The row says
  the limit is set "once the link is known", which under `fixed` it never is -
  a reader may take that for the system's own, or work the floor out from "at
  least 16 KiB"; neither row says which. A Part 4 campaign comparing shapes on a
  fast link would compare two unsent limits as well.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `out-stream/`, `out-floor/`, `out-quiet/`): every
  start under `fixed` said "limits the unsent bytes of each of its 1
  connections to 16384" under `SHMEM_DEBUG`; every start of the default shape a
  limit from its measured link - 559,632 to 967,916 bytes on the quiet VM, from
  links of 4.37 to 7.56 GB/s. The floor cost nothing there: alternating three
  rounds this morning at a load under 2, the 64 KiB put stream ran 10,335 to
  10,952 MiB/s under `fixed` against 10,782 to 10,950 under the default, and
  `shmem.floor`'s stream ratio 0.741 to 0.754 against 0.711 to 0.757 - at more
  than three times a 25 Gbps link's rate. So the challenge I set out to make, that the
  floor would starve a fast link as the record feared when it chose a limit
  scaled to the link (`2026-10-07-one-thread-profile.md`, question 2), finds no
  support in the one fast path here. Over loopback the receiving agent is the
  limit (README:1140); on a NIC, by the kernel's accounting, the limit counts
  only bytes TCP has not yet sent, while what keeps a device busy is what TCP
  has already handed it, which its small-queue limit bounds (*recalled*) - so
  the floor would cost not idle link but more sends a byte, each cut short at
  about 16 KiB, where the sending agent is the limit. Untimed: Part 4's.
- **Severity**: **minor** - two sentences, in the `OSOE_WIRE_UNSENT_BYTES` and
  `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE` rows and in design §3.3, best before Part 4 measures
  anything under `fixed`.
- **Confidence**: high on the mechanism and the documents; medium that the
  floor costs a NIC nothing.

  because its record is Part 0's - the README could name the tree it was taken

- **The reopening's wakes** (README:1154-1156: "a few microseconds a reopening
  at 32 such PEs, and no run showed the node's rate fall"): round six's
  measurement of 2026-10-04 (`phase1-release-fixes-review.md:126-135`), on
  128 KiB buffers where all but one woken writer slept again - kept after the

- **The zero-copy row** (README:1428; `2026-10-07-one-thread-profile.md:730-770`):
  "holds one peer's stream to about 512 KiB a round trip" is the arithmetic of
  both buffers lent, which the built rule - one lent, the other copying -
  replaced: one buffer a round trip by reference, and the other turning over by
  copy meanwhile, which no figure bounds and nothing has timed (also
  `wire-settings.h:55-57`); "a quiet waiting seconds" where the record says
  "about half a second at a time";
  "below about 10 KB pinning the pages costs more than the copy" is the
  kernel's `msg_zerocopy` documentation (*recalled*), not a measurement here;
  "a job of more than 3,274 nodes is refused" is the bound at no helpers -
  65 + 20(n - 1) ≤ 65,536 (`wire.c:49-61`) - and with helpers each ring holds a
  share and the bound is far above the 4,097-node ceiling (*observed in
  source*).
- **Träff's 2 and 4 MiB** (README:723: "1.43 and 1.46"): one run a side, at
  4 × 1 only (`2026-10-07-v0.2-step3/comparison/osu-reduce-big-4x1-*`); and
  "every size from 16 KiB was faster ... by OSU's benchmark and the project's
  probe" leaves out the third tool of the same comparison, `osoe-bench`'s loop
  of reductions back to back, whose 64 KiB median was the trees' (2.26 against
  2.24 ms, `docs/pi-cluster-results.md:847-849`) - and is still 2.27 ms at the
  merge point. The README names its tools, so this is precision, but a
  program reduces back to back more often than between barriers.
- **"Uncapped ... 14 to 22 ms"** (README:1441): its low end is the
  configuration before the ladder (128 KiB buffers, no filling); at step 3's
  merge point the lead's run today gives 17.2 to 19.9 ms. The "3.3 to 3.9" holds
  at the merge point: 3.87 to 3.88.
- **Severity**: **minor**, as a group. **Confidence**: high.

### S7. Minor, each one line

- **One data flow a node pair.** A peer's operations are one wire and one
  thread at every helper count (README:1119-1122), so on a network that caps or
  hashes per flow - a cloud's per-connection limit (5 Gbps on AWS outside a
  placement group, which `docs/decisions/2026-10-09.md` item 3 records), or ECMP
  in a fabric - a node pair gets one flow's share. Not among "What does not
  scale" (README:799-847) (*documented* in the record).
- **The mesh's accept loop is O(n²) on the highest lead**: before each of its
  2(n - 1) accepts it rebuilds a notice of the lower nodes still missing, two
  lookups a peer (`wire.c:194-213`) - about 4.2 million lookups at 1,024
  nodes, 67 million at 4,097, at the end of the start-up chain. Raised by Part
  2's first read and not answered (*observed in source*; the cost *inferred*).
  Build the notice only when a wait outlasts its threshold.
- **The ring's own ceiling**: at no helpers owner 0's ring holds 2(n - 1) wires
  and asks io_uring for 2 × wires + 1 submissions, rounded up to a power of two,
  where the kernel allows 32,768 (*recalled*) - 8,192 nodes, refused as "cannot
  create an io_uring for 16384 connections: Invalid argument" past them
  (`ring.c:69-78`). The README's ceilings stop at the budget's 4,097, so only a
  raised `OSOE_AM_BUFFER_BUDGET` reaches it; worth a word beside "raise it"
  (README:955).
- **Descriptors**: `init.c:292-298` still reasons from "a socket per peer node",
  "1,024 runs out short of 800 nodes" - at two wires and 256 PEs a node, short
  of about 380; and the proposal's "998 a node, within a default `ulimit -n`"
  (`progress-helpers-proposal.md:170-171`) leaves out the lead's 255 pidfds.
  The README's descriptor bullet (README:989-993) is right; at its 4,097-node
  ceiling a lead needs above 8,447 descriptors, more than the 4,096 hard limit
  some older systems set.
- **Severity**: **minor**, each. **Confidence**: high on each fact; the
  O(n²)'s cost and the ring's ceiling are arithmetic.

## Step 4's design against "nodes, not PEs"

It holds. Every structure step 4 added is a node pair's or an owner's: two wires
a peer node, each owner's ring sized by its wires, each owner's pool by its
wires' credits and owner 0's by the teams' forwards (`progress-host.c:607-614`),
each owner's slice of the node's arrays on lines of its own; the peer-to-owner
map is arithmetic (`owner-map.h`), so a PE that must wake an owner needs no
table; a helper's cost is a constant. Within a node, the costs that grow with
P are the ones the README already names: the landing zones (285 KiB a PE),
the wake of every writer at a buffer's reopening (README:1148-1156, S6), and one
thread's message rate for all of a node's PEs (README:1111-1118), which helpers
spread over peers but not over one peer's stream. Between nodes, start-up's
mesh doubled (two connections a pair, modelled 583 ms at 1,024 nodes,
`2026-10-08-control-wire.md:31`) and the link's measurement is constant in n.
Nothing grows with the job's PE count that the table says does not.

## The defaults, and hardware unlike the Pi

- **`OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS` 0**: justified, and S4's runs justify it better than
  the README says. On a 64-core node one thread moves every PE's traffic, as
  README:1111-1118 says; helpers, under `task`, share the lead PE's core.
- **`OSOE_WIRE_UNSENT_BYTES`'s rule**, the link's bandwidth × 128 µs, at least
  16 KiB: measured at a gigabit, where it is the floor (the Pi's leads measured
  116.5 MB/s); untimed above. Two things carry into a fast link besides S5.
  The bandwidth is one 512 KiB stream's rate against a 128 KiB one's at start,
  on fresh connections: under the evening's load nine starts measured 0.18 to
  4.09 GB/s, a limit by the rule of 23 KB to 523 KB, and on the quiet VM three
  measured 4.37 to 7.56 GB/s where the stream ran at 11 (*measured*,
  `out-stream/`, `out-floor/`, `out-quiet/`) - a link measured low is limited
  low. Over loopback even the 16 KiB floor cost the stream nothing (S5), so the
  rule's 128 µs, a guess at the agent's turnaround, is not shown to matter
  either way; on a NIC, at 64 PEs a node and hundreds of peers, no one has timed
  it. Part 4's to settle; the README says that no faster link has been measured
  (README:1129-1131), though not that the 128 µs is unmeasured.
- **`OSOE_AM_BUFFER` 256 KiB**: justified over loopback (1.4 to 1.5 times the
  stream at 128 KiB) and free at a gigabit; its cost is the measured 513 KiB a
  peer of `/dev/shm`, 250 MiB a node at 500 nodes, halved past 2,049 nodes
  where the gain goes with it (README:949-956). Safe.
- **`OSOE_WIRE_BYTES` × `OSOE_WIRE_BUFFERS`**, 16 KiB × 64 an owner: the memory
  claim holds; the fan-in cost grows with peers (S3).
- **The zero-copy send off**: justified - over loopback 60% of the copy's
  stream, on the Pi no gain and a stall when both buffers were lent. Safe.
- **The shape's `link` mode**: one pair's link shapes the job (said, README:740);
  measured in about 27 ms on the Pi, constant in n; at more than 512 nodes
  Träff's is off at the default ceiling (S2).

## Measurements and methodology

- **The Pi comparisons are sound in method**: alternating trees three times,
  medians of three, the same scripts at every part, raw outputs committed.
  Two weaknesses recur: the summaries print only the remote lines, so a
  collective that moves is seen only by whoever opens the raw files (S4, and
  the few per cent above); and "as information, once" runs are summarized by
  the lines that did not move.
- **Loopback figures describe a quiet VM's tree**, and were quoted after the
  tree changed (S6); re-taken at the tip, they hold. The step's own
  measurements of Parts 1 to 3 were all on the Pi, where the link is the limit
  and an engine's cost cannot show, so a regression of the engine's own would
  have reached the merge point unseen; this one did not happen.
- **The link measurement moves under load** (0.18 to 4.09 GB/s, hops of 55 to
  148 µs, over nine starts here), and with it the shapes and the unsent limit
  a job takes. On a cluster a job's start is usually quiet; on a shared or
  oversubscribed machine it is not, and `OSOE_LINK_*` is the remedy the README
  names.
- **The evening's own times are not quiet-VM numbers** and are used only as
  pairs: the stream under three limits 620 to 881 MiB/s in every arm at a load
  of 4 to 7 (`out-stream/stream.txt`), the floor ratios at a load of 5 to 10
  (`out-floor/`); the morning's quiet run (`out-quiet/`) is the one to quote.

## What is sound, and what convinced me

- **The memory model of the scaling table**: every row I could build is the
  code's - the lead's 8.1 KB a peer to 0.2%, its base to 2%, the connection
  area's 513.4 KiB a peer, the control segment's 288 KiB a PE, the region's
  log-growth, 2(n - 1) sockets - and doubling a node's PEs moves a lead by 544
  bytes.
- **Helpers are a constant per node**: a mebibyte, a ring, a thread; idle
  helpers sleep (0.3 to 0.8 ms in four seconds, at eight helpers too), and a
  helper made to spin before it sleeps fails `shmem.helper-wake`.
- **The control wire and the unsent limit do what the README says** on the Pi,
  at the merge point: a barrier behind a stream 11 times faster, a fetch-add 4.4
  times, the stream at line rate with 23% less sending CPU.
- **The zero-copy budget's 3,274 nodes** is the arithmetic of the completion
  queue at no helpers, refused by name.
- **The defaults are conservative where nothing is measured**: no helpers, no
  zero-copy, the unsent floor where the link is a gigabit.
- **The loopback stream at the merge point** is what the README says: 10.1 to
  10.8 GiB/s, 0.71 to 0.76 of the bare stream, on a quiet VM.
- **What I could not make fail**: no structure step 4 added grows with PEs; no
  idle thread spins. What I could: a helper that spins 200 µs before its idle
  wait passed 854 of the 855 tests at two helpers - all but the one that counts
  its wakes - and an agent that spins, 854 of 855 at none, the one failure a
  flake of the loaded run.

## What I could not check, and why

- **A fast link**, so S5's and S3's costs and the unsent rule's 128 µs: none
  exists (known: Part 4).
- **Hundreds of nodes, or 64 or 256 PEs a node**: 33 logical nodes and 2 PEs a
  node were the most this VM held with the others' runs; the slopes above are
  linear in what was built, and the rest is arithmetic.
- **The mutants' suites** ran in a loaded VM with the floor pins off - the
  helpers' at two helpers, the agent's at none - and the agent's mutant was
  timed in two rounds only.
- **The Pi beyond the lead's runs and S4's four minutes**: nothing else ran
  there.
