### S1. An excused reset is still a fatal: the same visit's send fails on it

**Severity: should-fix** - the node excuse that the record (item 5), design §3.15 and `progress.h`
describe, and that `abort_owners_model.py`'s `abrupt_p_ending` shows holding, is undone in the C for
one of the two ends it names; what follows is the outcome the first read's S1 called costly.

Location: `progress.c:861-866` (`receive_wire`: a failed or ended wire excused when the node's abort
is declared, and the visit goes on), `:1807-1816` (the visit: `receive`, `drain`, `flush`),
`:1180-1191` (`flush`: `flush_aborting` only once the owner aborts; otherwise an early return on
`c->peer_aborted` or `w->send_error`, none on `w->error`), `:1231-1236` (a segment on the wire offered
again), `wire.c:268-271` (`send_from`'s first line: `if (w->error) osoe_fatal("the connection to
node %u failed: %s")`), `ring.c:180-182` (a receive's reset is `w->error`). `flush_control` has the
same shape (`progress.c:1497-1504`, `:1521-1525`).

Scenario, *reasoned from the code*: node P runs helpers and is ending - a PE of P called
`shmem_global_exit`, or a non-lead's fatal - and its helper h has not yet reached the top of its next
turn (under `OSOE_TEST_ABORT_DELAY_MS`, for up to the delay). Peer node N's lead ends abruptly - a
lead's own fatal, which "ends at once, telling nobody", or a helper's fatal `_exit` - while h streams
puts to N, so N's process ends with input unread and resets the connection. h's pass reaps the reset
(`w->error = -ECONNRESET`); `receive_wire` excuses it (`node_aborting`) and returns; the same visit's
`flush` finds `c->sending` set - a stream nearly always has a segment on the wire, and one whose send
completed before the reset is still uncollected - and calls `osoe_wire_send_lent`, whose `send_from`
raises "the connection to node N failed: Connection reset by peer". On a global exit, `fatal()`'s
declaration loses and `osoe_abort_end` pauses the helper for ever (`abort.c:94-96`); P's agent waits
the whole grace, 10 s, in `wait_helpers`, h's other peers are never told, and the job ends 10 s late
with a failure line naming a peer whose end P had just excused. On a fatal, h `_exit`s P's lead at
once, its agent's and the other helpers' goodbyes cut off wherever they had got to, and P's peers
read a death. `busy()` keeps the connection visited while `c->sending` is set
(`progress.c:1660`), so the fatal comes at the latest at the next visit before the owner's own abort.
A FIN sets no `w->error`, and an idle wire offers nothing, so both are excused as the record says;
the reset under a stream is not.

Evidence, *run* (`abort-owners-send-model.out`), the tree's model with P's helper's flush added:

| Variant | Verdict | Trace |
|---|---|---|
| `abrupt_p_ending_fin_stream` | holds | |
| `abrupt_p_ending_reset_idle` | holds | |
| `abrupt_p_ending_reset_stream` | **fails** | N ends (RESET behind what it sent) ; P declares for another reason ; P helper reads RESET on D (excused) ; P helper flushes D: send_from on w->error: FATAL |

The window the tree's model leaves out is that a reading owner also sends: its P owners only read.
The claims that do not hold as written: the record's item 5 (`2026-10-08-helpers.md:60-62`, "A
reset or an end of stream without its goodbye is excused while the node's abort is declared ... at
every helper count"), design §3.15 (`shmem-ethernet-design.md:2366-2370`, "a failed connection ...
is a peer that is ending too, not a fatal"), `progress.h:110-112` and `progress.c:847-860`. Not new
in these commits - `node_aborting` is 64f48c4's - but the answer to the first read's S1 adopts a
model that says it holds. The hole is there at every helper count - at none, the agent's own pass
excuses by the node's word as a helper's does - and the Part 2 excuse by the other wire's abort
(`other_aborted`, `progress.c:862`) has it too, reached when an ending peer's data-wire goodbye was
lost to its grace.

Fix: in `flush` and `flush_control`, return early on `w->error` as on `w->send_error` - by then
`receive_wire` has judged the error, in the same visit, as a fatal or as excused; add the send side
and a reset to `abort_owners_model.py` (this read's model is a start). A test is within reach of the
hooks there are: under `OSOE_TEST_ABORT_DELAY_MS`, a non-lead's global exit on one node and, within
the delay, a lead's own fatal on another node it streams to.

## What was tried and holds

1. **The wait, and its test** (*read*, *run*). `osoe_progress_host_abort` nudges every helper before
   owner 0's goodbyes and waits for their done words within the same grace
   (`progress-host.c:1354-1360`); a helper's done comes after `osoe_progress_aborted`, which waits for
   the peer's kernel to have acknowledged each goodbye (`SIOCOUTQ`, `progress.c:1941-1954`), so the
   model's "a reset behind data already acknowledged" is what the C delivers - Linux hands a reader
   queued data before a reset's error. `wait_helpers` loads the agent's word before the done words,
   and `say_stop` stores before it bumps: no lost wake. `leave_helpers` joins the done and detaches
   the rest. The test fails on the wait removed and only then: `mutants-3/no-wait` fails `abort-wait`
   alone of 66 ("node 0 ended its connection without its goodbye"), `mutants-3/no-excuse` passes all
   66, and both are 7903b15 with one change (`mutants-compared.out`). With the wait removed it fails
   deterministically, by about 500 ms: the peer's agent sleeps 500 ms in `abort_arrived` between
   setting the control wire's `peer_aborted` and declaring, while the ending node's process, its
   helper still in its own delay, is gone once its control goodbye is acknowledged. The peer's delay is what lets the test see the
   wait at all - without it the node excuse would hide a missing wait. With the wait it passes in 1.3
   to 1.8 s in debug and ASan and 2.3 to 2.8 s under TSan (`verify-13/`, `smoke-9/`,
   `verify-11-stopped/`) against `global-exit.sh`'s 6 s.
2. **The hook** (*read*). `OSOE_TEST_ABORT_DELAY_MS` is parsed on the lead and refused past
   5,000 ms - half the grace - by name; `abort_delay_ns` is written in `osoe_transport_init`, before
   `osoe_agent_start` (`init.c:369`, `:382`), so before any thread that reads it exists; the agent's
   and helpers' signals are blocked, so `nanosleep` is not cut short; at 0 a helper aborts at the turn
   it sees the word, as before.
3. **S2's arithmetic** (*run*, `slices-check.out`): every owner's slice of the three arrays starts a
   line, lies inside its allocation, overlaps none and shares no line with another owner's non-empty
   slice, at both line sizes, up to 64 helpers, and with helpers past the peers (empty slices, never
   dereferenced: owner 0's at H > 0, idle helpers'); the second loop's starts are the first loop's
   sums; `lines_of`'s size is a multiple of the alignment, as `aligned_alloc` asks; the padding is at
   most 18 KiB (64-byte lines) or 54 KiB (128) of connections at 64 helpers.
4. **`settle`** (*observed*). An owner's busy bitmap is private memory of its owner's thread: its
   only readers and writers are `mark_busy`, `osoe_progress_attach`, `settle` and the pass, every
   caller on that thread (the agent's `osoe_progress_quiet` on owner 0, a helper's in `answer`). No
   wake rides it - a writer's mark is the area's seq_cst `dirty` word, then the wake word - so
   skipping a store that would not change it changes nothing another thread can see; §3.16 governs
   shared protocol words, and this is none.
5. **N2** (*read*). `answer` returns true for a quiet request found not quiet and the helper goes
   round before it sleeps; the next pass seals `detector_wanted` (`progress.c:1200-1203`, `:1299`,
   `:1308`). One extra pass a request and no loop: `due` is cleared first.
6. **N3** (*read*, and in `mutants-3/no-wait.full`): a helper's fatal now ends "; this PE was not
   waiting in the library"; `unpooled` is `_Thread_local`; `test-fatal-order.c` stubs the new call;
   the README row says a helper's thread-scope counters are not kept.
7. **N4 and N6's other items** (*read*): the departures section names each departure the first read
   listed and plan item 6 is amended; `node.h:376` says the seventh scope and `node.h:466-471` when
   the wires and the tid are written; design §3.15 counts the helpers in `/proc`, the sample and the
   excuse's start; the decisions index has the settings line.
8. **The pacer's fix** (*read*). The stop is agreed on every path: in seconds mode by one world
   max-reduction - an exact maximum - compared with the same `--seconds` everywhere, in rounds and
   quick modes by `p`. Reusing `max_source`/`max_result` is safe by the discipline at
   `osoe-pacer.c:755-760`: the world reduction follows the period's world sync, after every PE has
   read the last team reduction's result; the next write of `max_result` is `run_on`'s team

   and its end come from maxima and sums agreed over the team, and every exercise's clock reads are
   phase timings or a print. The hook's refusal is every PE's message and a global exit with 2; 0 is
   refused, as "a positive number" says.
9. **The README's counts** (*read*, `counts-12.txt`, `refused-12/`): 853 tests in debug, 893 in
   release of which 40 are models, 456 internode, 402 in the single-node build (397 and its five);
