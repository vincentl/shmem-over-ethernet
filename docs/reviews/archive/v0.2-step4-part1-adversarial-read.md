## Findings

### S1. Nothing can fail on an index taken for a slot outside owner-map.h and the writer's mark

**Severity: should-fix** (a property no check can fail on, as the fourth read's S1; and the
property the change exists to get right).

With no helpers every conversion the change introduces is the identity:
`osoe_owner_index(s, 0)` is *s* (`owner-map.h:36`), `osoe_owner_slot(0, i, 0)` is *i* (`:42`),
`osoe_owner_first(0, p, 0)` is 0 (`:59-60`), so `osoe_owner_position(s, p, 0)` is *s*. Every
engine test opens its area and its wires at 0 (`test-progress.c:284`, `:334`;
`test-barrier-am.c:92`, `:110`; `test-wire-pair.c:93`; `test-connection-area.c:107`) and so does
the library (`progress-host.c:531`). A swap of index for slot, or of position for slot, anywhere
on the owner's side compiles to code that behaves identically at 0, and passes every preset,
tier 3 and the models.

Scenario: three one-token mutations, each passing everything that runs today. At H = 3 on 8 nodes,
seen from node 5 (the worked example under property 1: owner 1 holds nodes 0, 3 and 7 as its
wires 0 to 2):
1. `wire.c:199-200` attaches with `slot` for `osoe_owner_index(slot, helpers)`. Node 7's wire, slot
   6, is tagged 6 on owner 1's ring of 3 wires: the reap ends the job at its first send's
   completion, naming "connection 6 of 3" (`ring.c:215-216`); a receive before that marks
   readable bit 6 (`ring.c:173`), which owner 1's pass visits as its wire 6
   (`progress.c:1404-1406`) - owner 3's socket to node 6.
2. `progress.c:700` reads `&o->wire[slot_of(o, i)]`. Owner 1's pass parses its wire 6, owner 3's
   socket to node 6, into the parser of its connection to node 7.
3. `progress-host.c:388` leaves out the `- 1`. A writer on node 5 marks owner 1's bitmap for node
   7 (`connection-area.h:139-142`, slot 6) and claims owner 2's wake (slot 7): N2's variant,
   which loses the wake, so a put to node 7 that finds owner 1 asleep waits for its sweep,
   100 ms.

What has no test at a helper count above 0: in `progress.c`, `slot_of`, `peer_of`,
`connection_of`, `conn_metrics`, `control_index`, `osoe_progress_acked`, the pass's bitmap by
owner and the parser's peer; in `wire.c`, the placement, the attach index and the ring per owner;
in `progress-host.c`, the slices attached per owner (`:628-637`), `owner_of_peer` and
`owner_bit`. What does have one: `test-owner-map.c`'s `check_map`, the map's two halves inside
`owner-map.h`, and `check_area`, the mark's owner and bit at 0 to 4 helpers - but at 9 nodes every
index and slot is below 64, so the word the mark chooses (`index / 64`, `connection-area.h:141`)
is never told apart from `slot / 64`.

The record says the brief's verification at 2 and 3 helpers cannot run, since "with no helper
thread, a helper count runs nothing". The engine needs no thread to run at a count: it is a
function of an owner (`progress.c:1389`), and a thread that enables several rings is the single
issuer of each. Left as it is, these lines first run in Part 3, under TSan, beside the helper
threads' own races and the stop protocol.

Fix: a test that runs owners 1 to H of each logical node from one thread, at H = 2 and 3 on 5 to
9 nodes, with point-to-point traffic only - puts, blocking and nonblocking gets and fetching AMOs
to every peer, each checked to land on the node it names, and the per-connection records read by
slot (`connection_metrics[s].peer` and its counts); no barrier, since the control plane is
refused at a helper count until Part 2 gives owner 0 wires of its own. Run it against the three
mutations above. And `check_area` at more than 64 wires an owner (300 peers at H = 2, say), so
that a word chosen by slot misses.

### N1. On x86-64 layout 5 moves the area's publication word: an old PE waits, not refused

**Severity: note** (a node mixing commits, on x86-64 only; cheap before layout 5 reaches `main`).

Layout 5 inserts `helpers` and `owners_offset` (`connection-area.c:57-58`) between `area_bytes`
and `ready`. With 64-byte lines `ready` moves from byte 64 to 128 and `opened` from 128 to 192
(`map_check_64.out`; layout 4's fields at 44f24bb's `connection-area.c:59`, `:68`). With
128-byte lines - aarch64, the VM and the Pi (`base/cacheline.h:13-14`) - the padding absorbs the
two fields, both words stay where they were, and the area at no helpers is byte for byte as large
as layout 4's (`map_check_128.out`). The struct's comment calls this a frozen prefix through which
"a PE built from a different commit finds everything else", and layout 3 appended its fields
after `opened`.

Scenario, x86-64: a node whose lead runs this branch and one of whose PEs runs `main` at 44f24bb.
The control segment's layout, its metric table and the handler checksum are the same in both, so
nothing refuses the mix before the area. The old PE's size check passes - the area is 64 bytes
larger (`:287-288`) - and it waits for `ready` at byte 64, which is now `helpers`, 0
(`:418-446`): for ever under the default no-progress rule, saying from 30 s on, if it is local
id 1, that the lead has not finished creating the area. The layout check that would refuse it by
name (`:483-491`) is never reached. The reverse mix is refused, by the size check naming `OSOE_AM_*`
(`:297-302`).

Fix: append the two fields after `opened`, where layout 3 put its own.

### N2. The design notes describe a `wrong_owner` the model does not run - the C's own risk

**Severity: note** (an untrue sentence in the design notes - property 7 - and a gap in the
model).

The new §3.10 paragraph (design `:1149-1151`) and the record (`2026-10-08-owners.md:62-64`) say
`wrong_owner` is "a writer that marks one owner's bitmap and wakes another". The model's
`wrong_owner` (`lead_wake_model.py:132-135`) takes the owner as *c* mod *H* for the mark, the flag
and the wake alike - one wrong owner for all three - as its docstring (`:82-85`) and 1dd7bf8's
message say. It fails for that reason: at H = 2 the writer of c0 marks owner 0's bitmap, which no
owner reads, and the writer of c1 marks owner 1's bit 0, which is c0's, so owner 2 sleeps on with
c1's commit.

The case the sentence describes is not in the model, and it is the shape the C invites: the owner
is computed twice, in the mark (`connection-area.h:139-142`) and in `owner_of_peer`
(`progress-host.c:386-390`), each from its own `peer < node ? peer : peer - 1`. Run here as a
variant - the mark to the connection's owner, the flag, claim and wake to the next owner round -
it loses a wake under SC and under TSO in 33 states (`apart.out`). S1's third mutation is that
defect in the C. Add the case to the model, and let the mark and the wake share one function that
names the owner; or correct the two sentences.

### N3. A PE that runs more helpers than its lead is refused naming `OSOE_AM_*`

**Severity: note** (for Part 3, when the count becomes a setting each PE reads).

A PE whose helper count differs from its lead's is refused by the header's check, naming the
count (`connection-area.c:492-495`), when it runs fewer, or when the two sizes coincide - with
64-byte lines 74 (nodes, H) pairs up to 1,100 nodes, the first 1,027 nodes at 2 and 3 helpers
(`map_check_64.out`). When it runs more and the sizes differ, which with 128-byte lines is every
such case, the size check refuses it first (`:297-302`), saying the lead's `OSOE_AM_*` settings
made the area and that every PE must set `OSOE_AM_*` the same. Read the lead's count from the header
before the size check, as `made_by` reads its pid (`:182-191`), or name the helper count in that
message.

### N4. With helpers, owner 0's arrays are owner 1's

**Severity: note** (fragile, not wrong).

At a helper count above 0 owner 0 holds no wire, and `progress-host.c:629-633` attaches it at
`first` 0 and `busy_before` 0: its `wire`, `connection` and `busy` are owner 1's, as its bitmap
pointer is (`connection-area.c:375-385`, an owner of no words). Every loop over them is bounded by
`count`, 0. The one index not bounded is `release_local`'s `control_index` (`progress.c:1331`),
which at a helper count is `OSOE_CONNECTION_NONE` for every child and would index
`o->connection[UINT32_MAX]`; it is unreachable because a team reaches `pending` only through
`release_down`, whose `forward` refuses such a child first (`:277-280`). The day a change indexes
owner 0 past its count while owner 1 runs, this is a word two threads write. A refusal in
`release_local`, as `forward` has, costs one compare; Part 2 gives owner 0 arrays of its own.

### N5. The PE's mask holds 64 owners, and nothing caps the count yet

**Severity: note** (for Part 3).

`owner_bit` shifts by `owner - 1` and `osoe_progress_host_wake_deferred` masks with
`(1 << helpers) - 1` (`progress-host.c:366-376`): 64 owners at most, the brief's cap.
`osoe_connection_area_open` takes any count and `shmem.owner-map` stops at 64; at 65 both shifts
are undefined. Part 3's setting should refuse 65 by name before the area is laid out.

### N6. `slot` names two things

**Severity: note** (`CLAUDE.md`'s naming rule; worth doing with S1, before Part 3).

`owner-map.h` calls a wire's place among its owner's wires its *index* and a peer's place in node
order its *slot*. `wire.h` and `ring.[ch]` still call the first `slot`: `struct osoe_wire.slot`,
"its place in the ring's wires" (`wire.h:62`), `osoe_wire_attach`'s and
`osoe_ring_arm_receive`'s `slot`, `ring.h:57`'s "by slot", the reap's "names connection %u of %u"
(`ring.c:214-216`). With no helpers they are one number; with helpers the field named slot holds
the index, one layer below the code where slot means the peer's. S1's first mutation is that
name taken at its word. Rename it `index` in those four files.

### N7. Smaller items

**Severity: note.**
- `ring.h:11-16` still says a ring carries every wire, over one provided-buffer ring that every
  wire shares, "so the buffers a node holds do not grow with its peers": true an owner at a time.
  With helpers they grow with *H*, and at a helper count in this part owner 0's ring holds a full
  pool - 64 buffers of 16 KiB by default - for no wire. For Part 3's memory rows.
- `osoe_wire_ring_open` reads the settings with `announce` (`wire.c:45`), so `OSOE_WIRE_SPLIT`'s
  line is printed once an owner.
- The record says `shmem.owner-map` checks "no two owners' words on one line"; it compares the
  flags' lines and the bitmaps' first lines (`test-owner-map.c:116-120`), not a bitmap against a
  flag's line. True by construction (`layout_of`).
- With 128-byte lines a layout-4 PE does reach the area's layout check, and is refused - by a
  message that names "different AM sizing" (`connection-area.c:486-491`). Older than this change.

## The eight properties, as tried

**1. Index and slot are never confused - held; no access found that goes to the wrong
connection** (S1: nothing would fail if one did).
- The peer slot, node-wide: the area's connections, reached only through `osoe_connection_to`
  (`connection-area.c:513-518`) - `connection_of` (`progress.c:50-53`) for the engine,
  `transport-internode.c` for the writers; `connection_metrics`, through `conn_metrics` and
  `slot_of` (`progress.c:132-136`), whose `peer` field `progress-host.c:647-649` fills by slot;
  the contexts' dirty sets, `high_segment`, `order`, `touched` (`transport-internode.c`,
  unchanged, all by `peer_slot`); osoe-stat, which reads those records.
- The owner's index: `o->wire[i]`, `o->connection[i]`, `o->busy[i / 64]` throughout `progress.c`;
  the owner's bitmap (`connection-area.h:139-142`, `progress.c:1395-1400`,
  `progress-host.c:877-886`); the ring's tags and readable bits, by `w->slot`, which `wire.c`
  sets to the index (`:179-180`, `:199-200`) - the ring tags by it (`ring.c:43-46`, `:121`) and
  marks by it (`:153-156`), and the pass takes the readable bits by the same index
  (`progress.c:1401`).
- The conversions: `slot_of` and `peer_of` (`progress.c:38-47`), `control_index` (`:62-68`),
  `osoe_progress_acked` (`:1618-1625`), `owner_of_peer` (`progress-host.c:386-390`), the mark,
  `wire_of` (`wire.c:132-136`), and the slices `progress-host.c:628-637` attaches against the
  positions `wire.c` fills and the rings it opens over them (`:202-204`).
- Node numbers against slots: the parser's peer (`progress.c:1262`), `barrier_team`'s
  `from_node` (`:441`), `control_index`'s callers - tree children are node numbers
  (`progress-host.c:255`), as is the detector's target - and the mesh's accept (slot = peer below
  this node) and connect (peer - 1 above it).
- Checked by arithmetic as well as by reading (`map_check.c`): the map at 0 to 64 helpers and 0
  to 1,000 peers, 32,532,500 slots - round trip both ways, each index below its owner's count,
  the positions a permutation, the owners contiguous, the PE's mask bit decoding to its owner -
  and the engine's arithmetic at 1 to 8 helpers over 2 to 40 nodes from every node: an owner's
  wire *i*, through `slot_of` and `peer_of`, reaches the area connection of the peer whose socket
  `wire.c` placed at that owner's position *i* and attached at index *i*, and a writer's mark for
  that peer sets owner *o*'s bit *i*. No mismatch.
- Worked example (`example.c`), H = 3, 8 nodes, node 5: owner 1 holds nodes 0, 3, 7 at positions
  0 to 2, owner 2 nodes 1 and 4 at 3 and 4, owner 3 nodes 2 and 6 at 5 and 6. A put from node 5 to

  connect loop made for slot 6, tagged 2 on ring 1, and counts into `connection_metrics[6]`,
  whose peer is 7.

**2. The wake reaches the owner whose bit was set - held** (N2 for the model's description).
- The 13 calls of `osoe_progress_host_wake_lead` at 44f24bb are all converted. Every mark is
  followed by a wake of its peer's owner or a defer of it: `reserve_waiting` (`:557-558`),
  `remote_put`'s parts (`:646`) then a defer or a wake (`:673`, `:676`), `send_message` (`:982`)
  for `remote_get` (`:1029`), `remote_get_nonblocking` (`:1292`, `:1294`) and `remote_rmw`
  (`:1345`), and `ask_acknowledgement` and `flush_dirty`'s own marks (`:1421-1422`,
  `:1462-1463`) for the wakes after them (`:1449`, `:1468`, `:1631`). The mark and the wake name
  the same owner, from the same slot.
- The mask is thread-local, so no other thread clears it; `wake_deferred` wakes every bit it
  clears but one naming an owner the session lacks, and `wake_all` clears it and wakes every owner
  a bit can name. Across threads: every wake the library holds back is made by the same thread in
  the same routine - put_signal's signal (`signal.c:102`), the last element or block of the
  strided and block loops (`rma-typed.c:101`, `:120`, `:162`, `:191`), alltoall's barrier after a
  last put that goes to the PE itself (`collectives.c:570-580`), the collectives' fenced adds, an
  rmw waking whatever its flags (`transport-internode.c:1345`) - and a wait that may hang on
  another thread's held request is per connection (`take_credit`, `reserve_waiting`: the same
  owner) or wakes every owner of a data wire (`make_room`, `complete_gets`, `complete_pair`).
  Across sessions: a leftover bit is masked out or is a spurious wake, and a session's own writes
  are woken by its finalize barrier's quiet (`flush_dirty`). With no helpers every path makes the
  one wake it made before - one `wake_owner(0)` for each `wake_lead` - and `am.wakes_*` count the
  same.
- The model, run here: 28 cases, all as expected; the 19 earlier cases' lines, state counts
  included, identical to 44f24bb's model run with its own engine. With the owner a parameter each
  owner is the old protocol on words of its own, which is what the C has; owner 0's wake word,
  `agent_wake`, is bumped by stop, abort and the posted arrivals too, which only wake it more. A
  held wake is a writer whose flag load comes long after its `fetch_or`, which the model's
  interleavings include. `wrong_owner` fails for the reason its docstring gives (N2).

**3. The sleep protocol per owner - held.** `osoe_progress_host_poll` (`progress-host.c:894-923`)
lowers the flag, takes the unsent request, runs the silent check, takes the posted arrivals and
passes; then `raise_flag_and_look` stores the flag `seq_cst` and loads every word of the owner's
bitmap `seq_cst` (`:877-886`), and the unsent request is loaded again - 44f24bb's order exactly.
The agent still loads `agent_wake` before the pass and waits on its ring with that value
(`:953-962`). With helpers owner 0's bitmap has no words, and what else wakes it - the unsent
request (`wake_owner(0)`, `:829`, rechecked after the flag), stop, abort and the posted arrivals
(bumps of `agent_wake`, caught by the futex compare) - is unchanged. Nothing owner 0 rechecked
before is missing.

**4. The area's layout - held at every helper count; the opener's checks are N1 and N3.**
`layout_of` (`connection-area.c:133-144`) puts helpers + 1 `owner_words`, a line each, after the
header, then each owner's bitmap from a line of its own, then the connections and the buffers; the
opener's pointers walk the same offsets (`:375-390`), and `osoe_am_area_bytes` is the same
function's end plus the buffers (`:146-151`), the size every PE maps. Reproduced at both line
sizes, 0 to 64 helpers and 2 to 1,100 nodes: nothing off a line, the bitmaps disjoint and ending
where the connections begin. The `goto again` paths free and clear both handle arrays before
starting over (`:421-429`, `:474-481`). `creator_pid` stays at byte 20 (the assertion,
`:100-101`, and `map_check`) and the magic at 0, so osoe-stat `--gc`'s 32-byte read
(`tools/stat/segment.c:720-725`) is unaffected.

**5. The engine per owner - held; with no helpers nothing behaves differently from 44f24bb but
the area's layout.** `osoe_progress_attach` is the old attach with the pool's initialisation
moved in; the pass runs `release_local` only on owner 0, the lead's agent; `idle` checks the
pending releases on owner 0; close, abort, aborted, the peers' end, closed, the silent check and
quiet loop over `count`, which is every peer; the counters and `publish_ring` go to owner 0's
record, bound to the node's `am` after the attach as before (`progress-host.c:642`), the attach
writing none. The pools: `descriptors_of(0, peers, 0, teams)` is peers × (C + slack) + peers +
degree × teams, 44f24bb's peers × (C + slack + 1) + degree × teams, and at any helper count the
owners' pools sum to it - the replies' term an owner's connections' (§3.8's first argument is per
connection), the forwards' and the detector's owner 0's, where `forward` and
`osoe_progress_deadlock_send` queue them (`progress.c:277-284`, `:1606-1615`) and
`release_left_queue` meets them (`:830-831`). A collective or detector message on another owner's
wire is refused before it touches the tree (`:438-440`, `:634-637`). What else a helper's pass
would write outside its own state is PE-side words several threads already write, each by an RMW
or in a record only one get's reply writes: `landed`, `completed`, `notify`, the credit, the abort
word's CAS.

**6. The mesh - held.** `wire.c:149-204`: every position initialised, each accepted or connected
socket placed at its slot's position and attached to its owner's ring at its index, each ring
opened over its own slice. A ring with no wires (owner 0 at a helper count): a submission bound of
1, a completion bound of the buffers and 1 (`ring.c:69-70`), one readable word, an enable that
arms nothing, and any completion naming a wire refused (`:215-216`).

**7. What helpers would break - nothing the change claims; one sentence of the design notes is
untrue (N2).** The unsent limit, the silent check, idle and quiet for the detector and the
teardown, the close, the abort and the ring's close act on owner 0's wires
(`progress-host.c:431-436`, `:908-913`, `:926-943`, `:811-820`, `:768-809`), and each comment
says so ("every wire while the node runs no helpers"); a helper's counters go to a private record
(`progress.c:1221`). With helpers, owner 0 holds no wire in this part, so the first forward ends
the job by name (`:277-280`): the control plane is placed, not yet carried. The sequencing hides
no defect: no helper count can run - the library opens its area at 0 (`progress-host.c:531`) and
the only other count is `check_area`'s, which runs no engine - so the counters' sums and the stop
and abort words, which matter only once a second thread writes, change nothing at 0. It does mean
the lines written for helpers run first in Part 3 (S1). The new paragraphs of the design notes
(`:1132-1151`, `:1345-1351`, invariant 34 at `:8017-8022`, §3.16's row at `:2521`) are true
sentence by sentence but for `wrong_owner`'s (N2), which the record repeats.

**8. The tests - `shmem.owner-map` tests the map and the writer's mark; nothing tests the owner's
side (S1).** `check_map` would catch a confusion inside `owner-map.h`: the round trip both ways,
the counts, the contiguity and the seen-once check make the positions a permutation (its position
check alone restates the function's definition). `check_area` would catch a mark to the wrong
owner or the wrong bit, not the wrong word. Every other confusion property 1 looked for - in
`progress.c`, `wire.c` and `progress-host.c` - is an identity with no helpers, and no test runs
another count.

## Verdict

No fatal finding. With no helpers the change is what it says: the engine, the host and the mesh
take the owner as a parameter, owner 0 is today's agent on today's structures, and every pass,
wake, pool and counter is 44f24bb's. The map is right, and so is every use of it: by reading,
by an exhaustive check of the arithmetic and by a simulation of every place the engine turns an
index into a slot or a node, no access reaches the wrong connection at any helper count to 64.
The model holds with the owner a parameter, its earlier cases unchanged.

What is wrong is what cannot yet be seen. Each conversion between index and slot is the identity
at zero helpers, so none of them can fail a test, and the change's own verification says a helper
count runs nothing, though the engine runs at any count from one thread. Before merging: S1's test
at 2 and 3 helpers with the three mutations it must catch, and `check_area` past 64 wires an
owner. The rest can follow, N1 and N2 before Part 3: append the area's two new fields after
`opened`, give the model the case the design notes name, and let the mark and the wake share the
function that names the owner.
