# v0.2 step 4: synthesis - the progress helpers' merge point

Review of `main` at `c897948`, run on 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-10 against
`docs/reviews/adversarial-review-prompt.md`, at the merge point of v0.2's fourth
step (`docs/prompts/v0.2-step4-progress-helpers-prompt.md`, "The merge point"):
round eight, the second of v0.2's two full reviews, and so of everything since
the first, `main` at `7053819` - step 2, the tree's shape from the link; step 3,
Träff's circulant reduce-scatter and allgather; and step 4's Parts 0 to 3, the
one thread's ladder with the zero-copy send behind `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY`, owners,
the control wire (two wires a node pair), and helper threads,
`OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS`, none by default (the records
`docs/decisions/2026-10-06-tree-shape.md` to `2026-10-09.md`). Part 4, a fast
link, is in 0.2's scope and not built (`docs/decisions/2026-10-09.md`, item 3):
the review asks whether 0.2 could be tagged from this tree without it, as the
author allowed.

Five investigators ran at once, each VM under its lock, each holding the first
review point's findings in its area as claims to check. Two of them -
correctness and scale-and-performance - stopped at the session's usage limit
with their reports nearly written and were resumed the next morning from their
own context, on a quiet host; scale-and-performance re-measured there and
lowered one of its own ratings (below). The two findings rated *fix before
release* went to fresh refuters, as the procedure asks. Then this synthesis, by
the lead, and the fixes, made on the branch that merges with it
(`docs/decisions/2026-10-10-step4-merge-point.md`).

The five deliverables: `v0.2-step4-conformance.md`, `v0.2-step4-correctness.md`,
`v0.2-step4-scale-and-performance.md`, `v0.2-step4-portability.md` and
`v0.2-step4-readiness.md`.

## Verdict

**The merge point passes after one named change, made with this review: release
after named changes. Nothing blocks.** In the procedure's terms, 0.2 could be
tagged from this tree with its fixes and without Part 4, once the release
checklist's per-tag items are run on the tree to be tagged.

Each of the five says so in its own area, and each with executable evidence
rather than a reading:

- **The new protocols hold.** All 46 model runs passed with every breakage
  failing; the helpers' request, answer, stop and turns code is the model's,
  order for order (correctness).
- **The new shapes give the specification's results.** A probe of 15 typed
  reductions at 16 sizes on seven teams, broadcasts from three roots and the
  deprecated `_to_all` - 1,327 checks a PE - passed on twelve maps at 0, 2 and
  3 helpers and on the Pi cluster, and a planted defect in Träff's blocks
  failed it on every team that spans nodes (conformance).
- **The helpers change nothing a program can see.** A peer's traffic stays one
  wire and one thread, so fence, put-with-signal and atomics keep their order
  by construction, and the first review point's whole-API probe printed the
  same bytes at 0, 2 and 3 helpers (conformance).
- **Memory grows with nodes, not PEs.** The scaling table's memory rows are the
  code's to within a few per cent; nothing step 4 added grows with a node's PEs;
  idle helpers sleep (scale-and-performance).
- **The step's one claimed gain held on the Pi** against step 3's merge point
  (below).
- **x86-64's memory order on steps 2 to 4 is clean.** The x86-ordered aarch64
  build ran the whole suite, the internode tests at two and three helpers and
  the stress tester, all passing (portability).
- **Every failure the steps opened is loud,** and `osoe-stat` finds it
  (readiness).

What the review found is of three kinds:

- **A rule the project keeps by hand that drifted:** the licence line, missing
  from 82 evidence programs (Q5-1, the one *fix before release*).
- **Sentences behind the tree:** settings rows, counts, quoted numbers and
  indexes. All minor, all made true here.
- **Guards thinner than the record says,** each rated *after release*:
  - the ThreadSanitizer gate at a helper count;
  - the deadlock detector's helper half, which only its model holds;
  - keepalive on one machine's loopback under load;
  - a reduce-scatter grant's head that two helpers write at once with the same
    bytes;
  - and, from the scale side, Träff's reduction tied to the fan-out ceiling past
    512 nodes, and waits other than a helper's still unguarded against
    spinning.

None is a defect a program at the defaults meets on a cluster. The one a user
can meet at the defaults - keepalive ending a job of tens of logical nodes on an
overloaded desktop - the README now tells `osoe-launch`'s users, with the
remedy.

## What is sound

The five agree on more than they found, and each says what convinced it:

- **The protocols match their models, and the models can fail.**
  - Every new model's breakages fail for the reason they state.
  - The C of the helpers' requests, answers, stop and turns is the model's,
    order for order.
  - The planes are refused on each other's wire before a byte lands.
  - The failed-wire flush of Part 3's second read is fixed on all three send
    paths (correctness).
- **Conformance is unchanged by the steps.**
  - Byte-identical output from the whole-API probe at every helper count.
  - The new collective shapes exact on every team, type and size probed, with a
    mutant to show the probe can see a wrong block.
  - An erroneous program on either side of a crossover ended by the deadlock
    detector, as the README says (conformance).
- **The scaling claims are the code's.**
  - A lead's state 8,118 bytes a peer node (the README's 8.1 KB), 544 bytes
    more in all when its node's PEs double.
  - The connection area 513.4 KiB a peer, sockets exactly 2(n - 1).
  - A helper about a mebibyte and a thread whatever the job's size.
  - The owner of each wire by arithmetic, with no table.
  - Idle helpers asleep at 0.3 to 0.8 ms of CPU in four seconds
    (scale-and-performance).
- **A kernel is asked for nothing new.** Every io_uring facility the ladder,
  owners and helpers use is Linux 6.0 to 6.2's, beneath the 6.7 floor; "one
  ring an owner" follows the single-issuer rules to the letter. The single-node
  build still passes 400 of 400 on Rocky Linux 9 (portability).
- **The new failures are diagnosable.**
  - A helper that ignores its stop ends the job naming it in 0.2 s.
  - A frozen helper is named under `OSOE_NO_PROGRESS_TIMEOUT` and visible in
    `osoe-stat`'s owner lines.
  - A deadlock is proved at two helpers as at none, and never while a helper is
    frozen.
  - A node stopped for 15 s under a stream ends nothing.
  - Every new setting's bad value is refused by name with its range (readiness).

## Refuted, or corrected

- **S5 (first S2), `fixed` and the unsent floor, *fix before release*:
  confirmed as a mechanism, refuted as a rating.**
  - The refuter traced every link of it in the source and found the finding's
    "every wire" too wide: the control wires keep the system's own.
  - **Under `fixed` the three `OSOE_LINK_*` still give the bandwidth** when set.
  - **The floor is `link`'s own limit at a gigabit,** so no number the README
    quotes changes.
  - **The limit counts only what TCP has not yet sent,** so 16 KiB is not all a
    device holds.
  - **Part 4's measurements do not depend on the shape.**
  - The investigator, re-measuring on a quiet VM, had come to the same rating.
  - The README's two rows now say what the code does, in the refuter's terms.
- **Q5-1, the licence line: confirmed** (above), with two slips corrected:
  - **The check globs the tree,** since the VM has no git.
  - **The 2026-10-04 pass the readiness report cited covered two scripts,** not
    every program.
- **SOS `spam` at two helpers: refuted as a finding.** The sweep's load (above).
- **The conformance investigator's own refutations,** each with its reason:
  - a put cut mid-element by Part 0's filling, which only a racing reader could
    see, as before;
  - ThreadSanitizer's two helpers landing on one program word, a race in the
    program;
  - a floating sum's bits depending on the shape, which §9.10.9 allows;
  - the detector with helpers, held by tests at two and three helpers.

## Questions for the author

None blocks the merge. Three are the author's to decide before the tag:

1. **Whether 0.2 waits for Part 4.** The author put it in scope with leave to
   ship without it (`docs/decisions/2026-10-09.md`, item 3). The cloud's costing
   is in that record, and every launch is the author's.
   - **P1, the zero-copy send's locked-memory limit,** is the one finding to
     settle before Part 4 measures the setting.
   - **Question 4's exploration, blocking gets,** is written up for the
     author's decision (`docs/design/blocking-get-proposal.md`).
2. **Whether Q2-1's remedy should precede the tag.** That is `osoe-launch`
   turning keepalive off for loopback logical nodes: a small change, but one to
   the launcher every test uses. The README's sentence is the minimum and is
   made; the rest is rated after release.
3. **Q2-4 is the brief's own stop-and-ask: "a word two owners must write".**
   - **What it is:** a reduce-scatter grant's head is a word two owners do
     write - not one they must. Each of a lead's receivers puts the same head,
     and one could put it alone.
   - **Why it is minor:** the bytes are identical, so it is harmless on any
     hardware. It is rated minor, and the fix - one writer, with step 3's field
     per receiver - is on the checklist for after the release.
   - **The question:** whether the author wants it before the tag instead. It
     changes Träff's grant, which `circulant_model.py` holds, so its model
     comes first.
