# v0.2 before the tag, conformance: is osoe OpenSHMEM 1.6? (round ten)

*Round ten of the adversarial review (T13), the conformance question, against `main` at
`5cefc7fb` (2026-10-30), checked out at `<repo>/build/scratch/wt/review-ten`: the changes since
round nine (`3b45472`, 2026-10-11), and the README's Limits, "every departure from the
specification", for this tree. The specification is `docs/reference/OpenSHMEM-1.6.pdf`, read with
`pdftotext -layout`; § is a section of it unless "design §" says otherwise. Each claim is labelled
*documented*, *observed in source*, *measured* or *recalled*. The probes, scripts and outputs are
in `<repo>/build/scratch/review-ten/conformance/`.*

## Verdict

**Yes, as far as the changes since round nine reach. Nothing blocks the release, and nothing
must be fixed before it.** Four findings, all minor: three in the documents and one a test
gap (below). The README's Limits are true of this tree. Among the changes since round nine I
found no departure they leave out, but for one gap: since P1 removed the section's only §9.1.6
entry this morning, the section says nothing of what `shmem_global_exit` still does
differently from a normal program end. "How a job ends" says it all (C1).

- **SOS at the defaults, the collectives' check on since P39** (*measured*). SOS's tests were
  built with this tree's `oshcc` and run under `osoe-launch` on two maps: four logical nodes of
  two PEs (the Pi's and the cluster's shape), and one node of four, where every collective meets
  within the node. **161 of SOS's 165 programs exit 0 on every map.** These include its eight
  threaded programs, `shmem_team_context`, `bigget` and `bigput` at their defaults, and the two
  that SOS's automake builds with extra flags. The check named no call in any run. Two of the
  four do not build: they pass `short *` to the `_all`/`_any`/`_some` generics, an extension of
  SOS's. The other two are refused by osoe's own argument checks, as on the Pi and on the
  cluster. **Both refusals conform** (*observed in source*, *documented*):
  - `shmemalign` asks `shmem_align` for an alignment of 2. §9.3.4: "The value of alignment
    shall be a multiple of sizeof(void *) that is also a power of two; otherwise, the behavior
    is undefined."
  - `shmem_team_b2b_collectives` collects 10 longs a PE into a `shmem_malloc` of 10 longs
    (`corpus/tests-sos/test/unit/shmem_team_b2b_collectives.c:62, 72`), so it writes past the
    object. osoe refuses this before writing (`collectives.c:485-498`). It measures the room to
    the end of dest's allocation, not to the end of an object it guesses, so a program that
    carves arrays out of one allocation still passes.

  (`sos.sh`, `sos.txt`; the cluster's third session ran with the check off: its tree,
  `2dd3a623`, predates P39's `d60515ba`, so no SOS run before this one had the check on.)
- **The collectives' check (P24, on by default since P39) cannot fail a conforming program**
  (*observed in source*, *measured*).
  - **What it compares** is only what the specification has every member pass alike: a
    reduction's operation, element and bytes; a broadcast's bytes and root; an fcollect's
    block; an alltoall's block and strides; a one-node scan's element, bytes and inclusiveness.
    A collect's block sizes, which §9.10.8 lets differ, are not compared (`bytes` 0,
    `collectives.c:516, 815`).
  - **Types that are one element compare alike**, `long` and `int64_t` for example
    (`internode.h:327-337`, `reductions.c:33-37`).
  - **Barriers and syncs** are all one kind. `shmem_barrier_all`, `shmem_sync_all`, a team sync
    and the whole job's active-set barrier therefore pass for each other.
  - **Where the words live**: each PE says its call in its own internal region's words for the
    team's number (`internal-region.h:222-250`). A team holds one number on every member, and a
    PE holds a number for one team at a time, so teams on different threads never share words.
  - **When they are compared**: the comparer reads them after every member's arrival and before
    the release that every member waits for. For a data collective the comparer is the lead,
    which also waits for the copy-outs; for a sync it is the last to arrive. So no member can
    move on and overwrite its word first.
  - **A probe of my own** (`teams-mt.c`) runs six threads a PE under `SHMEM_THREAD_MULTIPLE`,
    one team a thread: the world, `SHMEM_TEAM_SHARED`, a copy of the world, a `split_2d`
    x-team of one PE (P7), its y-team of every PE, and the even PEs. Each thread runs 160
    rounds of every data collective (collects of member sizes that differ, some 0; alltoalls
    with strides; both scans), and each round puts through a context made from its team, by
    team-relative PE number. It passed 18 runs out of 18, on one node of four, two of two, four
    of one, two of four, three of two (cyclic), and three of one at two helpers: every element
    right, and nothing named (`probe.sh`, `probe.txt`).
  - **Broken on purpose** (*measured*): a mutant whose call words are team number 0's for
    every team fails the probe in all 12 runs on the four maps with more than one PE a node
    (`mutant.sh`, `probe-mutant.txt`). In the mutant, `team_word_at` gives the call's three
    words number 0's place, and the collect's words keep their own. It fails with "PE 0, of
    this node, said no call for SHMEM_TEAM_WORLD's collective 29" and "the PEs of node 0
    called different collectives on team 6", and passes the six runs at one PE a node, where
    nothing is compared within a node. So the probe does reach threads interleaving different
    teams' calls at the moment the check reads them.
- **P1 conforms to §9.1.6** (*observed in source*, *documented*). §9.1.6 asks for four things:
  - **The job's status must be one of the callers' values.** It is the lowest-ranked of the
    nodes' carriers' (`abort.c:139-170`). The record's 30 runs on the Pi under `srun -K1`
    always returned a caller's status, and killed nothing.
  - **Standard I/O must be flushed.** Every PE that did not call leaves by `fflush(NULL)` and
    then `_exit(0)` (`abort.c:203-207`).
  - **A caller ends as a program does.** A caller the library sees leaves by `exit` (`:201`),
    so its handlers run.
  - **No caller returns.**

  A caller whose call another node's end outruns leaves as a PE that did not call. Its streams
  are flushed, but its handlers do not run. That is the race §9.1.6's "immediate notification
  to all PEs to terminate" makes, and the README states it. It is the Limits section that has
  lost track of it (C1).
- **The splits and the heap calls compare only what the specification requires alike**
  (*observed in source*). P6 compares `split_strided`'s triplet (§9.4.6: "All PEs must provide
  the same values for the PE triplet") and `split_2d`'s `xrange` (§9.4.7), and not the
  configurations. That is right: §9.4.7 says "The PEs in the parent team do not have to all
  provide the same parameters for new teams" (`team.c:1374-1391, 1573, 1618`). P23 compares, for
  each routine, exactly the arguments §9.3 says must be identical on all PEs:
  - `shmem_malloc`'s size;
  - `shmem_free`'s pointer, and `shmem_realloc`'s pointer and size;
  - `shmem_align`'s alignment and size;
  - `shmem_calloc`'s count and size;
  - `shmem_malloc_with_hints`' size and hints.

  P23's check goes through the finalize team's lines, which no application collective uses
  (`malloc.c:59-95`); P6's goes through the split's own reduction over its parent. A size of 0
  and a null pointer return before P23's check, as §9.3 has them return.
- **The README's Limits** are true of this tree in every entry whose claim the code or a record
  can settle. I took their measured numbers - timings, rates, the MPI and `RemoveIPC` trials,
  the x86-64 runs - from their records. I checked:
  - the teams' and the blocks' numbers, the team of one included;
  - the threads', the contexts' and the heap's limits;
  - the landing zone (P32);
  - the collectives' check and its gaps;
  - the floating-point statement, a maximum being `x > y ? x : y` (`combine.c:28, 35`);
  - the helpers' default.

  A failed split returns nonzero on every parent PE, both of `split_2d`'s handles invalid, as
  §9.4.6 and §9.4.7 ask (`test-team-split.c:535-539`). Every row of the environment variables'
  tables gives the code's default and range. A Sonnet agent read each row against the code, and
  I verified the discrepancies it reported (C3). §8's grammar for `SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE` is
  round one's parser, unchanged, the spec's examples among its rules (`base/size.h`).
- **Round nine's three findings**: C1 and C3 fixed, and fixed right. Three of C2's four parts
  are in the suite (T21, T22, T23). Its fourth, the partial active-set `collect64` at sizes whose
  gets take more than a piece, was dropped from the after-release list without a word (C4).

## Findings, ranked

Nothing rated *blocks release* or *fix before release*.

### Minor

- **C1. The Limits section no longer lists any of §9.1.6's departures, though the README's own
  reading of §9.1.6 leaves some** (*observed in source*, *documented*).
  - **Where**: `README.md`'s Limits, "Failures" (`:1295-1381`): the entry that P1 removed in
    `abfeba29` was "Under `srun -K1` ... PEs of more than one node calling `shmem_global_exit`
    at once can lose a caller's output, where the specification has every PE's output flushed
    (§9.1.6)". "How a job ends" (`:2189-2230`); `abort.c:186-207`; §9.1.6.
  - **What**: the README reads §9.1.6 as asking that the calling PE's handlers run ("The
    calling PE leaves with `exit(status)`, so its `atexit` handlers run and its streams are
    flushed, as the specification asks", `:2193-2195`). By that reading four things still
    differ from a normal program end, and "How a job ends" states each of them:
    - **An outrun caller runs no handler.** A caller whose call another node's end outruns -
      once the race is lost, `called` (`abort.c:23, 44-50`) is read as not set - leaves as a PE
      that did not call. Its streams are flushed and its handlers are not run.
    - **The handlers run on the library's thread, with signals blocked**, so a handler sees
      the library thread's `errno`, and a C++ caller's `thread_local` objects are not
      destroyed.
    - **The handlers have at most 10 s.** After that the library ends the caller with its
      standard output flushed alone (`abort.c:84-92`).
    - **A PE that did not call runs no handler.**

    The first is the old entry's residue. The kill is gone, and so is the lost buffered output,
    but in the shape P1 was built for - every PE detecting the same failure and calling at once
    - some callers' handlers still do not run. P1's own test found it: "the first version of
    the test, requiring every handler, failed on exactly that" (`2026-10-29-exit-carrier.md`).
  - **Scenario**: every PE of a four-node job finds the same bad input and calls
    `shmem_global_exit(2)`. Each has an `atexit` handler that writes its partial results. The
    job ends with 2 and every PE's `printf` output is there. On the nodes whose PEs the first
    goodbye reached before they called, the handlers did not run. A reader of the Limits, which
    say they list "every limit a program can meet", finds nothing about it.
  - **Why only minor**: §9.1.6's Notes ask that I/O "must be flushed for standard language I/O
    calls", which osoe does on every PE. Its "immediate notification to all PEs to terminate"
    makes the outrun caller a race the specification does not order. Other implementations end
    the other PEs by a signal: SOS through its runtime's abort, OSHMPI through `MPI_Abort`,
    *recalled*. The README documents all of it, only outside the section that claims to be
    complete.
  - **Fix**: one entry under "Failures": `shmem_global_exit` runs the handlers of the caller
    whose call it carries, and of a caller its node saw before another node's end - on the
    library's thread, for at most 10 s ("How a job ends"). Every other PE, a caller whose call
    another node's end outruns among them, flushes its streams and leaves without them.
  - **Severity**: minor. **Confidence**: high that the gap exists; the rating is a judgement of
    how much the section's completeness claim matters.

- **C2. The release note is stale where X1 will touch it, and does not say that 0.2 now ends
  some programs that 0.1.0 ran** (*observed in source*, *documented*).
  - **Where**: `docs/release-notes/0.2.0.md`.
  - **Its "Known limits"** (`:239-241`): "Tested on aarch64 only. x86-64 built and ran under
    emulation, without an x86-64 kernel". The README has said "Tested on aarch64, and on x86-64
    since 2026-10-12", after the three native x86-64 sessions on Google Cloud.
  - **Its "Settings" bullet** (`:130-137`): "eleven new ... five defaults changed ... None
    removed". It counts neither `OSOE_CHECK_COLLECTIVES`, `OSOE_TURNOVER_WAKE`,
    `OSOE_MESH_DATA_WIRES`, `OSOE_MESH_SLOT`, `OSOE_MESH_PORT_RANGE`,
    `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PLACES` nor `OSOE_MACHINE_PES`, nor the new defaults of
    `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS` (`auto`), `OSOE_CHECK_COLLECTIVES` (1) and
    `OSOE_GET_LANDING_BYTES` (the node's budget, P32).
  - **New refusals**: "What changed" names no refusal of a program that 0.1.0 let run, apart
    from the PID namespace's. Several now end such a job by name:
    - heap calls whose PEs pass different arguments (P23);
    - splits whose PEs pass different triplets or `xrange` (P6);
    - `iput`/`iget` strides below 1 (P22);
    - a collective the PEs of one node call differently (P39, which "Known limits" mentions).

    Every one of these is undefined behaviour, so a conforming program cannot meet them, and
    the note's "What a conforming program computes has not changed" stays true. But a program
    that ran, wrong or by luck, under 0.1.0 now ends, and the note is where its user would look.
  - **Severity**: minor; the first two belong to X1 (`docs/prompts/v0.2-open-items.md:135`, "the
    changes after `5748c97` added"), and the portability-and-readiness investigator's area.
    **Confidence**: high.

- **C3. Small inconsistencies in the settings' rows and figures** (*observed in source*; found
  by a Sonnet agent's row-by-row read, each verified here).
  - **(a) The missing marker**: `OSOE_MESH_DATA_WIRES`'s row (`README.md:1911`) says a job whose
    nodes differ is refused, but has no *checked between nodes* marker. The code compares every
    node's word with node 0's before any wire is made (`progress-host.c:955-968`).
  - **(b) The architecture entry's list**: "Every node of a job must have the same
    architecture" (`:1262-1264`) lists what the rendezvous compares, and omits
    `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PLACES`, which it compares too (`rendezvous.c:214-218`; its row is marked
    *checked*).
  - **(c) Two cost figures predate P33's exchange slots**:
    - "27.0 KiB at the default 1 KiB ... (at 500 nodes)" (`:927`) against the same README's
      29.3 KiB at 500 nodes (`:902`);
    - the `/dev/shm` check's "+ 25.9 MiB" a PE (`:994`) against the table's 25.30 to 26.46
      (`:893`).
  - **(d) `SHMEM_INFO`'s text** (`init.c`) says only "the same on every node" for settings that
    are also compared among a node's PEs (`OSOE_MAX_TEAMS`, the collectives' fan-out,
    pipeline, small size, shape and places, `OSOE_LINK_*`). It also leaves `shmem_team_ptr` out
    of `OSOE_FORCE_REMOTE`'s line, though the README's row names it and `team.c:1668-1676`
    honours it.
  - **(e) The single-node build's note** (`cmake/OsoeVersion.cmake:28`) and the README's
    sentence (`:1864-1867`) list fewer of the settings that build checks as values only than it
    checks: the mesh's, the turnover wake, the collect places, `OSOE_AM_*` and `OSOE_WIRE_*`.

  No default or range is wrong. **Severity**: minor. **Confidence**: high.

- **C4. Round nine's C2 (d) dropped without a record** (*observed in source*, *documented*).
  - **Where**: round nine's conformance C2 had four parts. Its fourth was the active-set
    `collect64` and `fcollect64` at sizes whose internal gets take more than a piece, with the
    fix "`aset.c`'s sizes into the partial active-set test".
  - **What happened to it**: the synthesis's after-release list carried the other three
    (`v0.2-before-part4-review.md:222-227`), the open items took them as T21, T22 and T23, and
    all three are built. This one appears in neither list, and `test-active-set-partial.c`
    still collects `BLOCK = 5` longs a member (`:29`).
  - **What has changed under it**: `active-set.c` has moved by two lines since round nine, but
    the landing zone it gets through has changed its default (P32).
  - **Severity**: minor (after release). **Confidence**: high that the gap exists; nothing
    found wrong in the path.

## What is sound, and what convinced me

- **The refusals are refusals of undefined behaviour.** I read every `osoe_fatal` added under
  `shmem/src` since round nine (`git diff 3b45472 HEAD`). Each one that a program's arguments
  can reach answers something the specification leaves undefined:
  - strides below 1 (§9.6.1.3, §9.6.1.7) or below `bsize` (§9.6.1.4, §9.6.1.8);
  - a stride of 0 with a size other than 1 (§9.4.6);
  - differing collectives, splits or heap calls;
  - a dest too short for a collect.

  The others are settings out of range, test hooks, or guards of the library's own
  invariants: leads disagreeing about a team's nodes, Träff's lane past its layout.
- **Teams of one (P7) behave as the specification's teams of one PE** (*measured*,
  *observed in source*):
  - `shmem_team_translate_pe` and `shmem_team_ptr` answer for the one PE;
  - a context made from such a team puts to team PE 0;
  - every collective returns the caller's own data, an exclusive scan 0 (§9.10.10:
    "0, if i = 0");
  - its sync returns at once;
  - it claims no block and touches no node-wide counter (`collectives.c:150, 502, 804, 1020`;
    `barrier.c:56`).
- **`OSOE_MAX_TEAMS` and `OSOE_TEAM_BLOCKS` are limits the specification makes room for, not
  departures** (*documented*, *observed in source*). §9.4.6 says that "If the new team cannot be
  created ... shmem_team_split_strided will return a nonzero value on all PEs in the parent
  team", and §9.4.7 says the same with both handles invalid. That is what osoe does when no
  number is free on every member, or a node's pool is dry, and the README says so in each
  entry.
- **Team contexts and the team's configuration**: any team may create contexts whatever its
  `num_contexts`, which is permissive. `shmem_team_get_config` returns 0 and does nothing for
  a mask of 0, returns nonzero for `SHMEM_TEAM_INVALID`, and returns the `num_contexts` the
  split was given (`team.c:940-955`). §9.4.4 as 1.6 clarified it asks exactly this, allowing
  the values to "differ from the parameter values that were assigned at the time of the
  team's creation".
- **`collect`'s three places (P29 to P31, P33)** are exact integers whichever way the leads
  take, and the suite's `shmem.collect-places.*` checks every byte on block, negative-stride,
  cyclic and out-of-order maps, under each way forced and the link's choice
  (`shmem/tests/CMakeLists.txt:3703-3812`). My probe's collects, of member sizes that differ and
  some 0, were right on every map.
- **The two-node choice (P4)** changes only which shape a size takes, from the same three link
  numbers on every node, so integer results are untouched. Floating-point ones move only as
  the README's floating-point entry already says ("may differ"). That entry, and the release
  note's matching sentence, are round nine's C1 fixed as recommended.
- **§8's variables**: `SHMEM_VERSION`, `SHMEM_INFO`, `SHMEM_DEBUG` and `SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE`
  are read with their `SMA_` spellings, and the `SHMEM_` one wins (`bootstrap.c:20-37`). §8
  frees implementations to define more variables. The new notice for an unknown name looks only
  at `OSOE_` names (`unknown-settings.c`), so a site's `SHMEM_` variables for another
  implementation are left alone. `SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE=0` is still refused, which the Limits
  list as a choice.
- **Every 1.6 addition** listed in Annex G.1 is declared in `shmem.h`: scans, `shmem_ibget` and
  `shmem_ibput`, `shmem_signal_add` and `shmem_signal_set`, `shmem_team_ptr`,
  `shmem_pe_quiet`, the session routines and `shmem_query_initialized`.

## Round nine's findings in this area

- **C1** (the floating-point variation understated) - **fixed, and fixed right**. The release
  note's sentence (`docs/release-notes/0.2.0.md:16-30`) and a Limits entry ("A floating-point
  reduction's result depends on the collectives' shape", `README.md:1384-1407`) say what the
  finding asked, with its numbers and with `fixed` as the way to repeat a run.
- **C2** (what neither SOS nor the suite exercised):
  - (a) and (b) closed by T21 (`shmem.multiple.default-zone.0-1`, `.both-paths.0-1`;
    `docs/decisions/2026-10-18.md:179-186`);
  - (c) closed by T22, special values through `test-reduce.c` (`7a35c2c1`), and by T23,
    `shmem.fixed-shape.*` (`shmem/tests/CMakeLists.txt:3217-3245`);
  - (d) not done and not recorded (C4).
- **C3** (design §3.12 and §4.10 said the caller copies a blocking get out) - **fixed**. §3.12
  (`docs/design/shmem-ethernet-design.md:1556-1562`) and §4.10's row (`:4132`) now say that a
  get of one piece is the caller's, and that the pieces of a larger one go to whichever of the
  PE's threads claims each.

## What I could not check

- **P1 on more than one machine.** The development VM's Slurm has one node, and the Pi is the
  lead's. For P1's runs under `srun -K1` I relied on the record's 30 Pi runs. The correctness
  investigator was running its own callers' probes in the VM meanwhile.
- **SOS at two and three helpers, and at 70 PEs.** These remain T4's, a run of the tag's. My
  runs used the `auto` default, under which `osoe-launch` on four CPUs takes no helpers; the
  probe ran one map at two.
- **The specification's undefined corners beyond those named.** I did not re-read the
  families that are unchanged since round nine: RMA, AMOs, locks, waits and signals.
- **The measured numbers in the Limits.** The timings, rates, MPI trials, `RemoveIPC` trials and
  x86-64 runs are taken from their records and were not measured again. Nor was the
  floating-point variation's table, which is round nine's measurement.
