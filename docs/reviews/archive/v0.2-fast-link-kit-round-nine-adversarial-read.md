## Verdict

**Merge after one named change.**

The fixes do what round nine asked, and their mechanics hold under test:

- **P9-2.** `salloc` with `srun` as its one step. The environment reaches the tasks, and the
  timeout's three paths end clean (124 on the step, 137, and 124 while still waiting for nodes).
- **R1.** `task/none`.
- **R2.** `--no-install-recommends`, which installs no ROCm package and every binary and plugin
  the kit uses, on arm64 by simulation and on amd64 by Debian's package relations.
- **S2.** `SHMEM_DEBUG`, whose lines `summarize.py` reads exactly.
- **S3.** `osoe-bench quick barrier collectives`, parsed line for line.
- **S5.** The credits arithmetic.
- **S1's count.** It is the exact time-weighted count it says it is. It agrees with the offline
  count, its window is right, and its memory bound holds at 32 PEs a node, 64 KiB, 10 s and
  23 Gbit/s. The record-keeping costs less than the probe it replaces.

**The one change is in how §5 tells the operator to read that count.** §5 sets `peers_in_calls`
alone beside the model's numbers and calls it "the model's quantity". But the model's PEs are
always on a wire, while a program's PE spends from a tenth to a third of its time waiting in a
quiet that the count does not charge to any peer. A put's call also returns while its bytes still
drain.

- **On the VM** the same order and the same traffic read about 4 of 7 at 1 MiB. They read 2.4
  to 2.95 at 64 KiB, and 1.8 to 2.1 for gets at a 16 MiB landing zone. Those figures fall in or
  below the old convoy's model range, 2.0 to 2.8, which §5 tells the operator to compare them
  with.
- **In a rented session** that reads as a convoy that is not there.
- **The fix is text:** §5, `summarize.py`'s header and `multistream.c`'s comment.

Eight notes follow. The open one the author asked about, echo-uring and IPv6, costs no session on

## What I checked and found sound

**S1, the count itself** (*observed in source*; *measured* by the commit's evidence and by me):

- **The sweep** (`multistream.c:242-305`) computes, for each peer, the length of the union of the
  node's calls toward it, clipped to the window. `extend` is the running-end union of intervals
  sorted by begin, and clipping by a constant keeps that order. The count summed over the peers
  and divided by the window is the time-averaged number of distinct peers.
- **Agreement.** The probe matches `analyze.py`'s offline count within the printed decimals in
  eleven jobs (`multistream-check.txt`), and again where a PE's room ran out
  (`multistream-room.txt`).
- **The window**, from the last PE's start to the first PE's last return, is one in which every
  PE of the node is in its loop. The start spread and the last calls cost milliseconds of ten
  seconds.
- **The memory bound holds as arithmetic.**
  - A record is 24 bytes (the `_Static_assert`), and the room is 2^20 records a node.
  - At 23 Gbit/s, 2,742 MiB/s, a node sends 43,870 blocks of 64 KiB a second: 438,700 in 10 s,
    42% of the room. That is 13,700 a PE at 32 PEs a node against room for 32,768.
  - A PE would need 2.4 times its share to run out. It then shortens the window and
    `peers_window_s` shows it.
  - The heap holds the records: 768 KiB a PE at 32 PEs a node and 3 MiB at 8, against the default
    256 MiB.
- **The record-keeping cannot perturb the window.** Each block costs two more vDSO clock reads
  and a 24-byte store into memory touched before the barrier. The probe it replaces made an
  atomic OR per block into one cache line that every PE of the node shared. The sweep, the
  private copy and the sort come after the CPU readings and the counters' end dump. The commit's
  cost check saw nothing on the VM.
- **The rest.** The symmetric statics; the node-mates' records read after a barrier; uneven
  nodes; and one-node jobs, which print no figure.

**P9-2** (*measured* in the VM's Slurm 24.11.5):

- **Process groups.** `timeout` and `salloc` share `timeout`'s group, and `srun` is in a group
  of its own, `salloc`'s child. So the timeout signals `salloc` alone, and `salloc` answers
  SIGTERM by revoking its allocation, which ends the step.
- **The environment.** A task sees:
  - `OSOE_NO_PROGRESS_TIMEOUT=60`, `SHMEM_DEBUG=1`, and a setting given to `launch`;
  - the step's `SLURM_STEP_NUM_TASKS`, `SLURM_NODEID`, `SLURM_LOCALID` and `SLURM_STEP_ID=0`,
    the names osoe's bootstrap reads (`bootstrap.c:88-94`).
- **Status 137, forced** (`timeout -k 3 5`, tasks ignoring SIGTERM):
  - `salloc` was killed at 8.0 s;
  - `srun` lived on, reparented, until KillWait ended the tasks, and the job was COMPLETING;
  - `launch`'s clean-up waited 21.9 s and left no job, step or process. That clean-up is
    `scancel` by name, then the `--gc` step under its own timeout.
  - The orphaned `srun`'s last lines land in the job's file after its "# exit" line. That is
