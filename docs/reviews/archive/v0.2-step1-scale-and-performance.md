## Verdict

**In this question's area the single-node build can be offered to desktop users
as the README describes it, and the full build is still what 0.1.0 was. Nothing
must change first.**

A job of one node runs the same instructions in both builds but the futex
system calls: of the 2,618 functions the two libraries share, the only ones
whose instructions differ - the disassembler's own artifacts aside - are the
futex file's six, the internode backend's entry points, which a one-node job
reaches only at start-up, and AM's `osoe_internode_init`, which allocates an
8 KiB array the single-node build does not (*measured*, below). The two system-call
forms cost the same on Linux 6.12 to within 5% - a wake of nobody 165 ns
classic against 164 futex2, a two-process wake round trip on one CPU 1.71 µs
against 1.69 - and so the builds' one-node timings agree: the library's wake
round trip 2.04 µs in the single-node build against 2.06 in the full build; the
one-node barrier and collectives at 2 to 32 PEs within the VM's noise, 38 pairs
of 32-PE barrier runs at a median ratio of 0.99; the stress tester's quick run
at 4 to 32 PEs, and the CPU each run used, alike. More PEs
than cores works, and nothing spins: with 31 PEs waiting on four cores, the
waiters used 0.02 CPU-seconds in three seconds and their agents 0.009, in both
builds and on Rocky. The agent's slice is accepted and ignored by Rocky's 5.14
and honoured by 6.12, and in a one-node job the agents it would schedule sleep.
`/dev/shm`'s need is the README's figure - 262.5 MiB a PE - and the check that
enforces it asks the same to the MiB in all three trees. In the full build the
seam costs a remote operation's caller one more branch: about 5 ns of an 8-byte
put's issue in this VM (6%), and nothing that a round trip, a stream or the
floor test can see.

Two things should change, neither before release: **the property the desktop
section leans on has no test** (Q3-1) - a wait that spins before it sleeps
passes both builds' whole suites, the floor pins included, and makes a 32-PE
barrier on four cores 24 to 35 times slower; and **the desktop example's first
run is refused on a 4 GiB machine**, the project's own Rocky VM among them, which
the record's verification did not show because it ran its 8- and 32-PE programs
at 16 MiB heaps without saying so (Q3-2).

## Findings, ranked

### Q3-1. "Nothing in the library spins" has no test in either build: a wait that spins before it sleeps passes both suites, the floor pins among them

- **Location**: README lines 156-160 (the desktop section: "More PEs than cores
  is fine: nothing in the library spins - a waiting PE sleeps on a futex") and
  614-617 (design goals: "Cores are not assumed spare ... nothing spins");
  `docs/decisions/README.md:38` ("no spin before blocking", a rule in force, whose
  named check, `shmem.memory-orders`, checks memory orders and not spins);
  `osoe_futex_wait_bitset` in `base/futex-classic.c` and `base/futex-futex2.c`, the
  one place the two builds differ; `base/tests/test-futex.c`, the contract the
  record says it "pins" for whichever file is built, which says nothing of CPU;
  `shmem/tests/test-floor.c`, the project's performance pins, all of them latency
  or rate (*observed in source*).
- **Scenario**: a change to a futex file, or to any wait above it, that polls or
  spins before it sleeps - the ordinary "spin a little for latency" change, which
  anyone tuning a ping-pong will want to make - goes in green, and the next
  desktop user who runs more PEs than cores pays for it in every collective. In
  the single-node build the futex file is the build's one difference from the
  full build, so it is exactly where the rule's "the same code but the wait
  syscall" leaves room for a regression only that build has. The one-node path
  has no performance test at all: `shmem.floor` and `env.echo-uring`, the only
  pins, are labelled `internode` or not built in the single-node build, and the
  brief's "floor tests of a one-node job" do not exist.
- **Evidence** (*measured*; the mutation is a loop on the word, returning at once
  when it changes, for up to 200 µs before the futex call, wakes and deadlines
  untouched - `mutate.py`, `mutate2.py`):
  - the single-node build with it, its whole suite (`ctest -LE model -j4`):
    **349 of 349 passed**, in 43 s;
  - the full build with it, configured `-DOSOE_TEST_PINS=ON`, its whole suite:
    **729 of 729 passed**, in 230 s, `shmem.floor` checking its pins under
    `OSOE_TEST_FLOOR_PINS=1` - and its ratios *improved*: a get's fastest trip
    1.06 times the bare io_uring echo's, against 1.89 to 1.99 unmutated in the
    same VM that morning, a put and quiet's median 0.72 of a get's against 0.966
    to 0.995, the stream 0.72 of the floor's against 0.14 to 0.39. A spin buys
    latency with a core, and every pin is a latency or a rate;
  - what it costs, one node, the single-node build against its mutant,
    alternating: a 32-PE barrier on four cores 1,634 to 2,498 µs against 58.5 to
    73.9 (three runs a side, 24 to 35 times pair by pair); an 8-byte reduction
    2,113 to 3,157 µs against 152 to 208;
    the CPU 31 idle waiters and their agents used in three seconds 0.385 + 0.187 s
    against 0.020 + 0.009, and at 8 PEs 0.088 + 0.043 against 0.006 + 0.003.
- **Severity**: **after release** - the code is right today (the idle
  measurements below); the guard is what is missing - and best before step 4,
  whose helpers rewrite the progress threads' waits. A test that times nothing
  but CPU catches it on any machine and under any load: `q3-idle`'s shape - more
  PEs than the machine has cores, PE 0 asleep outside the library for a second or
  two, the job failing when the others and their agents used more than a few
  percent of it - in both builds, not labelled `internode`.
- **Confidence**: high.

- **Location**: README lines 136-142 (`~/osoe/bin/osoe-launch -n 8 -- ./ring`, at

- **Confidence**: high.

## A job of one node, in the two builds

### The code

`fndiff.py` over the two release libraries of `7053819` (*measured*): 2,807
functions in the full build's, 2,619 in the single-node build's. The 189 only in
the full build are the internode backend's files and the PLT stubs of liburing
and the socket calls; the one only in the single-node build is its `unreachable`.
Of the functions in both, 42 differ in their instruction sequences: six of the
futex file's seven (`osoe_futex_check`, `_requirement`, `_timeouts`,
`_wait_bitset`, `_wake`, `_wake_bitset`; the seventh, `osoe_futex_wait`, is the
same tail call in both); the internode backend's 20 entry points and nine of
`internode.h`'s, of which a one-node job reaches the start-up check and init,
fini and a team number taken again - where AM's `osoe_internode_init` callocs
its arrivals array, a `uint64_t` a team block, 8 KiB at the default 1,024, and
the refusing backend's does not: memory, and no behaviour; and seven artifacts of the
disassembler naming an address or of padding (`osoe_abort_end` and
`osoe_agent_start`, whose only difference is the symbol `objdump` gives an `adrp`
target, two PLT stubs, `call_weak_fn`, a libgcc outline-atomics helper, and two
static functions named `resolve` in different files). `osoe_transport_put`, the
dispatch every operation takes, is the same 80-byte frame and instruction for
instruction in both. So "a job of one node behaves the same in both builds; only
the system call its waits sleep in differs" (README 147-149) holds of the
optimized binaries, not only of the source.

## What a desktop user meets

### More PEs than cores

- **It works, and the cost is the scheduler's.** 8, 16 and 32 PEs on four cores
  ran every probe, `osoe-bench` and `osoe-pacer --quick`, in both builds and on
  Rocky. The one-node barrier grows from about 40 µs at 8 PEs to 55 to 130 at 32
  in most batches in this VM, the collectives with it (the table above).
- **Nothing spins** (*measured*, `q3-idle`, three seconds, PE 0 asleep in
  `nanosleep`): the waiting PEs and their agents used, at 4, 8 and 32 PEs, 0.0046
  + 0.0024, 0.0063 + 0.0028 and 0.0201 + 0.0092 CPU-seconds in the full build,
  0.0045 + 0.0024, 0.0061 + 0.0032 and 0.0195 + 0.0090 in the single-node build,
  0.021 + 0.011 at 32 on Rocky - a sweep each 100 ms for every waiting thread
  and every agent, about 1% of a core at 32 PEs. Every library wait sleeps at once
  (`region-wait.c`, `osoe_region_wait`: the watch published, the word read, then
  the futex), and the one-node agent's wait is a plain futex wait on its word to
  the next sweep (`transport-am.c:202-208`), bumped by nothing in a one-node job
  but a stop or an abort (*observed in source*). The one loop the records know
  to run without sleeping - a team split's busy rounds while a block is on its
  way back, round five's minor 4, on the checklist's after-release list - is a
  loop of collectives, rare, and unchanged by this step.
- **The agents sleep.** An agent in a one-node job moves nothing: its pass returns
  false and its wake word is bumped only by a stop or the abort's
  (`agent_wake`'s writers: `agent.c`, `node.c`'s `osoe_node_wake_agents`,
  `internode-am.c` and `progress-host.c`, the last two only across nodes). Each
  agent used 0.4 to 1.2 ms of CPU in three seconds above.

### The agent's slice and CPUs

- **The slice**: `q3-slice` makes `agent.c`'s request alone. On Rocky's 5.14
  `sched_setattr` returns 0 and `sched_getattr` reads the runtime back as 0 -
  accepted and ignored; on Debian's 6.12 it reads 100,000 ns where it read
  2,100,000 before - honoured (*measured*). The README's "Linux 6.12 and later;
  an older kernel ignores it" holds, and since a one-node job's agents sleep, the
  desktop section's "not needed here" holds too.
- **The CPUs**: `osoe-launch` binds nothing (no affinity call in `launch.c`), so
  each PE's mask is every CPU and `OSOE_AGENT_CPUS=task`, the default, already lets
  its agent run anywhere (*observed in source*).

### `/dev/shm`

- **The figure is the check's.** 2,100 MiB for eight PEs is 262.5 a PE: the 256 MiB
  heap, about 6 MiB of the library's region, a 1 MiB statics object and the PE's
  share of the control segment (*measured*). The README's "about 263 MiB a PE" and
  the quick start's "about 1,050 MiB" for four are right, and the three trees ask
  for the same to the MiB (Q3-2's evidence).
- **What is written is a page here and there.** Under the 64 MiB heaps of these
  runs `/dev/shm`'s use while a 32-PE job ran was about 1 MiB (`statvfs` from PE 0); the
  check counts objects whole, as the README says it does.
- **So at the default heap a machine holds about two PEs a GiB of memory**: eight
  PEs need about 4.1 GiB of it, 16 about 8.2, 32 about 16.4, at the
  distributions' default `/dev/shm` of half the memory, and a little more in
  practice, the kernel's total being less than what is fitted (*arithmetic*,
  from the check's figure); the README gives the per-PE figure and the remedy
  (`SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE`), and the refusal names both (Q3-2).

### Past 32 PEs

Nothing has measured a one-node job past 32 PEs, and the README says so ("Only
the one-node barrier is timed past 4 PEs a node ... nothing beyond 32"). A
desktop with many cores is the single-node build's audience, and its one-node
release is one wake of every waiting PE by the last to arrive, O(P) serial
kernel work, which design §5.3 kept over a chained wake by measurement on four
cores and says "should be measured there before it is changed" at a high core
count (*documented*). Not a defect; the measurement for a many-core host, when
one exists, belongs beside the gated items of `task-v0.2.md`.

## The seam's price

The brief asked what the call across `internode-backend.h` costs every remote
operation of the full build.

- **Mechanism** (*observed in the binaries*): at 0.1.0 `remote_put` was static in
  `transport-am.c` and inlined into `osoe_transport_put`, a 192-byte frame; at
  `7053819` `osoe_transport_put` saves seven registers in an 80-byte frame,
  decides the route, restores them and tail-branches (`b`) to
  `osoe_internode_backend_put`, which then saves its own - some 15 to 20
  instructions more on the remote path, and a direct branch, not an indirect call: the
  library's hidden visibility leaves no PLT between them. The context's ordering
  state is one more load away, behind `context->internode`. On the local path the
  dispatch's frame is smaller than 0.1.0's, so a node-mate's put pays nothing.
- **Cost** (*measured*, `q3-issue`, two logical nodes of one PE, 0.1.0, the head
  and the head with link-time optimization rotating): in the steadiest five rounds
  of ten, an 8-byte put's issue (5,000 puts and a quiet, 60 repetitions, the
  median of each round's repetitions, then of the rounds) 83.5 ns at 0.1.0, 88.3
  at the head, 86.0 with link-time optimization; a non-fetching add 84.2, 89.8 and
  88.6. An earlier batch (20,000 puts, 15 repetitions) had the head 4 to 6 ns
  slower than 0.1.0 in four of six pairs, 19 ns in one and level in the last;
  under the host's heaviest load the three were indistinguishable (medians of
  nine rounds 153, 152 and 137 ns). Round trips do not show it: a fetch-add or a
  get 55 to 57 µs in both trees (one run of 0.1.0's at 70 to 72). Nor does the
  floor test: four alternating pairs
  of `shmem_test_floor`, pins checked, a get's fastest trip 1.917, 1.953, 1.971

## The scaling section and the defaults

- **The scaling section did not change in this step** (`git diff 53b8f0f 7053819
  -- README.md`, *observed*), and nothing this step added grows with the job's PE
  count: the internode backend's state for a context, now behind
  `context->internode`, is the same arrays as 0.1.0's dirty set -
  `high_segment`, `order`, `touched`, `shared_highest_segment` and `dirty_bits`,
  21 bytes and a bit a peer node, the README's "21 a peer node for each context
  in use" - and a one-node job allocates none of it; the refusing backend keeps
  nothing; `OSOE_INTERNODE_FANOUT_DEFAULT` is 0.1.0's 4. The control segment's
  layout is the same in both builds, as the adversarial read measured and the
  capacity check's identical figures confirm.
- **What a one-node job carries for many nodes, by design**: the landing zones,
  about 260 KiB a PE of the control segment, are laid out in every job
  (`docs/decisions/2026-10-06-single-node-seam.md`, item 4) and never used by one
  node, and the lanes, 2.7 MiB a PE of the region, are touched only by an
  internode phase. Both are address space, counted by the capacity check -
  about 3 MiB of the 262.5 a PE, 1.1% - and the settings parity they buy is the
  record's stated reason (*observed in source*).
- **The defaults** (`OSOE_COLLECTIVE_FANOUT`, `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PIPELINE`,
  `OSOE_GET_LANDING_BYTES`, `OSOE_AGENT_SLICE_US`, `OSOE_AGENT_CPUS`) are
  unchanged, and round six's table of them stands. On a desktop - hardware unlike
  the Pi - none of them matters to a job of one node: there is no internode tree
  to fan out or pipeline, the landing zone is address space, the slice is ignored
  before 6.12 and schedules a sleeping thread after it, and the agent's CPUs are
  every CPU under `osoe-launch`.

## Measurements and methodology

- **The VM's one-node wakes are its idle vCPUs'.** A wake across cores costs what
  waking a descheduled vCPU costs the host: the two-PE ping-pong took 40 to 160 µs
  across vCPUs and 2 µs on one; the 8-PE barrier fell from about 40 µs to 20, and
  the 32-PE one to about 65, when four busy loops kept every vCPU awake. So the
  VM's one-node figures, the README's among them, describe the VM, and a desktop's
  will differ - probably downward - and should be measured there.
- **The README's one-node barrier figures** - "11 µs at 2 PEs and 42 at 32 when
  the machine was quiet (design §5.3), 13 to 18 and 105 to 116 under a review's
  load" (README 719-722) - were not reproduced and not contradicted: on today's
  shared host 2 PEs took 12 to 21 µs, and 32 PEs 55 to 86 µs in the steadiest
  batches, on one CPU or four, and 66 to 420 in others; 0.1.0 took the same as
  the head in the same batches, so this step did not move them.
- **An unexplained tail, low confidence.** Of 38 pairs of 32-PE barrier runs on
  four cores, the single-node build was within 20% of the full build in 30, never
  more than 13% faster, and 1.22 to 2.13 times slower in 8. Four of the 8 came
  back to back in one batch whose full-build runs were themselves 1.4 to 1.8
  times that batch's later ones, the host loaded; one was a full-build run
  faster than its batch. In wall time only: the CPU per run was the same in every
  batch that measured it. And not the classic futex: in the two loaded rounds of
  the batch that ran the single-node build with the futex2 file beside the other
  two, that build and the single-node build were both 1.4 to 1.7 times the full
  build. Pinned to one CPU (10 pairs), at 8 PEs (13 pairs) and beside busy loops
  in the VM (16 pairs) the builds were equal. A difference that appears only on a
  loaded shared host, in wall time, and in a single-node build with futex2 as
  well, is scheduling or code layout, not the wait - but it is not explained, and
  one look on a quiet, unshared machine would settle it. Not a finding.
- **Bimodality** within and between runs, as round six found it: whole runs come
  out about twice as long as others, and `osoe-bench`'s local stream and pairs
  lines are bimodal on every tree. Every figure above is a within-batch
  comparison.
- **Not measured**: a quiet host; a native Linux desktop; x86-64; more than 32 PEs
  on one node; the deadlock detector or `OSOE_NO_PROGRESS_TIMEOUT` under heavy
  oversubscription for long runs (question 2's).

## For the other questions

- **Rocky Linux 9.8's 5.14 kernel has futex2** (question 4): `futex_wake` (454)
  and `futex_wait` (455) answer and sleep there - `q3-futex-cost`'s futex2 batches
  ran, and `/proc/kallsyms` has `__arm64_sys_futex_wait` - a Red Hat backport. The
  README's reason for the single-node build on Rocky 9, "Linux 5.14", is the
  version; the full build's real obstacles there are io_uring, off by default
  (`kernel.io_uring_disabled = 2`), and no `liburing-devel` in the enabled
  repositories (its runtime 2.12 is installed). The advice stands; the reason
  could say so. On that kernel the two futex forms cost the same (the table
  above).
- **A launcher that dies before its cleanup leaves a refused start's names**
  (question 5, minor): six refused starts piped as `osoe-launch ... 2>&1 | grep
  -m1 ...` left five control segments in `/dev/shm` (*measured*), the launcher
  probably ended by SIGPIPE when grep left, before it removes leftovers
  (*inferred*, not checked) - the pattern of `osoe-launch ... | head`.
  `osoe-stat --gc` removed all five, as the README says it does.

## What is sound, and what convinced me

- **The rule the step is held to holds for performance**: the shared functions
  are instruction for instruction the same in both optimized libraries but the
  futex file's; the system calls cost the same on 6.12 and on Rocky's 5.14; and
  every one-node comparison - the wake round trip pinned to a CPU, the barrier and
  the collectives at 2 to 32 PEs, whole stress-tester runs and their CPU, the
  start - came out alike within the VM's noise, which a single-node build given
  futex2 in place of the classic calls did not change.
- **The desktop section's claims are true of the code**: nothing spins (31
  waiting PEs, 0.02 CPU-seconds in three seconds); the agents sleep and the
  settings that schedule them do not matter on one node; the slice is ignored
  before 6.12; `/dev/shm`'s figure is the check's; more PEs than cores runs, and
  starts in tens of milliseconds at 32.
- **The full build is 0.1.0's** in everything timed: the floor ratios and round
  trips unchanged, the one-node lines unchanged, the layout unchanged; the seam's
  call costs 5 ns of an issue, with link-time optimization as the known remedy
  should it ever matter.
- **Round six's fixes in this area hold.**
- **What I could not make fail**: no one-node difference between the builds that
  survived a control. What I could: the tests' and pins' blindness to a spinning
  wait (Q3-1), which is why it heads the findings.
