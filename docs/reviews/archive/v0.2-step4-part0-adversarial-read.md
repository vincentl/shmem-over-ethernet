**What I reviewed:** `git diff 3ee3357 14cdbaa -- shmem tools` on the branch
`one-thread-ladder`. The worktree's HEAD is `8a0e2fd`. Its code is the same as
`14cdbaa`: the later commit changes only documents and evidence. I also checked

them.

The record's section "What a small message waits behind a stream, on the Pi"
and its evidence directory `2026-10-07-pi-head-of-line/` are uncommitted changes
in the worktree. They appeared while this review was running, and I did not

**How I checked it:** I read the C against the seven properties in the brief.

- I ran `tools/modelcheck/reservation_model.py` in full, from a copy. All 44
  cases match their expectations and it exits 0
  (`models/reservation_model-full.out`).
- I wrote three model variants of my own (`models/*.py`, outputs beside them):
  - the C's real turnover wait, in place of the model's "wake on any change of
    the head";
  - whole-message writers beside filling ones;
  - three more breakages, to confirm the checks can fail.
- Nothing ran in the VMs or on the Pi. Every timing figure below is a
  calculation, and I mark it as one.

**Verdict, in short:** the change keeps properties 1 to 6. I found no fatal
issue.

- **F1** is the one result worth acting on before the default buffer is chosen.
  A stream of puts now fills every buffer to the brim, so other writers' small
  messages and the agent's opportunistic replies no longer find room behind it.
  - They wait longer: about one more buffer's wire time, by calculation.
  - Their admission is now a race. This is a latency and fairness trade, not a
    hang.
  - The Pi head-of-line measurement in the record never ran the base, so it
    cannot show this cost.
- **F2:** the floor test's stream pin was set for the old turnover rate, and its
  margin against the regression it guards has shrunk.
- **F3 to F5** are small notes: a weak test round, stale comments, and dead
  code.

## F1 — should-fix (measure before choosing the buffer; the code is correct): a stream now takes the room small messages used to use

**Scenario (default sizes: 64 KiB chunk, 128 KiB buffers).** PE 0 streams
1 MiB puts to node 1. PE 1, on the same node, issues fetch-adds to node 1
(`hol.c`'s own scenario).

**Before the change:**

1. A buffer opens, and the stream reserves one 65,536-byte message at offset 32.
2. The room left is 131,072 − 32 − 65,536 = 65,504 bytes.
3. The stream's next 65,536-byte message does not fit, so the stream sleeps
   until the next turnover.
4. Those 65,504 bytes stay free until the buffer is sealed, about 0.5 ms later
   at 1 GbE (one 64 KiB segment on the wire).
5. A fetch-add's 64-byte request (`padded(56)`) arriving at any moment in that
   window is reserved at once.
6. The agent's opportunistic drain (`progress.c:722-726`) can also put small
   replies there.

So for streams of puts of half a buffer or more, every buffer kept slack that no
put could use.

**After the change:**

1. The stream's next part asks for 65,536 bytes and is given 65,504
   (`connection.c:76-79`), since that is at least its floor of 4,120
   (`transport-internode.c:615-623`).
2. The buffer is now full: less than 8 bytes remain.
3. The fetch-add's request finds `FULL` and sleeps until the next turnover.
4. At that turnover the open wakes the stream's writer and the fetch-add's
   writer together.
5. The fetch-add's request is admitted only if its CAS lands before the
   stream's second CAS. The stream's second CAS comes one 64 KiB `memcpy` after
   its first.
6. If the request loses, it waits another turnover.

The agent's opportunistic drain likewise finds the open buffer full on its next
pass. Replies toward the node this node is streaming to now leave with the next
pre-open drain, one buffer later. Fetch-add replies are the case that matters
here, since the whole get-reply pieces already needed the pre-open drain.

**Estimate at 1 GbE (calculation, about 8 µs per KiB on the wire):**

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Segments of a stream | about 64 KiB | about 128 KiB |
| Time each segment spends on the wire | about 0.52 ms | about 1.05 ms |
| Time before a small request's bytes leave the node | about 0.8 ms: ½ segment in flight + the open segment | about 1.6 to 2.1 ms: ½ turnover + the sealed segment + up to 64 KiB of the stream ahead of it in the new buffer |
| Each race the request loses | — | about 1.05 ms more |

**What this does and does not break:**

- Nothing hangs. The pre-open drain still gives replies half of every buffer
  (`progress.c:1098`), and a stream is finite.
- §3.2's bound of "roughly two segments" was written for full segments, so the
  change does not exceed it. Before, streams simply ran at half of it.
- One design claim is lost, though. §3.11 says "divisible ones do not starve"
  (`docs/design/shmem-ethernet-design.md:1173`).
  - That held for small writers only because big puts could not tile the
    buffer.
  - A small writer's chance to get in has shrunk from a whole send time to one
    `memcpy`.
  - The number of turnovers it can be passed over is no longer bounded by the
    protocol, only by the scheduler. On an oversubscribed node, the Pi's normal
    case, the scheduler gives no such bound.

**Evidence:**

- The arithmetic above.
- `connection.c:76-79` and `transport-internode.c:615-623`.
- The record's table "What a small message waits behind a stream, on the Pi"
  is of the branch's tree. The record says "the branch's tree at `8a0e2fd`".
  `hol.sh` and `lowat*.sh` take the tree as an argument, and the evidence
  directory holds only `hol-8a0e2fd/`, `lowat-1/` and `lowat-buffer-1/`. No base
  run is recorded.
- The record's "round trips … unchanged at 55 µs" was measured without a
  concurrent stream.
- With the kernel's unsent queue uncapped (21 ms), about 1 ms is lost in the
  noise. With it capped at 16 KiB, the configuration the record points toward
  (3.87 ms median, measured on the branch), it would be a large share.

**What would fix it:**

1. Run `hol.c` on 3ee3357 against 8a0e2fd at 128 KiB, with
   `notsent-lowat-experiment.patch` applied (16 KiB). Do this before item 10
   picks the default buffer from that table.
2. If the cost is real, a cut part could stop short of the end by a small tail
   T, of 512 B to 1 KiB and smaller than `least`. Whole messages of T bytes or
   less — requests, AMOs, small replies — would then always fit behind a stream,
   and the opportunistic drain would get a place again. The cost is at most T
   per buffer, under 1% of 128 KiB.
3. Model the tail as a `fill` variant with a whole writer beside a filling one.
   My `mixed` cases are a template.
4. Either way, update §3.11 (lines 1146-1155 and 1173) and the
   `reserve_waiting` comment (F4a).

## F2 — should-fix (verify): `shmem.floor`'s STREAM pin has lost most of its margin against a polling writer wait

**Scenario.** §8.4's regression: a writer's wait that polls instead of sleeping
on the turnover word. `test-floor.c:34-38` names it:

> There a 64 KiB put fills half the buffer and every other one waits for a
> turnover (§8.4), so this is the pin a writer's wait that polls fails:
> measured 0.21-0.43x, and 0.037-0.055x with the poll.

The pin is `STREAM 0.10` (line 95). The change halves the turnovers per byte, and
the comment is now stale.

**Estimate (calculation):**

- From the recorded figures, the poll cost about 72 µs per turnover. With it
  the library moved 0.046 × about 14,500 MiB/s, roughly 670 MiB/s: 93 µs per
  64 KiB against about 21 µs without the poll.
- On the branch the library moves about 6,500 MiB/s, which is about 19 µs per
  128 KiB turnover.
- Adding the same poll gives about 91 µs per 128 KiB, roughly 1,370 MiB/s, or
  **about 0.095 of the floor**.
- The pin's margin has fallen from about 2x to about 5%, so noise now decides
  whether this regression is caught.

**What would fix it:** rerun §8.4's poll mutant on the branch with
`OSOE_TEST_PINS=ON`. Then raise STREAM — the library now measures 0.43 to
0.45 — and rewrite the comment.

## F3 — note: the new signal round in `test-internode.c` quiets before it waits

The round runs `shmem_putmem_signal`, then `shmem_quiet()` (line 324), then
`shmem_uint64_wait_until` (line 325), then checks the bytes.

The quiet means the PE reads its inbox long after its sender's put and signal
have usually both been applied.

- The round does catch a part that was lost, misplaced or wrong (`pattern` is
  distinct per byte).
- It is weak evidence that the signal follows the last byte. That evidence is
  the model's ORDER check plus the structural argument (one reservable buffer,
  successive CASes by one thread).

The round of 64 bytes had the same shape before the change.

**Fix:** move the `shmem_quiet()` after the check. The barrier that follows
still completes the PE's own put.

The file's header comment (line 12) also needs a word changed. "A put shorter
than an AM chunk is one message" is no longer true: a put shorter than a chunk
can now be two parts.

## F4 — note: comments and design text that the change makes untrue

- **a.** `transport-internode.c:504-507` and design §3.11, lines 1146-1155,
  are stale. They say "the room is divisible for small messages only … room
  for one 64 KiB message … all but one sleep again".
  - A put's room is now divisible down to a sixteenth of a chunk.
  - At the default sizes, two large writers get in at each turnover: one whole
    part and one cut part.
  - The case for a hand-on wake changes accordingly.
- **b.** `transport-internode.c:629-632` says "the next chunk may have to
  wait…". It should say the next part.
- **c.** `test-connection-fill.c:77` says "every length from 1 to LONGEST turns
  up across the writers". `length_of` gives 891 distinct lengths out of 2,048 in
  its 1,200 messages (computed). All eight residues mod 8 do occur, and 45
  lengths are below the floor, so coverage is adequate; only the claim is wrong.
- **d.** The module docstring of `reservation_model.py` was not updated.
  - Line 62 still says "every message is 1".
  - The list of properties lacks OVERFLOW.
  - The list of breakages lacks `fill_stale`, `fill_no_advance` and
    `fill_floor`.
  - "What is mirrored from the C" still names only `osoe_connection_reserve`.
- **e.** `connection.c:72-73` cites `fill_stale` for "reserve past the buffer's
  end". The model's counterexample is a CAS into a *sealed* buffer: the head is
  67, which is SEALED with 3 used. It is also past the end. Saying "into a
  sealed buffer or past its end" would match the trace.
- **f.** These are approximations, and close enough.
  - `transport-internode.c:589` says "4 KiB at the default chunk". The figure is
    4,094 B, `limit / 16` with `limit` = 65,512.
  - Design §3.2 says "a sixteenth of a chunk". It is a sixteenth of the largest
    payload, and it is lowered to the remainder when the remainder is shorter.

## F5 — note: one mask is dead code, and one latent bug is now fixed

**The alignment mask.** `room & ~(OSOE_MESSAGE_ALIGN - 1)` (`connection.c:77`)
never changes a value in any reachable configuration.

- A buffer is a power of two of at least 2 KiB (`am-configuration.c:41-42`,
  `connection-area.c:132`).
- Every head is a multiple of 8, whether set by open, by `drain_reserve`, or by
  a reservation.

So a mutant that drops the mask survives every test, harmlessly. Keep it as
defence, or check the invariant once when the area opens.

**The latent wrap.** The new room arithmetic also fixes a wrap in the old
`h + len > capacity`. With h = 32 and len = 0xFFFFFFF8, h + len wraps to 24, the
check passes, and the CAS moves the head backwards. No caller passes such a
length, so it was never reachable. Now it returns `FULL`.

## What I tried to break and could not, with how

For each property: the scenario I suspected, and the reason it cannot happen.

### Property 1: one writer's messages arrive in the order it issued them

- **Parts arriving out of order.**
  - The parts of a put are successive reservations by one thread.
  - Each one comes after the previous one's CAS succeeded.
  - At most one buffer is ever unsealed.
  - So a later part lands either later in the same buffer, or in a buffer opened
    later with a higher seq.
  - Sealed buffers are sent in seq order, and a segment is applied in offset
    order.
  - The length of a part plays no part in this argument.
- **The signal.** The FENCED rmw goes through `send_message`
  (`least == most`), after the loop.
- **The model.** ORDER is checked per unit, including the signal's unit after
  the put's (`sizes=(3,1)`), under SC and TSO. It still holds with the C's real
  turnover wait in my variant.
- **Quiet.**
  - `mark(c, peer, r.seq)` runs for every part, and the seqs of one writer's
    parts never decrease. So the dirty set's highest seq is the last part's.
  - The model's QUIET check holds.
  - `OSOE_CHECK_QUIET`'s `returned_highest` is raised per part, as it was per
    chunk.

### Property 2: every byte lands once, at its own offset, with the right value

- **The cut arithmetic.**
  - A cut happens when r.len < padded(16 + n).
  - Then r.len is a multiple of 8, at most padded(16 + n) − 8, which gives
    n′ = r.len − 24 < n.
  - Also r.len ≥ least = 24 + roundup8(min(n, smallest_part)).
  - So n′ ≥ roundup8(smallest_part), which is at least 62 at the tests' sizes
    and 4,094 at the defaults.
  - If n < smallest_part, then `least == most` and the message is never cut.
  - So the loop always advances, and a cut part is never the last.
- **Advancing the cursor.** `at`, `offset` and `len` all advance by the new n.
- **The header and padding.** `h.len` is updated before the `memcpy`. A cut
  part has no padding: padded(16 + n′) = r.len.
- **Mutants.** The tier-2 mutants confirm all of this: `header_not_cut`,
  `payload_not_cut` and `payload_short` are each caught by 17 tests.
- **`OSOE_WIRE_SPLIT`.** The wire and the parser do not depend on where
  messages begin and end (`wire.c:28-37`, `wire.c:168-230`, `am.c:143-321`).
- **The tests' tiny sizes.** At chunk 1,024 and buffer 2,048, a 5,000-byte put
  is cut at its second message: the room is 992, against 1,024 asked for and a
  floor of 88. So the tier-2 suite does exercise cuts. The experimental patch's
  fixed 4,096-byte floor would have exercised none there; the committed
  `limit / 16` fixes that.

### Property 3: reservations stay inside open buffers, and the accounting is exact

- **Overflow, or a reservation into a sealed buffer.**
  - `len` is computed from `h`, and `h` is the CAS's expected value
    (`connection.c:59-62`, `76-77`, `87`).
  - `h` is unsealed, so a seal in between makes the CAS fail.
  - The reservation ends at or before `capacity` ≤ 2^30, below the SEALED bit.
- **The model's checks are live.**
  - `fill_stale` gives OVERFLOW.
  - My extra breakage `room_plus_one` gives OVERFLOW without any seal.
  - `commit_most`, which commits the remainder instead of the part, gives
    STUCK: an `ACCT` failure as a never-sendable buffer
    (`models/fill_breakages.out`).
- **`filled == head`.** The commit adds `r->len`, and `finalize`'s walk
  (`progress.c:878-893`) steps by `padded(h.len) == r.len`.

### Property 4: no hang, no permanent starvation

See F1 for the latency.

- **A floor no buffer could ever satisfy.**
  - The pre-open drain takes at most capacity/2 (`progress.c:1098`).
  - So a freshly opened buffer offers at least capacity/2 − 32, which is at
    least chunk − 32, which is at least 992.
  - A put's `least` is at most 4,120 at the defaults (88 at the tests' sizes).
  - `send_message`'s `least` is at most 64.
  - A FULL against an open buffer that is empty (`used == header`), which the
    lead would never seal, is therefore impossible.
- **A lost wake, or a false `missed_wake`.**
  - Room only grows when a buffer opens, and the open bumps the turnover word
    for registered waiters.
  - So `count_if_lost`'s premise still holds: a success after a sweep implies
    the word moved.
  - Model variant `fill_variants.py` uses the C's word: it is loaded before the
    attempt, the writer sleeps until it moves, and only `open` moves it, after
    the head store.
    - All 12 turnover cases hold, with liveness (+live): fill, mixed, TSO, and
      beside the pre-open drain.
    - The breakage in which open never bumps the word is STUCK.
  - So the model's abstraction, which wakes a writer on any change of the head,
    hides nothing (`models/fill_variants_live.out`).
- **The pre-open drain's guarantee.** It is unchanged.
  - A get piece is at most chunk − 32 (`piece_limit`,
    `transport-internode.c:971-976`).
  - So its reply is at most chunk, which is capacity/2, which is the budget.
- **The opportunistic drain.** It returns on `FULL` and never waits.

### Property 5: every other caller behaves as before

- **The whole-message case.**
  - `send_message` (`transport-internode.c:957-958`) and `reserve_for_reply`
    (`progress.c:726`) pass `least == most`.
  - In that case `len < least` holds exactly when `most > capacity − h`, which
    is the old `h + len > capacity` without its wrap (see F5).
  - Gets, AMOs, nbi forms, contexts and THREAD_MULTIPLE never reach the cut.
- **Collectives.** Their small header and grant puts are below `smallest_part`,
  so they are never cut. Their 16 KiB piece puts may be cut, and each is
  followed by a FENCED rmw on the same thread.
- **The model.** My `mixed` cases put whole writers beside filling ones, with
  and without the turnover word, and they hold with liveness.

### Property 6: the receiver applies shorter put messages correctly

- **Flags.** The receiver ignores a put's `flags` (`progress.c:79-85`,
  `426-436`). Puts only ever carry 0 or `OSOE_TRANSPORT_MORE` (`rma.c:44`,
  `signal.c:101`, the collectives).
- **Actions per message.**
  - Each message runs one `notify`, a range-overlap check
    (`watch.c:106-136`), and bumps counters.
  - In a stream, each buffer of twice the size carries one whole message and one
    cut part. So there are as many messages per byte as before: one per
    65,496 bytes against one per 65,512.
  - The record's `messages_applied`, `notify.calls` and `application_applied`
    are not pinned by any test. The deadlock detector's count only needs to
    increase.
- **Regions.**
  - The heap and the internal region are contiguous, and `resolve` checks each
    part's bounds (`progress-host.c:53-102`).
  - The statics: for the agent's own PE, `osoe_node_statics_at` splits at the
    data/bss seam by the start offset only (`node.h:1344-1351`).
    - A span across the seam is refused at the origin unless the two ranges are
      adjacent (`symmetric.c:17-38`).
    - A part is a sub-range of the put, so it cannot create a span the put did
      not have.
  - Word tearing at a cut that lies off 8-byte alignment in the target existed
    before, at chunk boundaries and at wire splits.
- **Payload alignment.** Every fixed part is a multiple of 8
  (`am.h:184-191`), so a cut keeps every following message's payload 8-aligned.

### Integer edge cases

- **`len` near `UINT32_MAX` or `SIZE_MAX`.** `n` is capped at `limit` and
  `len` is a `size_t`.
- **`limit / 16` when `limit` is small.**
  - `limit = chunk − 24`, at least 1,000, because chunk is at least 1,024 and
    buffer is at least 2 × chunk.
  - `payload_limit`'s branch `room < chunk` is therefore unreachable.
  - So `smallest_part` is at least 62, and the fallback `: 1` is dead.
- **`r.len − overhead`.** It cannot underflow: r.len ≥ 32 > 24.
- **`room & ~7`.** It is a no-op (F5).

### Property 7: does the model say what the C does?

**Step for step it does.**

- It scans head[0] then head[1].
- It takes the length from the loaded head, and the floor as
  `min(rem, least)`.
- A CAS on the same head, then seq, the copy, a commit of what was taken, and
  the advance. A lost CAS means looking again.
- Its abstractions are:
  - units in place of bytes, which loses only the dead alignment mask;
  - no per-part cap at `limit`, irrelevant at caps of 2 and 3;
  - a FULL wait on any change of the head, which my variant shows to be
    harmless.
- The three breakages, and my two, fail the way the property they guard
  predicts.

**What it cannot say:** anything about latency or fairness (F1). Its liveness is
AG EF done, true by construction while every stream is finite.

**Would a real bug slip past both the model and the tests?**

- Not at the reservation level: there are model cases plus
  `shmem.connection.fill` (WHOLE, ORDER, STALESEQ, FLOOR, FILL, BYTES, ACCT).
- Not in `remote_put`'s arithmetic: the tier-2 suite at 1 KiB and 2 KiB covers
  it, and five mutants were each caught.
- What could slip:
  - a regression to polling waits (F2);
  - an ordering regression in the signal round (F3), which only the model would
    catch;
  - a cost in small-message latency (F1), which nothing measures.

**What the fill test actually exercises.** It reimplements the cut logic rather
than calling `remote_put`. That is fine, because the tier-2 suite covers
`remote_put` itself. It drains replies only before open, never opportunistically,
and never runs a whole writer beside the filling ones.

## Verdict

**Properties 1 to 6 hold.**

- Order of delivery, every byte exactly once, reservations inside unsealed
  buffers with exact accounting, the turnover wait, the pre-open drain's
  guarantee, and every other caller (by the `least == most` equivalence) are
  intact.
- That is supported by the code, by the tree's model (all 44 cases as
  expected), and by my variants:
  - the C's real turnover wait;
  - mixed whole and filling writers;
  - two more breakages.

**Property 4, strictly:** nothing hangs, and nothing starves while a stream is
finite. But one practical guarantee is gone, the bounded admission of small
writers behind a large-put stream (F1).

**Before merging, or at least before item 10 is decided:**

1. Measure F1 on the Pi against the base, with the unsent queue capped.
2. Re-derive the floor's STREAM pin (F2).

F3 to F5 are cleanup.
