The diff read: `git diff 49c62d3 a4d88e3` (one commit, a4d88e3, the ladder's fourth rung, on
`main` at 49c62d3). Every file:line below is at a4d88e3.

**Verdict in one line:** nothing found that loses, repeats, reorders or corrupts a byte, or
holds a buffer across a reap. Two should-fix findings, neither in today's behaviour: a fatal whose
header promises a check it does not make, and a new rule (never read a buffer once it is given
back) that nothing checks. Three documents still describe the copy, the committed classifier
cannot show the 27% the commit cites, and the 1 MiB stream leans slightly down. The rest are
notes.

## Findings

### S1. `osoe_wire_consume`'s fatal checks the buffer, not the peek its header names

**Severity: should-fix** (a check weaker than its contract, and weakest exactly where it is
needed: under `OSOE_WIRE_SPLIT`).

`wire.h:151-153` says: "The first `n` bytes of the last peek are read ... Fatal past what the
peek returned." The code (`wire.c:246-248`) is
`if (w->queued == 0 || n > r->filled[b] - w->offset) osoe_fatal(...)`. That checks the buffer's
unread bytes. Without a split the two are the same number: the peek shows the whole remainder.
They differ only under `OSOE_WIRE_SPLIT`, which is where every suite runs the engine.

Scenario:
1. Take `shmem.progress`'s settings (`CMakeLists.txt:1013-1017`: `OSOE_WIRE_BYTES=256`,
   `OSOE_WIRE_SPLIT=3000`). Buffer b holds 256 unread bytes, and the peek is truncated to 40.
2. A caller consumes the buffer's remainder instead of the peek's count: `consume(w, 256)`. This
   is the shape a loop takes when written as "parse what the buffer holds, give it back", as a
   Part 1 owner loop might be. `256 > 256 - 0` is false, so there is no fatal.
3. If the caller parsed only the 40, the next 216 bytes are skipped. The parser mis-frames and
   ends the job later with a framing fatal naming a segment, not the misuse. If the caller parsed
   all 256, the injection is silently defeated: every split suite stays green while §8.2's
   partial reads go unexercised, and nothing says so.

Also slipping past:
- a second consume after one peek, which is checked against whatever buffer is now first and
  skips n bytes of it;
- a consume with no peek at all.

And one call the header's wording admits is fatal: `consume(w, 0)` after a peek that returned 0
(`queued == 0`). Its message then speaks of bytes "consumed past the provided buffer they were
peeked from" when nothing was peeked. No caller does either today: `receive()`
(`progress.c:658-662`) consumes exactly what it peeked.

Fix, either:
- record the peek: `w->peeked = n` in `osoe_wire_peek` (0 on an empty one), fatal on
  `n > w->peeked` in consume, then clear it. That is one store and one compare, and the message
  becomes true; or
- reword the header to what is checked: "Fatal past the buffer the peek showed".

### S2. Nothing would see a provided buffer read after it is given back

**Severity: should-fix** (the property holds today, by two adjacent lines; it is the change's
one new hazard, and design §3.3 sets the standard of asserting rather than remembering).

Before this change no provided buffer was read after `osoe_wire_recv` returned. Now one is read
for the whole parse, and the rule is that it goes back only after: `progress.c:661-662` parses,
then consumes. `io_uring_buf_ring_advance`'s release store (`ring.c:119`) is what orders the
parse's reads before the kernel's next write (property 4 below).

Nothing checks that order:
- `test-wire.c` (`drain`, 133-139) and `test-wire-pair.c` (146-153) check bytes before their
  own consume. That is the tests' order, not the engine's.
- The engine suites (`shmem.progress`, `shmem.barrier-am.*`, the internode and multinode suites
  at 256-byte buffers with splits) would see a reordering only if the kernel refilled that very
  buffer between an early recycle and the parse's read. Task work does run mid-pass:
  `do_signal` is 8.1-12.7% of the receiving agent in `profile-recv1`. So the corruption is
  possible, but rare and timing-dependent. It would surface as a framing fatal or a wrong byte,
  far from its cause.
- ASan cannot see it either, since every buffer is one live `malloc` block (`ring.c:62`).

The parser's half is stated and untested the same way. `am.h:284-287` now promises that nothing
points into `bytes` once `osoe_am_parse` returns. `test-am.c`'s `feed` (146-158) parses slices
of one array that is never overwritten. So a parser that kept a pointer into an earlier slice
would pass every cut. An example: an "optimisation" that uses a fixed part in place when it
arrives whole, then reads it again at `complete` after a payload split across calls.

A plausible regression: Part 1 moves `receive()` into an owner loop, and a rewrite returns
buffers sooner by consuming before parsing. Or it peeks several buffers and recycles them before
parsing them as a batch. Most runs of every suite stay green.

Evidence for the standard: design §3.3 as built (`shmem-ethernet-design.md:416-419`) says the
reap's check "is how 'never hold a provided buffer' became assertable rather than remembered".
The converse rule this change introduces, never read one given back, is remembered.

Fix, a few lines and none on the release path:
- In the sanitizer presets, poison each buffer when it goes to the kernel (at open, and in
  `osoe_ring_recycle`), and unpoison its `filled` bytes in `received()` (`ring.c:130-142`),
  using `ASAN_POISON_MEMORY_REGION`/`ASAN_UNPOISON_MEMORY_REGION`. Unpoison everything before
  `free` at close. The kernel's writes are not checked, and a read after a recycle, or past what
  the kernel wrote, becomes a deterministic report in the asan preset's split suites. The sizes
  the suites use are multiples of ASan's 8-byte granule.
- Or, in debug builds, `memset` a poison byte on recycle.
- In `test-am.c`, feed each cut from a copy that is overwritten after the call returns.

### N1. Three documents still describe the copy

- Design §3.3 as built, `shmem-ethernet-design.md:413-415`: "the pass then takes every byte of
  every readable wire through `osoe_wire_recv`, recycling each buffer the moment it is emptied."
  It is now `osoe_wire_peek` and `osoe_wire_consume`, each buffer parsed where the kernel put it.
  This is the paragraph the commit cites ("as design 3.3 describes").

  per-owner (`:295`). It is now nothing: an owner needs no staging, and per-owner memory is an
  AM chunk smaller. Line 103's file:line list is pinned to its own commit and can stay.

### N2. The 27% is right, and the classifier the project committed cannot show it

**Severity: note** (evidence tooling; the next step is the re-profile).

I split the tip's receiving-agent samples by the library frame under libc
(`profile-49c62d3/put64.agents.script`, tid 517356):

|---|---|---|---|

The committed classifier (`docs/design/evidence/v0.2-step4/2026-10-07-profile/classify.py:68-70`)

The brief makes the profile "the number every later claim refers to". So the classifier should
attribute a libc `[unknown]` leaf to the first `libosoe` frame under it (the stacks carry it), and
name `osoe_wire_peek` and `osoe_wire_consume` in `ENGINE`. They are counted under `receive` today,
which is harmless.

Seen at the end of this read: the worktree's uncommitted evidence

### N3. What the measurement does not cover (property 8)

**Severity: note.**

- **The cost moved; not all of it vanished.** Per MiB/s of each profiled run's throughput, the
  apply's copy (libc under `osoe_am_parse`) cost 16.5% more samples at a4d88e3 than at the tip
  with 64 KiB puts, and 32.8% more with 1 MiB. Its source is now the provided buffer, where it
  was a scratch chunk the first copy had just pulled through L1, so it takes the first-touch
  misses the scratch copy took. `osoe_am_parse`'s own time went 0.7 to 1.4% with 64 KiB puts,
  the headers' share of the same. The library's memcpy per byte still fell 46-52%. This is one
  profile per build of about 2,900 samples each, against each run's 30 s average: an estimate,
  not a measurement. The worktree's uncommitted record explains the 1 MiB stream the same way:
  the remaining copy costs more where the apply's megabyte leaves the cache.
- **The 1 MiB stream was lower in all three pairs**: -2.03, -0.08 and -0.77%. The ranges are
  disjoint: 9,668-9,732 against 9,740-9,882 MiB/s. The profiled 30 s runs went the other way
  (9,944 to 10,324). Three of three is p = 0.125 one-sided, so "unchanged" is the fair reading.
  A mechanism exists, though. Each buffer now goes back after its own apply, where before it
  went back before the up-to-64 KiB it was gathered into was applied. So during a slow apply
  (cold heap lines, many waiters notified) fewer buffers are free, and the ring runs dry
  sooner, with more `-ENOBUFS` and re-arms. The `stat*.json` snapshots carry per-PE counters
  only, so they cannot show it.
- **`do_signal` rose** from 0.9 to 12.7% of the receiving agent (64 KiB) and from 3.4 to 8.1%
  (1 MiB). Task work now interrupts a faster parse more often. That is the next rung's target
  (`measure-taskrun-1`), not this one's defect.
- **Small provided buffers.** A peek, a parse call and a consume are now paid per provided
  buffer, where `osoe_wire_recv` gathered up to a chunk into one parse call. At 16 KiB that is
  noise, but `OSOE_WIRE_BYTES` down to 64 is a supported setting, and partly filled buffers
  (small CQEs under small messages) do the same at any size.
- **Small-message throughput.** The mailbox (two rounds, +0.6 and -2.1%) is bound by its round
  trips (the 2026-10-07 record), so it cannot show a per-message parse cost.
- **The Pi.** The link is the bound there, so the change can show only as the agent's idle time.

What I would measure:
1. The 1 MiB stream, eight more alternating pairs.
2. `node.am`'s `receive_buffers`, `receive_enobufs` and `receive_rearms` per GiB, both builds,
   64 KiB and 1 MiB, from `osoe-stat`'s node record.
3. The 64 KiB stream at `OSOE_WIRE_BYTES=1024` and `4096`, with `OSOE_WIRE_BUFFERS` raised to
   keep 1 MiB.
4. A small-message stream that is not bound by round trips (the demo kit's index-gather or
   histogram, or `osoe-pacer` `mix`): messages a second and the receiving agent's CPU.
5. The caller-split classification per GiB, or L1D/L2 refill counters on the agent, to confirm
   the first bullet.
6. On the Pi, the receiving agent's `perf stat` at the link's rate.

### N4. Smaller items

- `osoe_wire_peek` leaves `*bytes` unset when it returns 0 (`wire.c:234-235`). Every caller
  reads it only when n > 0, but setting NULL costs nothing and keeps a future caller, or
  `-fanalyzer`, honest.
- The same seed no longer gives the same receive splits. A split is decided per peek, over the
  first buffer's remainder, where it was per receive over min(queued, len). An empty peek no
  longer advances `recvs`. No committed seed is a recorded failure: 20260919 and 20261007 are
  reproducibility seeds, and no record names a failing one. So nothing is lost, but §3.14's
  "commit any failing seed as a permanent regression case" (design 1909) reads as if patterns
  outlive a change, and this one does not keep them. One line in the record would say so.
- `test-wire.c:61-62` still says `short_recvs` counts "receives [that] returned less than was
  waiting". The new notion is stated at 121.

## The properties, as tried

**1. Every byte parsed exactly once, in order - held.**
- `receive()` (`progress.c:656-664`) peeks n, parses exactly n and consumes exactly n. A peek
  is at least 1 whenever `queued > 0`:
  - `truncate_to` never returns 0 for n of 1 or more (`wire.c:30-38`);
  - `filled - offset` is at least 1, since a buffer leaves its wire the moment `offset` reaches
    `filled` (`wire.c:251-257`) and `received()` queues only `res > 0` (`ring.c:130`).
- `queued` is the sum of the queued buffers' unread bytes, so the loop ends exactly when every
  one is consumed.
- Order: buffers are queued per wire in CQE order (`ring.c:134-138`), which for one multishot
  recv is stream order. The parse cursor is per connection, so a header, fixed part, payload or
  padding cut at a buffer edge or a split continues in the next call.
- The parser never reads past `length`: `accumulate`, the payload and the padding each take at
  most what is left (`am.c:75-77`, `259-261`, `278-280`).
- The tail is unchanged in what it sees:
  - `osoe_wire_ended` (`wire.c:269-272`) reads `queued`, which is 0 after the loop, as it was
    after the old loop;
  - `ended` and `error` are set only at a reap;
  - nothing called during the parse reads `queued` or `ended`. The detector's
    `node_idle`/`node_quiet`, reached from `deadlock_arrived`, read only `send_error`.

**2. No buffer handed back while readable, none held across a reap - held.** Every callback the
sink reaches was read, not only named:

| Callback | What it does |
|---|---|
| `destination` → `resolve` (`progress-host.c:54-104`) | arithmetic and bounds fatals |
| `reply_landing` → `landing_request` (118-136) | acquire loads |
| `complete` → `notify` → `osoe_transport_notify` (`watch.c:106-131`) | seq_cst loads, a bump, `futex_wake` |
| `osoe_rmw_apply` | atomics |
| `completed` (179-204) | stores and a futex wake |
| `landed` (149-168) | stores and wait-word wakes |
| `osoe_credit_give` (`reply.c:155`) | a CAS loop and a wake |
| `barrier_team`/`known_team` → `team_by_name`/`team_of_record` (228-274) | acquire loads |
| `book_arrival`, `release_down`, `forward` → `enqueue` | the reply pool, or `pool_exhausted`'s fatal |
| `abort_arrived` → `osoe_node_declare_abort` (`node.c:1672-1684`) | a CAS and every agent's wake |
| `deadlock_arrived` → `osoe_deadlock_message` (`deadlock.c:599-656`) | enqueues, prints, or declares |
| `segment_applied` → `osoe_connection_publish_acked` (`connection.c:237-243`) | a store and a wake |

- The host's `release` runs only from `release_local` (`progress.c:1235`), outside any parse.
- None of them peeks, consumes, recycles, reaps, waits or re-enters the pass. The pass is
  reached only from five calls in four functions of `progress-host.c` (586, 622, 685, 700, 781),
  all on the agent's loop.
- A fatal raised mid-parse on the agent writes, declares and `_exit`s (`fatal.c:99-120`).
  After a global exit it calls `osoe_abort_end`, which runs no pass. So no fatal path reaps
  with a buffer held.
- The agent blocks every asynchronous signal (`agent.c:283-292`), so no handler runs mid-parse.
- Held at the reap: every wire with a buffer queued was marked readable by that reap
  (`ring.c:142`), every readable wire is received in the pass (`progress.c:1285-1289`), and
  `receive` returns only with `queued` 0. This is unchanged from before.

**3. The parser's contract - held** (untested, S2). Reading `osoe_am_parse` whole
(`am.c:143-321`):
- `in` is a local.
- The segment header, message header and fixed part go through `accumulate` into
  `p->accumulator` (73-81), and from there into `p->segment` and `p->message` (156, 192).
- The sink receives `&p->message`, `p->accumulator`, `p->destination` and `&p->segment`
  (246-247, 250, 271-272, 135), never `in`.
- The payload is copied from `in` to the destination (265). Padding is counted, never read
  (277-290).
- `struct osoe_am_parser` holds no pointer into the input.

**4. The kernel writes no buffer the engine is reading - held.**
- A ring-provided buffer is the kernel's only while it sits in the buffer ring's [head, tail).
  `io_ring_buffer_select` loads the tail with `smp_load_acquire` and selects at the head.
- A multishot recv commits the head when it posts the buffer's CQE (`io_kbuf_commit`). On
  `-EAGAIN`, or on a 0 or negative result with nothing done, `io_kbuf_recycle_ring` leaves the
  head where it was, posts no buffer, and the kernel has written nothing.
- So from its CQE until `osoe_ring_recycle` advances the tail, the buffer is outside [head,
  tail), and no kernel path can select it. That covers task work run at any return to user mode
  on this thread (`TWA_SIGNAL`, since the ring has neither `COOP_TASKRUN` nor `DEFER_TASKRUN`),
  io-wq, and softirq, which only queues task work.
- The give-back is after the parse (`wire.c:256` runs after `osoe_am_parse` returns), and
  liburing's `io_uring_buf_ring_advance` stores the tail with a C11 release store. That orders
  the parse's reads before it, as the kernel's acquire load orders its next writes after.
- A buffer recycled mid-pass may be refilled before the next reap, once at most, which the CQ
  bound already assumes (`ring.c:36-42`; one fill a buffer between reaps holds as before).
- No incremental consumption (`IOU_PBUF_RING_INC`, `ring.c:56` passes 0) and no bundles
  (`ring.c:89-91`). Either would let the kernel keep a buffer it has posted.
- With `OSOE_WIRE_BYTES` not a multiple of 64, the kernel may write the next buffer's first
  bytes on a line the parse is reading: different bytes, no race.

**5. `osoe_wire_consume`'s fatal - memory-safe, never fires on the engine's sequence, weaker
than its header (S1).** Peek n then consume n has `queued > 0`, and n is at most
`filled - offset`, since `truncate_to` returns at most its argument. The `||` keeps
`filled[OSOE_RING_NONE]` from being read when nothing is queued.

**6. The tests - held, S2's gap aside.**
- `test-wire.c`'s new count (`n < whole`, 127-132) is exactly "the injection truncated a peek".
  That is the event the old `n < sizeof in && queued > 0` counted: with four 64-byte buffers and
  a 300-byte receive, a receive left bytes queued only when it was truncated.
- It cannot pass vacuously. A peek that ignored the split makes `short_recvs` 0 and line 245
  fails, and with seed 20260919 at 5000 per myriad it is deterministic.
- Lost: only `osoe_wire_recv`'s own gather and length cap, which no longer exist.
- Parses split inside a provided buffer still run in `shmem.progress` (`CMakeLists.txt:1013-1017`,
  256-byte buffers, split 3000) and `shmem.barrier-am.*` (275-279, 2000). They also run in these
  registrations, all at 256-byte buffers with splits of 2000-3000:
  - `shmem.multinode.*` (1088) and its force-remote variant (1164);
  - `shmem.tree.*` (1120);
  - `shmem.internode.*` (1214), with its slurm (1243), force-remote (1273) and heap-shift (1307)
    variants;
  - `shmem.differential` (1358);
  - the pipeline's stress case (2351) and the circulant's (3113);
  - tier 3's wire settings (3870, 3891) and its differential (4044).
- Every parse call there now starts at a buffer edge or a split, so there are more cut points
  than before, when a receive could join four buffers into one call.

**7. Stale references - none in code** (N1 for the documents).
- `osoe_wire_recv` survives only in design 415 and the dated 2026-10-07 profile evidence.
- The scratch is gone from `am-state.h`, `progress.[ch]` and `progress-host.c`. `scratch` in
  `shmem/src` now means only the internal region and `link-measure.c`'s.
- `osoe_progress_init` has the new signature at every call (`progress-host.c:530`,
  `test-barrier-am.c:119`, `test-progress.c:342`), and no tool or benchmark links `progress.c`.

**8. Performance - the claim holds, and so does the 64 KiB gain; the 1 MiB stream is unproven
either way** (N2, N3).
- The 27% is 27.6%.
- The 64 KiB stream gained 8.3, 10.0 and 9.3% in its three pairs, and `shmem.floor`'s library
  stream 23.9, 10.7 and 29.8% (ratio 0.59-0.69 to 0.73-0.75).
- The receiving agent went from 0.984 to 0.928 CPUs (`put64.cpu.txt`).

**The owner rule** (the step 4 brief asks every read for "a word two threads write, or a wake
that can be lost") **- held.** No word gains a second writer:
- a wire's `first`, `last`, `offset`, `queued` and `recvs`, and the ring's `filled`, `next` and
  `held`, are written by the agent alone, at the reap and in consume, on the thread invariant 19
  names;
- a provided buffer passes between the agent and the kernel only through the buffer ring and the
  CQ, as before;
- no wake is added, moved or removed.

## Verdict

No fatal finding. The parse in place is sound:
- every callback reachable from the parse was read, and none can reap, recycle or re-enter;
- the parser keeps nothing of its input;
- the kernel cannot select a buffer between its CQE and its recycle;
- the release store orders the parse before the kernel's next write;
- every byte is parsed once and in order, across buffer edges, splits and the stream's end.

The 27% the commit cites is right, and the 64 KiB gain is clear.

Before merging:
- S1, a few lines: record the peek, or reword the header.
- S2, a few lines in the sanitizer presets and `test-am.c`. It is the one check that would turn
  the change's new hazard from remembered into asserted.
- N1: `wire.h:7-9`, and the helpers proposal's per-owner scratch, since Part 1 builds from it.
  The design sentence is already rewritten in the worktree, uncommitted.

Not merge blockers, but worth taking before the re-profile: N2's classifier fix, and N3's
measurements 1 and 2 (the 1 MiB pairs, and the ring's counters for both builds).
