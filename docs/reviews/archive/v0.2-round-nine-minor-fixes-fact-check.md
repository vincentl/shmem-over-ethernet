## Problems

### 1. Another thread's wait never copies a blocking get's pieces - **wrong** (design §3.12), **overstated** (`transport.h`)

- **Where.** `docs/design/shmem-ethernet-design.md:1510-1514`: "one of more goes
  out as a nonblocking get's pieces on its thread's own context, and whichever
  thread of the PE claims a landed piece copies it - another thread's wait, or
  an issue that finds the zone full". `shmem/src/transport.h:413-416` makes the
  same list for "Of more, and a nonblocking get": "the wait that completes it,
  or another thread's wait, or an issue that finds the zone full".
- **What is wrong.** The pieces of a blocking get of more than one piece sit
  on the thread place's own block (`remote_get`, `transport-internode.c:1099-1105`).
  - A wait copies only its own context's pieces. `complete_gets` (:1172-1179)
    copies the pieces on its own pending list, by token. `complete_pair`
    (:1243-1251) copies records whose owner bits are its own block.
  - The only path that copies pieces of any context is `copy_out_any`
    (:807-815), and its one caller is `make_room` (:869), which is an issue
    that finds the zone full.
  - So the copier is the calling thread's own wait, or any thread's issue that
    finds the zone full. Never another thread's wait.
  - "Another thread's wait" holds only for a nonblocking get on a context that
    threads share.
  - The §3.12 parenthetical also leaves out the calling thread, which is the
    usual copier.
- **Sources.** `node.h:1087-1093` says it rightly: "the wait for its context's
  gets or for the blocking get's, or an issue that found the zone full".
  Conformance C3 says the same: "any thread of the PE whose issue finds the zone
  full may copy it (`copy_out_any`)". The phrase came from readiness Q5-7's
  "another thread's quiet", which the code does not bear out. §4.10's new cell
  (:3986), "the wait, or another", is ambiguous the same way.
- **Correction.**
  - §3.12: "... on its thread's own context, its pieces copied by the calling
    thread's own wait, or first by any thread of the PE whose issue finds the
    zone full (`copy_out_any`) ...".
  - `transport.h`: "the wait that completes it - for a nonblocking get on a
    shared context, any thread's - or an issue that finds the zone full".

### 6. The one-piece / more-than-one split leaves out the fallback - nit

- **Where.** §3.12 (`:1510`, "a blocking get of one piece only"), §4.10

- **Correction.** "of one piece, or of more from a place past block 4,095, a
  piece at a time".

### 10. The thread limit understates C9-3 and leaves out the deadlock detector - nit

- **Where.** `README.md:965-969`: "they land after it is gone - into its
  buffer, freed or an unwound stack, or held in the zone for the next thread in
  its place".
- **What is wrong.**
  - The pieces land in the zone. Another thread's issue that finds the zone
    full copies them into the buffer later.
  - C9-3's second branch is a wait, not merely held bytes. If the stale pieces
    leave room, the next thread's large blocking get waits for bytes nobody
    copies, until some issue of the PE's finds the zone full. C9-3 has this
    from the source, not a run.
  - `base/sweep.h:66-70` names another consequence of the same two acts. A
    thread cancelled in a wait, or leaving one by `siglongjmp`, stays counted
    as waiting by the deadlock detector. "a program that does any of them turns
    the detector off (`OSOE_DEADLOCK_SECONDS=0`)".
- **Correction.** Add both: "... an issue that later finds the zone full copies
  them into its buffer, freed or an unwound stack, and if they leave room the
  next thread in its place may wait in a large get until one does; and the
  deadlock detector counts such a thread as still waiting - a program that does
  either sets `OSOE_DEADLOCK_SECONDS=0`".
