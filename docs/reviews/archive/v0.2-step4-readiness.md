## Verdict

**Nothing in this question blocks v0.2. One thing should change before the tag
(Q5-1); the rest are minors, and two notes for after it - the seam's and a
view's.**

**The README tells an MPI programmer the truth about what these steps added.**
What helpers are (threads of the lead's process holding a share of the data
wires, the agent keeping every control wire), when to set them (not by default:
nothing has measured what they buy; a stream to one peer stays one thread's;
under Slurm's binding they share the lead's core unless `OSOE_AGENT_CPUS=node`),
what they cost, and how `osoe-stat` shows them - every clause I checked holds
in the source and in a run: the peer-to-helper map, idle helpers keeping a ring
and buffers, the threads named `osoe-helper-N`, none taking a place of
`OSOE_MAX_THREADS`, none made in a job of one node, 64 of them running. The
control wire, the shapes and the circulant are described as built. Every new
setting is real and its default and range right - three rows say less than the
code in a clause each (Q5-7) - and every bad value tried was refused by name
with its range (*measured*: eight new settings, five older ones beside them).

**The failures these steps open are loud, and `osoe-stat` finds them.** A
helper that ignores its stop ends the job at `shmem_finalize` naming the helper
and calling it the library's defect, in 0.2 s; a helper that stops turning
altogether - made here by ptrace, beyond the test hook - is waited for with a
notice naming it, and under `OSOE_NO_PROGRESS_TIMEOUT` ends the job naming it;
mid-run it holds its peers' PEs in `shmem_put` and `shmem_quiet`, and
`osoe-stat`'s owner lines show its passes frozen between two looks while the
other helper's move. A deadlock is proved at two helpers exactly as at none,
and not while a helper is frozen; a node stopped for 15 s under a stream ends
nothing; a peer's death while a node is ending is excused, and in
`shmem_finalize` named; a node's PEs given different counts are refused by
name. What is wrong is in the words around them (Q5-3, Q5-4) and in the
records' indexes, which stopped short of these steps (Q5-5, Q5-6).

**What should change before the tag:**

1. **Q5-1**: 82 of the 199 programs and scripts these steps added or changed -
   every one in the new evidence directories - lack the licence line the
   README promises on every source file. The first review point found one such file; nothing
   checks the rule, so it recurred 82 times.

Cheap enough to make with it: the stale numbers in Building (Q5-2), the death
message the README quotes (Q5-3), the provenance sentence (Q5-5) and the
indexes (Q5-6).

### Q5-3. The death message the README quotes is not the one the default prints, and at helpers every end says it twice (minor; high)

- **Location**: README "When a job fails or hangs", :539-541 ("each of the
  others writes a line naming ... the node it lost - 'ended its connection
  without its goodbye' of a node whose lead ended"); `shmem/src/progress.c:886-890`,
  which names the wire (`name`, "control wire" or "connection"); `fatal.c:36-124`,
  which prints any thread's fatal, a helper's included, with no
  once-a-process guard.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, three nodes of two PEs, node 2's lead killed while
  the others wait in `shmem_finalize`):
  - at no helpers, the default, each surviving lead says "node 2 ended its
    **control wire** without its goodbye: its lead has exited or died (§6.4)" -
    the agent reads its control wires first in a pass - and nothing says
    "connection": a user who searches the README for the line they got finds
    nothing;
  - at two helpers each surviving lead says both - the agent's "control wire"
    line and its helper's "connection" line, each with its own "control
    segment" line - four lines a node where there were two (two runs, both so;
    and the same in a class-split refusal's other nodes);
  - at two helpers the ends of a job grow noisier besides: a non-lead killed
    mid-stream has each lead PE write its agent's "ending: the job is aborting
    on behalf of PE 3" and then its own thread's "shmem_put: the job is
    aborting on behalf of PE 3; this PE cannot make progress" with a control
    segment line - one line a PE at no helpers. The cause still comes first in
    every run, and every line names the right PE or node; what grows is what a
    reader must skip.
  - Two phrasings that contradict themselves or say nothing to a user: a fatal
    in `shmem_finalize` that the agent raises ends "...; this PE was not
    waiting in the library" (the deaf helper's, and the frozen helper's under
    `OSOE_NO_PROGRESS_TIMEOUT`) - the PE is in `shmem_finalize`, but not in a
    wait the blocked record names; and the count refusal ends "(owner-map.h)",
    a header, where every other message cites a design section.
- **Fix**: the README quotes "ended its control wire (or data wire) without its
  goodbye"; the data wire's message says "data wire"; a second fatal in one
  process after the first is written stays silent (one word, first writer
  wins), or the README says that a lead with helpers may write two; "this PE
  was in shmem_finalize" where the blocked record is empty and the PE is
  finalizing; "(§6.2)" or nothing for the header.

### Q5-4. "A helper thread" in the deadlock limit reads as the progress helpers (minor; high)

- **Location**: README Limits, "Failures", :1069-1071: "A PE computing for
  ever, or with any thread outside the library's waits - a helper thread, an
  idle OpenMP pool - is never judged deadlocked".
- **Scenario**: a reader who has just met `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS` - "helper
  threads of the lead's process", "the helpers" - reads that a PE with a helper
  thread is never judged deadlocked, and concludes that turning helpers on
  turns the detector off on the lead's node.
- **Evidence** (*measured*): it does not. A deadlock after a 2 s stream,
  detection at 2 s, was proved at two helpers in 5.4 s with the same report as
  at none (5.3 s); the detector leaves the helpers out of the lead's threads by
  the tids in their owner records (`deadlock.c:104-118`, `:135`, *observed in source*).
- **Fix**: "a thread of the program's own - a worker, an idle OpenMP pool
  (osoe's own progress helpers do not count)".

### Q5-8. The seam: the deadlock detector leaves the lead's backend threads out through AM's owner records, which the backend's contract does not mention (after release; medium)

*Observed in source.*

- **What holds**: the seam's two headers barely moved in three steps.
  `transport.h` gained one call, `osoe_transport_link` (the link's bandwidth at
  the end of `shmem_init`, which a transport may use or ignore), and
  `internode.h` none; `internode-backend.h` gained its share,
  `osoe_internode_backend_link`, and the sentence the first review point asked
  for (Q5-8 there): an RDMA backend replaces `transport-am.c` too. Owners,
  helpers, request words and the control wire live wholly in the internode
  backend's files (`CheckBoundary.cmake`'s lists: `owner-map.h`,
  `progress-host.c`, `progress.c`, `ring.c`, `wire.c`), and the README's RoCE
  sketch now has the control wire as a queue pair of the lead's. As a contract
  for a backend of AM's shape, it holds.
- **What a porter of RDMA would meet**: the deadlock detector, a runtime file
  above the boundary, counts each PE's threads through `/proc` and leaves out
  the agent, io_uring's workers - and since Part 3 the lead's helpers, by the
  tids each helper writes into its record in the node segment's owner section
  (`deadlock.c:104-118`, `:135`; `node.h:465`, `struct osoe_am_owner_metrics`, sized
  `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS + 1` by `node.c:804`). `internode-backend.h` says
  nothing of a backend's own threads. An RDMA backend with a thread of its own
  - the README's sketch has one: "a per-node polling thread in place of the
  target-side counters RDMA lacks" (:1212-1213) - must write its tid into AM's
  owner records, or the detector counts it as the program's, and a thread
  that polls is never asleep: that node's samples fail and no deadlock is ever
  proved. It fails safe, ending nothing; what is lost is §3.15's proof,
  silently. The owner section is also AM's counters by AM's name, as the
  first review point's Q5-8 said of the `am.*` scope.
- **Fix**, after the release: one entry in `internode-backend.h` - the tids of
  the backend's threads, or a predicate the detector asks - and the owner
  records on the checklist with the `am.*` names.
