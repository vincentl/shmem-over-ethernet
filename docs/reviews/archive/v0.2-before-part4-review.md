## Verdict

**Release after named changes. Nothing blocks.** In every area the library is
what the README says it is: conforming (the blocking get's change included),
correct (all 45 models, the six `SHMEM_THREAD_MULTIPLE` ones for the first time
since 0.1.0's tag; the suite whole beside eight busy loops), as large as its
tables say, portable to x86-64 and Rocky Linux, its soname kept honestly (programs
built against 0.1.0 run on it with identical output), and loud wherever it fails.
The Pi's hour of `mix` and hour of `threads` passed.

The named changes are four, none in the library's protocols:

1. **The verification's ThreadSanitizer gate passes tests that failed** (C9-1):
   fix the gate, re-run, and correct the five documents that say it checks more.
2. **Linux 6.14 and later charge io_uring's rings to the user's locked memory**
   (P9-1, C9-2, the Pi rehearsal): one machine's logical nodes run out of it,
   and the tag's own tier-2 check on a Pi node fails at the default limit. The
   README and the runbook say so and raise the soft limit where they can; whether
   `shmem_init` raises it itself is the author's call.
3. **The fast-link kit, before a node is rented** (S1, S2, S3, P9-2, and two more
   the refuters found): its concurrency figure measures the wrong thing, its jobs
   past a node's CPUs are refused by Slurm, and the helpers' decision would be
   made without the collectives.
4. **A README sentence on mixed releases** (Q5-1): a job mixing 0.1.0 and 0.2
   nodes waits at `shmem_init` with nothing naming a version.

## The changes to make before the tag, ranked

### 1. ThreadSanitizer's gate passes tests that failed their own checks (C9-1, correctness; stands, wider)

- **What.** ThreadSanitizer replaces a process's exit status with its own, 66,
  once it has reported a race - on a nonzero return from `main` and on its own
  deadly-signal handler alike - and `tsan-gate.py` reads any 66 as "the tool's
  reports alone". At no helpers the suite does not back it up either: 333 of the
  preset's 606 tests are judged by a pass expression, which makes ctest ignore the
  exit status, so a race reported in a passing test is never read.
- **Measured.** At two helpers the gate passed a planted failing `CHECK` three runs
  of three, a planted segmentation fault six of six, and a planted wrong byte in
  `shmem.circulant.4` two of three; at no helpers a planted race passed ctest five
  runs of five.
- **What it does not mean.** The runs did check their results; the gate did not
  read them. Under a corrected gate the suite passes its own checks - 606 of 606
  at no helpers, 605 of 606 at two and at three (`stopped-bulk`'s explained
  timeout) - every report a heap race naming a helper thread. Nothing is hiding in
  this tree.
- **Fix.** `TSAN_OPTIONS=exitcode=0:abort_on_error=1:log_path=...` (`exitcode=0`
  alone lets a crash pass); every test must pass as ctest judges it; the report
  files gated at every helper count, no helpers included; the thread leaks the
  change exposes - helpers unjoined on a death path - explained or joined; round
  eight's Q2-2 with it (a helper's frame in every accepted report, the library's
  internal region told apart). Correct the README, the release note, the merge
  point's and the helpers' records, and the release note's fact-check, which
  marked the sentence true on the gate's word. And the integration verification of
  2026-10-10 did not run its own gate - its path to `tsan-gate.py` was wrong - so
  its recorded verdicts came from the evidence's copy script, which runs the gate
  with `|| true`: the gate must fail a verification, not annotate it. The four
  death-path tests whose helper threads the remedy shows unjoined are
  `shmem.helpers.deaf.0-1`, `shmem.reply-pool.overflow`,
  `shmem.kill-after-init.TERM` and `shmem.dead-pe.lead.0-0-1-1`.

### 2. Locked memory on Linux 6.14 and later (P9-1 portability, C9-2 correctness, the lead's Pi rehearsal; stands, narrower)

- **What.** From 6.14 io_uring charges every ring to one count of locked memory
  per user per host, against each process's `RLIMIT_MEMLOCK`; all of one
  machine's logical nodes count together. On the Pi (6.18, 16 KiB pages, 8 MiB):
  the largest jobs of logical nodes are 112, 56 and 42 at no, two and three
  helpers, and 113, 57 and 43 are refused by name - the investigators' arithmetic
  to the node; `shmem.circulant.64` and `.70` and `shmem.shape.chosen.pi.64` and
  `.loopback.64` are refused even alone at two and three helpers; and **the tag's
  tier 2 on a Pi node, as the runbook runs it (`-j4`), failed four runs of four**,
  758 or 759 of 760.
- **Bounded.**
  - Under Slurm a step's hard limit is unlimited on the Pi: `ulimit -S -l unlimited`
    in the step makes tier 2 pass 760 of 760 and lets 254 logical nodes run. Only
    `osoe-launch` on a desktop at the default hard limit needs an administrator.
  - Real clusters are not exposed: one lead's rings cost at most 2.6 MiB at the
    4,097-node ceiling (emulated on the Pi), and 256 PEs as two logical nodes of
    128 on one Pi start and finish.
  - The refusal ends a job in 0.2 to 1.0 s and names the limit.
  - 6.14 and later had run tier 2 at no helpers since 2026-09-28; what had never
    run there is the suite at a helper count.
- **Fix.** The runbook's tier-2 line raises the soft limit in its step; the README
  says it at `osoe-launch`'s 254 logical nodes and in its Limits. Or `shmem_init`
  raises the soft limit to the hard one, as it does for open files - the author's
  call; the release checklist carries that question for Part 4 "with zero copy
  on", and it belongs to the tag either way. A `RESOURCE_LOCK` on the large tests
  would not fix it: each is refused alone at a helper count.
- **Seen beside it:** `shmem.stopped-node.no-progress` failed once in a
  default-limit tier-2 run on the Pi, its 5 s no-progress limit expiring at
  start-up beside the large jobs - a margin to widen.

### 3. The fast-link kit, before any node is rented (S1, S2, S3 scale; P9-2 portability; R1, R2 from P9-2's refuter)

The tag waits for Part 4, and Part 4's decisions come from this kit; each of
these would cost rented hours or mislead a decision.

- **S1 - "peers at once" measures the rate, not the concurrency** (stands; fix
  changed). It counts the peer nodes whose blocks *returned* in each millisecond
  (the code marks the slot after the call; its comment says "starts"), so a slot
  can hold no more than the blocks that return in it - about 2.7 of 1 MiB a
  millisecond at 23 Gbit/s - and it could not tell the convoying order from the
  spread one. **Fix:** an exact time-weighted count of the peers a node's PEs are
  in calls toward (50 µs slots, or each PE's intervals swept after the window),
  the quiets counted or reported apart, and only that compared with the model.
- **S2 - §5, §8 and §10 record no job's link or unsent limit** (stands, narrower):
  each job measures its own; §7 and §9 already record it in their `osoe-stat`
  dumps. **Fix:** `SHMEM_DEBUG=1` in `launch`, and `summarize.py` printing each
  row's link and limit.
- **S3 - the helpers' default would be decided without the collectives** (stands;
  scenario and fix changed). On the Pi two helpers made the mailbox about a fifth
  faster while a 64 KiB broadcast and a 4 KiB alltoall lost 2 to 35%: a sweep
  shaped like §5 would have read helpers as a gain. **Fix:** `osoe-bench quick`'s
  barrier and collectives in §5's rotated rounds, tabulated by PE and helper count,
  the decision taken on the 64 KiB and 4 KiB lines.
- **P9-2 - Slurm refuses `--ntasks-per-node` past a node's CPUs, whatever
  `--overcommit` says** (stands; fix changed): cons_tres tests the task count before
  the overcommit (its source, 24.11 and `master`); every 32-PE-a-node job on a
  `c4-standard-16` would fail. A larger `CPUs=` does not stick; `-n` is a trap.
  **Fix:** each `launch` in its own `salloc -J osoe-kit -N<n> --exclusive`, the
  kit's `srun` its one step - measured, timeouts and the clean-up working as before.
- **R1 - `on` and `spawn` steps get one CPU**: task/affinity turns
  `--cpu-bind=none` into the mask of a one-task job's allocation, so the floor's
  `echo-uring` and `iperf3` - §5's yardstick - run on one core a node.
  `TaskPlugin=task/none` fixes it (measured).
- **R2 - `slurmd -C` crashes in the ROCm GPU plugin** that `slurm-wlm`'s
  Recommends pull in (measured in the VM), and `slurm-setup.sh` then stops with no
  node lines: install with `--no-install-recommends`, or check before renting.

### 4. The README on mixed releases (Q5-1 readiness; stands, narrower, firmer)

- **What.** A job mixing a 0.1.0 node and a 0.2 node waits at `shmem_init` both
  ways round: a node drops the other's hello as a stranger's, the notices blame the
  hello and the ports, and nothing names a version; under `srun` only the step's
  time limit ends it. The README asks for "the same install on each" node, but
  never says what a mixed start does, and its architecture item promises that the
  rendezvous compares "the build's table of message handlers" - which design §3.13
  says "catches mismatched binaries across nodes immediately with a clear message"
  - while the hello's version check drops the other release before that is reached.
  The release note's Known limits say the README lists "run the same osoe"; it
  does not.
- **Fix.** Fold "and run the same osoe" into the architecture item and qualify its
  comparison (two builds of one release are refused by name; two releases wait);
  name the step's time limit as the bound under `srun`, and `SHMEM_VERSION=1` as
  the way to see the mix; gloss "no answer to the hello" where a user searching the
  notice lands. A message naming the other version is after the release.

## Minor, worth making before the tag

- **Q5-2 - a blocking get waiting for room names the wrong node.** When another
  node's stalled gets hold the landing zone, a blocking get's wait for room names
  its own target (measured: "no progress toward node 1 (PE 2)" with node 2 stopped).
  It came in with the blocking gets' read fixes (`c06ac75`); 0.1.0 named no node
  there. Name the target only while the get's own pieces hold part of the zone.
- **S4's sentence.** `shmem.helper-wake` cannot see a spin placed *before* the owner
  raises its sleeping flag (a 200 µs mutant there passed five runs of five at 23 to

  comment, say the calling thread copies a blocking get's reply (conformance C3,

  §4.2, §9.2): a piece counted once, after its copy, with a seq_cst add the waiting
  thread must see; another thread's copy is the PE's own action; no program
  context touched. 17 runs, 9,190 blocking gets and 7,197 nonblocking ones from up
  to 12 threads a PE, every path and setting, without a failure; mutants caught.
  The release note's exact claims hold (headers byte for byte, 3,595 names with

- **Correctness.** All 45 model tests pass, every breakage failing for its stated
