## Findings

### S1. The abort across owners is neither modeled nor tested: each of its two mechanisms is load-bearing, and no test fails without either

**Severity: should-fix** - a verification gap on the path that decides whether an ending job reports
its own status or a peer's death, and whether a global exit stalls for the grace.

Location: `progress-host.c:1279-1290` (`osoe_progress_host_abort`: nudge, `abort_wires`,
`wait_helpers(&grace)`, `leave_helpers`), `:1073-1078` (a helper reads the node's word at each turn),
`progress.c:861-866` (`receive_wire`'s excuse, `node_aborting`). `helper_request_model.py` has no
abort; the proposal asks for "a stop-and-abort model" (`progress-helpers-proposal.md:379`, and
`:270` "in the form of the existing abort models"), and Part 1's record deferred "the lead's request
word, the owner's done word, the lead's wait for it ... and their model" to this part
(`2026-10-08-owners.md:91-96`).

Evidence. *Read* (`../mutants/mutants.txt`): `abort-without-helpers` (the wait removed) passes 65 of
65 abort, fatal, global-exit, kill and stopped tests at `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=2`;
`no-abort-excuse` (the node excuse removed) passes 65 of 65. *Run* (`abort-owners-model.out`): two
nodes, each an agent on the control wire and a helper on the data wire, every interleaving of N's
goodbyes and end with P's reads:

| Variant | Verdict | Trace |
|---|---|---|
| `wait` (as built) | holds, 57 states | |
| `wait_no_excuse` | holds | |
| `no_wait` | **fails** | N agent sends ABORT on C ; N ends ; P helper reads END on D: FATAL |
| `abrupt_p_ending` | holds | |
| `abrupt_p_ending_no_excuse` | **fails** | N ends ; P declares for another reason ; P agent reads END on C: FATAL |

Scenarios, *reasoned* from the model and the C:

- *The wait.* Without it, N's process ends once its control goodbye is in P's kernel, its helper's
  data goodbye perhaps unsent; P's helper takes the data wire's end before P's agent has applied the
  control goodbye - unexcused (`progress.c:885-890`), "node N ended its connection without its
  goodbye", and P ends as a death, not with N's status. The suite passes because P's agent, woken
  by the control wire's completion a process exit earlier, almost always applies it first. With the
  wait this is the record's residual window (`2026-10-08-helpers.md:182-190`), reached only when a
  grace runs out.
- *The excuse.* With the wait in place it is redundant for N's own goodbyes (`wait_no_excuse` holds):
  it matters when P is already ending and a peer ends with no goodbye - a fatal's `_exit` on any
  thread of a lead's process (`fatal.c:98-120`), or a lead ending from a sweep of its own close
  (N1) - before P's owner of that wire has reached the top of its next turn. Unexcused, the owner
  raises a fatal while P's word is declared; on a global exit `fatal()` then calls
  `osoe_abort_end`, where a helper pauses for ever (`abort.c:94-96`), and P's agent waits the whole
  grace, 10 s, for its done word (`progress-host.c:1289`), its wires untold.

The record says what each is for (item 5, plan item 5) but names no test of either, and its plan
item 9's "the mutation each catches" has no answer for these two; the second mutant set queued in
`../mutants-2/` removes both at once, which would show the pair needed and not which case each holds.

Fix: a model of the abort across owners in `tools/modelcheck/` with these breakages (this read's is
a start, in `carrier_model.py`'s form); a test that fails without the wait (a hook delaying a peer's
owner 0 after an abort arrives, so that its helper takes the data wire's end first) and one that fails
without the excuse (a hook that ends one node's lead without its goodbyes once its abort is
declared, during a global exit, the other nodes' ends timed against the grace).

### S2. The owners' slices of the engine's arrays share cache lines: every helper stores to one busy-bitmap line at every visit

**Severity: should-fix** - performance only, on the path helpers exist to scale, where the record
says the opposite was done.

Location: `progress-host.c:702-707` (one `calloc` each for the wires, connections, busy words and
reply descriptors of every owner), `:746-756` (each owner attached a slice:
`&osoe_am.busy[busy_before]`, `busy_before += (wires + 63) / 64`); `progress.c:1669-1676` (`settle`
stores the busy word at every visit, `|=` or `&=` unconditionally) and `:283-286` (`mark_busy`).

Scenario, *observed in source*: at H = 2 on 8 nodes, helper 1 holds 4 wires and helper 2 holds 3;
each has one busy word, `busy[0]` and `busy[1]`, 8 bytes apart in one line. Both helpers store to
that line at every visit of every connection, so under traffic to several peers - the case the
README says helpers are for - the line moves between their cores at every visit. The connections
are 288 bytes (`sizes2.out`: 4.5 lines of 64, 2.25 of 128) in one array from `calloc`'s 16-byte
alignment, so the last connection of one owner and the first of the next share a line, written on
every send and receive; the same holds for the wires (`osoe_am.wire`, grouped by ring; `struct
osoe_wire`, `wire.h:59-95`, neither over-aligned nor a line in size) and the 48-byte descriptors at
each pool's edge. *Observed*: what is on lines of its own is `struct osoe_am_owner`
and `struct osoe_progress_owner` (`aligned_alloc`, `:694`, `:700`) and, in the control segment, each
connection's record (`sizes3.out`: 128 bytes, line-aligned) and each owner's (256). Not measured
here.

The record (`2026-10-08-helpers.md:34-36`): "Each owner's host state and engine state on lines of
their own (`aligned_alloc`), since with helpers adjacent owners are different threads writing every
pass"; `am-state.h:50-53` the same. The proposal's §3.2 has "Everything an owner needs is in one
`struct osoe_progress_owner` ... its per-connection state" (`:207-210`); as built most of it is a
slice of a shared array - a departure (N4).

Fix: give each owner its slices on lines of their own - round each owner's busy words, connections,
wires and descriptors up to a line where they are carved, or allocate them per owner with
`aligned_alloc` - and only store a busy word when its bit changes. The rings' small `calloc`s
(`ring.c:91-94`, `readable` 16 bytes at a few wires) are adjacent heap chunks of different owners
too (*reasoned*: glibc packs them).

### N1. The agent's sweeps during its close end the process without its helpers

**Severity: note** - parity with no helpers for the goodbyes; three sentences claim more.

`osoe_progress_host_exit`'s four waits sweep through `osoe_sweep_expired` (`progress-host.c:1161`,
`:805` from `flush_before_close` and `close_connections`, `:1200`), which on the node's abort ends the
process there (`sweep.c:34-44`, `:63`): no control-wire goodbye, no nudge, no wait for the helpers, no
join or detach. The helpers find the abort at their own sweeps and race the agent's end with their
goodbyes. Before helpers the agent's close ended the same way, so no peer is told less than it was.
But:
`progress-host.c:795-800` says "only the agent ends the process ... a helper that ended it would leave
its peers untold", and here the agent does just that; design §3.12's new note (`design:1400`) says the
agent "waits for each to be done before its process ends" (not here, nor past the grace on an abort,
`leave_helpers`); and the record's residual window (`2026-10-08-helpers.md:186-190`, "a peer ends with
one of its goodbyes unacknowledged only once its grace has run out") misses a peer that ends from a
sweep of its own close, with no control goodbye to have applied. Under ThreadSanitizer a helper that has
returned is left unjoined there - tsan-6's thread leak again, reached by no test. Fix: route these
sweeps through the abort's nudge, wait and `leave_helpers`, or say the parity in the three places.

### N2. A helper sleeps on the acknowledgement request its own quiet answer just marked

**Severity: note** - liveness of the detector only. Being fixed in the worktree.

`helper_main` takes `moved` from the pass, then answers (`progress-host.c:1090-1096`); a quiet answer
that finds an application segment unacknowledged marks the connection busy for an acknowledgement
request (`progress.c:2060-2066`); `raise_flag_and_look` reads only the dirty bits (`:578-587`), so the
helper sleeps with the request unsealed until the next nudge or the 100 ms sweep. The agent's own view
runs before its pass in the same turn (`agent.c:237-239`), so it never waits so. The cost is one of the
second half's 1 ms retries (`deadlock.c:296`, `:305`) out of its 5 ms patience (`:46`), a sample that
fails where the round trip is slow, and a backed-off round. Outside both models:
`deadlock_owners_model.py` sends acknowledgements unasked. *Observed in the worktree, uncommitted
after 64f48c4*: `answer` returns whether a quiet request found the owner not quiet and the loop takes
`moved |= answer(...)` - which closes it; an idle answer marks nothing.

### N3. Two thread-scope counters vanish at a helper count, and a helper's fatal no longer says where its PE was

**Severity: note** - observability.

A helper counts into a block nobody reads (`metrics.c:598-602`); the put's notify, which the lander
runs, counts `transport.notify.calls` and `.wakes` (`watch.c:112`, `:129`) - at H > 0 every internode

usual way a node's death reaches its peers - loses the PE's routine and does not say a helper raised
it (only the close's two fatals add "(progress helper h)", `:869-874`, `:913-918`).

### N4. Departures from the proposal and the brief that the record does not own

**Severity: note** (the brief's stop-and-ask: "any point where the build departs from the proposal").
Owned already: no abort request word, every owner reading the node's word (plan item 3); the stop's
deadline replaced by turns and the no-progress rule (`:156-160`); the silent-peer check kept the

- **Where helpers start.** Proposal §3.7 and the brief's item 1: created in `shmem_init` after the

- **The owner's state** is not in `struct osoe_progress_owner` (§3.2): S2.
- **The stop-and-abort model** is a stop model: S1.
- **The README's cost of a count above the lead task's CPUs** (§3.7, `proposal:278-280`) is not said.
  Under `OSOE_AGENT_CPUS=task`, the default, a helper inherits the agent's CPUs and widens nothing

- **Plan item 6** ("Owner 0's counters stay the node record's", `:191`) is not what was built (item
  7: all owners in the owner section) and is not among the items "amended" (`:13-15`). Small: a third
  request kind, idle, beside the plan's quiet, stop and abort (`:138-139`).

## What was tried and holds

1. **One writer a word** (*observed*): `request` and `posted`, `started`, `thread` the agent's;
   `answer`, `stop`, `turns`, `unsent_applied`/`_reached` and `unsent_taken` the helper's; each owner's
   flag, ring, connections, busy words and pool its own. The per-connection record's data counters are
   its helper's and its control-queue gauges owner 0's (`progress.c:179-206`), distinct words. Only
   acquire, release and seq_cst (the audit grep).
2. **The store-buffering argument** (*run*, *observed*): the agent's seq_cst store of the stop before
   its seq_cst load of `turns` (`:957`, `:1138`), the helper's seq_cst store of `turns` before the next
   turn's seq_cst load of the request (`:1100`, `:1074`) - XCHG on x86-64, STLR/LDAR on aarch64; the
   model's `snapshot_first` and `turns_plain`/TSO fail as they should. Every iteration loads the request
   before it counts; the turn that reads the stop returns without counting; a helper is declared deaf
   only after two whole turns.
3. **The wake** (*run*, *reasoned*): the wake word (acquire) before the request (seq_cst) and the abort
   word (`:1073-1075`); the request stored before the bump (`:957-958`); an abort reaches a helper
   through the agent's nudge after its own acquire of the word, a chain of synchronization. A ring's
   stale armed futex is completed by any wake (`ring.c:295-306`); the agent's word has at most one
   waiter when `say_stop` wakes one - the ring's armed wait is consumed by the stop's or the
   declarer's wake (`node.c:1729-1735`, `INT_MAX`) before the exit or abort path, and
   `close_connections` closes the ring before `wait_helpers(NULL)`.
4. **The detector** (*run*, *reasoned*): an answer names its request (2^29 sequences before a wrap,
   `:955`), is read with acquire before the helper's applied count (`:1250-1251`), and is accepted only
   for the kind posted (`:1253-1254`). The C's second half does not wait for the answer as the model's
   does: each 1 ms retry posts again and takes the answer to the last retry's request, posted after the
   first half, with the activity read again - the model's argument, a later request. Helpers are left
   out of `/proc` by the tids in their records (`deadlock.c:104-112`); one not yet named fails a sample
   (the safe way); the control segment is per session (`node.c:202-206`), so no tid outlives its
   thread within one. The mutant `helpers-counted` fails four deadlock tests.
5. **Lifecycles** (*observed*): start at the first pass, exit or abort, each from the agent (its mask);
   an agent that never passed enables owner 0's ring in both paths; `join_helpers` only after every done
   word; `leave_helpers` joins the done and detaches the rest; a second session resets `osoe_am`,
   `request_sequence`, the unsent words and masks `deferred` by the new count; a forked child has no
   helpers and its finalize returns (`init.c:547-551`); helpers past the peer count hold rings with no
   wire (submission bound 1, `ring.c:69-71`) and answer quiet.
6. **No engine-wide state on a helper's path** (*observed*): data-plane handlers touch no team or
   pending state, `check_plane` refuses control kinds on a data wire (`progress.c:98-108`),
   `release_local` and `pending` are owner 0's (`:1772-1773`); the host callbacks a helper runs are
   atomics or reads; `osoe_transport_notify` was multi-threaded already; the helper's `osoe_count`s go
   to its own block.
7. **The carrier rule** (*run*): `control_only` holds; the C judges owner 0's control wires alone at
   H > 0 (`progress.c:1976-1986`), a control wire's end being its process's with `keep_stream`.
8. **Layout 36** (*run*, *observed*): the directory 312 bytes and an owner record 256 at both line
   sizes (the README's figure); the join refusal compares the lead's `owner_count` (`node.c:1229-1235`);
   `osoe-stat` reads owners only from a directory that has them (`BEFORE_OWNERS`), clamps their count to
   the segment, and a one-node job has none; the tests sum and pacer totals the owner scope.
9. **The TSan attribution** (*run*): every data race in tsan-6 is at a heap address
   (`osoe.N.N.N.N.heap.N`); in every report whose frames are in order (52 of 62 and 52 of 64) one side
   is a helper's `osoe_am_parse` or `osoe_rmw_apply` and the other another helper's landing or the
   application's own access - `main`, `shmem_calloc`'s memset, a team number's zeroing, a test's read -
   none the engine's own state. The 14 thread leaks are global-exit tests, every mode of which reaches
   `osoe_progress_host_abort`, where `leave_helpers` now joins or detaches (*reasoned*, not run).
10. **The tests** (*read*): `test-helper-wake.c` counts futex completions on the sender's own node, the
    detector off, and fails when the wake goes to the other helper (mutant `wake-other-owner`); a mark
    in one owner's bitmap with the wake of another would hang its quiet. `shmem.helpers.deaf.0-1`
    fails when the turns check is removed (mutant `deaf-unwatched`: the launcher's 60 s). Untested
    besides S1: an abort during a helper's close, H = 64 at run time, and `leave_helpers`' detach.
