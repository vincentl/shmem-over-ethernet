# Adversarial read: zero-copy-send at bd87633, the zero-copy send behind OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY

The diff read: `git diff 2dcf8d6 bd87633 -- shmem tools docs/design/shmem-ethernet-design.md
README.md` (commits a6204c6, the model; ef9da73, the code; 765037d, the wire test's fix; bd87633
merges `main` at 2dcf8d6, documents only). The library at bd87633 is ef9da73's:
`git diff ef9da73 bd87633 -- shmem tools` touches `test-wire.c` alone. Every file:line below is
at bd87633.

and `progress-host.c` whole; `connection.[ch]`, for every writer of a buffer's bytes and words;
`agent.c`'s loop; `transport-internode.c`'s `reserve_waiting`, `ask_acknowledgement`,
`flush_dirty`, `wait_acknowledged` and `sleep_on`; `deadlock.c`'s sampling and watch;
`metrics.c`'s table and `node.h`'s record; the four touched tests, their CMake registrations and
`tools/pacer/CMakeLists.txt`; `reservation_model.py` and `engine.py` whole; design §3.3, §3.14
and the invariants; the decision record's item 7 and the step 4 brief's amendment, item 4.

were off limits. Run here, under `build/scratch/ladder/review-zero-copy/`:
`reservation_model.py zero_copy zc_` (`model-run.out`, 44 s); a variant of its kernel thread in
which each notification comes after its part's delivery, in any order, and re-reads the part
(`reservation_model_ooo.py`, `ooo-run.out`); an exhaustive model of one connection's visits -
`flush()`'s five steps in the C's order, `busy()`, the pass's visit rule - in four modes
(`visit_model.py`, `visit-model.out`); the two mutant tarballs' sources extracted (`tars/`) and
diffed against `zero-copy-1.tar` and the worktree; the per-test times of the five smoke runs, each
zero-copy suite against its copying twin in the same run. Audit controls, per `CLAUDE.md`: the
grep that finds no `ZERO_COPY` under `tools/pacer` finds the four lines in
`shmem/tests/CMakeLists.txt`; the grep for callers of `osoe_connection_open` finds its one caller
and its definition.

**Verdict in one line:** the buffer protocol is right - nothing writes a buffer the kernel may
still read, the counting rests on io_uring's guarantees, the completion queue keeps its bound and
the setting off changes nothing - but lent buffers give a connection a way to owe a seal that its
busy bit does not record: a quiet's acknowledgement request, or the goodbye, is stranded when both
buffers come back from the kernel in one visit, and then waits for the deadlock detector (1 s, then
2 s, 4 s) or hangs. An exhaustive model of the visit rule finds both strandings, and the smoke
runs bear their timing signature in 11 of 36 zero-copy suite runs. One fatal, two should-fix, the
rest notes.

## Findings

### F1. A connection whose two buffers come back from the kernel in one visit loses its pending seal

**Severity: fatal** (a hang with the detector off, and at the close; with the setting on only -
the default path is unchanged, property 5).

Scenario (a put stream, then `shmem_quiet`, toward a peer with nothing to send back - one waiting
in a barrier for this node, say):
1. Buffer 0 is sealed, sent by reference and its completion collected: LENT, `owed[0]` 1
   (`progress.c:1036-1044`). Buffer 1 is opened, filled, sealed and promoted, and its send
   submitted (`:1063-1074`, `:1029-1033`). No buffer is open, and neither is FREE (`:1116-1118`).
2. The quiet asks (`transport-internode.c:1400-1415`: `osoe_connection_request_ack`, the dirty
   bit, the claimed wake). Both segments were finalized before the request, so neither carries it:
   it is a late request (`progress.c:1090`). The visit the dirty bit brings cannot seal it - no
   buffer is open (`:1083`) - and the connection stays busy, since none is (`:1215`).
3. Buffer 1's completion arrives, and one TCP acknowledgement covering both segments; one reap
   takes the completion (`owed[1]` 1) and both notifications (`owed` 0, 0) (`ring.c:224-248`).
4. The next visit: the release loop frees buffer 0 (`progress.c:1020-1025`); buffer 1's last
   completion is collected with `owed[1]` 0, and it is released at once (`:1039-1041`). The seal
   step runs with no buffer open and does nothing (`:1083`); then the open step opens buffer 0
   (`:1116-1139`). `busy()` (`:1210-1219`) reads a buffer open, nothing sealed, sending or lent,
   no `owe_ack`, `carrying` or queue: false, and `settle` clears the bit (`:1222-1228`).
5. Nothing visits the connection again until something else marks it: a local PE's commit toward
   that peer, an arrival from it, a reply queued. `wait_acknowledged`
   (`transport-internode.c:1485-1515`) asked once and now only looks at `acked` each sweep
   (`sleep_on`, a futex wait of 100 ms); it never marks the connection again. The late request,
   which only `flush` evaluates (`progress.c:1090`), is never sealed, the peer is never asked,
   and the quiet waits.

With the copying send this cannot happen. A buffer is released at its last completion, so "no
buffer open" at the seal step means the other is SEALED or SENDING, which keeps `busy()` true
after the open step reopens the released one, and the next visit seals. Lent buffers add a way for
both to be FREE at the open step - both released by one visit, step 4 - and `busy()` has no term
for a seal still owed.

What rescues it today is the deadlock detector, on by default (`deadlock.c:31`). The root starts a
round once its node has looked quiet for `watch_ns()` - 1 s, doubled after each failed round
(`deadlock.c:40`, `:435-441`) - and a sample calls `osoe_progress_quiet` (`deadlock.c:247`),
which marks every connection whose application segments are unacknowledged
(`progress.c:1510-1513`); the round's probes also make wires readable. With
`OSOE_DEADLOCK_SECONDS=0`, unless other traffic happens to reach that connection, the quiet hangs,
and the peer waiting for this node with it.

The close strands the same way. `flush_before_close` can end with both buffers lent:
`osoe_progress_idle` counts a connection with no open buffer as idle (`progress.c:1356-1357`).
`osoe_progress_close` marks each connection once (`:1398-1404`). If the peer's goodbye and end of
stream are taken in the visit that releases both buffers, or before it, the goodbye condition
(`:1094-1096`) is never evaluated with a buffer open, `busy()` is false (it has no term for
`closing` with the goodbye unsealed), and no receive event can follow: the stream has ended and
is not re-armed (`ring.c:183-191`). `close_connections` (`progress-host.c:611-640`) waits on
`osoe_progress_closed` (`progress.c:1471-1483`) for a goodbye never sealed - the detector does
not run in the agent's exit, and `OSOE_NO_PROGRESS_TIMEOUT` is unset by default - and the peer
waits for that goodbye too. The peer's goodbye segment carries the TCP acknowledgement that frees
this node's buffers, so the two tend to arrive together.

Evidence:
- `visit_model.py`, an exhaustive search of one connection: `flush()`'s steps in the C's order,
  `busy()`, the visit rule `dirty | readable | busy` (`progress.c:1318`), one writer that writes
  and then asks, the kernel's completions and notifications, a peer that answers only a segment
  that asks. The copying send: no stuck state at 1 to 4 writes. As built: stuck from 2 writes,
  the trace above (`visit ; write ; visit ; visit ; write ; visit ; complete 0 ; visit ; visit ;
  ask ; complete 1 ; notify both ; visit`). The close: stuck from 2 writes (`... ; close ; notify
  both ; peer goodbye and end ; visit`). Both fixes below: no stuck state, quiet or close.
- The smoke runs. In the five preset runs of the zero-copy suites (`smoke-zero-copy-1-{debug,asan}`
  at ef9da73; `smoke-zero-copy-3-{debug,asan,tsan}`, whose library is bd87633's), nine of the 36
  zero-copy suite runs took 1.0-1.1 s longer than their copying twin in the same run, and two 7.3 s
  longer: `shmem.internode.zero-copy.0-0-1-1-2-2` 7.49 s (asan, bd87633) and `.0-1-2-0-1-0` 7.47 s
  (asan, ef9da73). None of the 36 copying runs of the same maps took longer than its preset's
  usual time (internode 0.07-0.17 s, multinode 0.31-0.77 s). 1 s is the detector's first watch,
  and 7 s is 1 + 2 + 4, its first three; the sweep is 100 ms (`base/deadline.h:16`), the loopback
  delayed acknowledgement tens of milliseconds and TCP's least retransmission timeout 200 ms, and
  none of those fits. The suites passed because the detector rescued them: nothing bounds how long
  a quiet may take (S1).
- `reservation_model.py` cannot see it: its progress thread waits on "a pass would do something"
  over the whole state (`todo`, `:466-484`), not on the C's busy bits, and acknowledgement
  requests and the goodbye are not modelled ("The acknowledgement *request* protocol is
  ack_model.py's subject, and is not modelled here at all").

`busy()`'s own comment records a defect of the same class, fixed once for the pre-open drain: "the
pre-open drain runs after the pass's chance to seal, and the first barrier of an 8-node job hung
about one run in eight" (`progress.c:1203-1207`). The open step runs after the seal step, so
whatever wants a seal at the moment a buffer opens must keep the connection busy.

Fix, either (both pass `visit_model.py`):
- (preferred, targeted) keep `busy()` true while a seal is owed: a late request - `flush` keeps
  the `wanted` it loaded in the connection's private state, so `busy()` stays "private state
  only" - and, while closing, an unsealed goodbye (`p->closing && c->goodbye ==
  OSOE_GOODBYE_NONE`); or
- (structural) after the open step opens a buffer, run the seal step again in the same visit for a
  late request, an owed acknowledgement or the goodbye. Run for data too, it would also make
  `carrying` unnecessary, but would send a pre-open drain's replies at once rather than with the
  writers' next data - a design call.

And a check that can fail: run the zero-copy suites with `OSOE_DEADLOCK_SECONDS=0`, so that a
stranded wait is a launcher timeout (`-t 60`) rather than a second of rescue; and put the visit
rule into a model (`visit_model.py` is a sketch of one).

### S1. The zero-copy suites cannot fail on whether anything went by reference, and the wire test cannot fail on a miscount

**Severity: should-fix** (checks that cannot fail on the property they are named for, as the
second read's S2).

(a) Nothing checks that an engine send went by reference. Scenario: a later change - Part 2's
control wire, Part 3's helpers replumbing the send - has `flush` call `osoe_wire_send` where it
calls `osoe_wire_send_lent` (`progress.c:1031-1032`). Every engine send copies, `owed` stays 0
and no buffer is ever lent; the eight `shmem.multinode.zero-copy.*` and
`shmem.internode.zero-copy.*` suites pass - they check the program's results, and
`test-multinode.c` reads node metrics (`osoe_test_node_metric`, `:211-234`) but not
`transport.am.zero_copy_*` - and `shmem.wire.zero-copy` passes too, calling
`osoe_wire_send_lent` itself (`test-wire.c:212`).

(b) The wire test cannot see a count that drops early. Under the mutant that zeroes the count at a
notification (`ring.c:236`, `w->owed[region] = 0`), `shmem.wire.zero-copy` ran both streams to the
end and printed the unmutated tree's line, "578 sends by reference, 578 notifications, 578
copied" (`smoke-mutant-zc-release-at-first-notification/tests.full`): over loopback a region
never has two notifications outstanding when one arrives. Its failure in both mutant runs -
"bytes toward node 1 were offered while another send was in flight" - is not a detection but the
bug 765037d fixed: the mutants were built from ef9da73 (their `test-wire.c` is
`zero-copy-1.tar`'s, which is ef9da73's), and unmutated ef9da73 failed the same test the same way
(`smoke-zero-copy-1-debug`, `-asan`). The release-at-completion mutant
(`progress.c:1039`, `if (1)`) is in code the wire test does not run. So the eight suites caught
both mutants - 8 of 8 each, with the messages claimed, on library code identical to bd87633's -
and the wire test caught neither. Its checks `notifications <= sends` and `copied <=
notifications` (`test-wire.c:349-350`) are also weaker than what holds once every region owes
nothing (`:236-241`): equality.

(c) Nothing bounds a suite's time, so F1's stalls passed; and nothing reaches the cap - the suites'
2 KiB buffers go in a part or two each, so `owed` stays far below 16, and a mutant without the cap
(`wire.c:213`) or without its term in the completion bound (`ring.c:70`) would pass everything.

Fix: in `test-multinode.c` and `test-internode.c`, with `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY=on`, require the
node's `transport.am.zero_copy_sends` and `zero_copy_notifications` above 0 at the end (published
every pass, `progress.c:1284-1286`); run the suites with `OSOE_DEADLOCK_SECONDS=0` (F1); in
`shmem.wire.zero-copy`, a phase whose receiving end is a plain socket with a small `SO_RCVBUF`,
left unread until the sends stop - nothing is acknowledged, so `owed` climbs to the cap and the
sends past it copy - and `==` once drained.

### N1. The completion queue's bound passes the kernel's limit inside the job sizes the README promises

**Severity: note.** With the setting on, `ring.c:70` asks for `buffers + 18 × wires + 1`
completions, and the kernel refuses more than 65,536 without `IORING_SETUP_CLAMP`
(`IORING_MAX_CQ_ENTRIES`). At the default 64 receive buffers that is 3,637 peers, a job of 3,638
nodes; at `OSOE_WIRE_BUFFERS=32768`, 1,821 nodes. Past it every lead ends at start with "cannot
create an io_uring for N connections: Invalid argument" (`ring.c:76-77`), which names neither the
setting nor the bound, where README:933 promises 4,097 nodes at the defaults (copying, the same
limit is 32,736). Suggest refusing `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY=on` by name past it, or giving each wire
`min(16, room / wires)` notifications and copying past that.

### N2. A lent buffer waits for the peer's TCP, not for the link

**Severity: note** (for Part 4). A buffer's last notification needs the peer's acknowledgement of
its last byte. Linux acknowledges every second full segment at once, but a lone trailing segment -
the end of a burst toward a peer that sends nothing back - can wait for the delayed-acknowledgement
timer, tens of milliseconds (`tcp_send_delayed_ack`); a loss holds the buffer for a retransmission
timeout, 200 ms at least. While both buffers are so held, writers wait for turnover where a copying
send would have given them one at once. The "about 512 KiB a round trip" of README:1381 and design
§3.3 (`:389-394`) is the steady stream's; Part 4's measurements should include a bursty pattern and
a lossy one before a default is chosen.

### N3. Under the unsent limit, the threshold is applied to what is offered, not to what the kernel takes

**Severity: note.** `send_from` decides by `n`, the bytes offered (`wire.c:209-213`). Under
`TCP_NOTSENT_LOWAT` L - 16 KiB at a gigabit - a send is accepted while the unsent queue is under L
and retried once it falls under L/2, so each send by reference carries about L/2 and one segment:
some 10 KiB without segmentation offload (tier 3 turns it off), some 70 KiB with it. A 256 KiB
buffer then takes from four to more than twenty sends, each owing a notification and the small
ones pinning less than the "about 10 KB" the README and §3.3 give as the break-even; without
offload the 16-owed cap (`wire.c:213`) is reached inside the first buffer and the rest copy. Not a
defect; the Pi measurement should report `transport.am.zero_copy_sends` against `sends`, which
says the mix.

### N4. A lent buffer is invisible to the engine's idleness

**Severity: note.** `osoe_progress_idle` (`progress.c:1341-1367`) - used by `flush_before_close`
and by the deadlock detector's watch - counts a connection with no open buffer as idle, and
`osoe_progress_quiet` adds only acknowledgements. A lent buffer is work the network finishes
without any PE, as a SENDING one is, and SENDING counts. The detector's argument holds today
through TCP's cumulative acknowledgement - a peer that has acknowledged applying a segment has
acknowledged its bytes, so the notification is on its way - but that is written nowhere, and it is
weaker for a driver that frees transmitted buffers late. One line, a lent buffer as not idle,
keeps the argument where it was.

### N7. "No byte written while lent" can be checked directly under ASan

**Severity: note.** Over loopback the kernel copies a send's pages when loopback delivers them,
usually before the send's completion is posted and always before its notification, so no test can
see a premature write as a corrupt byte; only the counts can (S1). Poisoning a buffer's bytes in
the lead's process from its promotion until its release - as `ring.c:25-31` poisons provided
buffers since the third read - would report any write into a buffer still sending or lent by the
agent (the pre-open drain) or by the lead PE's own threads, whatever the count says. Writers in
other processes are not covered.

## The eight properties, as tried

**1. No byte of a connection buffer is written while the kernel may still read it - held.**
- Every writer, and what it can reach: application writers reserve only in an unsealed buffer
  (`connection.c:41-107`), and only `osoe_connection_open` clears the seal (`:114-142`); the
  pre-open drain writes a buffer between its release and its open (`progress.c:1128-1133`); the
  opportunistic drain writes the open buffer (`:836-845`); `finalize` writes the header of the
  sealed buffer before it is promoted (`:882-922`, `:1066`); the goodbye and the ack-only segments
  are open buffers sealed like any other. The abort writes no buffer: `flush_aborting` finishes the
  segment on the wire with copying sends (`:945`) and sends its own `abort_segment` (`:958`), and
  releases and opens nothing. `osoe_connection_release` and `_drain_reserve` write the shared
  words, never the bytes.
- So every write follows the open step, which checks `owed == 0` first (`progress.c:1123-1127`),
  and the open step is `osoe_connection_open`'s only caller (`:1134`).
- The count is exact when the check reads it: a buffer becomes FREE only once its last send's
  completion has been collected (`c->sent == c->sending_bytes`, `:1036`), and with one send in
  flight (`wire.c:192-204`) every completion of its sends - each with `F_MORE` when by reference -
  was reaped before. `owed[k]` cannot rise while k is FREE, DRAINING, OPEN or SEALED: only a send
  tagged k raises it, and k is sent only as `c->sending`, which the open step excludes (`:1118`).
- Short sends, `OSOE_WIRE_SPLIT`'s truncations and the cap's copies: each send by reference owes
  its own notification (`wire.c:212-220`); a copy owes none and is safe at its completion. A send
  by reference that fails (`-EPIPE`, `-ECONNRESET`, any other) still carries `F_MORE` and is
  counted before its error is (`ring.c:242-257`); after `send_error`, `flush` returns before it
  releases or opens anything (`progress.c:987-988`), and after `error` the next send is fatal. The
  peer's reset purges the socket's queues, so the notifications come. The switch of `c->sending`:
  a send's region is in its tag (`ring.c:43-46`), so whoever collects the completion - `flush`, or
  `flush_aborting` through `osoe_wire_send` - the count goes to the buffer the bytes came from.
- The model agrees, and so does a harsher one: `reservation_model.py zero_copy zc_` here, 8 cases
  ok with liveness (1,637 to 884,318 states, one under TSO) and all three breakage runs failing;
  the caller's full run (`reservation-model-zc-full.txt`) shows the earlier 32 cases and 17
  breakages unchanged. My variant, in which each part's notification comes after its delivery in
  any order and re-reads the part, failing on any unit written since (REUSED): 8 cases ok with
  liveness, up to 3,660,304 states under TSO, and the three breakage runs fail with REUSED.

**2. No buffer is lent for ever, and no wait hangs on one - the first held, the second not (F1).**
- A notification comes once the peer's TCP has acknowledged the bytes and the stack has freed what
  held them. A stopped peer's kernel acknowledges until its window shuts, which the copying send
  also waited for (§3.15); a peer that ends with input unread resets the connection, and the reset
  purges the send and retransmission queues (`tcp_reset`, `tcp_write_queue_purge`); a vanished
  host is `osoe_progress_check_silent`'s, as before - unacknowledged is unacknowledged, lent or
  copied.
- Nothing waits for `osoe_wire_owed` to reach 0: the abort (`flush_aborting`,
  `osoe_progress_aborted`) and the close (`osoe_progress_closed`) look at segments and goodbyes; a
  buffer still lent at the end is never written again, and the kernel holds its pages by reference
  after `munmap`.
- The agent's sleep wakes on a notification: a CQE ends `io_uring_submit_and_wait_timeout`
  (`ring.c:279`), and without `IORING_SETUP_DEFER_TASKRUN` the notification's task work signals
  the agent (`TWA_SIGNAL`; `IOU_F_TWQ_LAZY_WAKE` is ignored). A lent connection stays busy
  (`progress.c:1218`), so the pass after the reap visits it and releases the buffer.
- What fails is what follows the release: F1.

**3. The counting is exact - held.**
- Every `IORING_OP_SEND_ZC` whose notification has been allocated completes with `F_MORE`:
  `io_send_zc` sets it on every result, a count short or whole or an error; a failure on the way
  (`-EOPNOTSUPP`, `-ENOTSOCK`, a preparation refused after the allocation) goes through
  `io_sendrecv_fail`, which adds `F_MORE` while `REQ_F_NEED_CLEANUP` says the notification
  exists, and `io_send_zc_cleanup` flushes it. A request refused before its notification exists
  has neither, and is `w->error`, fatal at the next send.
- One notification a request: `-EAGAIN` under `IO_URING_F_NONBLOCK` arms a poll, and the retry
  reuses the request's notification; without `MSG_WAITALL` (`wire.c:218` passes `MSG_NOSIGNAL`
  only) `io_net_retry` is false, so a short send completes with its count and is not retried
  inside. Notification stacking in newer kernels (`io_link_skb`) ties several to one head but
  still posts each its own CQE with its own user data.
- After its request's completion: issued inline, the request's completion is batched and posted at
  `io_submit_state_end`, before any task work runs, and the notification is task work; issued from
  task work (a poll's retry), the notification's work is queued only when the request drops its
  reference and runs behind the batch that holds the request's completion; in io-wq the flush waits
  for cleanup, after `io_req_complete_post` has posted the completion - the kernel's comment says
  why. The completion queue is FIFO and its overflow list keeps the order. So the reap's "which
  owed none" fires only on a count gone wrong.
- `IORING_SEND_ZC_REPORT_USAGE` sets bit 31 of the notification's `res` and nothing else (N5 for
  its meaning). The tag: kind in bits 0-3 (1-3), region in 4-7 (0-2), slot from bit 8
  (`ring.c:33-46`); a receive's region bits are 0 and never read, the futex's tag is 3 and tested
  first, and neither path carries `F_NOTIF`. A copying send never says a notification follows:
  no bundles, no multishot sends.

**4. The completion queue cannot overflow - held** (N1 for the kernel's ceiling).
Between two reaps, per wire: at most one send's completion - one in flight, and a send is submitted
only after its predecessor's completion was collected, which needs a reap; `release_local`'s
second `flush` in a pass finds the send in flight and gets 0 - its notification, and the
notifications owed at the last reap. A send goes by reference only while `owed[0] + owed[1] < 16`
(`wire.c:213`), and `owed` only falls between submissions, so the notifications outstanding never
exceed 16, the new send's included: 17 completions on the send side at most, and one terminal
receive, the 18 a wire of `ring.c:70`. Receive completions, one a provided buffer, and the futex's
one are as before; the abort's sends copy.

**6. The model says what the C does - held for the buffer protocol; the visit rule is outside it

## Verdict

One fatal finding. The zero-copy send itself is right: the model, a harsher variant of it and the
code agree that no byte of a buffer is written while the kernel may read it, the counting rests on
what io_uring guarantees, the queue keeps its bound, and the default path is unchanged. But lent
buffers give a connection a state - both buffers back from the kernel in one visit - after which a
seal it owes, a quiet's acknowledgement request or its goodbye, is never made, because the open step
runs after the seal step and `busy()` does not know a seal is owed. With the deadlock detector on,
a quiet then waits for the detector's next round (1 s, then 2 s, 4 s); with it off, or at the
close, the job hangs. Eleven of the smoke runs' thirty-six zero-copy suite runs carry the
detector's 1 s and 7 s in their times, and passed because it rescued them.

Before merging: F1 (either fix; `visit_model.py` checks both), with the zero-copy suites run with
the detector off so that it cannot return unseen; S1's checks (a send went by reference, nothing
waits long, the wire test's equality and a phase that reaches the cap); S2's pacer run with the
setting on. The rest can follow, N1 before anyone turns the setting on past a few thousand nodes.
