# v0.2 before Part 4, question 4: will it run anywhere else?

Round nine of osoe's adversarial review, run 2026-10-10 and 11 against `main`
at `3b45472` (the worktree `review-nine`), with
`docs/reviews/adversarial-review-prompt.md`'s fields and the common brief of
the round. The question: everything has run on aarch64 - the development VMs
and the Pi - under one kernel family, but for the single-node build's Rocky
Linux; how much of that is a gap, and how much a risk. Round eight's answer
(`docs/reviews/archive/v0.2-step4-portability.md`, at `c897948`) is held as a
set of claims to check. Labels: *documented* (a source says so: the tree, the
Linux or Slurm source or documentation), *observed in source*, *measured* (run
here, on this tree), *recalled* (from memory, not checked).

## Verdict

**The library is as portable as round eight found it, and what this tree added
is too: x86-64, Rocky Linux, the build and install, the soname and the trust
model hold. Nothing blocks. Two things should be settled before 0.2 is tagged,
both cheap, and neither changes what a job across real nodes does today:**

- **P9-1, fix before release (medium-high confidence; computed, not run):** on
  Linux 6.14 and later io_uring charges every ring to one locked-memory count
  per user on a host, held to `ulimit -l` - 8 MiB, soft *and* hard, by default
  on Debian 13 and Rocky 9.8, so a user cannot raise it. On one machine every
  logical node's rings count together. From the rings this tree makes (their
  sizes measured) and the kernel's accounting (read in v6.14's source):
  `osoe-launch`, which the README offers to 254 logical nodes, is refused past
  about 128 at 4 KiB pages and no helpers, 96 at three helpers, 42 at three
  helpers on the Pi's 16 KiB pages; and the suite's largest jobs,
  `shmem.circulant.64` and `.70`, need 9.0 to 13.1 MiB each at two or three
  helpers on the Pi's own kernel - each refused alone - and 9.7 MiB together
  with `.33` and `.17` at no helpers. The README says the charge exists, and a
  refused ring is named well (measured, by emulation), but nothing says that
  logical nodes and parallel tests add up to it, and no kernel of 6.14 or later
  has run the suite.
- **P9-2, fix before release (high confidence; measured):** the fast-link kit
  starts every job with `srun --overcommit --exclusive --ntasks-per-node=P`,
  meaning to run more PEs than a node has CPUs - "32 PEs a node of 16". Slurm
  24.11.5, the version Debian 13 ships, refuses exactly that at allocation:
  "Unable to allocate resources: Requested node configuration is not
  available", with or without `--exclusive`; `-n` with `--exclusive` instead
  queues a job that never starts. Inside an exclusive allocation the same step
  runs. So on the runbook's own `c4-standard-16` every job of 32 PEs a node -
  §3's start-up check, a third of §5's helpers sweep, §10's start-up timings -
  would fail: caught at §3, on rented time, with no form in the kit that works.

**What is sound, and measured where a machine allowed:**

- **x86-64**: both builds compile for x86-64 with GCC 14.2 without a warning,
  every static assertion holding at 64-byte lines; the README's
  per-architecture sizes are right; both libraries export the same 3,595 names
  as aarch64's. Clang's front end takes every one of the build's 311
  translation units, for x86-64 and for aarch64, without an error or a warning.
  Under Rosetta the single-node build passes 396 of 404 and the full build's
  one-node tests 393 of 400, every failure the emulator's, the cross build's,
  or P9-7's split line. **The x86-ordered aarch64 build** - the checklist's
  x86-64 item, which round eight asked to run at two and three helpers - passes
  the release suite whole, 867 of 867, the 467 tests across nodes at two
  helpers and at three, 467 of 467 each, and the stress tester at both counts,
  `failures=0`.
- **Kernels**: nothing this tree added asks more of a kernel than round eight
  found (Linux 6.7; nothing new past 6.2). Every refusal I could make io_uring
  give - `ENOMEM` at the start-up check, at the provided buffers, at a lead's
  own ring after the check had passed, `EPERM` as the sysctl gives it - ended
  the job at once, by name, the limit or the sysctl in the message.
- **Rocky Linux 9.8**: the README's desktop section as printed builds without a
  warning and installs, and the suite passes 402 of 402; its kernel has the
  futex2 calls, backported, and refuses io_uring. The full build with the
  hardening flags Ubuntu's GCC adds by itself (`_FORTIFY_SOURCE=3` among them)
  builds clean and passes 867 of 867.
- **The soname**: kept, rightly. The installed headers and the linker script are
  0.1.0's byte for byte, the exported names and their kinds identical. A
  program built by 0.1.0's `oshcc` ran on this tree's library after `cmake
  --install` over the 0.1.0 prefix, on one node and two; 0.1.0's own installed
  `osoe-pacer` ran `--quick`, `mix` and `threads` on it, `failures=0`; 0.1.0's
  whole suite, with 0.2's library in front of its own, passed 670 of 718, every
  failure a test of 0.1.0's own defaults, shapes or counters. Each version's
  `osoe-stat` reads a running job of the other, as design §3.14 promises. What
  breaks is mixing: a job whose nodes, or whose PEs on one node, run 0.1.0 and
  0.2 waits at start-up until the launcher ends it (P9-3).
- **Slurm, otherwise**: the kit's names, signals, limits and `slurm.conf` are
  what Slurm's documentation and Debian's units say, and its locked-memory
  recipe is the one P9-1 wants.
- **Trust**: stated, in the design and the README, and the code holds every
  peer's offset and length to the region it names.

## Findings

### P9-1. Linux 6.14 and later charge every ring of a user's processes on a host to one 8 MiB count: logical nodes, the suite and many PEs on large pages add up to it (fix before release; medium-high)

- **Where**: `README.md:484-487` ("up to 1,024 processes on 254 logical
  nodes"), `:1034-1038` (the charge, as a limit on launching), `:1312-1314`
  (the suite at `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=2` and `3`), `:1529` (the zero-copy
  row); design §3.3 (the same); `shmem/src/ring.c:77-100` and
  `shmem/src/wire.c:41-73` (each owner's ring: `2 × wires + 1` submissions,
  rounded up to a power of two, `buffers + 2 × wires + 1` completions, a
  provided-buffer ring of `OSOE_WIRE_BUFFERS`); `shmem/src/owner-map.h:54-97`
  (owner 0 holds every control wire, and every data wire at no helpers);
  `shmem/src/transport-am.c:115-118` with `transport-internode.c:229-261` and
  `base/ring-check.c` (every PE of a job of more than one node makes a ring of
  its own to check the kernel).
- **The kernel** (*documented*, Linux v6.14): `io_allocate_scq_urings` puts the
  completion ring and the submission entries in a region each through
  `io_create_region`, each `PAGE_ALIGN`ed; `io_register_pbuf_ring` puts a
  provided-buffer ring, the application's own memory included, in a region the
  same way; and `io_create_region` charges `size >> PAGE_SHIFT` pages through
  `__io_account_mem(ctx->user, ...)` whenever `ctx->user` is set - whenever the
  process lacks `CAP_IPC_LOCK` in the initial user namespace. The count is the
  user's, shared by every process of the user on the host, and held to the
  calling process's own `RLIMIT_MEMLOCK`. The record of 2026-10-10 measured the
  consequence on the Pi's 6.18: under `ulimit -l 0` the start-up check's ring
  and the provided-buffer ring fail `ENOMEM`
  (`docs/decisions/2026-10-10-zero-copy-locked-memory.md`).
- **The rings this tree makes** (*measured*, `scripts/r-rings.sh`: each lead's
  io_uring descriptors read from `/proc/self/fdinfo` once every node is
  connected, one PE a logical node; submission / completion entries):

  | Logical nodes | No helpers | Two helpers | Three helpers |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2 | 1 ring, 8 / 128 | 3 rings: 4 / 128 twice, 1 / 128 | 4 rings: 4 / 128 twice, 1 / 128 twice |
  | 33 | 1 ring, 256 / 256 | 128 / 256, and 64 / 128 twice | 128 / 256, and 32 / 128 three times |
  | 70 | 1 ring, 512 / 512 | 256 / 256, and 128 / 256 twice | 256 / 256, and 64 / 128 three times |
  | 128 | 1 ring, 512 / 1024 | 256 / 512, 256 / 256, 128 / 256 | 256 / 512, and 128 / 256 three times |

  Every one is what `ring.c`'s two bounds give, so the charges follow from the
  code's own arithmetic (`calc/ring-charge.py`; every job above ran to its end
  on 6.12, which charges nothing).
- **What that charges** (computed: a lead's rings, each a region of its
  completion ring, one of its submission entries at 64 bytes each, one of its
  64 provided buffers' descriptors; whole pages):

  | | 4 KiB pages | 16 KiB pages (the Pi 5) | 64 KiB pages |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `shmem.circulant.70`, no / two / three helpers | 3.3 / 4.7 / 4.4 MiB | 4.4 / 9.8 / 13.1 MiB | 13.1 / 39 / 53 MiB |
  | `shmem.circulant.64` | 2.0 / 3.3 / 3.5 MiB | 3.0 / 9.0 / 12.0 MiB | |
  | `.17`, `.33`, `.64` and `.70` at once, no helpers | 6.5 MiB | 9.7 MiB | |
  | the most logical nodes one job keeps under 8 MiB, no / two / three helpers | 128 / 113 / 96 | 112 / 56 / 42 | 42 / 14 / 10 |
  | each PE's start-up check, a job of more than one node | 12 KiB | 48 KiB | 192 KiB |

- **Scenarios**:
  1. **The suite on the project's own Pi** (Raspberry Pi OS, Linux 6.18, 16 KiB
     pages, `ulimit -l` 8 MiB): `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=2 ctest --preset release
     -j4`, as "How osoe is tested" describes the project's runs -
     `shmem.circulant.64` and `.70` are each refused at start-up, alone; at no
     helpers `ctest` runs the four largest circulant jobs side by side (they are
     registered together, and `ctest` starts its costliest tests first once it
     has their times, *recalled*), 9.7 MiB, and the last of them to start is
     refused. The start-up checks of `.70`'s 70 PEs add 3.3 MiB more for as long
     as their rings take to be torn down.
  2. **A stranger on Fedora 42 or later, Arch, Ubuntu 25.04 or later, or
     Debian's backports** (kernels of 6.14 and later, *recalled*; 4 KiB pages):
     the suite at two or three helpers with `.64` and `.70` overlapping, 7.9 MiB
     beside whatever else runs; `osoe-launch -m` past 128 logical nodes.
  3. **A node of many PEs on a large-page kernel** (64 KiB pages, as some arm64
     servers run): every PE's start-up check takes 192 KiB while it lasts, so
     256 PEs starting at once can pass 8 MiB in that moment alone. On 4 KiB
     pages it is 3 MiB, and a real cluster's leads' own rings stay far below the
     limit (a lead of a 500-node job holds about 200 KiB).

  In each case the job ends at start-up by name - "cannot create an io_uring
  for N connections: Cannot allocate memory (on Linux 6.14 and later io_uring
  counts its rings against the locked-memory limit, ulimit -l, which is 8192
  KiB here)", or the start-up check's equivalent: a promise the README makes
  and the platform breaks, loudly, not a hang.
- **Evidence for the failure mode** (*measured*, Linux 6.12, the refusal emulated
  by a seccomp filter that answers `io_uring_setup` with `ENOMEM`,
  `deny/deny-ring.c`, `scripts/k-deny.sh`, `k-deny2.sh`): every ring refused - the
  start-up check names the limit; the provided buffers refused - the start-up
  check names them and the limit; rings above four entries refused, so that the
  check passes and each lead's own ring fails - "cannot create an io_uring for
  2 connections: Cannot allocate memory (on Linux 6.14 and later ... 8192 KiB
  here)", the job over in 0.05 s, at no helpers and at two, and with
  `osoe-launch -k` the node-mates ending "on behalf of" their lead; a job of one
  node with every ring refused runs. Not measured: the charge itself, which no
  machine here has a kernel for.
- **Fix** (the first three are documents, a test property and one command):
  - The README's `osoe-launch` paragraph and its limit on launching: on Linux
    6.14 and later, logical nodes' rings count against one user's locked-memory
    limit together - about 128 logical nodes at the default 8 MiB with 4 KiB
    pages, fewer with helpers or larger pages - and raising it is an
    administrator's change (`/etc/security/limits.d`; under Slurm
    `PropagateResourceLimitsExcept=MEMLOCK` with `slurmd` unlimited, as
    `docs/fast-link/slurm-setup.sh` does).
  - "How osoe is tested": the same for the suite at helpers and on large pages;
    and a `RESOURCE_LOCK` over the circulant jobs of more than 16 nodes, so that
    `ctest -j` never runs two at once.
  - Before the tag, on one Pi node:
    `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=2 ctest --test-dir build/release -R 'shmem\.circulant\.(64|70)$'`
    - this finding predicts both refused, naming the limit.
  - After the release: only a node's lead makes the start-up check's ring (the
    others take its refusal through the segment, as they do its other
    refusals), and a submission queue sized to its wires.

### P9-2. The fast-link kit's `srun` cannot start a job of more PEs a node than the node has CPUs (fix before release; high)

- **Where**: `docs/fast-link/common.sh:10-13` and `:31` (`KIT_SRUN` "-K1
  --cpu-bind=none --overcommit --exclusive: whole nodes, no binding, and more
  PEs than CPUs - 32 PEs a node of 16 - which srun refuses without
  --overcommit"), `:91-92` (each job `srun -J osoe-kit $KIT_SRUN -N<nodes>
  --ntasks-per-node=<pes>`); `docs/fast-link.md:59` (the sweep "up to 32 PEs a
  node"), `:78` (`c4-standard-16`, 16 vCPUs), `:155-157`, `:187` (§3's start-up
  check at 32 PEs a node); `helpers.sh` (8, 16 and 32 PEs a node), `scale.sh`
  (start-up at 32).
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `scripts/s-slurm3.sh`, Slurm 24.11.5 in the
  development VM - one node, 4 CPUs, `CR_Core`, `task/affinity`, the kit's own
  `SelectType` and `TaskPlugin`; eight tasks on four CPUs standing for 32 on 16;
  `out/osoe/s-slurm3.txt`):

  | `srun` form | Result |
  |---|---|
  | `-N1 --ntasks-per-node=4` | 4 tasks run |
  | `-K1 --cpu-bind=none --overcommit --exclusive -N1 --ntasks-per-node=8` - the kit's | "Unable to allocate resources: Requested node configuration is not available" |
  | `-N1 --ntasks-per-node=8 --overcommit` | the same |
  | `-N1 -n8 --ntasks-per-node=8 --overcommit` | the same |
  | `-N1 --ntasks-per-node=8 --overcommit --exclusive --cpus-per-task=1` | the same |
  | `-N1 -n8 --overcommit` | 8 tasks run |
  | `-N1 -n8 --overcommit --exclusive` | "queued and waiting for resources", never started |
  | `salloc -N1 --exclusive` then a step `srun --overcommit --ntasks-per-node=8` (or `-n8`) | 8 tasks run |

  So the job allocation refuses `--ntasks-per-node` above the node's CPUs
  whatever `--overcommit` says; `--overcommit` lets a *step* exceed them. The
  documentation reads otherwise ("By specifying --overcommit you are explicitly
  allowing more than one process per CPU"), which is how the kit came to rely
  on it.
- **Scenario**: Part 4 on the runbook's machines. §3's
  `srun ... --ntasks-per-node=32 --overcommit --exclusive` is refused at once,
  and every 32-PE-a-node job of §5 and §10 after it; the runbook's guard ("the
  second line must start a job of 32 PEs a node ... before the sweep is
  started") stops the run there, which is the right place to fail - but with no
  form in the kit known to work, so the fix is designed on rented time.
- **What I could not show**: more than one node; the development VM's Slurm has
  one. The refusal is of the per-node count, so I expect the same on many.
- **Fix**, any, measured in the development VM's Slurm before a node is rented:
  run each section's jobs as steps of one exclusive allocation (`salloc -N<n>
  --exclusive`, the steps `--overcommit --ntasks-per-node=P`, and `--overlap`
  for `floor.sh`'s concurrent ones, which is what the kit's "not inside an
  allocation" avoided); or have `slurm-setup.sh` give each node a `CPUs=` at
  least the sweep's 32; or keep the sweep within the CPUs. And correct
  `common.sh`'s and §1's sentence.

### P9-3. Mixed osoe versions in a job wait for ever rather than being refused by name, and the README does not say a job must run one osoe (after release; high)

- **Where**: `shmem/src/listener.c:446-449` (a hello with osoe's magic and
  another version is dropped "as a stranger"); `shmem/src/node.c:1093-1145`
  (`open_control` waits on `ready` before it checks the layout, whose message
  already says "mixed library versions?"); `shmem/src/node.h:862-905` (layout
  32, v0.2 step 2, put the shape's and the link's words before `ready`, so the
  word moved); `docs/release-notes/0.2.0.md:91-96` (says it of nodes); the
  README's Limits (say nothing of it).
- **Scenario**: a site that installs osoe on each node rather than once on a
  shared file system - which the README allows ("the same install on each") -
  upgrades its nodes one at a time; or one rank of a job is started with
  another `LD_LIBRARY_PATH`. With the soname kept, a program built against
  either library loads whichever the node has.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `scripts/a-abi.sh run`, a program built by 0.1.0's
  `oshcc`, `osoe-launch -e RANK:LD_LIBRARY_PATH=...` giving some ranks 0.1.0's
  library):
  - **Node 1 on 0.1.0, node 0 on 0.2**: each PE prints its version, then node
    1's lead says at each notice (the test hook's, from 5 s; a job's come at 30
    s and then every minute) that node 0 gives "no answer to the hello on a
    candidate port", and node 0's that it waits for node 1 "(1 caller part-way
    through the hello)" - until the launcher's timeout, 60 s here. Under `srun`
    nothing times out but the job's own time limit.
  - **Rank 1 on 0.1.0, rank 0 (the lead) on 0.2, one node**: rank 1 waits "for
    the local lead, rank 0, to finish the node's control segment ..., which it
    has created", and rank 0 for local id 1 to join - until the timeout. Rank 1
    reads `ready` where layout 31 had it, a word layout 37 keeps elsewhere, and
    never reaches the layout check that would have named the cause.
- **What is sound**: the notices come every minute, so the job is not silent;
  the release note says nodes of 0.1.0 and 0.2 do not form one job; and the
  connection area already pins its `ready` and `opened` for exactly this
  (`connection-area.c:108-116`). The control segment does not.
- **Fix**: a sentence in the README's Limits now - every node and every PE of a
  job must run the same osoe; after the release, a hello with the magic and
  another version ends the job naming both, and the control segment's `ready`
  and `failed` keep their place from layout 37 on, asserted as the connection
  area's are, so that a later osoe meeting this one is refused by its layout.
  0.1.0's own PEs will always wait.

### P9-4. "Raise it, as far as the hard limit allows" is no remedy at the defaults (minor; high)

- **Where**: `README.md:1529` (the zero-copy row: "raise it for the job -
  `ulimit -l` in its batch script, as far as the hard limit allows"); the
  notice in `shmem/src/progress.c:1760-1772` ("raise it for the job").
- **Evidence** (*measured*): `ulimit -Sl` and `ulimit -Hl` are both 8192 on
  Debian 13 and Rocky 9.8, so a user's batch script can raise neither, and under
  Slurm's default `PropagateResourceLimits=ALL` a step takes the submitting
  shell's 8 MiB. The fast-link kit's recipe - `limits.d`,
  `PropagateResourceLimitsExcept=MEMLOCK`, `slurmd` at `LimitMEMLOCK=infinity`,
  which Debian's own `slurmd.service` already has - is the one that works.
- **Fix**: say that raising it is usually an administrator's change, and point
  at the kit's recipe; P9-1's sentence can carry it.

### P9-5. Round eight's portability notes not taken (minor; high)

- **The 6.7 floor still has never run**, and the README still does not say
  which kernels have: 6.12 (the VMs), 6.18 (the Pi), 5.14 for the single-node
  build (round eight's P5, first note). The floor is reasoned from the
  facilities, soundly; one clause would say so.
- **The checklist does not name the models** (`lead_wake_model.py`,
  `helper_request_model.py` under x86-TSO) as what stands for x86's order on the
  helpers' wakes until an x86-64 kernel times them (round eight's P3, third
  fix). The README's passage and the checklist's item at two and three helpers
  were taken.
- **The probe has no line for `IORING_OP_SEND_ZC`** (round eight's P1, fourth
  fix). It prints the locked-memory limit, the other half, and the zero-copy
  send is refused by name where the opcode is missing, so this is the least of
  it.
- **A cross build's nested check still finds the host's liburing**:
  `shmem.strict-c11` fails in the x86-64 full build (*measured*, as at round
  eight's P5). Cross builds are not promised.

### P9-6. The public headers are not tested under C23, which GCC 15 makes the default for every program `oshcc` builds (minor; high)

- **Where**: `shmem/tests/CMakeLists.txt:2464-2473` (`c99 c11 c17`).
- **Evidence** (*measured*): `headers.c` compiles with `-Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic
  -Werror` under `-std=c23` and `-std=gnu23` with GCC 14.2 and under `c23` with
  Clang, and `headers.cpp` under C++20 and C++23 with g++ 14.2; the README's
  ring under `gnu23`. Nothing is wrong today; GCC 15 and later compile a
  program as `gnu23` when it names no standard - Fedora 42 and Ubuntu 25.10
  have it (*recalled*) - and nothing keeps the headers so.
- **Fix**: `c23` in the list where the compiler has it.

### P9-7. A library line over 128 bytes printed by `fprintf` reaches stderr in pieces, which other PEs' output splits (minor; high)

- **Where**: the library's 22 direct `fprintf(stderr, ...)` calls, among them
  `shmem/src/liveness.c:129-131` (the pidfd notice), `progress-host.c:510-552`
  (`SHMEM_DEBUG`'s unsent and keepalive lines), `listener.c:804-806` (a name
  that resolves to loopback), `node.c:1028` and `shm-object.c:119` (the
  capacity warning), `init.c:99` (`SHMEM_INFO`'s text). `osoe_notice` and the
  fatal path (`fatal.c:84`, `:138`) format into a buffer and `write` once; these
  do not.
- **Evidence** (*measured*):
  - glibc 2.41 (Debian 13), natively: four processes each printing 2,000 lines
    of 150 bytes to one pipe with `fprintf(stderr, ...)` - 5,873, 7,672 and
    6,067 of the 8,000 lines broken by another's bytes in three runs; at 120
    bytes, none (`stderr/split.c`, `split-120.c`). The unbuffered stream is
    written through a staging buffer of 128 bytes (*recalled*: glibc's printf
    since 2.37), one `write` each.
  - In the suite, under Rosetta, which has no `pidfd_open`: the lead's pidfd
    notice came out as 128 bytes, another PE's "running 2" line, then ")" -
    split at exactly 128 bytes both times it was caught - so
    `shmem.launcher-signals.int.one-node`, which counts lines that begin
    "running", never saw four and failed: 2 of 10 runs of the single-node
    build's, alone, with that output; and both runs of the x86-64 full
    build's, whose output I did not keep.
- **Scenario**: the single-node build supports Linux 4.17 and later, and before
  5.3 every job's lead prints that notice; there the test can fail the same
  way. And on any glibc of 2.37 or later, `SHMEM_DEBUG` from several leads of
  one machine, or several PEs' warnings, interleave mid-line - in a terminal,
  and in `srun`'s merged output.
- **Fix**: those calls through one `write`, as `osoe_notice` does.

### P9-8. Two comments give one architecture's line (minor; high)

- `shmem/src/node.h:1032`: a context block is "fixed-size, one cache line" - it
  is 128 bytes on both architectures (*measured*, `sizes/sizes.c`), two lines
  on x86-64.
- `shmem/src/connection-area.c:91`: "One line holds 512 connections' bits" -
  1,024 on aarch64's 128-byte line.

### P9-9. The README's Trust paragraph does not say what joining a job gives a host (minor; medium)

- **Where**: `README.md:1681-1690`; design §3.4 and §6.2 say it ("All PEs of a
  job trust each other completely ... assumed buggy but not hostile").
- **Scenario**: a reader deciding whether to set `OSOE_JOB_SECRET` reads that
  without it "any host that can reach the ports can join", and may take that to
  mean it can disturb the job. Joining means being a node: reading and writing
  every PE's symmetric heap and every global and static variable of the
  program (`resolve` in `progress-host.c:61-111` holds a peer to a region's
  bounds, and to nothing less) - the processes themselves.
- **Fix**: one clause - a host that joins can read and write every PE's
  symmetric memory, the program's statics among them.

## Slurm

Read against Slurm's pages (*documented*), and run in the development VM's
24.11.5 where one node allows (*measured*); the kit has run on no cluster, as
`docs/fast-link.md` says.

- **`srun`'s options**: `-K1` is the form the page asks for; `--cpu-bind=none`
  binds nothing; `-J` names the job and `scancel -u USER -n osoe-kit` cancels by
  that name (status 0 with nothing to cancel, measured), every `osoe-kit` job
  of the user at once, which the kit's one section at a time makes safe. **But
  `--exclusive --overcommit --ntasks-per-node` above the CPUs is refused at
  allocation: P9-2.**
- **Signals**: `srun` forwards `SIGTERM`, `SIGQUIT` and `SIGHUP` to its tasks as
  `SIGKILL` (the 26.05 page's `--ignore-signals` list; long-standing,
  *recalled*). So the kit's `timeout` around `srun` kills a job's PEs outright,
  and a job killed in `shmem_init` leaves names in `/dev/shm` - which the kit's
  `osoe-stat --gc` after a timeout is there for.
- **Limits**: `PropagateResourceLimitsExcept=MEMLOCK` with `slurmd` at
  `LimitMEMLOCK=infinity` gives every step an unlimited locked-memory limit, and
  Debian's `slurmd.service` already has the latter (*measured*): the recipe P9-1
  and P9-4 want. It also means Part 4 will not see the zero-copy refusal at the
  default limit, which `zero-copy.sh` records rather than tests.
- **The rest of `slurm-setup.sh`**: `InactiveLimit=120` ends a killed `srun`'s
  allocation; `SlurmctldPidFile=/run/slurmctld/slurmctld.pid` is the directory
  Debian's unit makes for the `slurm` user (`RuntimeDirectory=slurmctld`) -
  better than the development VM's own `/run/slurmctld.pid`, which that user
  cannot write; `MpiDefault=none` and `proctrack/linuxproc` run in the
  development VM's 24.11.5; Debian 13's `/etc/profile` and `login.defs` keep
  `/usr/sbin` off a user's `PATH`, as the kit says (Lima adds it in its VMs).
- **`sbatch` against `srun`, and the environment**: a program run straight from
  a batch script is refused by name, pinned by a captured 24.11 fixture
  (`captured-batch-script.env`); block, cyclic and plane distributions are
  captured; a heterogeneous job is refused by `SLURM_HET_SIZE`, from a fixture
  written from the documentation and still marked provisional. The step's names
  the bootstrap reads - `SLURM_PROCID`, `_STEP_NUM_TASKS`, `_NODEID`,
  `_LOCALID`, `_GTIDS`, `_STEP_NODELIST`, `_STEP_ID` - are all still on the
  current (26.05) `srun` page.

## Trust

Stated in the design (§3.4: the PEs of a job "trust each other completely ...
assumed buggy but not hostile"; other local users are kept out by mode 0600,
`O_EXCL` and the owner's uid; the network is trusted as Slurm's own traffic is;
§6.2: the secret's proof, by SipHash-2-4 over the hello and both nonces, never
sent) and in the README's "Trust" (what the secret does not cover:
connection-holding, a squatted port, the traffic after the hello). Nothing since
round eight changed it: the control wire and the helpers' wires take the same
hello and secret. The code holds a peer to what the design says (*observed in
source*): `resolve` refuses a region the build does not carry, a PE past the
job, and an offset or length past the heap, the statics or the internal region
(`progress-host.c:61-111`); a get's reply is held to its landing record. What is
not stated is P9-9.

## What is sound, and what convinced me

- **x86-64 is no further away than at round eight, and the new code as portable
  as the old**: no warning from GCC for either architecture or from Clang's
  front end for both; the line-dependent layouts asserted, and documented per
  architecture correctly; the exports identical across builds and
  architectures; x86's memory order over the whole suite at no helpers and the
  tests across nodes at two and three, and the stress tester at both.
- **A kernel that refuses is named**: every refusal I could provoke ended the
  job at once, the cause in its first line.
- **Rocky Linux 9.8 is the desktop build's**, as printed, 402 of 402; and the
  full build survives a hardened toolchain.
- **The soname decision is right on the evidence**: identical headers, script
  and exports; 0.1.0's programs, its stress tester and its suite's interface
  tests running unchanged on 0.2; each version's `osoe-stat` reading the other's
  jobs.
- **The kit's Slurm is right but for P9-2**, which one command in the
  development VM shows and one design choice fixes.

## What I could not check, and why

- **A kernel of 6.14 or later**, where P9-1's charge lives, or of 6.7 to 6.11:
  none here. P9-1 is arithmetic from measured ring sizes and the kernel's
  source; the Pi could confirm it in one command, and was the lead's tonight.
- **An x86-64 kernel**: none. Rosetta refuses io_uring, so the network path has
  run as x86-64 code nowhere; the x86-ordered aarch64 build stands in for its
  memory order, the models for its wakes.
- **Slurm across machines**: the development VM's Slurm has one node, so P9-2

- **Debian 12, Ubuntu 22.04 and 24.04, Fedora, Alpine**: their images would be
  downloads; reasoned from Rocky's older toolchain.
- **CMake 3.25 exactly, GCC 15 and 16, upstream Clang on Linux**: none
