## Verdict

**The protocols new since step 1 are correct as far as I could run and read
them, and the helpers' own are what their models say; nothing blocks the merge
point and nothing must change before the release.** What I found is one rule
whose reach is shorter than the design says - the one finding a user can meet
at the default settings - and evidence around the helpers that is weaker than
the record says, not a protocol that is wrong:

- **Q2-1 (after release)**: TCP keepalive on idle loopback wires ends healthy
  jobs of many logical nodes on an overloaded machine. At three helpers under
  six busy loops the suite's 64- and 70-node Träff tests died at 125 s with 91
  "stopped answering ... The host has vanished, or the network between is down"
  verdicts; at no helpers, with the keepalive budget shortened to 50 s, the same
  two tests died with 107 while the kernel counted 4,751 connections aborted on
  a timeout and the loopback dropped 64,170 packets in 52 s. Not the helpers'
  doing - they add load - and not a stopped node: the kernel's loopback itself
  drops the probes, which the design's "contention does not stop" does not
  foresee.
- **Q2-2 (after release)**: the ThreadSanitizer gate Part 3 relies on accepts
  any data race in a heap object, and the heap object holds the library's own
  collective region: 63 of the 95 reports it accepted at `232278a` are there,
  not in a program's heap, and a planted race of a lead's read of a Träff's row
  against a helper's landing - the defect a wrong credit would make - passes
  it, as does one between two application threads with no helper at all. A
  real race among the helpers' landings would hide in it.
- **Q2-3 (after release)**: the deadlock detector's helper half - a fresh
  answer, the helpers' activity, a pass after the request - can each be removed
  with every detector, helper and pacer test passing at two helpers under load
  (three mutants, 126 of 126 each). `deadlock_owners_model.py` alone holds
  them, and its breakages show each removal declaring a healthy job deadlocked.
- **Q2-4 (minor)**: the reduce-scatter grant's head - epoch, identity, bytes -
  is put by every one of a lead's ⌈log₂ N⌉ receivers into the same 16 bytes
  with plain stores, so with helpers two owners write it at once with nothing
  ordering them, through another process or otherwise. The bytes are identical,
  so nothing goes wrong on any hardware; it is a word two owners write, outside
  §3.16, and 39 of the gate's 95 accepted reports.

What convinced me, in brief (the full account below): all 46 model runs pass,
every breakage failing, and for the new models each for the reason it states;
the suite passed 855 of 855 at two helpers and 852 of 855 at three under six
busy loops, the three being Q2-1's two and a timeout I cannot explain, and the
pacer's `mix` ended clean at none, two and three; the helpers' request,
answer, stop and turns code is the model's, order for order; the planes are
refused on each other's wire before a byte lands; the failed-wire flush is
fixed on all three send paths; and the one hypothesis of mine the VM could
refute quickly - that a node's PEs could disagree on the shape setting
unrefused - it refuted.

## Findings, ranked

### Q2-1. Keepalive on idle loopback wires ends healthy jobs of many logical nodes on an overloaded machine (after release; high confidence; *measured*)

- **Location**: `shmem/src/wire.c:112-140` (keepalive on every node-pair
  socket, `60,10,6` by default, `bootstrap.h:79-81`), whose comment says, as
  the design notes' "As built: keepalive" does
  (`docs/design/shmem-ethernet-design.md:2439-2442`), that keepalive "asks
  whether the peer's *kernel* is there, which contention does not stop";
  `shmem/src/progress.c:874-877`, the `ETIMEDOUT` fatal; README `:1062-1063`
  and the `OSOE_KEEPALIVE` row (`:1401`); `osoe-launch`'s logical
  nodes, "up to 1,024 processes on 254 logical nodes" (README `:481-485`).
- **Failure scenario**: a job of tens of logical nodes on one machine has
  2N(N-1) sockets, most of them idle from start-up to the end - in a Träff's
  reduction or a tree, a lead talks to O(log N) others - so 60 s after
  start-up every idle socket begins its probes, all in the same few ticks, and
  each needs its peer's kernel's answer. On loopback the peer's kernel is the
  same kernel, and a probe and its answer go through the per-CPU backlog, 1,000
  packets (`net.core.netdev_max_backlog`), drained by a softirq that an
  overloaded machine defers to `ksoftirqd`, a thread among the rest
  (*recalled*: Linux's loopback path; the drops below are *measured*). Once the
  backlog overflows, probes and answers are dropped; six in a row on one socket
  and its kernel aborts it with `ETIMEDOUT`, and the job ends naming a vanished
  host. Nothing vanished, and no node was stopped.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `vm/logs/`):
  - At `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=3`, six busy loops, `ctest -j4` over tests 431 to
    645 (`h3-c.full`): `shmem.circulant.64` and `.70`, started together, died at
    125.0 and 125.1 s with 91 lines of "node X stopped answering: its kernel
    acknowledged neither keepalive probes nor retransmissions (§3.15). The host
    has vanished, or the network between is down", their PEs in
    `shmem_barrier_all` and the reductions; `shmem.circulant.16.tree` ran past
    its launcher's 240 s. At two helpers under the same load the three took
    101, 99 and 98 s, under the 120 s budget, and passed (`h2-c.full`). Each
    passes alone at three helpers, loaded or not (the reruns), and the two
    together at three helpers under six and then ten busy loops passed in 80 s
    (`pair-h3-a`, `pair-h3-b`) - short of the budget.
  - The mechanism, at no helpers, the budget shortened to 50 s
    (`OSOE_KEEPALIVE=20,5,6`), ten busy loops, `circulant.33`, `.64` and `.70`
    together (`pair-h0-ka50.full`): `.64` and `.70` failed at 52 s with 107
    "stopped answering" lines; the kernel's `TcpExt` counters moved
    `TCPAbortOnTimeout` 1,584 → 6,335 (4,751 connections aborted on a timeout,
    of the two jobs' 17,724 sockets, at the keepalive budget's length) and
    `TCPKeepAlive` 791,747 → 871,251, and `/proc/net/softnet_stat`'s drops
    213,219 → 305,284 over the pair runs, 64,170 of them in this one. During
    the passing run before it (`pair-h3-b`, 80 s) the backlog dropped 19,658
    packets while 27,040 probes went out.
  - Load is what makes the drops last: 64 idle logical nodes with no busy loops
    and the budget at 22 s (`OSOE_KEEPALIVE=10,2,6`, `shmem_test_sleeper`,
    `idle-n64-ka22.txt`) lived their 60 s, 58,562 probes sent and no abort.
- **Why it matters**: the design notes and `wire.c` present keepalive as
  unable to mistake contention for a vanished node, and the README promises
  only that a vanished node is found in one to two minutes; on a host running
  logical nodes - the suite's large tests, any `osoe-launch -m` job of tens of
  nodes - contention is exactly what drops the answers, and the verdict is wrong and
  misleading ("the network between is down"). Helpers raise the threads a
  node runs and so the load, which is how the suite at three helpers met it;
  Part 2's second wire doubled the idle sockets probing. A loopback socket
  gains nothing from keepalive: a logical node that dies has its sockets
  closed by the kernel it shares, and a stopped one is still answered. On a
  real cluster each node's kernel answers its own 2(N-1) sockets' probes and
  the NIC's queues are not the loopback backlog, so I do not expect it there
  (*recalled*; untested at scale).
- **Remedy options**: `osoe-launch` setting keepalive off (`OSOE_KEEPALIVE=0`)
  for loopback logical nodes, or the bootstrap doing so when every peer
  address is loopback; spreading the sockets' keepalive phases; or, at least,
  a sentence in the README's limits and the `wire.c` comment narrowed to
  separate machines.

### Q2-2. The ThreadSanitizer gate at a helper count accepts races in the library's own collective region, where a helper's real race would hide (after release; high confidence; *measured*)

- **Location**: `docs/design/evidence/v0.2-step4/2026-10-08-helpers/verification/tsan-gate.py:62-74`
  (a report passes if its location names a `.heap.` object and every address
  starts `0x0100`); `shmem/src/internal-region.h:1-12` and `node.c:1554-1566`
  (`OSOE_REGION_INTERNAL` is "the tail of every PE's heap mapping", the heap
  object's size the heap's plus the region's); README `:1243-1257`.
- **Failure scenario**: a helper lands a peer's piece in a lead's Träff row
  while the lead still reads the previous piece there - a credit counted one
  too early, say. Under ThreadSanitizer at two helpers the race is a report of
  the lead's read in `round_take` against the helper's `memcpy` in
  `osoe_am_parse`, at an address of the heap object `osoe.<...>.heap.<pe>`.
  The gate passes it as "a landing in a heap, ordered through another process"
  - and the launcher has ended the job's other ranks once one exited with
  TSan's 66, so the test's own checks of the result may never have run. The
  same holds for any race between two of the lead's own threads in that
  region: the gate never asks for a helper's frame.
- **Evidence**:
  - The gate's own run at `232278a`, re-read (*measured*,
    `tsan/verify-14-classified.txt`, from the lead's
    `<repo>/build/scratch/ladder/part3/verify-14/tsan-OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS_{2,3}.full`):
    of the 95 reports in the failing tests it accepted whose accesses the log
    shows (a 96th is a header interleaved with another process's report),
    **63 are in the internal region** - placed by the region's own layout
    functions for each test's settings (`tsan/layout.c`; the circulant tests
    run with `SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE=12m`, the lanes tests with 256 MiB): 39 on the
    reduce-scatter grant's head (Q2-4), 10 on the tree's grant words, and 14 of
    the lanes tests' team-number zeroing (`internode-generic.c:2855`, `:2860`)
    against a landing and on a small row's lane word. Only 32 are in a
    program's heap.
  - Two planted logs (*measured*, `tsan/planted.full`, `planted-2.full`):
    `round_take` (`internode-generic.c`) reading a Träff row against a helper's
    landing at `0x010000e88488`; and two application threads writing the same
    lane slot from `to_children`, no helper frame anywhere. `tsan-gate.py`
    prints `TSAN GATE PASSED` for each.
  - The project's own reason for not suppressing these reports by name, beside
    the multi-node maps' exclusion: "Suppressing it by name would hide real
    races in the parser" (`shmem/tests/CMakeLists.txt:1338`). The gate accepts
    the parser's `memcpy` against anything in a heap object, which is that
    suppression and wider.
- **Why it matters, and why not more**: the no-helper preset still gates, and
  it sees the lead's own threads against the agent through the handoff, so a
  race that does not need helpers is still caught there. What the gate cannot
  catch is the class helpers add - a landing a helper makes against the lead
  thread's reads of the collective's words, where with no helpers the one
  agent's single thread made it ordered. The models hold the protocols; the
  gate was meant to hold the C to them at a helper count, and does not. The
  README's account of what was accepted - "a put or an atomic a helper applies
  to a heap a local thread holds ... the ordering that makes it safe runs
  through another process" - is wrong for two thirds of it: most are the
  library's own words, and 39 have no ordering at all (Q2-4).
- **Remedy**: tell the internal region apart - by its offset in the heap
  object, `page_round(SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE)` on, which the gate can be given
  per test, or by the frames of both accesses - and pass a report there only
  against a short allowlist with a reason each (the zeroing at a number's take
  against a landing, ordered by the split's sync; the tree's grant words and
  the small rows' lane words, by the previous collective's end); require a
  helper's frame in every accepted report; with Q2-4's head written once,
  nothing else should remain. And correct the README's sentence.

### Q2-3. The deadlock detector's helper half has no test that fails without it (after release; high confidence; *measured*)

- **Location**: `shmem/src/progress-host.c:1315-1331` (`helpers_say`: an
  answer counts only if it names the request posted last; each helper's applied
  count added to the activity), `:1158-1166` (`helper_main` answers after the
  pass that follows its read of the request); `deadlock.c:258-310`.
- **Failure scenario**: each of the three is what keeps a sample from taking a
  helper's stale view for the node's: a stale "quiet", an activity that misses
  what a helper applied, or an answer from before a message the helper was
  about to take. The model's breakages (`helper_unseen`, `answer_unpassed`,
  `helper_unasked`) each end in "declared, but PE 2 is not blocked for good" or
  "declared, but a put_silent from node 1 to node 2 is in flight" - a healthy
  job ended (*measured*, `models/logs/deadlock_owners.log`).
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `vm/mutant.sh`, `vm/logs/mutant-*.full`): three
  mutants of the debug build - any yes answer accepted (`stale-answer`), the
  helpers' applied counts left out (`helper-activity-out`), the answer before
  the pass (`answer-unpassed`) - each passed all 126 tests matching
  `shmem\.deadlock\.|shmem\.helpers\.|tools\.pacer\.` at two helpers under six
  busy loops.
- **Why it matters, and why not more**: a false positive is the detector's one
  dangerous failure, and the helper half is new; but the windows these guard -
  every PE asleep for `OSOE_DEADLOCK_SECONDS` while a helper still holds
  unacknowledged or unapplied work - are too narrow for a black-box test to
  reach, which is why the model exists. Part 3's mutants (the record's table)
  covered the stop, the wakes, the unsent limit, the abort and the detector's
  leaving the helpers' threads out of a PE's (`helpers-counted`), not these
  three. Like the first review point's K2, worth a white-box test (a helper
  made to answer stale under a test setting, the sample then required to fail)
  or a sentence in the README's model paragraph saying the model alone holds
  it.

### Q2-4. The reduce-scatter grant's head is a word two owners write at once (minor; high confidence; *observed in source*, and in ThreadSanitizer's logs)

- **Location**: `shmem/src/internode-generic.c:1762-1793` (`circulant_grants`:
  each lead puts `{epoch, collective, bytes}` into the grant words of each of its
  `rows` senders, then adds to their grant counters, fenced),
  `:1604-1640` (`take_round_grants` reads and clears after the counter shows all
  `rows`), `:526-530`.
- **What happens**: a lead's ⌈log₂ N⌉ receivers each put the same 16-byte head
  into its grant words as they enter the reduction; with helpers their data
  wires are different owners' (owner-map.h), so two helpers `memcpy` the same
  bytes at once. With no helpers the one agent applied them one after another.
  Nothing orders the two puts - not even through another process: the receivers
  enter independently - so in C11 terms it is a data race on a plain protocol
  word written by two threads, which §3.16's rule ("every shared protocol word
  an `atomic_*_explicit`") and the brief's "any word two owners write" ask about.
  The reader waits for every add, each fenced after its puts, so it reads after
  every write; and the bytes are identical unless the calls differ, which
  `take_round_grants` reports by name either way. No hardware tears identical
  bytes into different ones, so nothing goes wrong (*recalled*).
- **Evidence**: the 39 reports at internal offsets `0x318288` (five to seven
  nodes, three rows) and `0x328308` (nine nodes, four rows) in the lead's
  verify-14 logs: line `rows + 2` of team number 0's circulant words, plus 8 -
  the grant counter's line, the head past it - each two `osoe-helper-N`
  threads' `osoe_am_parse` (*measured*, Q2-2's classification). The record
  read them otherwise: Part 3's adversarial read found that "no word has two
  plain writers, and every TSan race is a landing in a heap ordered through
  another process", and the verification places `circulant.5.capacity-1`'s
  reports "at one word of each node's heap, the slot that capacity reuses each
  round" (`docs/decisions/2026-10-08-helpers.md`). For five nodes at the
  default fan-out, teams and small bytes the circulant words run from internal
  offset `0x318000`, 896 bytes a team number, and the lanes - where the slots
  are - begin at `0x628000`; `0x318288` is team number 0's line 5, the grant
  counter's at three rows, plus 8 (*measured*, `tsan/layout.c`, the region's
  own inline functions).
- **Remedy**: one writer for the head - round 0's receiver alone puts it, the
  others their lane field and the add - or the head as atomic stores; or say
  in the record why identical plain bytes are accepted. Either way the gate's
  allowlist (Q2-2) should not have to carry it.

### Unexplained, not a finding

`shmem.circulant.16.tree` ran past its launcher's 240 s at three helpers in
the chunk where Q2-1's two tests died, its last word a PE in
`shmem_uint8_sum_reduce` after the launcher's kill, with no deadlock declared
and no connection judged. Everywhere else it passed: at two helpers, over the
same range of tests, in 98 s; alone at three in 45 s (three runs) and
unloaded; beside the same three large tests at three in 97 s; at none under
sixteen busy loops in 127 s; and in the whole range run again after midnight,
six busy loops, at three in 134 s and at two in 123 s, 215 of 215 each time
(*measured*, `h3-c-again.full`, `h2-c-again.full`). Its time follows the
load, from 45 s alone to 134 s in the range, and the failing run was
the VM's busiest hour (load average about 45, the other sessions' runs beside
mine, and 134 of Q2-1's processes dying around it at 125 s), so slowness is
the likelier reading; but nothing looked at the job while it ran, so stuck is
not ruled out, and it stays unexplained.

### Not findings

- **A node's PEs disagreeing on `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE` or `OSOE_LINK_*`**: I
  expected these to pass unrefused, since `node.c`'s own checks name the
  fan-out, the pipeline, the small bytes, the teams, the threads and the
  helpers but not them, and every PE reads them in `osoe_link_establish`. They
  are refused by name at start-up ("OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE differs between PEs:
  the local lead has link, this PE has fixed"; "OSOE_LINK_* differ between
  PEs"), every PE leaving and nothing left in `/dev/shm` (*measured*,
  `osoe-launch -e 1:...`, `vm/logs/mismatch-*.txt`).
- **The detector's second half re-posts its request at every look**
  (`helpers_say` posts after reading): a helper that cannot answer within one
  of the agent's turns, about a millisecond, sees its answer superseded, and the
  sample can run out its 5 ms patience (`deadlock.c:46`) and be retried. A
  delay, never a false declaration; and every positive deadlock test passed at
  two and three helpers under six busy loops (*observed in source*,
  *measured*).

## What convinced me

**The models, all of them, and their breakages.** All 42 `*_model.py`, with
`selftest.py`, `shape_timing.py check` and `grid-compare`, and the circulant
digests - 46 runs, 9,682 s of model time, three at a time on the Mac - exit 0,
and no case reports UNEXPECTED (*measured*, `models/logs/summary.txt`). One
first run failed for want of the Pi's evidence directory beside the copy
(`shape_timing.py` reads `docs/design/evidence/pi-cluster/2026-09-26`); with it,
it passes in 73 s, both broken variants failing. For the models new or changed
since step 1 I read each breakage's verdict against the check it was written
for: `helper_request`'s seven fail as stated - `request_first`, `no_bump` and
`deaf_unwatched` STUCK, `done_early` on RING, `stale_answer` on FRESH,
`snapshot_first` and `turns_plain` under TSO on FALSE DEAF - and each of its
three checks is reached by a breakage; `circulant`'s eight ("fails, as it
must": LANE, UP, SENDER, QUIET among them); `control_ack`'s ten, each "as
expected" on its own property; `deadlock_owners`' three, each a false
declaration; `abort_owners`' three (`no_wait`, `abrupt_p_ending_no_excuse`,
`abrupt_p_ending_reset_stream_unchecked`); `carrier`'s `two_wires`; `visit`'s
`zc_unmarked` and `zc_both_lent`, STUCK from two writes; `lead_wake`'s helper
cases holding and `wrong_owner`, `mark_wake_apart`, `lead_release` and
`no_recheck` at two helpers losing a wake; `deadlock`'s `control_wire` failing
and `control_wire_acked` holding.

**The helpers' words, in C as in the model.** The agent's `post` stores the
request seq_cst, then bumps and wakes the helper's word
(`progress-host.c:1010-1017`); the helper loads its word with acquire before
the request with seq_cst
(`:1138-1139`); the stop is acknowledged with release and the agent reads it
with acquire; the turns are stored seq_cst after each turn and read seq_cst
after the stop's store (`:1170`, `:1208-1214`) - the store-buffering pair the
model's `turns_plain` breaks. The answer names its request (`:1043`) and is
taken only for the request posted last (`:1323`). Every word has one writer, as
`am-state.h:36-50` says (*observed in source*).

**The planes kept apart, the failed wire left alone.** `check_plane` refuses a
control message on a data wire and the converse before `destination()` resolves
a byte (`progress.c:98-108`, `:150`). A failed wire is sent nothing more on all
three send paths: `flush` and `flush_control` test `w->error` since `232278a`,
and `flush_control_aborting` already did (*observed in source*). A helper's
fatal ends the lead's process at once, as the agent's does, its message naming
where the PE waited (`fatal.c:46-52`).

**The other owners' words.** Writers set a dirty bit in the owner's bitmap by
a seq_cst RMW (`connection-area.h:153-159`) and claim an owner's wake by CAS
on its flag, the owner raising its flag seq_cst before its second look
(`progress-host.c:358-381`, `:579-604`) - `lead_wake_model.py`'s protocol with
the owner a parameter. The unsent limit is one request a
session from the application thread (`transport-internode.c:278-285`), taken
by each owner for its own wires and said by the agent once all have
(`progress-host.c:459-516`). The detector leaves out each helper by the tid it
names before its first turn, and counts one not yet named as the program's,
which can only fail a sample (`deadlock.c:97-112`). Every remote atomic is
applied seq_cst (`rmw.h:46-57`), so a piece put before its fenced add is
visible to the lead that sees the add, whichever helper applied either
(*observed in source*). The one plain word of the library's I found two
owners writing is Q2-4's.

**The suite at helper counts, under load.** The debug suite in four chunks,
`ctest -j4`, six busy loops on the four vCPUs: at two helpers 855 of 855
(95 + 38 + 285 + 113 s); at three 852 of 855 (126 + 36 + 295 + 142 s), the
three being Q2-1's two and the unexplained timeout, each passing alone at
three helpers, at none, and unloaded, and the whole range passing again at
three and at two (*measured*, `vm/logs/h2-*.full`, `h3-*.full`,
`rerun-*.full`, `*-c-again.full`). With every send of 64 bytes or more lent to
the kernel (`OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY=on`, `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY_BYTES=64`), all 458
tests labelled `internode` passed at two helpers under six busy loops
(`zc-h2-*.full`), so the lent buffers hold with an owner a helper. And
`osoe-pacer mix`, eight PEs on four nodes for 150 s under the same six busy
loops, ended with `failures=0` and no deadlock line at no
helpers, at two and at three (158, 158 and 152 s), as did `mailbox` at two for
120 s (*measured*, `vm/logs/pacer-*.txt`): its periods' end agreed by the
max-reduction of `bc0751d`, no run hung at its deadline.

**The first review point's findings.** K1, K3 and K5 have the tests the
answers name (`shmem.agent-wake.L4`, `shmem.dead-pe.no-pidfd.*`, the one-node
