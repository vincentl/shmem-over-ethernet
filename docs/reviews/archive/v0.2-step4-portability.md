Review of `main` at `c897948`, run 2026-10-09 against
`docs/reviews/adversarial-review-prompt.md`, at v0.2's second review point, the
merge point of step 4 (progress helpers). It covers everything since the first
review point (`7053819`): step 2, the tree's shape chosen from the link; step 3,
Träff's circulant reduce-scatter and allgather; and step 4 - Part 0's one-thread
io_uring ladder (fill the room, the unsent limit, the 256 KiB buffer, provided

## Verdict

**Steps 2 to 4 ask of a kernel nothing the 6.7 floor did not already ask, and
x86-64 is no further away than at the first review point; nothing blocks and
nothing must change first.** Every io_uring facility the ladder, the owners and
the helpers use is Linux 6.0 to 6.2's - provided-buffer rings, multishot
receives, `SINGLE_ISSUER`, `IORING_OP_SEND_ZC` and its usage report - beneath
the 6.7 that the futex2 calls and `IORING_OP_FUTEX_WAIT` already set. The
library links liburing symbols of version 2.4 at most. `shmem_init` and the probe
check what has no fallback, the zero-copy send's opcode is refused by name when
asked for and missing, and "one ring an owner" follows the 6.7 kernel's
single-issuer rules to the letter. Both builds compile for x86-64 with no
warning. Their one-node suites under Rosetta pass everything the emulator can
run. And the first run of x86's memory order over steps 2 to 4's protocols is
clean: the x86-ordered aarch64 build passed the whole suite at no helpers, the
internode tests at two and three helpers, and the stress tester at both counts.
The single-node build still builds and passes on Rocky Linux 9, 400 of 400.

**One new finding is worth fixing before the zero-copy send's default is
decided (Part 4).** Each send by reference charges the user's locked-memory
limit (`ulimit -l`) for the rest of its buffer, until its notification comes. A
job that goes past the limit ends with "the connection to node N failed: Cannot
allocate memory", which names neither the setting nor the limit. It was
measured at the default 8 MiB: 16 logical nodes on the emulated Pi link. It was
also measured on a pair at a lower limit. The rest is minor.

What convinced me:

- **The kernel's floor, feature by feature** (*observed in source*,
  *documented*): the table under "What steps 2 to 4 ask of a kernel". Nothing
  new needs more than 6.2. The Linux source shows that "one ring an owner" is
  exactly what 6.7 allows a `SINGLE_ISSUER` ring. Every owner's ring is
  created disabled on the thread that makes the mesh, with its buffers
  registered, and each owner enables it on its own thread.
- **x86-64 under Rosetta** (*measured*): the single-node build 395 of 402 and the
  full build's one-node tests 391 of 397 with round three's futex2 shim. Every
  failure is the emulator's - `seccomp` refused, io_uring refused, `ldd` of
  another architecture - or this review's cross build. The library needs libc
  alone in the single-node build and exports the same 3,595 names in both builds.
- **x86's memory order on the new protocols** (*measured*): with round six's
  `x86tso.h`, a store-buffering litmus shows 418,421 of 2,000,000 rounds both-zero
  for a release store and a `seq_cst` load, where the default build shows 0. The
  library then holds 356 `ldapr` and no `ldar`. Results: 855 of 855 at no
  helpers, 458 of 458 internode tests at two helpers and at three, and
  `osoe-pacer` `mix` (2 minutes each at two and three helpers), `collectives`
  with the Pi's link set and `mailbox` all `failures=0`. The models of the
  owners' wake and the helpers' requests run under x86-TSO besides
  (*observed in source*).
- **Rocky Linux 9** (*measured*): the desktop section's commands, no warning,
  400 of 400 in 50 s. 16 of the single-node build's 64 library sources changed
  since step 1, and they build against Rocky's glibc 2.34 and GCC 11.
- **The step-1 round's portability fixes hold** (below), and `shmem.static-link`
  now passes in a cross build, where it failed at step 1.

Ranked:

- **P1, after release - before the zero-copy send's default changes**: the

## New findings

### P1. The zero-copy send charges the user's locked-memory limit, and a job past it ends naming a connection (after release, before the default changes; high)

- **Where**: `shmem/src/wire.c:301-309` sends a lent buffer with
  `io_uring_prep_send_zc(sqe, w->fd, buffer, n, ...)`, `n` the rest of what the
  caller offered. A short write - the rule under `TCP_NOTSENT_LOWAT` - offers
  the remainder again, and a wire may owe 16 notifications
  (`OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY_OWED_MOST`). `shmem/src/ring.c:253-260` takes any other
  send error as the wire's `error`, and `wire.c:270-271` ends the job: "the
  connection to node %u failed: %s". Neither `wire-settings.h:54-71`, the
  README's row (`README.md:1428`) nor the records name `RLIMIT_MEMLOCK`.
- **The kernel** (*documented*, Linux v6.7):
  - `io_uring/net.c`'s `io_send_zc`, for a buffer not registered, calls
    `io_notif_account_mem(zc->notif, zc->len)`. That is the length asked for,
    not the length taken.
  - `io_uring/notif.h` charges `(len >> PAGE_SHIFT) + 2` pages to `ctx->user`.
  - `io_uring/rsrc.c`'s `__io_account_mem` adds them to `user->locked_vm`
    against `rlimit(RLIMIT_MEMLOCK)`, and returns `-ENOMEM` past it.
  - `io_uring/io_uring.c` sets `ctx->user` unless the process has
    `CAP_IPC_LOCK`.
  - The pages are given back with the notification, which over TCP is the
    peer's acknowledgement. The charge is per user, so every process of the user
    on the host shares one limit.
  - The `+ 2` pages are 8 KiB a send with 4 KiB pages, 32 KiB on a Pi 5's
    16 KiB pages, and 128 KiB on a 64 KiB-page kernel.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `osoe-x86`, kernel 6.12, 4 KiB pages; `zc-stream`
  puts 1 MiB from every PE to every other, nonblocking, then quiets, 20 rounds;
  logical nodes of one PE; default buffers):

  | Link | Nodes | `ulimit -l` | Zero copy | Result |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | loopback | 2 | 64 KiB | off | status 0 |
  | loopback | 2 | 64 KiB | on | "the connection to node 1 failed: Cannot allocate memory; this PE was waiting in shmem_init" - where the link's measurement streams 1.9 MiB, at this setting by reference (by reading) |
  | loopback | 2 | 512 KiB | on | the same, in `shmem_put` |
  | loopback | 2 | 1 MiB, 2 MiB, 8 MiB | on | status 0 |
  | loopback | 16 | 2 MiB | on | "the connection to node 3 failed: Cannot allocate memory" |
  | loopback | 4, 8, 16 | 8 MiB | on | status 0 |
  | `tier3.sh pi` (1 Gbit/s, 100 µs each way) | 2 | 1 MiB | on | "... node 0 failed: Cannot allocate memory" |
  | the same | 2 | 2 MiB | on | status 0 |
  | the same | 8 | 4 MiB | on | fails the same way |
  | the same | 2 (on and off), 4 and 8 (on) | 8 MiB | as given | status 0 |
  | **the same** | **16** | **8 MiB, the default** | **on** | **"the connection to node 9 failed: Cannot allocate memory; this PE was waiting in shmem_put"** |
  | the same, PE 0 alone sending | 8, 16, 32 | 8 MiB | on | status 0 |

  The project's own `shmem.internode.zero-copy.0-1-2` passes at 256 KiB and
  fails at 64 KiB with the same message (*measured*).
- **What it means**:
  - Logical nodes on one machine - the suite's own topology, and what
    `osoe-launch -m` offers - share one user's charge. So do several jobs of one
    user on a shared node.
  - One sending lead stayed under 8 MiB with 31 peers on the emulated gigabit,
    so a gigabit cluster with one lead a node would probably not meet it at the
    default. That holds with 4 KiB pages; a Pi's 16 KiB pages charge more a
    send.
  - A faster link holds more bytes unacknowledged, and so more pages charged.
    The default limit is 8 MiB on Debian 13, Rocky 9.8 and the Pi
    (*measured*); a site or a container runtime may set less.
  - The connection is intact when this happens: the charge fails before a byte
    is sent. Ending the job is a choice the code makes, not a broken stream.
- **Fix**, any of:
  - Take `-ENOMEM` from a send by reference as a refusal to lend, offering the
    same bytes by copy - nothing went - and counting it for `osoe-stat`. Or at
    least name it: "the zero-copy send's pinned pages passed this user's
    locked-memory limit (`ulimit -l`, N KiB): raise it, or set
    `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY=off`".
  - Add a sentence to the README's row: each send by reference charges the rest
    of its buffer and two pages against `ulimit -l` until its notification, and
    every process of the user on a host shares the limit.
  - For Part 4, read `VmPin` in `/proc/<lead>/status`, where io_uring's charge
    shows, beside each fast-link run before choosing the default.
  - Have the probe print the limit and whether `IORING_OP_SEND_ZC` is there.

### P4. `init.c`'s descriptor comment predates the control wire (minor; high)

- **Where**: `shmem/src/init.c:292-298`: "a lead holds a socket per peer node and
  a pidfd per other local PE (liveness.h): at 256 PEs a node, 1,024 runs out
  short of 800 nodes".
- **Evidence**: since Part 2 a lead holds two sockets a peer node (README 989-993
  and 772 say so). At 256 PEs a node a soft limit of 1,024 runs out at about
  385 nodes (2 × 384 + 255 = 1,023). The limit is still raised to the hard limit
  first, and running out is loud. With the hard limit lowered to 32, a job of 16
  logical nodes ends "osoe: PE 3: cannot create a socket: Too many open files"
  (*measured*). 40 descriptors ran it at no helpers and at three.
- **Fix**: the comment's two numbers.

### P5. Notes

- **The 6.7 floor has never run.** The VMs run 6.12 and the Pi cluster now runs
  `6.18.50+rpt-rpi-2712`; every Pi node answered so (*measured*), and
  `docs/pi-cluster-results.md` records it. Nothing step 2 to 4 uses is newer
  than 6.2 (the table below). Untested are only the behaviour of the kernels
  between, and the fixes their stable series took in io_uring's futex
  operations (*recalled*). The README states the floor without saying it is
  reasoned rather than run. One clause would say which kernels have run.
- **The zero-copy refusal's bound is owner 0's at no helpers.** The README's
  "with it on, a job of more than 3,274 nodes is refused" comes from owner 0's
  ring, which with helpers holds no data wire and so owes no notification.
  Each helper's ring is checked for its own share, at about 3,637 peers a helper
  (`wire.c:47-60`, `:259-263`, `owner-map.h:53-91`; *observed in source*, not
  run).
- **A cross build's nested check finds the host's liburing.** In the x86-64
  full build, `shmem.strict-c11` failed with "/usr/lib/aarch64-linux-gnu/liburing.so:
  ... file in wrong format", because its nested configure ran without the
  `PKG_CONFIG_LIBDIR` the outer one had. With it set when `ctest` runs, it passes
  in 36 s (*measured*). Cross builds are not a promised use, but the same class
  as step 1's Q2.
- **`OSOE_CACHELINE` is 64 on everything but aarch64** (`base/cacheline.h`). On
  x86-64 the build is clean and its static assertions hold (*measured*). Intel
  cores fetch lines in pairs, so two owners' adjacent 64-byte lines can still
  interfere. POWER's lines are 128 bytes. Both cases cost performance, not
  correctness: everything a peer node addresses uses the fixed
  `OSOE_INTERNAL_LINE_BYTES` of 128, so that nodes of two architectures agree
  (`internal-region.h:117-142`).
- **The per-owner and per-peer sizes the README's scaling table gives** ("256
  bytes an owner", "128 bytes a peer node") are aarch64's lines. On x86-64 a
  line is half that (by reading; not measured).
- **The libraries' imports** (*measured*): liburing symbols of version 2.4 at
  most (`io_uring_setup_buf_ring`, `io_uring_free_buf_ring`,
  `io_uring_enable_rings`), so the README's "liburing2, 2.6 or newer" for a
  node that only runs jobs is conservative - the inline helpers need 2.5's
  headers at build time (*recalled*: `io_uring_prep_futex_wait` arrived in 2.5).
  Built on Debian 13 the x86-64 library needs glibc 2.38, for
  `__isoc23_sscanf` - which `_GNU_SOURCE` selects from glibc 2.38 on
  (*recalled*). Built on Rocky, it needs glibc 2.34. A library is only portable to the glibc it was
  built against or newer. That matters only to someone who copies a build
  between distributions.

**One ring an owner, against the 6.7 kernel** (*documented*):

- `io_uring_create` sets a single-issuer ring's submitter at creation only
  without `R_DISABLED`.
- `io_register_enable_rings` sets it to the enabling task when unset.
- `__io_uring_register` refuses any task but the submitter once it is set.

**The code keeps to all three** (*observed in source*):

- The thread that makes the mesh creates every owner's ring disabled,
  registers its buffers and probes it while no submitter is set
  (`wire.c:44-74`, `:259-263`).
- Each owner enables its own ring on its own thread - a helper as its first act
  (`progress-host.c:1128`) - and closes it there (`close_connections`,
  `progress-host.c:948-982`).
- An owner that aborts before it has enabled its ring enables it first, on its
  own thread (`abort_wires`, `progress-host.c:1058-1072`). The agent aborts
  owner 0's (`:1349-1358`), each helper its own (`:1086-1091`).

Nothing of this differs between 6.7 and 6.12 in the source read.

**The probe** (*measured*, 6.12): `PROBE PASSED (0 failures)` - the futex2 calls,

## Helpers on topologies unlike the Pi and the VM

- **Placement** (*observed in source*):
  - A helper is created by the agent (`progress-host.c:1181-1193`), so it starts
    with the agent's signal mask and CPUs.
  - It then calls `osoe_agent_place_this_thread` itself (`:1120`): the short
    slice, and with `OSOE_AGENT_CPUS=node` the union of the node's tasks' CPUs,
    which never leaves the job's allocation (`agent.c:142-156`).
  - The CPU words hold 1,024 CPUs, glibc's `CPU_SETSIZE`. Where the kernel's
    mask is wider, `sched_getaffinity` with a fixed set fails and `node` quietly
    does nothing. That is a 1,025-CPU machine, which no target has.
- **Slurm's binding, on the Pi** (*measured*): one task of `srun -N4
  --ntasks-per-node=1` has one CPU (`nproc` 1, affinity `0`), under
  `TaskPlugin=task/cgroup,task/affinity` and `CR_Core_Memory`. At the default
  `OSOE_AGENT_CPUS=task`, the lead's main thread, its agent and every helper
  share that core. The README's helper row says exactly that, and that `node`
  lets them spread; the Launching limits advise `--cpu-bind=none`
  (*observed in source*). Helpers are off by default, so a bound job loses
  nothing it had.
- **Many cores, SMT, NUMA** (by reading):
  - Nothing in the helpers assumes a core count. 0 to 64 helpers;
    one above the peers is idle with its ring and 1 MiB of buffers.
  - A helper's receive buffers are allocated where the rings are made, on the
    thread that makes the mesh, but first written by the kernel in the helper's
    own context, so they land on the helper's NUMA node. Nothing pins a helper to a socket; `node` may move it across one.
  - SMT changes nothing the code knows of.
  - None of this ran: no machine here has more than four cores or a second
    socket.
- **The wake paths a helper adds** (*observed in source*):
  - A helper sleeps in an `IORING_OP_FUTEX_WAIT` on its wake word in the
    connection area, a shared mapping. Writers wake it with a shared futex
    wake (`nudge`, `wake_owner`). The agent waits for a helper's
    acknowledgement on a private word.
  - Both calls are the full build's futex2, from the same 6.7 floor.
  - The writer's mark and the owner's flag are `seq_cst` on both sides, the
    store-load pattern x86 needs fenced (`progress-host.c:358-381`, `:579-598`;
    `connection-area.h:140-160`).
- **Descriptors**: P4. The control wire uses the same listener and port range as
  the data wire (listener handshake version 4), so a firewall sees no new port
  (*observed in source*).

## Settings and their refusals

The settings steps 2 to 4 added are `OSOE_LINK_HOP_NS`, `_MESSAGE_NS` and
`_BYTES_PER_SECOND`, `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE`, `OSOE_SMALL_COLLECTIVE_BYTES`,
`OSOE_WIRE_UNSENT_BYTES`, `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY` and `_ZERO_COPY_BYTES`,
`OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS`, and two test settings. Each has an `.invalid` or

nodes, which suits nodes of different sizes. What no setting can refuse is a
limit of the system: P1's memlock and P4's descriptors. The second is named by
its cause at least.

## What is sound, and what convinced me

- **The kernel floor is exact for what steps 2 to 4 use**, and the checks match
  it: `shmem_init` and the probe cover every facility without a fallback, and
  the one optional facility, the zero-copy send, is refused by name when it is
  missing.
- **"One ring an owner" is what the 6.7 kernel's single-issuer rules allow**:
  read in the source, and run at two and three helpers.
- **x86-64**: both builds compile clean. The one-node suites pass under Rosetta
  but for the emulator's refusals. The exports are unchanged. x86's memory order
  ran over every new protocol in the suite and the stress tester, at no helpers,
  two and three. The protocol most exposed to x86's one reordering, the owners'
  sleep flag, is `seq_cst` on both sides and model-checked under x86-TSO.
- **The single-node build is still the desktop build**: Rocky Linux 9's
  section as printed, 400 of 400; libc alone; every new setting checked as a
  value.
- **The previous round's fixes hold**, and one of them, the configured compiler
  in the nested checks, now shows in a cross build.
- **The helpers' placement under Slurm is what the README says**, measured on
  the Pi's binding.

## What I could not check, and why

- **An x86-64 kernel**: no x86-64 machine. Rosetta runs x86-64 user code on
  this aarch64 kernel and refuses io_uring, so the full build's network path has
  never run as x86-64 code. Known, and the author's to rent.
- **Kernels 6.7 to 6.11, or any but 6.12, 6.18 and Rocky's 5.14**: no such
  kernel here, and fetching one was out of scope.
- **The zero-copy send's charge on a fast link**: only loopback and netem's
  emulated gigabit ran. How far below 8 MiB a lead's own peers stay at 10 Gbit/s
  and up is Part 4's to measure.
- **Many cores, SMT, NUMA, more than one socket**: no such machine. These are
  reasoned from the source.
- **GCC 15 and 16, Clang**: not installed in the VMs. Installing them, or the
  container images step 1's round used for the other userlands, means
  downloading packages, which this review did not do. GCC 11.5 (Rocky) and 14.2
  (Debian, both targets) ran with no warning.
- **The models' memory and time at `-j2` on 4 GiB** with the 40 models (P2):
  not run. The lead's verification ran them one at a time.
- **64 KiB pages; ppc64le, s390x, riscv64; a job mixing architectures**: no
  hardware or toolchain.
- **A lost wake under x86's order** (P3): my mutant did not show one in 210,000
  rounds, so the runs cannot tell such a defect from its absence. The models are
  the evidence there.
