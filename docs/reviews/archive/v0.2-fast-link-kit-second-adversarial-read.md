**4. After a killed job the kit can wait without bound** (*observed in source*; `InactiveLimit`'s
default from the 24.11.5 `slurm.conf` manual; what `slurmctld` does without it, *recalled*)

- **Where:** `docs/fast-link/common.sh:88-94`; `slurm-setup.sh:43-64`, which sets no
  `InactiveLimit`.
- **The `--gc` step has no timeout.** After status 124 or 137, `launch` runs
  `srun -N"$NODE_COUNT" --ntasks-per-node=1 osoe-stat --gc`. That `srun` waits until it is
  allocated every node. `srun`'s manual: by default "the command will block until resources become
  available".
- **On 137, the job is likely left allocated.**
  - Status 137 means `srun` had not gone 30 s after `SIGTERM`, and was killed.
  - `InactiveLimit` is the setting that ends the allocation of a command that "abnormally
    terminates". Its default is unlimited, and `slurm-setup.sh` sets none.
  - So the job that `srun` had allocated is left running (*recalled*: with no limit, `slurmctld`
    does not even ping `srun`).
  - That job is `--exclusive`: the `--gc` step then waits on it for ever, and every later job until
    its own `KIT_TIMEOUT`.
- **The same follows a node gone down or drained,** a "Kill task failed" after a kill among the
  causes. Every job pends until `KIT_TIMEOUT` and ends 124, and the `--gc` step after it pends for
  ever.
- **Why it matters.** It is finding 5's stall, moved: a long sweep left running waits on the clock
  with nobody watching.
- **Fix:**
  - Put `timeout 60` around the `--gc` step, or give it `--immediate=60`.
  - Name `launch`'s jobs (`-J fast-link`), and after a 137 run `scancel -n fast-link` before the
    `--gc` step.
  - Before each job, stop the section if `sinfo -h -t down,drain,fail -o %N` names a node.
  - Or set `InactiveLimit` in `slurm.conf`, to 300 say.

**5. `floor.sh`'s `end_after` ends the shell that waits on the servers, not the servers**
(*measured* under bash 3.2; that bash 5.2 does the same, *recalled*)

- **Where:** `docs/fast-link/floor.sh:35-40`, `:44-49`, `:62-63` and `:70-76`; `common.sh:104-112`.
- **What.**
  - `on ... &` runs `on` in a subshell, and `$!` is that subshell.
  - In the subshell, `timeout` is a child, in a process group of its own.
  - `end_after`'s `kill` ends the subshell alone. `timeout`, `srun` and the servers go on,
    reparented, until `timeout`'s own `KIT_TIMEOUT`: 300 s from their start, in `floor.sh`.
- **Measured.** `floor.sh`'s osoe-launch branch, with stand-ins, one client failing before its
  `QUIT`:
  - `end_after` returned after 30 s, and the loop went on;
  - `floor.sh` ended at 68 s;
  - still running then, reparented to process 1: `timeout -k 30 90 bash echo-servers.sh`, its
    `bash`, and the stranded server.
- **Why it matters.**
  - Every wait is now bounded, so finding 5's stall is gone. But "servers whose client never came
    are ended" (the runbook's §4, `:209-210`, and the commit) is not what happens.
  - A stale server keeps its port. The next count's server for that port cannot listen ("Address
    already in use"), and the stale one serves its client. iperf3's `-s -1` behaves the same.
  - A stale `on` job at `floor.sh`'s end holds a core of a node. The next section's first
    `--exclusive` jobs then pend until their own `KIT_TIMEOUT` (120 s in `helpers.sh`) and fail.
- **Fix:** signal the subshell's child. In `end_after`, `pkill -TERM -P "$1"` before (or instead of)
  `kill "$1"`. `timeout` forwards the signal to `srun`, and `srun` to the step.

**7. The new per-peer bytes cannot show what finding 1 was about** (*measured* in the model;
*observed in source*)

- **Where:**
  - `multistream.c:9-17`, `:137-144`, `:243`, `:262-265` and `:291-299`;
  - `summarize.py:156-160` and `:234-236`;
  - the runbook's §5, `:244-248`.
- **What finding 1 was about.** Time: how many of a node's peers carry traffic at once.
- **What the new line counts.** Each peer's bytes over the whole window; `summarize.py` prints the
  least peer's share of the most's. Every PE goes round its whole list many times in ten seconds,
  so the bytes come out even whatever the order.
- **In the model** (Appendix), node 0's least peer over its most:
  - at 400 blocks a PE: the old, convoying order 0.884 to 0.917; the new 0.990 to 0.993;
  - at 2,000 blocks a PE: the old 0.977; the new 0.998.
- **On the cluster.**
  - At the link's 23 Gbit/s and 8 PEs a node, ten seconds is about 3,400 blocks of 1 MiB a PE,
    over 60 passes of the list. A convoy would print about 0.98.
  - At 32 PEs a node it is about 860 blocks a PE, under four passes of 224. The old order would show
    some unevenness, but from where the passes stop, not from the convoy.
- **Why it matters.**
  - The model is the only evidence that the new order spreads a node's traffic over its peers, and
    the first read called it a caricature. On a NIC, connection buffers and TCP may couple the PEs
    in ways it does not.
  - The commit says the line puts the spread "in the output rather than assumed". It puts evenness
    there, and the convoy had that too.
- **Fix:** measure concurrency directly.
  - Each PE marks, in a bitmask a millisecond slot of the window, the node it was putting to or
    getting from.
  - The node's PEs OR their masks together: an OR reduction over `SHMEM_TEAM_SHARED`, or atomic ORs
    into the first PE's symmetric array.
  - PE 0 prints each node's mean count of peers busy a slot, and `summarize.py` prints it where it
    prints the ratio now. About twenty lines.

### Notes

**8. The credits' "about 512 KiB" is the convoy's number** (*observed in source*; the count
derived)

- **Where:** `gets.sh:7-10`; the runbook's §8, `:315-318`.
- **What a credit bounds.** A nonblocking get takes a reply credit for each piece
  (`transport-internode.c:1276`). So a connection's 64 credits bound its pieces in flight.
- **Where 512 KiB came from.** The first read assumed a node's 8 PEs on one connection:
  8 × 512 KiB ÷ 64 KiB = 64.
- **Under the new order.**
  - A PE's 8 gets between quiets go to 7 different nodes, and its pieces in flight are bounded by its
    zone, the zone ÷ 64 KiB.
  - Spread over 7 connections, a connection carries about 8/7 of one PE's pieces. The 64 credits
    are then reached at a zone of about 3.5 MiB (56 pieces), not 512 KiB.
  - So at 1 MiB the zone holds, not the credits; at 4 MiB both do; at 16 MiB the credits.
- **Fix:** say so, or drop the number.

**9. Inside an allocation, `floor.sh`'s steps would wait on each other** (*observed in source*:
`srun`'s 24.11.5 manual)

- **What the manual says.** Inside `salloc` or `sbatch`, each `srun` is a step, and by default "job
  steps will not share CPUs, but job steps will be allocated all CPUs available to the job".
- **What `floor.sh` would then do.** The clients' steps wait for the servers' steps on the same
  node, and the servers wait for their clients. Each count stalls until `on`'s timeout (300 s),
  and then its clients find no server.
- **Outside an allocation** - the runbook runs every script from the first node's shell - each
  `srun` is a job of its own, and `on`'s one-core jobs share a node (*recalled*: by cores, under
  `CR_Core` and the partition's default `OverSubscribe`).
- **Fix:** say "outside any allocation" in §1, or give `on`'s `srun` `--overlap`.

**10. `--exclusive` with `--overcommit`** (*observed in source*: `srun`'s 24.11.5 manual;
`slurmctld`'s check, *recalled*)

- **The comment's three claims,** at `common.sh:10-13`:
  - whole nodes: `--exclusive`, "the job allocation can not share nodes ... with other running
    jobs";
  - no binding: `--cpu-bind=none`, "Do not bind tasks to CPUs";
  - more tasks than CPUs: `--overcommit` "applies to job and step allocations" and "can be used to
    launch more than one task per CPU".
- **What the manual does not say.** I recall that `--overcommit` drops `slurmctld`'s check of tasks
  times CPUs a task against a node's CPUs. The 24.11.5 text I could read does not say how it
  combines with `--exclusive`. §3's new 32-PE job checks it before the sweep: the right place.
- **The step `srun` makes in its own allocation.** Whether it takes `--exclusive`'s step meaning
  (`--exact`) I do not know. With `task/affinity`, no cgroup plugin and `--cpu-bind=none`, it
  would change no CPU a task may use.

**11. `slurm-setup.sh` reads the munge key on the first node while writing it there** (*observed in

## What is sound

**`multistream.c`'s new start** (`:171-217`), checked against the C rather than its comment.
- **The fcollects.**
  - osoe declares `shmem_int_fcollect(shmem_team_t team, int *dest, const int *source,
    size_t nelems)` (`shmem.h:851-866`): 1.5's and 1.6's signature.
  - Source and destination are statics, symmetric under `oshcc`, which `build.sh` compiles the
    probes with.
  - `firsts` and `ranks` hold 4,096, and `npes` is checked against that.
- **The node table.**
  - `SHMEM_TEAM_SHARED`'s rank is the PE's local id, and its PE 0 is local id 0
    (`team.c:270-310`). So `firsts[p] == p` picks exactly one PE a node, and a node's index is the
    order of those PEs.
  - That one order indexes everything:
    - `node_of` and `node_size`, from `node_first`, ascending;
    - `to_node` and `peer_bytes[mine][n]`, by `node_of`;
    - PE 0's print loop, which counts the nonzero `node_pes` over first PEs ascending: the same
      sequence.
  - `peer_bytes` is used only at 64 nodes or fewer, so `mine` and `n` stay in range.
- **The lists, rebuilt in Python from the C's steps:**
  - a permutation of the other nodes' PEs, at 8 × 8, 8 × 16, 8 × 32, 5 × 8, 5 × 32 and 6 × 7;
  - consecutive blocks on different nodes, the wrap included, at every node count above 2;
  - in lockstep, every peer node in use, and no two of a node's PEs on one target PE;
  - identical to the first read's "interleaved" lists.
  - Uneven nodes (3, 2, 2 and 4, 1, 3) find every target, and at two nodes each PE takes the other
    node's PEs from its own rank on.
- **The dump** now comes before the barrier, so PE 0's window starts with the others' (note 14).
- **The five probes** compile clean against osoe's header.

## Appendix: the lists and the model

`multistream.c`'s lists at `d3e4ebf` (`:185-217`), from the C's own steps. `firsts` and `ranks` are
what its fcollects gather; for the block layout of N nodes of P PEs, `firsts[p] = (p // P) * P` and
`ranks[p] = p % P`.

```python
def c_targets(firsts, ranks, me):
    npes = len(firsts)
    node_first = [p for p in range(npes) if firsts[p] == p]
    nodes_count = len(node_first)
    node_of, node_size = [None] * npes, [0] * npes
    for p in range(npes):
        for n in range(nodes_count):
            if node_first[n] == firsts[p]:
                node_of[p] = n
                node_size[n] += 1
    largest, mine, my_rank = max(node_size[:nodes_count]), node_of[me], ranks[me]
    targets = []
    if nodes_count > 1:
        for i in range((nodes_count - 1) * largest):
            n = (mine + 1 + (my_rank + i) % (nodes_count - 1)) % nodes_count
            rank = (my_rank + i // (nodes_count - 1)) % node_size[n]
            targets.append(next(p for p in range(npes) if node_of[p] == n and ranks[p] == rank))
    return targets, node_of, nodes_count
```

- the order "c": each PE's list from `c_targets`, as node numbers;
- node 0's blocks to each peer, summed as the run goes, with the least over the most reported;
- the mean count of distinct helpers among node 0's busy peers, at H = 2 and 4, peer slot s (node
  order without node 0) being helper 1 + s mod H's.
