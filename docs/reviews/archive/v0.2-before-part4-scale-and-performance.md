## Verdict

**In this question's area the tree can go to 0.2 once Part 4 has run: nothing
blocks, the scaling table is the code's, and every number the README and the
release note quote traces to a record and its raw output (one raw output lies
outside the tree, S10). But the kit Part 4
will run has three defects in its measurement design, each cheap to fix and
each able to mislead the decision the tag waits for - the progress helpers'
default - and they should be fixed before a node is rented.**

What holds (*measured* or *observed in source*): every memory row and settings
cost I could compute matches the code - the internal region 15.63, 16.04 and
16.32 MiB at 4, 64 and 500 nodes with the grant fields in, a team number 24.25
and 27.0 KiB, a thread place 896 bytes of the control segment on aarch64 and 768
on x86-64 with its 128-byte context block, and the control segment's per-PE sum
exactly the 293,760 bytes the blocking gets' record measured. Nothing added
since round eight grows with the job's PE count. The blocking get is the
nonblocking get's time, as claimed: at the tip, alternated with the two trees
before it in a loaded VM, a 1 MiB blocking get took 683 µs at the median
against its nonblocking get's 771, where both earlier trees' took 1,930 to
2,070. The helpers' percentages, the barrier's and the fetch-add's times behind
a stream, Träff's and the shape's ratios (by round eight's trace; S9 on the
trees they were taken on) and start-up's 27 ms each trace to their raw files.
No line of `osoe-bench` or the floor test moved between round eight's tree and
the tip beyond the loaded runs' noise.

What should change, in order:

1. **The kit's "peers at once" does not measure concurrency** (S1): it counts the
   peers whose blocks returned in the same millisecond. At five logical nodes of
   eight PEs it read 1.6 to 1.7 peers of four for 1 MiB puts and 1.1 to 1.2 for
   1 MiB gets, where the PEs were inside calls toward 2.9 to 3.1 and 3.2 to 3.4 -
   the model's own number for that layout, 2.7 to 3.0. On the cluster it would
   show the first read's fatal convoy where there is none, and it rises with the
   rate a helper count buys.
2. **Every job of the sweeps measures its own link and sets its own unsent
   limit, unrecorded** (S2): sixteen starts in a few minutes set limits from 165
   to 810 KB. The helper table would mix that draw with the helper count's
   effect, and nothing in its outputs could separate them.
3. **The helpers' default would be decided without the collectives** (S3), where
   on the Pi helpers cost a broadcast and an alltoall 2 to 35%: the kit times
   them once at each count, unalternated, which a decision in force says cannot
   be compared run against run.
4. **Round eight's S1 is still half open, in a new place** (S4):
   `shmem.helper-wake` cannot see a spin before the owner raises its sleeping
   flag. A mutant that spins there 200 µs passed both runs five times of five -
   every owner woken 100 times in 100 puts - while `osoe-bench` took 23 to 38
   times the CPU. The decisions index says the test catches "a spin before" an
   idle wait; it catches one after the flag.

The rest are minor (S5 to S11).

## Findings, ranked

### S1. `multistream`'s "peers at once" counts the peers whose blocks returned within a millisecond, not the peers its PEs send to at once - *fix before release*

- **Location**: `docs/fast-link/multistream.c:17-23` (the comment: "how many
  peer nodes its PEs start blocks toward at once ... for the millisecond the
  block starts in"), `:248-259` (one OR a block, into the slot of `now()` read
  after the call has returned), `:285-294` (the mean over slots with any bit);
  `summarize.py:157-160`, `:246-247`; `docs/fast-link.md:237-241` (§5: "in a
  model, about four of seven at 8 PEs a node, five at 16, six at 32, where the
  first order of the kit's had two or fewer"). It is the kit's second read's
  finding 7, whose fix asked that each PE mark, in each millisecond's word, "the
  node it was putting to or getting from" (*observed in source*, *documented*).
- **What it counts.** A block sets one bit, in the millisecond its call
  returned, and a node's figure is the mean count of bits over the milliseconds
  with any. A 1 MiB put returns once its last part is in a connection buffer,
  after much of it has gone, so a block that lasts several milliseconds marks
  one of them, and the figure is bounded by the blocks a node finishes in a
  busy millisecond. A 64 KiB put returns in microseconds, so one PE marks several
  peers in one millisecond and the figure is a union over its consecutive
  blocks. Either way it is set by how long a block lasts against the
  millisecond - by the rate - and not by how many peers carry traffic at once,
  which is the model's quantity (the second read's Appendix: distinct peers with
  a block in progress, time-averaged).
- **Scenario**: §5 on eight nodes at 8, 16 and 32 PEs a node. For 1 MiB puts
  the figure stays near the blocks a node finishes in a busy millisecond - two
  or three at 23 Gbit/s - where the model says four, five and six of seven. Read
  against the model as §5 invites, it says the convoy the first read found fatal
  is back at 1 MiB and that four helpers are starved; a session is spent
  repeating it, or the four-helper row is discounted. And since it rises with
  the rate, a helper count that raises the rate raises its "peers at once" too,
  which reads as the cause.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `out-probes/`): `multistream-interval.c` is the
  kit's `multistream` with eight lines added - each call also ORs its peer's bit
  into every millisecond from the call's start to its return, printed as
  `peers_in_calls` beside the kit's `peers_at_once`. Five logical nodes, so four
  peers a node, four-second windows, two rounds; each cell the range over the
  five nodes of both rounds:

  | PEs a node | Workload | `peers_at_once` | `peers_in_calls` | A node's MiB/s |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 8 | put, 1 MiB | 1.61 to 1.74 | 2.87 to 3.06 | 742 to 852 |
  | 8 | get, 1 MiB | 1.13 to 1.16 | 3.17 to 3.36 | 352 to 387 |
  | 8 | put, 64 KiB | 3.53 to 3.59 | 3.33 to 3.55 | 865 to 955 |
  | 2 | put, 1 MiB | 1.71 to 1.98 | 1.85 to 2.07 | 587 to 752 |
  | 2 | get, 1 MiB | 1.05 to 1.09 | 1.84 to 1.97 | 322 to 341 |
  | 2 | put, 64 KiB | 3.70 to 3.80 | 2.87 to 3.15 | 713 to 871 |

  From two PEs a node to eight the kit's figure for 1 MiB puts does not rise
  (1.7 to 2.0, then 1.6 to 1.7), while the PEs' calls go from two peers to
  three - and three is what the
  model gives this layout: 2.67 to 3.00 at five nodes of 8 PEs (the second
  read's table, "5 × 8, peers of 4", the order as built). The kit's own dry run
  shows the rate dependence
  (`docs/design/evidence/v0.2-step4/2026-10-10-fast-link-dry-run/after-reads/helpers-summary.txt`,
  five nodes of two PEs): 1 MiB puts read 2.33 at no helpers, 1.87 at two and
  1.74 at four, as a node's rate fell from 1,885 to 1,526 and 1,485 MiB/s; 64
  KiB puts read 3.89 to 3.94 of four peers at every count, with two PEs a node.
- **Fix**: mark every slot from the call's start to its return, as the variant
  does (the cost is a few atomic ORs a block, on the node's own first PE), and
  name the figure for what it is: the peers a node's PEs were inside a call
  toward, a millisecond at a time. Check it on the VM against the model before
  renting, as above. For blocks shorter than a millisecond any per-millisecond
  union counts several consecutive blocks; §5 should say so, or take the 1 MiB
  rows as the ones that answer finding 1.
- **Confidence**: high.

### S2. Each job of the sweeps measures its own link and takes its own unsent limit, recorded nowhere: on the VM sixteen starts set limits from 165 KB to 810 KB - *fix before release*

- **Location**: `docs/fast-link/helpers.sh:25-33`, `zero-copy.sh:36-41`,
  `gets.sh:20-25`, `fan-in.sh:23-27` - no `SHMEM_DEBUG`, no `OSOE_LINK_*`, no
  `OSOE_WIRE_UNSENT_BYTES` - against `ladder.sh:41`, which runs every case under
  `SHMEM_DEBUG` for this reason; `shmem/src/link-measure.c:21-33` (the
  bandwidth the slope of a 512 KiB stream over a 128 KiB one, three tries each,
  to the root's first child), `init.c:446-448`, `wire-settings.c:90-102` (a data
  wire's limit the bandwidth times 128 µs, at least 16 KiB); the README's
  `OSOE_WIRE_UNSENT_BYTES` row (*observed in source*).
- **Scenario**: §5 compares helper counts by a node's rate. Each of its 144 jobs
  sets every data wire's unsent limit from its own start's measurement, so each
  row's three rounds carry three draws of the limit. §6 exists to find whether
  the limit matters on the fast link (the runbook: "its rule's 128 µs of
  turnaround is a guess no one has timed on a fast link"), and it runs in the
  session after §5. If it does matter, the helper table's spread is the helper
  count's effect and the limit's draw together, and its outputs say nothing of
  the second. And the draw need not be independent of the helper count: the
  bandwidth's stream goes through the data wire's owner, a helper from H = 1, and
  the message cost's fan through several owners at once.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `out-link/link-helpers.txt`): five logical nodes of
  two PEs, the kit's `startup` probe under `SHMEM_DEBUG`, `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS`
  0, 1, 2 and 4 in turn, four rounds, PE 0's link line and node 0's limit line:
  the bandwidth 1.29 to 6.33 GB/s, the unsent limit 164,929 to 810,167 bytes,
  the message cost 5.9 to 31.7 µs, the hop 23.6 to 50.2 µs. The four starts at
  no helpers measured 1.29 to 4.66 GB/s (median 2.4), those at one, two and four
  helpers 2.1 to 6.3 (medians 4.9, 3.6, 3.3) - too few starts to call a helper
  effect, enough to see a fivefold spread within minutes. Round eight saw 0.18
  to 4.09 GB/s under load and 4.37 to 7.56 on a quiet VM. What a cloud NIC's
  starts spread over is not known, and nothing §5 keeps would show it.
- **Severity**: fix before release - one line a script.
- **Fix**: `SHMEM_DEBUG=1` on every job, and `summarize.py` reading node 0's
  "limits the unsent bytes" line and PE 0's link line into each row, at least;
  better, hold them still where they are not the question - for §5, §7, §8 and
  §9 the three `OSOE_LINK_*` set from one measured start's `SHMEM_DEBUG` line at
  the session's beginning (which also holds every job's collective shapes, which
  the hop and the message cost choose), or `OSOE_WIRE_UNSENT_BYTES` at the
  rule's value from it - so that the helper count, the zero-copy setting or the
  zone is the only thing a row varies, and §6 alone varies the limit.
- **Confidence**: high on the mechanism and the VM's spread; medium on how much
  the limit moves a NIC's rate, which is §6's question.

### S3. The helpers' default would be decided from streams and the mailbox alone; the collectives, where helpers cost the Pi most, run once a count in §10, unalternated - *fix before release*

- **Location**: `docs/fast-link.md` §5 (`:219-264`: the workloads are three
  streams and the mailbox) and §10 (`:347-364`), the "Not measured by the kit"
  paragraph (`:389-392`); `docs/fast-link/scale.sh:18-25` (`osoe-bench` once at
  each count of `HELPERS`, in that order, no rounds); README:1180-1186 and the
  `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS` row; `docs/decisions/README.md:166` ("A Pi comparison
  of small collectives pools its runs, or binds them: under `--cpu-bind=none`
  the barrier and the 8-byte reduce land in one of two modes a job, about 25 µs
  apart") (*documented*, *observed in source*).
- **Scenario**: §5 finds that two helpers lift a node's stream toward its share
  of the link, and the default becomes two. On the Pi two helpers made a 64 KiB
  broadcast and a 4 KiB alltoall 2 to 9% slower unbound and 6 to 35% bound
  (README:1180-1186). The kit's only collective numbers at a helper count are
  §10's: one `osoe-bench` at no helpers and then one at the chosen count, under
  `--cpu-bind=none` - one run a side, in a fixed order, under the binding the
  decisions index says lands small collectives in one of two modes a job. A
  default chosen so can slow every collective-bound program without the run
  that would have shown it.
- **Severity**: fix before release: add `osoe-bench barrier collectives` (or
  `osoe-pacer collectives`, which times its cases) to §5's `WORKLOADS`, run with
  the helper counts in their rotation and rounds - 36 jobs of about ten seconds
  at three PE counts - and say in §5 that the default weighs them beside the
  streams.
- **Confidence**: high that the kit cannot tell; medium that a fast link's
  collectives would move with helpers as the Pi's did.

### S4. `shmem.helper-wake` cannot see a spin before the owner raises its sleeping flag: such a mutant passes both runs, every wake counted, at 23 to 38 times the CPU - *after release*; the decisions index's sentence *minor*

- **Location**: `shmem/tests/test-helper-wake.c:13-37` and its wait for the flag
  before each put (`:125`, `:195-200`); `shmem/src/progress-host.c:1204` (a helper's
  `raise_flag_and_look`) and `:1525` (the agent's);
  `docs/decisions/2026-10-10-helper-wake.md` ("What it tells from a sleeper is a
  spin longer than a round trip"), whose mutants, like round eight's
  (`.../2026-10-10-helper-wake/helper-wake-mutants/mutate.py`,
  `mutate-agent.py`), spin after the flag, just before the ring's wait;
  `docs/decisions/README.md:42` ("`shmem.helper-wake`'s two runs fail on a spin
  before a progress helper's or the lead's agent's idle wait longer than an
  acknowledgement's round trip"); `docs/release-checklist.md:319-321` ("the
  helpers' and, since 2026-10-10, the agent's idle waits are" guarded)
  (*observed in source*, *documented*).
- **Scenario**: the "spin a little for latency" change round eight's S1 named,
  placed where it is usually placed: after a pass that moved nothing, poll the
  ring's completions and the dirty bits for a while, and only then raise
  `sleeping` and wait. Spinning before the flag is the natural place, since a
  raised flag makes every writer pay a futex wake. The test waits for the flag
  before each put, the flag rises only once the spin is over, and the put then
  finds a sleeper, which its wake completes and counts. Every count is perfect.
  The README's own sentence is exact - it catches "an owner that spins after
  raising its flag" (README:1339-1342) - but the index and the checklist say the
  idle waits are guarded.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `out-preflag/`): `preflag-mutant.py` makes a helper,
  and the lead's agent, poll their ring's completions, wake word and dirty bits
  for up to 200 µs after a pass that moved nothing and before
  `raise_flag_and_look`, going round on any work; built release, no warning.
  `shmem.helper-wake.0-1-2-3` and `.agent.0-1-2-3`: five runs of five each
  passing, as the tip's; in the verbose run every wire's owner woken 100 times
  in 100 puts, PE 0 claiming 100 of 100, every other owner 0. What it costs,
  `osoe-bench barrier collectives` at four logical nodes of two PEs, the tip and
  the mutant alternating, two rounds: at no helpers the mutant's job 33.6 to
  34.6 s and 51.5 to 51.6 s of user CPU against 8.2 to 10.8 and 2.1 to 2.2; at
  two helpers 46.6 to 49.4 and 78.0 to 78.4 against 8.8 to 12.8 and 2.1 to 2.2.
  (Four vCPUs shared by eight PEs and their owners, so the spin takes the PEs'
  CPU; on a machine with idle cores round eight's agent mutant halved the
  barrier's time instead, which a benchmark rewards.)
- **Severity**: after release - the code does not spin, and this is the guard;
  the index's line and the checklist's item should say "after it raises its
  flag" before the tag (*minor*).
- **Fix**: count what a spin costs rather than what it skips: the owner's thread
  CPU across the hundred rounds from `/proc/<pid>/task/<tid>/schedstat` (a 200 µs
  spin a round is 20 ms a wire), or the time from a round's acknowledgement to
  the flag's rise; and run the mutant above against it.
- **Confidence**: high.

### S5. Smaller things in the kit - *minor*

- **`gets.sh:8-10`** still says the credits may hold "a zone above about 512
  KiB" at 8 PEs a node: the convoy's number, which the runbook's §8 corrected to
  "only from a few MiB" after the second read's note 8.
- **§8's one-PE probe is held by the credits from a 4 MiB zone**: a nonblocking
  piece takes one of its connection's 64 credits until it lands
  (`transport-internode.c`, `issue_get_pieces`), so one PE has at most 64 pieces
  of 64 KiB, 4 MiB, in flight, and `get-pieces` at the 16 MiB zone measures the
  credits (*observed in source*). The runbook says the credits bind the stream
  "from a few MiB", not that they bind the probe; a flat line from 4 to 16 MiB
  would read as the zone ceasing to matter.
- **§5's command passes `out/helpers` alone** (`fast-link.md:223`), and
  `summarize.py` prints each directory apart (`:299-304`), so "the bare
  aggregate ... which is what a node's rate is set beside" is in another run's
  section - the second read's finding 3 asked for `out/floor` beside it.
- **§5's statistics** are the median and range of three rounds, with no rule
  stated in advance for "raises the rate" and no pairing by round. Printing each
  round's rate at a count over the same round's at no helpers would cancel the
  drift between rounds that the rotation spreads.
- **§7's decision variable is the named threads' CPU**, the kernel's softirq work
  left out (said in §5, as the first read's note 14 asked). Part of what a send
  by reference saves or costs - the pages it pins, freed as the peer
  acknowledges - is done there (*recalled*); `/proc/stat`'s busy time over the
  window on each node would bound the whole.

### S7. The rings' locked memory on Linux 6.14 and later is stated, not sized - *minor*

- **Location**: README:1034-1038 ("io_uring also charges a node's rings to its
  user's locked memory ... a ring the limit refuses ends the job at start-up");
  `base/ring-check.c:12-68` and `transport-internode.c:239` - every local PE of a
  job of more than one node makes and closes a check ring, a provided-buffer
  ring with it, at start-up; `ring.c:84-97` (*observed in source*).
- **What it comes to** (*inferred*, from those sizes and the kernel's page
  rounding, *recalled*): the lead's ring at no helpers is about 200 KiB at 500
  nodes and 2.6 MiB at 4,097, well inside 8 MiB; but each PE's check takes about
  three pages, charged to the one count of the user's processes at the same
  moment as its node-mates' - 3 MiB at 256 PEs with 4 KiB pages, 12 MiB with
  16 KiB pages, 48 MiB with 64 KiB pages. With large pages and many PEs a node,
  the checks alone can meet the default limit, and the job is refused at
  start-up for want of memory that the README's sentence does not lead anyone
  to expect.
- **Not checked**: no 6.14 kernel was at hand (the VM runs 6.12, which charges
  no rings; the Pi was not mine). A sentence with the sizes, and with what a
  node of many PEs on large pages should set.
- **Confidence**: medium (arithmetic; the overlap of the checks unmeasured).

### S8. The zero-copy send's refusals are a scale question Part 4 cannot meet - *minor*

- **Location**: the README's `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY` row; `docs/fast-link/slurm-setup.sh:78-84`
  (memlock unlimited); `fast-link.md` §7 (*documented*, *observed in source*).
- **What**: a connection lends one 256 KiB buffer at a time, charged its length
  and two pages until notified, against one count for the user's processes on
  the host. At the default 8 MiB about thirty connections can lend at once; a
  node of a few hundred peers in an alltoall would meet refusals on most of
  them, each connection then copying for the rest of the job. Part 4's eight
  nodes lend at most seven buffers (1.8 MiB) and run with no limit, so a default
  of "on" chosen there says nothing of a large job at the default limit. If
  Part 4 turns it on, the row should say what a job of many nodes needs.
- **Confidence**: high on the arithmetic (*inferred*); the refusal's handling is
  the record's and is sound.

### S9. The release note's collective ratios are steps 2 and 3's trees' - *minor*

- **Location**: `docs/release-notes/0.2.0.md:36-44` (1.43 to 1.47, 1.20 to
  1.26, 1.32 to 1.41); their records `2026-10-07-shape-data-path.md` and
  `2026-10-07-circulant-data-path.md` (*documented*).
- **What**: measured at v0.2 step 2's and step 3's merge points. Step 4 then
  changed the engine under them, and the grant fields (`4a557b1`) changed
  Träff's grant - one put a receiver where there were two, and a check a field -
  without a timing anywhere. The merge point's Pi run kept the 64 KiB lines
  within 1.6% (round eight); no 1 MiB run since. The note marks its checks "at

### S11. One large blocking get now shares the zone with the PE's other threads - *noted, the README's sentence holds*

- **What**: a blocking get of more than a piece now holds up to the whole zone
  (four pieces), and the thread that issues it usually reclaims each run it
  frees in `make_room` before a woken waiter runs
  (`transport-internode.c:839-880`); the README says another thread's get

  the Limits; on a fast link the zone against the round trip's bytes is §8's,

  failure: safe.
- `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS` 0: the Pi's measurements support it; safe.
- `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY` off: loopback 60% of the copy's stream, the Pi no gain;

## What I could not check, and why

- **Quiet-VM timings**: the host carried two other VMs' heavy runs throughout
  (above); the loopback figures the README quotes from quiet runs were traced,
  not reproduced, and every timing here is a within-batch comparison.
- **A fast link, an x86-64 kernel, Linux 6.14 and later**: none at hand. S7's
  sizes are arithmetic, and whether S2's limit moves a NIC's rate is §6's.
- **Hundreds of nodes or 256 PEs a node**: the memory claims rest on the code's
  own size functions and structure sizes; nothing was built past five logical
  nodes here.
- **The Pi**: held by the lead's rehearsal; its numbers are its records'.
