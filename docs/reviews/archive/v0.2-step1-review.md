## Verdict

**The step passes its merge point after the named changes - each made in the
fixes that follow this review (`docs/decisions/2026-10-06-single-node-review-fixes.md`);
in the procedure's terms, release after named changes. Nothing blocks.** All
five say, each in its own area, that the single-node build can be offered to
desktop users as the README describes it and that the full build is still what
0.1.0 was: four as the tree stood, readiness after one sentence. The two builds
are one library for a job of one node - 58 of the 59 objects they share
disassemble alike, the 59th differing in a string's length; the same output
from a specification-derived probe, from SOS and from 160 settings; the same
timings within a loaded host's noise. The classic futex keeps futex2's contract
but for the timer slack; the refusal is by name, before anything is made, under
every launch path tried; the single-node build passed its four presets on six
userlands and two architectures, and the new question's answer is yes: an
x86-64 build's one-node jobs run under Rosetta on the classic futex.

What the review found is of three kinds: a view the README described as each
team's barrier in progress, which on one node - every job of the single-node
build - walks nothing; tests that the single-node build lacked, or that could
not fail - the one-node half of the global exit and the deadlock detector, the
pid fallback below Linux 5.3, the agent's one wake, a spin before a sleep, the
one-node quiet's fence on x86-64; and words grown stale with the step - the
x86-64 passages, Rocky's obstacle, the directory READMEs, the version of `main`.
And one defect, older than the step, which the lead confirmed from the scale
investigator's note: a launcher whose output's reader had gone died of SIGPIPE
before it removed a refused start's control segment.

  one node - the same headers, exports and control segment, the same output
  from a specification-derived probe at seven PE counts and two thread levels
  and from SOS, the same answer to 160 settings; the refusing backend's 25
  unreachable entry points never reached in about 1,500 single-node runs; the
  refusal by name, early and clean, its test failing without the check; the
  classic futex keeping futex2's contract; the full build identical to 0.1.0
  under the same probes on one node and six maps of logical nodes.
- **Correctness** (*measured*): 58 of the two builds' 59 shared library
  objects disassemble alike, `init.o` differing in one string's length; the
  full build against the tag differs in 14 objects, each by a change the
  step's records describe; nine of eleven breakages caught - all seven of the

  K2 and K3; every entry point of the refusing backend guarded, the job ended

## Refuted, or corrected

Nothing rated *fix before release* was refuted. Narrowed by the refutation,
which held Q5-1's facts, scope and severity:

- **"The README sent every hang there"**: its hang bullet names `osoe-stat JOB`
  first, and it already sent a reader who sees "nothing open" to the PE lines;
  what was false on one node was its description of the summary - "each team's
  barrier or sync in progress", "whose epochs each node's progress thread
  keeps" - which the fix scopes to the walk between nodes.
- **"Its last column names the PE blocked longest, not the culprit"**: by
  design the summary never names a culprit PE in any job; its walk ends at a
  node, whose PE lines say which. On one node the walk's one hop is trivial,
  which is why the summary now says so and points at them.

Not findings, recorded:

- **Scale**: in 8 of 38 paired 32-PE runs the single-node build was 1.2 to 2.1
  times slower, always while the host was loaded, in wall time only - its CPU
  time matched, and a single-node build given the futex2 calls did the same, so
  not the classic futex. A quiet machine would settle it; the shared host never
  was one.
- **Correctness**: a failed start's tombstone left by one of eight loaded runs
  and none of seven more. No test of the suite pipes the launcher into a reader
  that exits early, the one way found to leave one.
- **Duplicates**: portability Q6 and Q7 are readiness Q5-2 and Q5-3, and the
  conformance investigator's note on the desktop example is Q5-2's.

Found by the lead on the way: the scale investigator's note for readiness,
unconfirmed there - a refused start piped into `grep -m1` "can leave its
control segment" - holds, and its cause is the launcher: killed by SIGPIPE at
its first line, it never reached its removal of leftovers; and a PE's fatal
path, killed by the same signal, left with 141 rather than the refusal's
status. And portability Q2's fix, once `shmem.strict-c11` compiled with Clang,
found Clang's one warning in the tree: a test's ticket read unwritten had a
credit take wrongly succeeded (`test-reply.c`).
