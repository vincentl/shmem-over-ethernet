# v0.2 before the tag, scale and performance: does osoe scale, and perform, as the README says?

Round ten of the adversarial review (T13), question 3 only, run on 2026-10-30
against `docs/reviews/adversarial-review-prompt.md` (its axes, ground rules and
finding fields) and the round's common brief. It covers what changed since
round nine (`3b45472`) to `main` at `5cefc7fb`: the progress helpers' default
`auto` (P36), the data wires on demand and the mesh schedule (H23), the landing
zone's per-node budget (P32), team blocks after P7, layouts 42 and 43, the
window of 8 (P37), the turnover wake's room count (P2), the two-node choice
(P4), the collectives' check on by default (P39), Träff's past 512 nodes (P3,
A13), and the three sessions on eight `c4-standard-16` nodes
(`docs/fast-link-results.md`). Round nine's report in this area
(`v0.2-before-part4-scale-and-performance.md`, S1 to S11) is held as claims.
Labels: *documented*, *observed in source*, *measured*, *recalled*,
*inferred*. Severity: *blocks release*, *fix before release*, *after
release*, *minor*.

## Verdict

**In this area the tree can be tagged: nothing blocks. The scaling table is the
code's at hundreds of nodes of 256 PEs - nothing added since round nine grows
with the job's PEs, or with nodes faster than the table says - and every
fast-link number the README and the release note quote that I traced holds to
its record and raw output. One claim should be fixed first: the README's and
the design's numbers for Träff's past 512 nodes are the cost model's at the
window of 4, not at the default of 8 since 2026-10-28, and overstate its gain
there by up to 2.4 times by the model's own simulation, while the model test
that guards that claim still runs at the old window (S1).**

What holds (*measured* or *observed in source*): the internal region 25.30,
26.17 and 26.46 MiB at 4, 64 and 500 nodes, the lane 1,153 KiB, a team number
24.6 to 29.6 KiB, 28.7, 33.1 and 42.1 MiB at 1,024, 4,096 and 65,536 nodes;
the control segment's structures (a team block 1,024 and 640 bytes, 128 bytes a
peer, 256 an owner) and its 5.2, 18.9 and 72.8 MiB; the io_uring ceilings of
12,288 and 16,384 nodes on demand; the mesh schedule's model and tier-3
numbers; P2's room count, recomputed from its raw lines; the defaults' records
read as the README reads them. The defaults are defensible: P36, P37, P2, P4
and P39 each rest on a record that says what it measured and where it did not.

What should change, in order:

1. **Träff's past 512 nodes at the default window** (S1, *fix before release*):
   "3.2 to 4.3 times faster ... and its event simulation 2.7 to 2.9 times" is
   capacity 4's; at 8 the closed form gives 2.05 to 2.47 and the simulation
   1.19 at 513 nodes and 1.18 at 1,024. `check_circulant_wide`, run at the
   default, would fail its own bound; and at 4 MiB on 513 nodes the chooser
   takes Träff's where the simulation's best tree is 10% faster.
2. **The collectives' check's cost at 256 PEs a node is mostly a start-up
   cost** (S2, *minor*): the barrier's 672 µs to 975 is osoe-bench's short
   `quick` run catching each PE's first comparison, P - 1 minor faults the first
   time a PE arrives last at a sync. In H12's shape in the VM the check cost
   `quick`'s barrier 28 to 109% and the full run's nothing.
3. **The helpers' default on the Pi's own shape, and the tag's Pi runs** (S3,
   *after release*, with a recommendation for T10 now).
4. **Documentation drift** in numbers and decision lines (S4, *minor*).

## Findings, ranked

### S1. Träff's gain past 512 nodes is quoted at the window of 4, where the default has been 8 since 2026-10-28; at 8 it is 1.2 times by the model's simulation, not 2.7 to 2.9, and the model's own check of it still runs at 4 - *fix before release*

- **Location**: README.md:856-860 ("past 512 nodes there is no measurement of
  it, only the cost model, which puts a 1 MiB reduction 3.2 to 4.3 times faster
  by Träff's than by the best tree from 513 to 4,096 nodes, and its event
  simulation 2.7 to 2.9 times at 513 and 1,024"); design §5.8,
  `docs/design/shmem-ethernet-design.md:6464-6468`, the same numbers, written by
  P3 on 2026-10-24 (`a4c497b0`) when the capacity was 4;
  `tools/modelcheck/shape_timing.py:1049-1072` (`check_circulant_wide`, the
  chooser asked at capacity 4, `:1059`, and the trees simulated at a window of
  4, `:1064`; its docstring the same 3.2 and 2.9) and `:881-898`
  (`check_choices`, every simulation and choice at a window of 4); the default
  capacity 8 (`bootstrap.h:206`) and the chooser's window always the capacity
  (`collective-shape.c:255`, `shape.window = capacity`) (*observed in
  source*, *documented*).
- **What changed under the sentence.** P37 doubled every lane and every tree's
  window. The record of that change regenerated the grids ("2,376 and 2,340
  rows added, none changed", `docs/decisions/2026-10-28.md`) and updated the
  sizes beside these numbers ("a row of 64 KiB ... at the capacity of 4 it was
  until 2026-10-28"), but not the ratios, which a window of 8 changes most: it
  halves a deep tree's pace where its window binds, and Träff's, bound by its
  bytes on the wire on the Pi's link, not at all.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, by the tree's own model on the Mac; the Pi's
  fitted link, a hop of 86 µs, 7 µs a message, 116 MB/s, pieces of 16 KiB; the
  best tree over fan-outs 2 to 8; Träff's window the capacity times the lane's
  rows over its rounds, as the C has it):

  | 1 MiB reduction | Closed form, capacity 4 | Closed form, capacity 8 | Simulation, window 4 | Simulation, window 8 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 513 nodes: best tree, Träff's, ratio | 64.5, 20.2 ms, 3.20 | 41.3, 20.2 ms, 2.05 | 50.8 (k = 5), 17.7 ms, 2.87 | 22.3 (k = 4), 18.7 ms, **1.19** |
  | 1,024 nodes | 72.2, 20.2 ms, 3.58 | 41.7, 20.2 ms, 2.07 | 48.0 (k = 4), 17.7 ms, 2.72 | 21.6 (k = 4), 18.3 ms, **1.18** |
  | 2,048 / 4,096 nodes | 3.95 / 4.26 | 2.27 / 2.47 | - | - |

  So the README's four numbers are reproduced exactly at capacity 4, and none
  holds at the default. Below 513 nodes the same: at 256 nodes the simulation's
  gain is 3.02 at a window of 4 and 1.53 at 8; the README's other model claim,
  "about 1.7 times on seven nodes and more", holds at both windows (1.74 at
  seven nodes by the closed form). `check_circulant_wide` asserts the
  simulation's best tree at least twice Träff's time on 513 nodes; at the
  default its own computation gives 22,265 µs against 18,712, so moved to the
  default capacity it fails. And at 4 MiB on 513 nodes, at the default, the
  simulation runs a tree of four children in 64.9 ms and Träff's in 71.2 ms
  while the chooser takes Träff's (`traff-choice-w8.py`): the closed form
  charges its tree of three children on 513 nodes 41.3 ms at 1 MiB where the
  simulation runs that tree in 23.5 (and one of four in 22.3) - 1.76 times,
  where `check_circulant_wide`'s own docstring puts the closed form's trees at
  "about 1.3 times the simulation's at this size", true at a window of 4 (64.5
  against 50.8 ms) - and the choice between the tree and Träff's is where that
  errs; its choice among
  the trees is not - `check_choices` at a window of 8 holds its bounds on its
  grid to 31 nodes (p90 0.027, worst 0.169 against the sender links' 0.08 and
  0.20; `choices-w8.py`), and at 64 to 513 nodes it costs 0 to 12%
  (`choices-wide.py`).
- **Scenario**: a reader sizing a job of a thousand nodes takes Träff's at
  three to four times the best tree's speed for a megabyte, as the README
  says; at the shipped default the model itself says 1.2 times - and since
  H21 measured the simulation's Träff's 12 to 22% optimistic from 256 KiB on
  the Pi (`docs/pi-cluster-results.md`, "H21"), the gain past 512 nodes on a
  Pi-like link may be nil (*inferred*). Meanwhile the model's test of the
  claim passes because it tests the window the job no longer has.
- **Severity**: fix before release for the README's and design's numbers and
  the model checks' capacity (both cheap: the numbers from `shape_timing.py`
  at capacity 8, `check_circulant_wide` and `check_choices` at the default, the
  former's bound restated); after release for the chooser's choice at 4 MiB
  past 512 nodes, a model's 10% at a scale nothing has measured.
- **Fix**: say the closed form's 2.05 to 2.47 and the simulation's 1.18 to 1.19
  at the default, with the capacity of 4's numbers as history if wanted; run
  both checks at `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PIPELINE_DEFAULT` (and at 4 beside it); note
  in the chooser's header that its tree cost at a window of 8 runs high by up
  to half at hundreds of nodes.
- **Confidence**: high (the model's own code, run as its tests run it).

### S2. The collectives' check's cost at 256 PEs a node, "the barrier's 672 µs to 975", is mostly each PE's first comparison faulting in its node-mates' pages, which osoe-bench's short `quick` run catches; the steady cost is a few per cent - *minor*

- **Location**: README.md:880-883, :1344-1347 and :1947 (the
  `OSOE_CHECK_COLLECTIVES` row: "it cost nothing one round could see but a
  barrier's at 256, 672 µs to 975"); design
  `docs/design/shmem-ethernet-design.md:5644-5648`;
  `docs/fast-link-results.md:458-461`; `docs/decisions/2026-10-28.md:202-210`
  (P39's question). The mechanism: `barrier.c:62-66` - every member says its
  call, and "the last to arrive, whichever member it is" compares;
  `collectives.c:343-372`, which reads each node-mate's words in that
  node-mate's own heap object (`member_copy`, `collectives.c:89-97`); a data
  collective's comparer is the node's lead (`collectives.h:62-74`). The run:
  `docs/fast-link/many-pes.sh:62-69`, `osoe-bench quick barrier collectives`,
  200 trips after 200 of warm-up (`osoe-bench.c:66`, `:92`) (*observed in
  source*, *documented*).
- **What it is.** A page of another process's heap object costs the reader a
  minor fault the first time it reads it. At a sync the comparer is whichever
  PE arrives last, so over a run every PE becomes it in turn, and each, the
  first time, faults in the page of each of its P - 1 node-mates' regions that
  holds the team's said call - a few hundred microseconds at 256 PEs a node, on
  the barrier's critical path. A reduction's comparer is always the lead, whose
  faults fall in the warm-up: which is why in H12 only the barrier moved. For
  every PE of a node to have been last once takes about P ln P barriers - some
  270 at 64 PEs a node, 1,400 at 256 - so `quick`'s 200 warm-up trips absorb it
  from 16 to 64 PEs a node and not at 256, and with two nodes a trip is slow if
  either node's comparer is new.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, the VM, release build of `5cefc7fb`, `check-cost.c`:
  each PE's minor faults over a loop of barriers, and PE 0's median over
  windows of the loop's trips; `osoe-bench` as the kit runs it):

  | Run | Check off | Check on |
  |---|---|---|
  | one node of 64 PEs, 4,000 barriers, four runs: minor faults a PE | 7.0 | 68.1 to 69.0 |
  | the same, median after 1,000 trips, µs | 139.7 to 144.4 | 141.7 to 150.4 |
  | one node of 256 PEs, 2,000 barriers, four runs: minor faults a PE (most) | 3.0 (6) | 214 to 241 (258) |
  | the same, median of trips 0 to 50 / 200 to 400 / 1,000 on, µs | 578-665 / 561-649 / 577-610 | 670-1,155 / 651-831 / 609-650 |
  | H12's shape, two nodes of 256: `osoe-bench quick barrier`, two runs, µs | 2,000, 1,967 | 4,103, 2,552 |
  | the same, `osoe-bench barrier` (2,000 trips after 200) | 2,299 | 2,096 |
  | the same, the probe: faults a PE; median of trips 0-50, 200-400, 1,000 on | 6.0; 2,316, 1,998, 1,978 | 258.5; 2,866, 2,396, 2,044 |

  The faults a PE with the check on are its P - 1 node-mates' pages, at most
  258 at 256 PEs where 3 is the run's own; the cost falls along the run as the
  PEs that have not yet compared run out, to 3% after 1,000 trips in H12's
  shape; and the project's own tool, in the kit's mode, shows the check costing
  a barrier 28 to 109% where its full run shows nothing. On one node of 256 the
  tool's `quick` and full runs both showed 4 to 8%, the two-node product of new
  comparers being what lifts `quick`'s median.
- **Scenario**: a user at 256 PEs a node reads that the default check costs a
  barrier 45% and turns it off - losing the check of divergent collectives,
  whose only cost after the first thousand-odd barriers is a few per cent; and
  the one cost that is real and unstated, a fault storm on each PE's first
  comparison (and again for a team whose said words lie on a new page), is
  what a program of few barriers pays.
- **Severity**: minor: the default is right, the more so; the sentence states
  its cost wrongly in kind.
- **Fix**: say the check's steady cost - a few per cent of a barrier at 256 PEs
  a node in the VM - and its start-up cost: P - 1 minor faults a PE the first
  time it ends a sync's arrival on a team; H12's 672 to 975 µs is `quick`'s 200
  trips. A kit that times a default at many PEs a node warms for P ln P trips
  or reports the windows.
- **Confidence**: high on the mechanism and the VM's numbers; medium on how
  much of H12's 303 µs it accounts for on a `c4-standard-16`, which I could not
  run.

### S3. The helpers' default takes two on the Pi's own shape, which the Pi measured a loss for broadcasts and alltoalls, and the tag's Pi runs would change three defaults at once against the release note's Pi numbers - *after release*, with a recommendation for T10 now

- **Location**: README.md:1446-1452 ("the rule counts PEs against CPUs, not
  spare cores, so the Pi's four nodes of four PEs take two by default, where
  the measurement above found them a loss for the broadcast and the
  alltoall"); the release note's helpers paragraph and its Pi ratios
  (`docs/release-notes/0.2.0.md:47-64`, taken at no helpers, a window of 4 and
  every writer woken); `docs/decisions/2026-10-28.md`, question 2, whose
  recommendation reads "Where the helpers buy nothing they cost little"; T10 in
  `docs/prompts/v0.2-open-items.md` (*documented*).
- **The challenge.** The decision weighed H26's "one CPU a PE" (two helpers
  costing the 4 KiB alltoall 15%, the 8-byte ones 5 to 6%) and not the Pi's own
  bound numbers, which the README quotes beside it: a 64 KiB broadcast and a
  4 KiB alltoall 6 to 35% slower under Slurm's default binding, 2 to 9% with
  `--cpu-bind=none` - the shape `auto` gives helpers on, four PEs on four
  cores. The README is honest about it, so this is an accepted trade-off
  challenged, not a discovery: on the project's reference cluster the default
  is a configuration it measured slower for two collectives, and option (b)
  of that question, counting the affinity, would not have changed it under
  `--cpu-bind=none` either. A rule of fewer PEs than CPUs would have, at the
  price of H4's 16 PEs on 16 vCPUs, where two helpers bought the streams 36%.
- **For the tag now** (*inferred*): T9 to T11 will run at the new defaults -
  two helpers, a window of 8, the room count's wake - where the release note's
  1.43 to 1.47, 1.20 to 1.26 and 1.32 to 1.41 were taken at none, 4 and every
  writer woken. A T10 run at the defaults alone cannot say which of the three
  moved a line. Run the 1 MiB lines at `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=0` beside the
  default, and say in the note which the numbers are.
- **Confidence**: high on the facts; the rule's merit is the author's call.

  place for every peer and checks `/dev/shm` for the buffers of the wires a node

- **The mesh's numbers** trace to `docs/decisions/2026-10-19.md`'s tables: the

**The defaults** (*documented*, *measured* where said):

- **P36, two helpers where a node's PEs fit its CPUs**: the README's H4 and
  H26 numbers are the results file's and its summaries' (the streams 67 to 80%
  of egress to 97 to 99%; at a core a PE the streams a fifth faster and the
  mailbox 925,000 to 456,000; at one CPU a PE 98,000 to 123,000 and the 4 KiB
  alltoall 1,297 to 1,489). Each helper is a thread, a ring sized for its share
  of the wires and 1 MiB of receive buffers: O(1) a node. Safe; its merit on
  the Pi's shape is S3.
- **P37, the window of 8**: the broadcasts' 17 to 27% from 256 KiB are
  1,148 to 949, 4,377 to 3,358 and 16,917 to 12,374 µs in
  `out/window-summary.txt`, five rounds; consistent with step 2's Pi
  measurement, a 1 MiB reduction at k = 2 in 19.4 ms at 8 against 23.0 at 4
  (`docs/decisions/2026-10-06-tree-shape.md:377`); and the chooser's choice of
  fan-out at 8 meets the suite's regret bounds (S1). Its price, every lane
  doubled, is in the table: 19.1 MiB a PE of address space, 4.9 GiB of the
  `/dev/shm` check at 256 PEs a node.
- **P2, the room count**: its table recomputed from
  `herd-room-2026-10-29.txt`'s raw lines (`herd-check.py`): 1.00, 1.08, 1.00,
  1.18, 1.94 at 1 MiB and 0.99, 1.09, 0.96, 0.91, 1.44 at 4 KiB, as recorded. It
  was timed only in the VM, which says so; there it ranged over 1 to 16 writers
  a vCPU, H12's ratios included, so the untimed case is many writers on many
  CPUs, where waking four rather than all may wait on the four's wakes - and
  the README says "measured on no cluster yet". Safe.
