## Verdict

**Yes, in this question's area.** Nothing blocks the release and nothing must
precede it; three minors, below.

- **The blocking get after its change conforms** (*observed in source*,
  *measured*). §9.6.1.5 asks that a blocking get "return after the data has been
  delivered to the dest array", and Table 14 (§9.12) puts it under neither fence
  nor quiet. A get of more than a piece now completes on its thread's own context
  block, and any thread of the PE may copy a landed piece into the caller's `dest`
  - which §9.2 item 2 makes the PE's own action, and which the code orders before
  the return: each piece is counted once, by the one thread whose compare-and-swap
  won it, after its copy, with a `seq_cst` read-modify-write the waiter's `seq_cst`
  load must see. It touches no program context's dirty set or counters, so it
  neither completes nor reorders a fence's or a quiet's operations; a get to
  itself or a node-mate still never reaches it. 17 runs of a probe written from
  §9.6, §9.12 and §9.2 - 68 PEs, 9,190 blocking gets (2.5 GiB) and 7,197
  nonblocking ones, 19,055 checks of every byte at return and of guard bytes
  either side, from up to twelve threads a PE, on the default, private and C11

  0, 2 and 3 helpers, with every thread place on the fallback and with a mix of

- **The README's Limits are true of the tree**, the entries added since round

### What the specification asks of a blocking get

One thing of its own: "The routines return after the data has been delivered to
the dest array on the local PE" (§9.6.1.5). Table 14 lists a blocking get under
neither fence nor quiet: it is complete when it returns, and neither orders nor is
ordered by them. §4.2 keeps `dest` in use until the operation completes, so nothing
may write it after the return. §9.2 has every operation a thread starts be "an
action of the PE as a whole" (item 2), a blocking call block only its calling
thread (item 3), and every call be safe from concurrent threads under
`SHMEM_THREAD_MULTIPLE` (item 4). §9.6's preamble numbers the target by the
context's team. 1.6 does not say which thread of the PE moves the bytes, and does
not promise that a blocking get after an unquieted put to the same PE sees the put
(its Example 46 quiets first).

### By construction (*observed in source*)

- **Delivered at return, and nothing after.** The calling thread issues every piece
  before it waits (`remote_get` :1072-1074), so the `issued` that `complete_gets`
  loads (:1138) is the call's whole, its place's block having been at equality since
  its last call. The wait returns only when `bytes_copied[0]` of that block reaches
  it (:1152-1163). Each piece adds its length there once - by the one thread whose
  compare-and-swap took its record from LANDED to COPYING (:781) - and only after
  its `memcpy` into `dest` (:789-799), with a `seq_cst` read-modify-write; the
  waiter's `seq_cst` load reads that add or a later one in the same release
  sequence. So every copy, the caller's or another thread's, happens before the
  return, and none can start after it: a counted piece's record holds no token any
  more, and the next call's pieces carry new ones (`make_token`, :892-897). The
  lead's bytes reach whichever thread copies the same way: the parser writes them,
  then `landed()` stores LANDED `seq_cst` (`progress-host.c:157-180`), which the
  copier's compare-and-swap acquires.
- **Another thread may copy a landed piece into the caller's `dest`** - from
  `make_room`'s `copy_out_any` (:807-815), when its own issue finds the zone full.
  It is a thread of the same PE and process, so the address is valid there, and the
  copy is the PE's own action (§9.2 item 2). A conforming program cannot see it: it
  may not touch `dest` during the call (§4.2), and nothing writes it after.
- **It neither completes nor reorders a fence's or a quiet's operations.** It counts
  on its place's block, `contexts_per_pe + place` (`node.h:1272-1275`), which no
  `shmem_ctx_create` or team context can claim, so no quiet waits for its pieces -
  the serial wait walks its own list (:1146-1151), the shared one and
  OSOE_CHECK_QUIET's promise filter by the context's block (:1220-1222,
  :1768-1770) - and it waits for no other context's: its list and its counters are
  its own. It calls no `flush_dirty`, no serializing fence and no acknowledgement
  wait; what it adds is `osoe_progress_host_wake_all` before it sleeps (:1170, :847),
  which sends sooner what the PE held back and reorders nothing. Its requests go on
  the one connection to the target's node, behind whatever the thread put there
  before (:1365), a peer's operations riding one wire and one thread whatever the
  helpers (round eight, §3); so a blocking get after an unquieted put to the same
  PE still sees the put - more than 1.6 promises, as before the change. The one
  thing it does to another context is `copy_out_any`'s early delivery of that
  context's landed nonblocking gets, credited to their own block: §9.6.2.2 leaves
  `dest` undefined until the quiet, and allows delivery before it.
- **To itself or a node-mate** a get never reaches it: `osoe_transport_get` sends a
  get toward a PE that routes in the process to `osoe_transport_local_get`, a
  `memcpy` before return (`transport-am.c:344-356`), and a job of one node never
  enters the file.
- **Under `SHMEM_THREAD_MULTIPLE`, on contexts private and shared.** Each thread has
  its place (`metrics.c:609-651`), its place's block and its own `_Thread_local`
  list (:1035). The context a call names only numbers the PE (`rma.c:47-56`), so a
  private context, the shared default one and a team context take one path, and
  several threads' gets at once share the zone and the credits alone, each piece
  with extents and a token of its own.
- **The fallback.** A place whose block passes 4,095 keeps the piece-at-a-time path
  (:1071), whose records go to LANDED_BLOCKING, which `copy_out_any` never claims;
  the two paths share the zone and no record.
- **One blocked get can hold the zone** - four pieces at the default - so another
  thread's get of any size waits for one of them to land. §9.2 item 3 is about
  blocking, not shared resources: the wait ends as the pieces land, whatever the
  target PE does, and the waiting thread copies them out itself. The README says so
  (`README.md:996-1003`).

## 3. The README's Limits, checked

Every entry read against the code at `3b45472`; the eleven hunks the section took
since round eight (`git diff c897948 3b45472 -- README.md`) against the code and,
where a program can meet them, against this round's runs.

- **"A large get from another node is held by its PE's landing zone"**
  (`README.md:996-1013`): true (*observed in source*, *measured*). A piece is the
  smaller of a run of 64 extents and the wire's payload - 65,536 less the 8-byte
  header and the 24-byte reply head (`connection.h:105-111`, `am.h:110-117`) -
  65,504 bytes, a multiple of 16, so no piece splits an element of the widest
  type; a mebibyte is seventeen pieces, the last 512 bytes; each takes 64 extents,
  four in the default zone's 256; the credits, 64 a connection, bind from sixteen
  PEs of a node at four pieces each. The ratios are the record's tables. The
  fallback: a place's block is `OSOE_MAX_CONTEXTS + place`, a token carries 12 bits,
  and the seventeenth place passes 4,095 from `OSOE_MAX_CONTEXTS` 4,080; places go
  first come, the agent's among them, so "the last places'" holds of the program's
  threads. A limit of time, not a departure: §9.6.1.5 bounds nothing but completion.
- **io_uring's rings and locked memory on Linux 6.14 and later** (`:1034-1038`):
  true of the code; `ring.c`'s refusals and the transport's check add the limit and
  its value to an `ENOMEM` (`ring.c:57-112`, `transport-internode.c:244-259`).
- **The architecture limit's list** with the shape and the link set by hand
  (`:1068-1076`): true.
- **Keepalive off where every node has a loopback address** (`:1107-1114`): true
  (`progress-host.c:797-822`: every node's address in 127/8 and `OSOE_KEEPALIVE`
  unset; the silent-peer check reads the same flag). Failure detection is not the
  specification's.
- **The deadlock limit's "a thread of the program's own"** (`:1115-1126`): true.
- **§9.1.6 under `srun -K1` or `osoe-launch` without `-k`** (`:1159-1164`): true, and
  marked as the departure it is.
- **Past 8,192 nodes the ring cannot be made** (`:977-979`): true of `ring.c:89-99` -
  a submission bound of two entries a wire and one, two wires a peer at no helpers,
  against io_uring's 32,768 - and reachable only past the 4,097 nodes the default
  budget allows.
- **The zero-copy send refused for want of locked memory** is in its setting's row
  (`:1529`), not the Limits, rightly: it is off by default, and its fallback changes
  nothing a program computes - `order.c` at 4 × 2 at no helpers and at two, and
  `bget.c`, passed with it on under a 64 KiB limit, the refusal's notice printed
  (*measured*).
- **The helpers' cost on the Pi and the loopback stream's numbers** (`:1180-1203`):
  scale's to check; no conformance claim in them.

**The entries round eight checked**, true as it found them: no file of the API
layer changed since (`git diff c897948 3b45472 -- shmem/src` touches the backend,
the node segment and the generic internode phase only), and since v0.1.0 the API
layer's files changed in comments, one macro of `wait.c` that means what it did,
and `init.c`'s start-up (*observed in source*).

**Completeness.** Among what v0.2 changed I found one choice the specification
leaves open that a program can meet and the section does not list: a floating-point
reduction's result at the default shape varies with the measured link (C1). The
blocking get adds no departure: who copies `dest` is invisible to a conforming
program, and its shared zone is listed.

## Findings, ranked

Nothing rated *blocks release* or *fix before release*.

### Minor

- **C1. The release note's "but for the last bits of a floating-point reduction"
  understates what the shape changes, and the Limits section does not list that a
  floating-point reduction's result may change between runs at the default shape**
  (*measured*, *observed in source*).
  - **Where**: `docs/release-notes/0.2.0.md:16-21`; `README.md`'s Limits (no entry)
    and `:1494` (`OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE`'s row, "the setting that makes a run
    reproducible"); `shmem/src/combine.c:34-38` (a floating max is `x > y ? x : y`,
    a sum `x + y`, combined in the shape's order); §9.10.9, Notes.
  - **What varies** (`fpvary.c`, 4,096 doubles over the world, past the small
    class; `out/head/out-F-*.txt`, `out/v010/out-F010-*.txt`):

    | five nodes, one PE each | `fixed` (and 0.1.0) | `tree`, the link set | Träff's |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | +1e308 + 1e308 - 1e308 at three PEs an element, rotating (exactly 1e308) | 2,458 infinite | 1,639 infinite | 819 infinite |
    | max over values with one NaN an element, rotating | 819 NaN, none below the largest number | 819 NaN, 819 a number below the largest | 820 NaN, 656 below the largest |
    | max over +0.0 and -0.0 | 2,048 are -0.0 | 2,048 | 2,050 |
    | a sum that rounds | one hash | another | a third |

    On 4 × 2 the NaN case (512 results below the largest number under `tree` and
    Träff's, none under `fixed`) and the rounding sum differed between the shapes,
    and the overflow and the zeros did not. A NaN met in the middle of the order
    drops every value combined before it - `x > y ? x : y` with `x` a NaN yields `y` -
    so a max comes out below the largest number among its inputs (`fpmax.c`,
    `out/fpmax.txt`). Within a run every member held the same bits; a shape run twice
    gave the same bits; `fixed` gave 0.1.0's bits exactly, on both maps.
  - **Scenario**: a program max-reduces a field in which a failed point is NaN, or
    sums terms near the range's edge, and compares two runs, or compares 0.2 with
    0.1.0. At the default shape the link measured at start chooses tree or Träff's
    for each size, so two runs whose measurements fall either side of a crossover
    give results that differ not in the last bits but in whether an infinity
    survives, in a zero's sign, and in a max over a NaN that comes out below its
    largest input under one shape and not under another.
  - **Why only minor**: §9.10.9's Notes allow "variations in the result of
    OpenSHMEM arithmetic reduction operations on floating-point datatypes,
    including NaN values", and the README's row says `fixed` makes a run
    reproducible. But the note promises less change than there is, and the Limits
    section, which says it lists every choice the specification leaves open that a
    program can meet, does not mention the one 0.2 makes by default.
  - **Fix**: the note's sentence: "for a floating-point reduction's result, which
    now depends on the collectives' shape - its last bits, and, where a sum
    overflows on the way, a zero's sign or a NaN is among the inputs, more"; and one
    Limits entry: at the default shape
    a floating-point reduction may differ between runs whose measured links differ;
    `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE=fixed`, or the link set by `OSOE_LINK_*`, makes it the same
    run to run, and `fixed` with `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_FANOUT=4 OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PIPELINE=2`
    makes it 0.1.0's.
  - **Severity**: minor. **Confidence**: high.

- **C2. What neither SOS nor the suite exercises, among what v0.2 changed**
  (*observed in source*; each right today by this round's probes).
  - **Several threads' blocking gets of more than a piece at the default landing
    zone.** Every registration of `test-multiple.c` across nodes runs at
    `OSOE_GET_LANDING_BYTES=8192` (`shmem/tests/CMakeLists.txt:3142-3165`), where its
    20 KiB get is three pieces; at the default zone it is one, and takes the slot.
    SOS's threaded programs get ten longs or fewer.
  - **A PE whose threads take both paths at once** - the fallback past block 4,095
    and the pieces together - which `OSOE_MAX_CONTEXTS` from 4,080 makes;
    `shmem.get-pieces.fallback.0-1` has one thread, all on the fallback.
  - **What a floating-point reduction varies by**, and that `fixed` reproduces a run
    and 0.1.0 bit for bit (C1): no test of the suite has a NaN, an infinity or a
    signed zero in a reduction.
  - **The active-set `collect64` and `fcollect64` at sizes whose internal gets are
    of more than a piece**: `test-active-set-partial.c` collects five longs a member
    (`:29`), and its 20,000-long reductions are the only multi-piece path there.
  - **Fix**: `bget.c`'s default-zone run and its mixed `OSOE_MAX_CONTEXTS` run, and
    `fpvary.c`'s three shapes against a pinned hash under `fixed`, into the suite;
    `aset.c`'s sizes into the partial active-set test. After the release.
  - **Severity**: minor (after release). **Confidence**: high that the gaps exist;
    nothing found wrong in them.

- **C3. The design notes still say the calling thread copies a blocking get's
  reply out** (*observed in source*).
  - **Where**: design §3.12 (`docs/design/shmem-ethernet-design.md:1504-1505`:
    "blocking get or fetching AMO: the calling thread, already waiting in the
    library") and §4.10's boundary table (`:3977`: "a blocking get's reply lands in
    the landing slot and the calling thread copies it out").
  - **What**: since the change a get of more than a piece lands in extents of the
    zone, and any thread of the PE whose issue finds the zone full may copy it
    (`copy_out_any`); §4.11 (`:4222-4239`), `node.h:1087-1093` and the code say so.
    The change's read listed the comments it made false; these two sentences were
    not among them.
  - **Scenario**: a reader of the design record reasoning about who writes a
    blocking get's `dest` - a porter, or a reviewer of a thread-safety claim -
    concludes it is the caller alone.
  - **Fix**: two clauses, "of one piece", and "or, for more than a piece, whichever
    thread of the PE claims it". **Severity**: minor. **Confidence**: high.

### Refuted, or no finding

- **"A blocking get must not complete the thread's nonblocking operations"**: 1.6
  asks no such thing; early completion is allowed (§9.6.2.2), and the blocking get
  completes no put and delivers early only landed nonblocking gets, crediting their
  own contexts (§1).
- **A blocked large get holding the zone against another thread's get** (§9.2 item
  3): a wait for a shared resource that lands whatever the target PE does, and the
  waiter copies it out itself; listed in the README (`:996-1003`).
- **The new imports in the reviewed library** (`aligned_alloc`, two liburing probe
  calls): no newer symbol version, so no requirement 0.1.0 did not have (§2).
- **The suite not seeing a blocking get's late copy**: it does, every run of its
  threaded tests (§1, broken on purpose), though a single test not every time.

## What is sound, and what convinced me

- **The blocking get keeps every promise 1.6 makes of it.** Not because the record
  says the models cover it - I agree they do, its one issuer being its own waiter, a
  schedule inside `get_completion_model.py`'s - but because three facts in the code
  give "delivered at return" in C11's own terms, a piece counted once, by its
  copier, after its copy, with a read-modify-write the waiter's load must see; 9,190
  gets from up to twelve threads a PE checked byte by byte at return found nothing
  wrong; and the probe and the suite both fail when the count is moved before the
  copy.
- **It is invisible to every context**: no quiet waits for it, it waits for no
  context, it touches no dirty set, and its requests queue behind the thread's
  earlier operations on the one connection to the target's node.
- **The public interface did not move**: headers byte for byte, the same 3,595
  exported names, the same soname, and programs built against 0.1.0 run on the
  reviewed library with the same output.
- **What a conforming program computes did not change** but for floating-point
  reductions, as the specification allows: the whole-API probe prints the same bytes
  on 0.1.0 and on `3b45472`, and `fixed` gives 0.1.0's floating-point bits.
- **Träff's with the grant fields gives the specification's results** on seven teams
  from 3 to 34 nodes, with two teams' reductions at once on every PE, at 0, 2 and 3
  helpers.
- **The Limits are true**, the eleven hunks since round eight included, and round
  eight's findings are fixed or on the checklist as rated.

## What I could not check

- **The Pi cluster and a fast link**: the lead's tonight; its SOS result is read,
  not run. Part 4's measurement of the blocking get on a fast link is what the
  record names as able to reopen (a), and is not a conformance question.
- **x86-64**: nothing here ran on it; aarch64's weaker order is the harder case for
  the copy-out's visibility, and it held there.
- **More than twelve threads a PE, or 256 PEs a node**, and the fallback's mix at
  `OSOE_MAX_THREADS` 1,023; and the models, which the correctness question re-ran.
- **Cancellation inside a blocking get**, which the library does not support
  (`base/sweep.h`) and the change's read reasoned about: not tried.
