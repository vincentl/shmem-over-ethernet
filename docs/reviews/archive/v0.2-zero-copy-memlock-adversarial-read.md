## Verdict

**Nothing fatal in what the library does.** I traced every path of the fallback, and it is correct
on each:

- a refusal with `IORING_CQE_F_MORE` and one without;
- nothing moved before a refusal;
- the region's notifications counted whatever their order;
- at most one region ever owing;
- a notification after lending has stopped;
- a peer's reset;
- the abort.

The stream cannot lose or repeat a byte, and no buffer can be written while the kernel may read it.

**Six findings are rated *should fix*.**

- The test is registered for ThreadSanitizer, where its configuration reports a race the suite
  excludes everywhere else. The tsan preset should then go red.
- A correct library fails the test on Linux 6.14 and later, as root, and possibly by timing. Since
  6.14 io_uring charges its own rings to the same limit: on the Pi's 6.18 with 16 KiB pages, and at
  two or three helpers anywhere, the test fails at ring creation on every run.
- After a refusal the bytes do not go again "at once". The pass that sees the refusal sends nothing,
  and a pass that moved nothing else sleeps up to 100 ms first.
- The test does not pin that the wire stops lending: a mutant that keeps lending passes. The launcher's
  "left behind" failure also passes.
- The notice, the README, the comment and the record say the limit is shared; the count is, the limit
  is each process's own. They also say a send "holds its pages", where it is charged for the length
  it offers, and they present `-ENOBUFS` as the limit's refusal, which io_uring never gives.
- Design §3.3 still says every send failure but a reset is the connection's, and `wire.h` agrees.

Eleven notes follow. One of them matters beyond this change: on 6.14 and later, every ring counts
against `ulimit -l` with zero copy off, and ring creation past the limit fails without naming it (N1).

**2. A correct library fails the test on Linux 6.14 and later, as root, and possibly by timing**
(*observed in source*; the 6.12 behaviour *measured* by earlier reviews; not run).

- **Where**: `shmem/tests/CMakeLists.txt:1403-1413`: `sh -c "ulimit -l 64 && exec ..."`, passing only
  on the notice, failing on "Cannot allocate memory".
- **What, (a): since Linux 6.14, io_uring's own memory is charged to the same count.** In the kernel
  source:
  - `io_uring/memmap.c`'s `io_create_region`, at `v6.14` and `v6.18`, calls
    `__io_account_mem(ctx->user, nr_pages)` under `if (ctx->user)`. The call comes before the branch
    between user memory and kernel-allocated pages, so it charges every region.
  - At `v6.14`, `io_uring.c`'s `io_allocate_scq_urings` makes the rings (the SQ array and the CQ) one
    region, `ring_region`, and the SQEs another, `sq_region`.
  - `kbuf.c`'s `io_register_pbuf_ring` makes a provided-buffer ring in the user's memory a region
    too. That is the ring this library registers: `io_uring_setup_buf_ring` with flags 0, `ring.c:85`.
  - `io_uring_create` sets `ctx->user` before `io_allocate_scq_urings`.
  - `rsrc.c`'s `__io_account_mem` adds to `user->locked_vm`, compares the total with
    `rlimit(RLIMIT_MEMLOCK) >> PAGE_SHIFT`, and returns `-ENOMEM` past it.
  - At `v6.13`, the rings still came from `io_pages_map`, and `v6.12`'s `kbuf.c` pins a
    provided-buffer ring with no accounting. The step-1 review measured rings created on 6.12 at
    limits of 64 KiB and 0 (`docs/reviews/evidence/reports/q1-omissions.md:264`).

  This test's ring has 16 submissions, 128 completions and 64 provided buffers: three regions of one
  page each. That is 12 KiB with 4 KiB pages, and 48 KiB with 16 KiB pages.
  - **The Pi** runs 6.18 with 16 KiB pages (`v0.2-step4-portability.md:121`, `:166-168`). There
    `ulimit -l 64` is 4 pages, and three nodes need 9, so a node's ring is refused at creation on
    every run: "cannot create an io_uring for 4 connections: Cannot allocate memory" (`ring.c:77-78`).
    The FAIL expression then reports that as the regression the test guards against.
  - **4 KiB pages, no helpers**: the rings take 9 of the 16 pages, and the test still reaches the
    fallback when it runs alone.
  - **4 KiB pages, two or three helpers**: a node has three or four rings, 9 to 12 pages, so the second
    node's rings pass 16 on every run. The suite is verified at no helpers, two and three: the scripts
    export `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS` and run `ctest -j4`
    (`<repo>/build/scratch/ladder/merge-point/presets-helpers.sh:15`).
  - **Any page size under `ctest -j4`**, the README's command: the user's other tests' rings count
    in the same total, so ring creation fails whenever they are running.
- **What, (b): root, or any process with `CAP_IPC_LOCK`, is never charged.** `io_uring_create` sets
  `ctx->user` only `if (!ns_capable_noaudit(&init_user_ns, CAP_IPC_LOCK))`, and the charge is taken
  only `if (ctx->user)`. So no send is refused, no notice prints, and ctest reports "Required regular
  expression not found". Nothing says the suite must run unprivileged. A privileged container, one
  route the README's container paragraph leaves open, has the capability.
- **What, (c): with 4 KiB pages on 6.12, whether the limit is met is timing.** The limit is 16 pages,
  and each send by reference of up to 2 KiB (`OSOE_AM_BUFFER=2048`) holds 2 until the peer's
  acknowledgement. The portability review measured the sibling failing at 64 KiB and passing at
  256 KiB (`v0.2-step4-portability.md:189`), without saying how many runs. The record's verification
  is empty (record:44). A run whose peak stays under the limit prints no notice and fails.
- **Why it matters**: the test exists to fail on a regression. These make it fail without one, and
  its FAIL expression names the failure as the regression.
- **Fix**: make the refusal certain and the rings' charge irrelevant. Lower `RLIMIT_MEMLOCK` to 0
  *after* the rings are open, which `setrlimit` always allows:
  - in `shmem_test_internode`, after `shmem_init`, under an argument of its own (the program already
    takes its configuration from its environment and arguments, as `test-wire.c` does);
  - and in `shmem_test_wire`'s zero-copy mode, after `osoe_ring_enable`.

  Then every wire's next send by reference is refused on any kernel, whatever else the user holds.
  Skip where the process can lock memory anyway: with the limit at 0, `mlock` of one page succeeds
  only with `CAP_IPC_LOCK`, so print a skip line and use `SKIP_RETURN_CODE` or
  `SKIP_REGULAR_EXPRESSION`. Drop `ulimit -l 64`.

**3. After a refusal the same bytes are not offered again "at once": the pass that sees the
refusal sends nothing, and a pass that moved nothing else sleeps up to 100 ms first, once on each
wire that meets the limit** (*observed in source*; the notification's timing *recalled*; not
measured).

- **Where**:
  - `ring.c:262-265`: "the same bytes go again at its owner's next pass, which the mark brings at
    once". The record says the same at :26-27.
  - `ring.c:269-271`: `w->sent = 0` and `mark`.
  - `wire.c:277-289`: `send_from`'s branch for a send in flight.
  - `progress.c:1240-1244`: `flush`'s offer, and `if (n > 0) moved = true`.
  - `agent.c:238-247` and `progress-host.c:1171-1173`: each owner's wait.
  - `base/deadline.h:16`: `OSOE_SWEEP_NS`, 100 ms.
- **What**:
  - The refusal is reaped at the start of a pass (`progress.c:1790`), and the mark puts the wire in
    that same pass's visit. That visit's `flush` offers the bytes. Because the send is still marked in
    flight, `send_from` only collects its result: it returns 0, clears `sending` and starts nothing.
    `flush` counts that as no movement.
  - The copy is offered at the wire's next visit. The wire stays busy (`busy`, `c->sending`), so the
    next pass visits it, but nothing makes that pass come soon. A pass that moved nothing else ends in
    the owner's idle wait, which lasts until a completion, a wake, or `OSOE_SWEEP_NS`.
  - Nothing is in flight on that wire. The refusal's notification is normally reaped in the same pass
    as the refusal itself: io_uring posts it from task work that runs before the submitting
    `io_uring_enter` returns (*recalled*). And the peer of a one-way stream has nothing to send until
    this segment arrives.
  - So a put followed by `shmem_quiet` waits up to 100 ms. Lending stops after the refusal, so this
    happens once on each wire that meets the limit.
- **Why it matters**: it is invariant 37's third clause in another form: work that only a later
  visit does, which nothing brings. The comment and the record promise the opposite. A refusal that
  lands during `shmem_init`'s link measurement would be measured as the link, and every collective
  shapes itself from that number. That last case is reasoned, not seen.
- **Fix**: in `ring.c`'s refusal path, clear `w->sending` and set `w->sent = -1` instead of
  `w->sent = 0`. The same pass's `flush` then offers the bytes as a new send, by copy, and its
  completion wakes the owner. Or have `send_from` treat a completion that took nothing as no
  completion and submit at once. Or count a collected refusal as movement.

**4. The test does not pin the fallback's second half, and passes one launcher failure**
(*observed in source*; the mutant's outcome reasoned, not run).

- **Where**: `shmem/tests/CMakeLists.txt:1411-1413`; `ring.c:268`.
- **What**:
  - **A mutant without `w->zero_copy = false;` passes.** The wire keeps lending, so each re-offer goes
    by reference again. It is refused while the user's count is past 64 KiB, and accepted once the
    job's own notifications have given pages back. The notice prints once, and the job completes.
    Nothing in the program or the expressions asks whether the sends after the refusal copied.
  - **`PASS_REGULAR_EXPRESSION` makes ctest ignore the exit status** (CMake's rule, *recalled*). So
    `osoe-launch`'s own finding passes: "every process succeeded but N shared-memory object(s) were
    left behind", with status 1 (`tools/launch/launch.c:598-602`). The launcher's tests list "left
    behind" for that reason (`CMakeLists.txt:1885`, `:1893`, `:1924`, `:1936`).
  - The other failures are caught. The old behaviour fails on "Cannot allocate memory". A variant that
    ends the job fails on "exited with status 1". A hang ends at the launcher's timeout, whose
    `SIGKILL` prints "killed by signal 9".
- **Why it matters**: stopping lending on the wire that met the limit is what keeps the job of a user
  whose count stays saturated, for instance by another job, from retrying by reference on every
  segment. The README promises it.
- **Fix**:
  - With the limit at 0 after the rings open (finding 2), a lending mutant never succeeds and runs to
    the timeout, which already fails.
  - In `shmem_test_wire`'s zero-copy mode at limit 0, check that `ring.counts.zero_copy_refused` is 1
    for each sending wire, that `zero_copy_sends == zero_copy_refused`, and that the stream arrives
    whole. `stream_lent` (`test-wire.c:193-243`) already checks the bytes and the owed counts.
  - Add `left behind` to the FAIL list.

**5. The notice, the README, `ring.c`'s comment and the record misstate what is shared and what
is charged, and name `-ENOBUFS` as the limit's refusal** (*observed in source*).

- **Where**:
  - the notice, `progress.c:1756-1758`: "(ulimit -l, RLIMIT_MEMLOCK, shared by the user's processes
    on this host)";
  - `README.md:1503`: "holds its pages against the user's locked-memory limit (`ulimit -l`, shared by
    every process of the user on a host)";
  - `ring.c:256-260`;
  - the record, :10-13 and :21.
- **What**:
  - **Shared.** `v6.14` `rsrc.c`'s `__io_account_mem` adds each charge to `user->locked_vm`, where
    `ctx->user` is `get_uid(current_user())`. That is one count for every process of the user's real
    UID on the host. It compares the count with `rlimit(RLIMIT_MEMLOCK)`, the *charging process's
    own* limit. So the count is shared, and the limit is each process's. A process with a low limit
    is refused for pages other processes hold, which is exactly what the test relies on. The remedy
    is the job's own `ulimit -l`, in the batch script or through the launcher's propagation, not a
    setting of the host.
  - **"Holds its pages".** The charge is `(len >> PAGE_SHIFT) + 2` pages of the length *offered*:
    `io_notif_account_mem(zc->notif, zc->len)` in `v6.7` `net.c`, the arithmetic as P1 read it. It is
    not the bytes taken. A short write, the rule under `TCP_NOTSENT_LOWAT`, is charged the whole rest
    of its buffer, and the rest offered again is charged again while the first is held. At `v6.7`
    the charge sits in `io_send_zc`, before `sock_sendmsg`, and runs at every issue, so a send that
    found the socket full is charged again at each retry. The charges are given back only with the
    notification. P1 wrote "for the rest of its buffer".
  - **`-ENOBUFS`.** io_uring's charge fails with `-ENOMEM` (`rsrc.c`). At `v6.14`, `net.c`'s SEND_ZC
    paths return no `-ENOBUFS`; its `-ENOBUFS` returns are the receive paths'. In `tcp.c`,
    `tcp_sendmsg_locked`'s branch for a caller's `msg_ubuf`, io_uring's, does no accounting and
    returns neither. Its `-ENOBUFS` belongs to `MSG_ZEROCOPY`'s own `msg_zerocopy_realloc`, which
    io_uring bypasses. Taking `-ENOBUFS` as a refusal is harmless, since nothing was sent and a copy
    is always correct, but the comment and the record present it as the limit's.
- **Why it matters**: the notice is the one line a user acts on, and the README is to be true at
  every commit.
- **Fix**:
  - The notice: "... refused: io_uring counts the pages of every send by reference of this user's
    processes on this host against each process's own locked-memory limit (ulimit -l, N KiB here):
    raise it for the job, or set OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY=off; the connections that met it copy from here
    on". `getrlimit` gives N. P1 proposed the remedy's words.
  - The README: "Each send by reference charges the rest of the buffer it offers, and two pages,
    until its notification comes; io_uring counts every process of the user on the host together,
    against each process's own `ulimit -l`".
  - `ring.c`'s comment and the record: `-ENOBUFS` is taken defensively. It is `MSG_ZEROCOPY`'s own
    refusal, not io_uring's.

**6. Design §3.3 and `wire.h` still say every send failure but the peer's reset is the
connection's, and say nothing of the refusal** (*observed in source*).

- **Where**:
  - `docs/design/shmem-ethernet-design.md:491-495`: "Such a failure — `-EPIPE` or `-ECONNRESET`, the
    peer's reset — is the send side's alone ...; any other failure is the whole connection's";
  - `:423-445`, the zero-copy send as built, which has no limit and no fallback;
  - `:1930-1932`, §3.14's counter `zero_copy_copied`: "notifications that said the bytes did not go
    by reference after all";
  - `shmem/src/wire.h:71-72` (`error`: "a send for any reason but the peer's reset"), `:88-94` (the
    wire's `zero_copy`), and `:147-158` (when `osoe_wire_send_lent` goes by reference).
- **What**: the change adds a third kind of send result: a refused send by reference, which is not a
  failure. It also adds a switch per wire that turns lending off. And a refused send's notification
  carries `IORING_NOTIF_USAGE_ZC_COPIED`: `v6.14` `notif.c` sets it when `zc_used` is false, and no
  socket buffer of a refused send ever completed. So `zero_copy_copied` now counts refusals too.
  None of these places says any of it.
- **Why it matters**: the notes are the as-built account the reviews grade against. A reader of §3.3
  today is told that `-ENOMEM` ends the job.
- **Fix**: a sentence in each place. For §3.3: "a send by reference that the locked-memory limit
  refuses moved nothing; it goes again by copy, and the wire lends no more". Also the as-built
  paragraph, §3.14's counter, and the three comments in `wire.h`.

### Notes

**N1. On 6.14 and later every ring counts against `ulimit -l`, with zero copy off, and its creation
past the limit names neither** (*observed in source*; outside this change).

- The source is finding 2's. A ring at the 3,274-node bound holds 65,536 completions (1 MiB) and
  16,384 submission entries (1 MiB). That fits the default 8 MiB, one job a host.
- At a lower limit, ring creation fails "cannot create an io_uring for N connections: Cannot allocate
  memory" or "cannot register N receive buffers: ..." (`ring.c:77-78`, `:85-87`). A lower limit
  means 64 KiB, the kernel's default before 5.16 (*recalled*), or several jobs of one user, or 16 KiB
  pages, where three of this test's rings already pass 64 KiB.
- It is portability P1's class without zero copy. The step-1 finding "RLIMIT_MEMLOCK is not a
  concern" (`q1-omissions.md:264`) holds for 6.12 only.
- A checklist item: name the limit on `-ENOMEM` at open, and have the probe print `ulimit -l`, as P1's
  last suggestion asked.

**N2. `osoe-stat` and the internode test count a refused send as a send by reference.**

- It is counted in `zero_copy_sends` at submission (`wire.c:313`), and its notification counts as
  "copied after all" (finding 6). So `osoe-stat` cannot tell a refusal from loopback's copy.
- The check in `test-internode.c:368-376`, `zero_copy_sends > 0`, is the guard that the zero-copy
  suites do not pass on the copying path (the fourth read of step 4, S1). Refusals alone now meet it.
- At the default limit those suites meet no refusals, so nothing passes today that should not. The
  record's "no new counter" leaves it so.

**N3. One refusal stops lending on that wire for the rest of the job, however brief the pressure:**
another job of the user, or a burst. It is a choice, and the README states it. Part 4, which decides
the default on a fast link, may want lending back after a while.

**N4. `reservation_model.py` has no part that owes a notification and moves nothing, and no lending
turned off** (`send_parts`, `:560-590`).

- The count argument covers the refusal: a region is released only at owed 0, whatever order the
  notifications come in.
- An alternative in `send_parts` would keep the model the complete account, as the project asks of a
  protocol change: a part by reference that owes one and moves none, after which every part copies.

**N5. A `-ENOMEM` that is not the limit takes the same path:** for instance `io_alloc_notif`, or a
request's async data, failing to allocate at prep. That is harmless, since the copy is correct and a
copied send that then fails is still fatal, but the notice would name the limit. It is rare enough
to leave.

**N6. `init.c` raises the soft `RLIMIT_NOFILE` to its hard limit (`init.c:292-303`).** Doing the same
for `RLIMIT_MEMLOCK` with `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY=on` would push the refusal back wherever a site's hard
limit is above its soft one. A suggestion for Part 4.

**N7. Other branches change the same README counts.** This branch's 858 and 459 (`README.md:302-303`,
`:311`, `:1324`, `:1327`) are `main`'s counts plus this test. Two branches in flight change the same
lines from `main`'s 857: `loopback-keepalive` to 859 and 460, and `single-node-configure` to 858.
Whichever merges second must recount.

**N8. The record cites `docs/decisions/2026-10-10.md` (record:6).** That file is on branch
`loopback-keepalive` (`e2bb1e9`), not on `main` or this branch. Merged before it, the citation
dangles.

**N9. WIP, expected before merge:**

- the record's verification is "[to be filled in]" (record:44);
- `docs/decisions/README.md` has no line for the record;
- the commit says it is "to be rewritten".

**N10. Style.** The once flag is a `static atomic_bool` declared inside the function. The tree's other
process-private atomics are `_Atomic` words at file scope with a "process-private" comment
(`agent.c:30-45`, `abort.c:20-22`).

**N11. Naming.** "memlock", in the test's name and the record's, is the kernel's spelling of "locked
memory" (`RLIMIT_MEMLOCK`), which the names rule would spell out. The brief names this file the same
way, so this is the lead's call.

## What is sound

- **The order of the count and the fallback.** `F_MORE` counts the notification owed
  (`ring.c:246-252`) before the refusal is handled (`:253-273`).
  - At `v6.7` and `v6.14`, `io_sendrecv_fail` sets `IORING_CQE_F_MORE` on a failed SEND_ZC whenever
    `REQ_F_NEED_CLEANUP` is set, that is, once its notification was allocated in prep. The cleanup
    then flushes the notification.
  - At `v6.14`, `io_send_zc` ends with `io_req_set_res(req, ret, IORING_CQE_F_MORE)`, whatever
    `ret` is.
  - So a refusal owes exactly the one notification that follows it, and the fatal "owed none" cannot
    fire. A refusal without `F_MORE`, when the notification's allocation failed, owes none and needs
    none (*observed in source*).
- **Nothing moves before a refusal.** The charge is taken before `sock_sendmsg`: in `io_send_zc` at
  `v6.7`, in `io_send_zc_import` at `v6.14`. A short send is retried only under `MSG_WAITALL`
  (`io_net_retry`), which this library never sets. So `done_io` stays 0, a positive result is an
  ordinary short send, and the refused bytes are offered again from the same offset. The stream
  stays whole (*observed in source*).
- **At most one region owes.**
  - The refused send went by reference only while the other region owed nothing (`wire.c:305-306`),
    and that region was released at the top of the same `flush` (`progress.c:1226-1235`).
  - Lending stops after the refusal, and only one segment of a connection is on the wire at a time.
  - So BOTH LENT cannot arise. The refused region becomes `LENT` after its copy completes, if its
    notification has not yet come, and is released at owed 0. The check on open (`:1334-1341`) still
    guards invariant 37.
- **Notifications after lending stops.** The `F_NOTIF` branch (`ring.c:228-245`) does not look at
  `zero_copy`.
- **A peer's reset at the same time.** The copy then meets `-EPIPE` or `-ECONNRESET` and sets
  `send_error`. A receive-side error stops `flush` before it offers anything (`progress.c:1195-1196`).
- **The abort.** `flush_aborting` offers the same pointer by copy (`progress.c:1147-1148`), which
  `send_from`'s check of the bytes in flight accepts.
- **Control wires** never lend (`wire.c:175`). A copied send's `-ENOMEM` stays fatal. The bounds of the
  submission and completion queues are unchanged: a refusal costs one completion and one notification,
  as any send by reference does.
- **The once flag.**
  - It is race-free across owners, and an exchange at `seq_cst`, as §3.16's table asks of any RMW.
  - It passes `CheckMemoryOrders.cmake`'s patterns, and as a static it starts zero-initialized, which
    C11 makes a valid atomic state.
  - It is read only in a pass that saw a refusal, at most once a wire.
  - `publish_ring`'s early return cannot skip it, because a refusal is a completion.
- **One reader of the flag.** `w->zero_copy` has one reader, `send_from`, so turning it off changes
  only that decision.
