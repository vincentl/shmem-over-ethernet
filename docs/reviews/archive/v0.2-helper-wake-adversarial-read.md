## Verdict

**Nothing fatal.** The flag does what it was built for:
- With PE 0 the only writer, and its last bit taken before its quiet returns, an owner whose flag
  is up at the put is asleep or about to be.
- Every path from there ends in a counted wake: preempted before its wait, mid-wait, or after a
  sweep. The measured owners never fell below 100 wakes in 100 puts, alone or loaded.
- No path in the window wakes the other helper or the agent.

The `/proc/self/maps` lookup, the asserted offsets, the agent run's registration and the README's
counts are right.

**Three findings are rated *should fix*.** All three are about what the test is said to prove:
1. **A spin shorter than a round's own latencies is counted as a sleep.** The count is of futex
   completions. A wait the owner armed when its spin ran out earlier stays armed after the
   acknowledgement's completion ends it, so the next put's wake is counted while the owner spins.
   PE 0, asleep in its quiet when the flag rises, also arrives late. In the change's own runs the
   30 µs mutant passed its bound on four wires of fifteen (94 to 198), and two runs failed by one
   wire. The comment's, the record's and the README's "never sleeps, near none" is false. The
   mutants ran only on an idle machine, and RUN_SERIAL's removal now puts the test under load.
2. **The quiet's wake stands in for the put's: the bound counts rounds, not puts.** The misroute
   mutant's owner was still woken 100 times on every wire. A put whose own wake is lost passes.
   At two helpers, a put's wake sent to the agent would pass both counts.
3. **RUN_SERIAL's removal rests on release-build runs made one test at a time.** The counting form
   had failed under AddressSanitizer, four tests at a time. The README's "20 of 20 ... 8 to 9 of
   20" sets a release result against a range whose 9 was AddressSanitizer's.

Six notes follow.

## Findings, ranked

### Should fix

**1. A spin shorter than the acknowledgement's round trip, or than PE 0's own wake from its quiet,
is counted as a sleep** (*observed in source*; *measured*: the lead's raw outputs)

- **Where:**
  - the claims:
    - `shmem/tests/test-helper-wake.c:24-27`: "sees the wake word move during its spin and never
      sleeps, so its wakes stay near none however long PE 0 waits";
    - `:105-108`: "within a microsecond or two of its rise on an idle machine - inside even a
      short spin after it";
    - the record's `:28-36`;
    - `README.md:1326-1328`: "an owner that spins, not one slow to sleep";
  - the mechanism: `ring.c:231-238` and `:342-373`, `transport-internode.c:483-488` and
    `:1553-1581`, and `progress-host.c:1202-1204`.
- **What the count is.** `transport.am.wakes` counts the completions of the futex wait on the
  owner's ring (`ring.c:236-237`), whenever the owner reaps them. It does not count sleeps that a
  wake ended. The ring keeps one armed wait across idle periods, by design (`ring.c:346-354` and
  `:363-371`; review D3). When a socket's completion ends a wait, the futex wait stays armed, and
  the next futex_wake on the word completes it.
- **The first way a spinner is counted.** An owner that spins after raising its flag (`mutate.py`,
  `mutate-agent.py`) still sleeps whenever its spin runs out first. In a round, that is while it
  waits for the acknowledgement it asked for: a round trip through the peer.
  1. The acknowledgement's receive ends that wait, and the futex wait stays armed.
  2. At the next round the owner raises its flag and spins.
  3. PE 0's put claims the flag and bumps the word. Its futex_wake completes the armed wait left
     over from the earlier sleep.
  4. The spin sees `io_uring_cq_ready`, and the next pass counts a wake.

  The owner never slept in that idle period.
- **The second way.** PE 0 is not polling when the flag rises:
  - its quiet sleeps on a futex (`sleep_on`, `transport-internode.c:483-488`);
  - the pass that publishes the acknowledgement wakes it;
  - the owner raises its flag one pass later (`progress-host.c:1202`, where `moved` is tested
    first).

  So PE 0's first look at the flag comes after its own wake-up latency - in a VM plausibly tens of
  microseconds (*recalled*, not measured here) - and finds the flag already up. A spin shorter than that has already ended
  in a sleep, and the put then ends that sleep.
- **What the change's own runs show** (*measured*,
  `helper-wake-flag-check/mutant-spin*-*.full`). The owner's wakes on node 1's, 2's and 3's wires,
  five runs each, on an idle VM; **bold** passes that wire's bound:

  | Mutant | Run 1 | Run 2 | Run 3 | Run 4 | Run 5 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | A helper spinning 30 µs | 2, 1, 53 | 1, **94**, **100** | 1, 1, 1 | 1, 2, 1 | **197**, 43, **198** |
  | A helper spinning 200 µs | 1, 32, 1 | 1, 2, 24 | 1, 24, 19 | 1, **112**, 13 | 2, 14, 1 |

  - At 30 µs four wires of fifteen passed, and runs 2 and 5 failed by a single wire.
  - 197 and 198 are two counted wakes a round, as a sleeping owner's are under load.
  - The agent's mutants counted 0 to 2 wakes on every wire. That suggests the round trip at no
    helpers stayed under 30 µs on that machine.
  - The record's "every run failing" is true of these runs. The margin is the point.
- **Why it matters.** The S1 guard is the point of this change: "a preempted owner is told from one
  that spins".
  - What the test can tell apart is a spin longer than a round trip from one that is not, on the
    machine and under the load at hand.
  - The scale review measured what a 30 µs spin costs: 9.3 CPU-seconds against 1.3 (S1). Here the
    test caught it by a margin of one wire, in two runs of five.
  - Every mutant run was on an idle VM. With RUN_SERIAL gone, the suite runs this test beside three
    others, which lengthens both the round trip and PE 0's wake-up. Those are the conditions under
    which a spinner is counted. So do sanitizers and slower machines.
  - The counting form's runs of the same mutant saw 1 or 2 wakes (the scale review, five runs) and
    10 of 10 short (the merge point). The mechanism is common to both forms, so the difference is
    the machine's state, which is the dependence described above.
- **Fix:**
  - Say what the guard catches, in the test's comment, the record and the README: a spin longer
    than an acknowledgement's round trip and than PE 0's wake from its quiet. That is the 200 µs
    mutant, and not reliably the 30 µs one.
  - To catch shorter spins, take each round's second wake from a running PE:
    - after the put, PE 0 waits for the owner's flag to rise again, which the owner raises once it
      has sent the put, and only then quiets;
    - the quiet's request (`flush_dirty`, `transport-internode.c:1518-1526`) then claims the flag
      within a microsecond or two of its rise;
    - ask two wakes a round, at least 180 in 100.

    A sleeper is woken by both. A spinner's leftover wait, if it had one, was completed by the
    put's wake, so the quiet's wake lands inside its spin and counts nothing: at most 100 on an
    idle machine.
  - Then run the mutants beside the suite's load - four tests at a time, and four busy loops -
    before saying what the guard holds there.
  - The scale review's other suggestion, CPU per idle transition, is the alternative.

**2. The quiet's wake stands in for the put's: the bound counts rounds, not puts** (*observed in
source*; *measured*: the misroute mutant)

- **Where:**
  - the claims:
    - `test-helper-wake.c:14-18`: "so every put finds it asleep, or about to be, and must wake it";
    - the record's `:29-31`: "Every put then finds the owner asleep or about to be, and must wake
      it: the bound is nine wakes in ten rounds";
    - the CMake comment, `shmem/tests/CMakeLists.txt:1671-1673`;
    - `README.md:1326`: "a put that does not wake that owner";
  - the check: `test-helper-wake.c:150-151` and `:157-162`;
  - the mechanism: `transport-internode.c:1638` and `:1510-1526`, and `progress-host.c:358-380`.
- **What happens.** Every round's `shmem_quiet` asks the peer to acknowledge, through
  `osoe_connection_request_ack`, the dirty bit and `osoe_progress_host_defer`. It then calls
  `osoe_progress_host_wake_deferred`, which runs `wake_owner` on the same owner. If the put's own
  wake did not claim the flag, the quiet's does, and the owner, still asleep, is counted once. So
  a round counts at least one wake whether or not the put woke anyone.
- **Measured.** The record's misroute mutant sent each put's wake to the other helper. The owner was
  still woken 100 times on every one of its fifteen wires (`helper-wake-flag-check/check.txt:34`,
  "15x100"). The mutant failed on the other helper's count alone: 98 or 100, against at most 2.
- **So two defects would pass:**
  - a put whose wake is lost counts 100 in 100. That is a `remote_put` that defers the wake for
    good (`transport-internode.c:681-686`), or one that loses the call;
  - at two helpers, a put's wake sent to owner 0 passes both counts, since the agent is not judged
    there (`test-helper-wake.c:156-157`).
- **Why it matters.** The record's headline is that every put must wake its owner, nine times in
  ten. The count cannot say which of a round's two wakes woke the owner. A lost wake costs latency
  alone, as a misrouted one does, and that is the reason the test exists (`:8-10`).
- **Fix:**
  - Finding 1's two wakes a round, at least 180, catches a lost put wake too: the round then counts
    one.
  - Short of that, read PE 0's own count of claimed wakes before and after each put. That is
    `am.wakes_sent`, thread scope (`metrics.c:92`), and it must rise at nearly every put.
  - Judge the agent at two helpers too, at most 2. Nothing PE 0 does in the window wakes owner 0
    (`progress-host.c:395-439`); arrivals, the stop and the abort all come outside it.
  - Add a mutant that drops the put's wake to the record's three.

## What is sound

- **The flag is an honest sign at the put, in this test.**
  - It is up from `raise_flag_and_look`'s store to the next pass's `lower_flag`
    (`progress-host.c:592-611` and `:1169-1204`; for the agent, `:1510-1527` and `agent.c:183-245`).
  - So it is also up while the owner goes round - after finding a writer's bit or an unsent request
    once the flag is stored - and from a wait's return to the next pass.
  - At PE 0's put neither can happen:
    - PE 0 is node 0's only writer, and it is waiting;
    - its last bit, the quiet's request, was taken by the pass that published the acknowledgement,
      before the quiet returned;
    - the unsent limit is asked once, at init (`init.c:446`), before the loop.
  - Were another writer's bit there, PE 0's claim would wake an owner that is going round. That
    wake is counted if a wait is still armed or if the owner's `seen` predates the bump; otherwise
    it is lost.
- **Every way from a raised flag to a sleep ends in exactly one counted wake for the put.**
  - Asleep: the armed wait completes.
  - Preempted before arming: the arm compares the bumped word with a `seen` loaded before the pass
    and fails with `-EAGAIN`, which is counted (`ring.c:236`).
  - After a sweep's timeout: the wait is still armed, and it completes.
  - A single bump cannot count twice: the writer marks before it wakes, so a pass that reaps the
    completion also takes the bit.
  - The only loss is a claim that a pass's `lower_flag` beats after a sweep: at most once in 100 ms
    for each owner.
  - The quiet's wake adds a second count when the owner is back asleep first. That is the 100 to
    200 seen under load.
  - Measured: of the 300 owner-wires in the record's four sets of runs, none fell below 100.
- **Nothing else wakes the judged owners in the window.**
  - The deadlock detector is off, and `wake_all` belongs to gets.
  - `flush_dirty` asks only the context's dirty peer, which each quiet clears
    (`transport-internode.c:1640-1651`).
  - The agent's requests nudge helpers only for the detector and the stop.
  - The agent's word is bumped only by PE 0's put and quiet. Arrivals (`internode-am.c:130`), the
    abort (`node.c:1736`) and the stop (`agent.c:340`) all come outside the window.
  - The other helper: 0 in every passing run.
- **The lookup.**
  - The library maps the area once per process, from offset 0 (`connection-area.c:371-376`). The
    whole-range `madvise` (`:386`) splits nothing.
  - No other object the library names contains `.conn.`. The others are the control segment
    `/osoe.J.S.N.session`, `...heap.N`, `...statics.N` and `/osoe.J.S.N.job.session`.
  - The words the test reads lie in the first 512 bytes, whatever the page size.
  - Test and library take the line size from one header, and the asserts hold at both sizes on both
    targets ("What ran").
- **Polling with `sched_yield`.**
  - osoe-launch binds nothing, and a helper starts on its agent's CPUs, which are its PE's.
  - A yield gives a shared CPU to a runnable owner; EEVDF moves the yielder's deadline on by a
    slice (*recalled*).
  - A poll lasts until the owner's next pass.
  - Three ten-second flag waits fit inside `-t 60`.
- **The agent run's registration.**
  - It takes the same form as `osoe_tier2_map_test`'s: `cmake -E env`, `-n 4 -m 0,1,2,3 -t 60`,
    tier2, 90 s and the internode label.
  - It sits inside `if(OSOE_INTERNODE AND NOT ...thread)`, so neither the single-node build nor
    ThreadSanitizer registers it. `shmem.single-node.configure` passed at 5a9e34d.
  - Its environment overrides a suite-wide `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS`.
  - With `helpers` read as 0 from the area, the owner is 0, `flag[0]` is the agent's, and the agent
    alone is judged.
- **Removing RUN_SERIAL risks no other test:** the test shares no state with them, and it runs four
  PEs for about 70 ms.
- **The README's counts:** 864 is the integration verification's ctest total, and
  864 − 464 − 1 = 399.
- **The measured trees:** `302ba07`'s test and `connection-area.c` are byte-identical to 5a9e34d's.
  `62f060f`'s test differs only in fixing the helper count at two and leaving the agent unjudged.
