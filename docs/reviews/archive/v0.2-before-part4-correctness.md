## Verdict

**Correct as far as I could run and read it, and nothing blocks the tag.** The
protocols new since round eight are right as built, each read against its
record and its model and each broken on purpose: a blocking get's pieces,
copied in the wrong order, are caught by `shmem.multiple.*` in the release
build and by ThreadSanitizer at no helpers in every run; the zero-copy refusal's
completion paths, the keepalive default and the grant fields read as their
records say. All 45 model tests pass - the release preset's 40, with
`get_completion`'s one serialized case inside its full model, and, for the
first time since 0.1.0's tag, the six `SHMEM_THREAD_MULTIPLE` models - every
breakage failing for the reason it states. The suite passed whole beside eight
busy loops at no helpers and at two, its internode tests at three, and the
newest tests 1,100 runs without a failure; the pacer's `mix` and `threads`
stayed clean under the same load, and its `threads` still catches the defects
it was built for.

Two findings to settle before the tag, neither in the library's protocols:

- **C9-1 (fix before release)**: ThreadSanitizer's gate at two and three
  helpers passes a test that failed its own checks. ThreadSanitizer replaces a
  reporting process's exit status with its own 66 when the process exits or
  returns from `main`, so a test's failed `CHECK` in any process that also
  reported a race in a heap mapping reads to the gate as "the tool's reports
  alone" - shown with a planted failing check that the gate passed three runs
  of three at two helpers. So for the thirteen to fifteen tests a helper count
  fails under the tool, those runs establish nothing about their results, and
  the README and the release note's draft say more than the gate establishes.
  Round eight's Q2-2 - the gate accepts any race in a heap mapping, the
  library's own words among them - is unchanged in the gate and stands beside
  it.
- **C9-2 (fix before release, if the lead's look confirms it)**: the Pi
  rehearsal's one failure, `shmem.circulant.70` at 1.52 s, is very probably the
  charging, on Linux 6.14 and later, of every io_uring ring to the user's
  locked-memory limit, all of the user's processes on the host together: on the
  Pi's 16 KiB pages its 70 leads needed 4,480 KiB while `circulant.17`, `.33`
  and `.64`, running beside it at `-j4`, held 5,472 KiB of the default 8,192.
  The same arithmetic puts the most logical nodes one host can start at the
  default limit at about 112 on the Pi's pages (56 at two helpers), where the
  README promises `osoe-launch` 254. The development VM's 6.12 charges no ring,
  which is why no VM run sees it.

The rest are minor: a thread cancelled inside a blocking get - outside what
osoe supports - has its buffer written after it is gone (C9-3); the pacer has
no pattern for the newest protocol, which rests on one test program (C9-4);
two stale or tight numbers in the models' registration (C9-5). On correctness,
0.2 with Part 4 added can be tagged from this tree once C9-1 and C9-2 are
settled.

## Findings, ranked

### C9-1. ThreadSanitizer's gate at a helper count passes a test that failed its own checks (fix before release; high confidence; *measured*)

- **Location**: `docs/design/evidence/v0.2-step4/2026-10-10-integration/tsan-gate.py:77-83`
  (a rank's nonzero status must be 66, "TSan's status"; its docstring, lines
  13-15: "every rank that left nonzero left with TSan's status, 66");
  `verify-integration.sh:52-55` (the gate the verification applies at two and
  three helpers); README `:1328-1332` ("Every failure of those runs is checked
  to be the tool's reports alone"); `docs/release-notes/0.2.0.md:134-137` ("at
  two and three helpers every failure the tool's own reports of landings
  through another process"); `docs/decisions/2026-10-08-helpers.md:188-191`
  (the gate "requires each failing test to have failed by the tool's reports
  alone ... every bad exit the tool's status"). The tests' `CHECK`
  (`shmem/tests/check.h:11-16`) records a failure and lets `main` return
  nonzero.
- **Failure scenario**: under ThreadSanitizer at two helpers a test reports a
  helper's landing in its heap - as about a dozen tests do at each count - and
  in the same process a real defect makes one of its checks fail. The process leaves
  with 66 rather than 1, the gate reads the 66 as the tool's own, and the test
  passes the gate as "the tool's reports alone". The same holds for a test that
  never printed its result: the launcher ends the job's other ranks with SIGTERM
  once one leaves with 66, so a test whose pass line comes from a rank so ended
  fails ctest's `PASS_REGULAR_EXPRESSION`, and the gate passes that too.
- **Evidence** (*measured*):
  - **The status** (`tsan/exitprobe/race-exit.c`, gcc's ThreadSanitizer in the
    development VM): a program that has reported a race and then fails leaves
    with 66 when it returns 1 from `main` or calls `exit(1)`, and keeps its own
    status only through `_exit(1)` or `_Exit(1)` (1) or `abort()` (134). So
    `CHECK_FATAL` and the library's fatal, which `_exit`, are seen; `CHECK`,
    which returns from `main`, is not.
  - **End to end in osoe** (`vm/job5-tsan.sh`, `vm/logs/tsan-demo-*.full`): a
    copy of `test-ack-echo.c` with one planted `if (me == 1) CHECK(0 == 1);`,
    compiled and linked as the test is, in the ThreadSanitizer preset. At two
    helpers, three runs of three: PE 1 reports a helper's landing against its
    main thread's store (`osoe-helper-1` in `osoe_am_parse` against
    `test-ack-echo.c:42`, in the heap mapping), prints
    `demo-ack-echo.c:62: CHECK(0 == 1) failed`, "rank 1 exited with status 66",
    and `tsan-gate.py` prints **TSAN GATE PASSED**. At no helpers, three of
    three: no report, status 1, **TSAN GATE FAILED** - the strict preset would
    have failed it anyway.
  - **What the integration's runs judged** (the lead's
    `verify-integration-4df0047/tsan-OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS_{2,3}.full`, 4df0047's
    code being 3b45472's): 15 failed tests at two helpers and 13 at three, every
    one passed by the gate; of them 5 and 4 never printed their result line
    (`shmem.circulant.*`: "Required regular expression not found"), and 11 and
    14 ranks were ended by the launcher's SIGTERM. For those tests the runs at a
    helper count said nothing about whether the result was right.
  - **Round eight's Q2-2 is unchanged in the gate**: `diff` of
    `2026-10-08-helpers/verification/tsan-gate.py` and the integration's copy
    is the licence line alone. It still requires no helper's frame and accepts
    every race in a heap mapping, the library's own words included. At three
    helpers it accepted two helpers writing one library word in
    `shmem.circulant.6` on nodes 3, 4 and 5: heap address `0x010000e88488`, which
    the region's own layout functions place (`tsan/layout/layout.c`, 6 nodes,
    `SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE=12m`) at internal offset `0x288488`, team number 0's
    tree grant words - successive collectives' parents writing one child's
    grant, ordered through other processes, as the README says; the gate
    cannot tell that from a race.
- **Why it matters, and why not more**: the tag's checklist runs the four
  presets "at no helpers and at two and three", and the release note will
  report ThreadSanitizer's part as above. A wrong result that shows only under
  ThreadSanitizer's timing at a helper count - the runs whose timing differs
  most from the others' - would pass. Not more, because debug, release and
  AddressSanitizer run the same tests at the same helper counts and check their
  results, and ThreadSanitizer at no helpers fails on any report (the credit_first
  mutant below failed it in every run).
- **Remedy**: run ThreadSanitizer at a helper count with
  `TSAN_OPTIONS=exitcode=0` and its reports to a file
  (`log_path`), so that a report changes no rank's status and ends no job
  early; require every test to pass as ctest judges it, and gate the reports
  alone, read from the files. Smaller: fail the gate on any `CHECK(` line, any
  `osoe: PE` fatal and any test whose pass expression is missing - the last
  common now, since the launcher's early SIGTERM is what hides the pass lines.
  Either way, correct the README's and the release note's sentences to what is
  checked, and take round eight's Q2-2 remedy (a helper's frame in every
  accepted report; the internal region told apart) with it.

### C9-2. The Pi's `shmem.circulant.70`: io_uring's rings charged to one locked-memory limit across all of a user's processes (fix before release, if confirmed; medium-high confidence; sizes *measured*, kernel accounting *recalled* and *documented*)

- **Location**: `ring.c:90-97` (each owner's ring: a submission queue of the
  next power of two above 2 × wires + 1 entries, a completion queue of the
  receive buffers + 2 × wires + 1, rounded up by the kernel) and `:107` (the
  provided-buffer ring); README `:485-487` (`osoe-launch`: "up to 1,024
  processes on 254 logical nodes") and `:1034-1038` (on Linux 6.14 and later
  io_uring charges a node's rings to the user's locked memory, every process
  of the user on the host together, against each one's `ulimit -l`, 8 MiB by
  default); `docs/decisions/2026-10-10-zero-copy-locked-memory.md` ("What it
  was": on the Pi's 6.18 under `ulimit -l 0` the start-up check's ring and the
  provided-buffer ring are refused). The lead's rehearsal log
  `pi-rehearsal-3b45472/a/tier2.txt:984-1006`.
- **What happened** (*documented* in the lead's log): `shmem.circulant.70`
  started while `shmem.circulant.17`, `.33` and `.64` were running
  (`ctest -L tier2 -j4` inside `srun -N1 -n1 -c4`, kernel 6.18.50, 16 KiB pages,
  and `osoe-probe`'s "locked-memory limit (ulimit -l): 8192 KiB" on every node,
  `a/probe.txt`), and failed in 1.52 s - inside `shmem_init` - with its result
  line missing; the other three passed in 11.6, 18.0 and 24.1 s. The run kept
  no test's output, so its message is not in the log.
- **The mechanism** (*recalled*: Linux 6.14 and later account a ring's regions
  - the rings, the submission entries, and a registered provided-buffer ring -
  in whole pages to the user's `locked_vm`, against the calling process's
  `RLIMIT_MEMLOCK`; the zero-copy record measured the refusal on the Pi). Each
  lead's ring regions, read from `/proc/PID/maps` of a running job
  (`vm/job6-rings.sh`, *measured*; bytes are the kernel's, whatever the page):

  | Logical nodes | Submission entries | Rings | On 16 KiB pages, with the provided-buffer ring's page |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 17 | 8 KiB | 8 KiB | 48 KiB a lead |
  | 33 | 16 KiB | 8 KiB | 48 KiB |
  | 64 | 16 KiB | 12 KiB | 48 KiB |
  | 70 | 32 KiB | 12 KiB | 64 KiB |
  | 70, two helpers | three rings: 8+8, 8+8, 16+8 KiB | | 144 KiB |
  | 254 | 64 KiB | 36 KiB | 128 KiB |

  The three running tests held 114 × 48 = 5,472 KiB; `circulant.70`'s 70 leads
  needed 4,480 KiB more: 9,952 KiB against 8,192. Room for 42 of its leads;
  the 43rd's ring is refused, and a refused ring ends the job at start-up
  naming the limit (README `:1036-1038`), which a 1.52 s failure fits. Each PE's
  start-up check besides makes and frees a small ring and buffer ring of its own
  (`base/ring-check.c:20-68`), three pages more for a moment, so a job's start
  is tighter than its steady state.
- **What follows** (arithmetic from the measured sizes, *observed in source*
  for the bounds): at the default 8 MiB, one host's logical nodes - all of a
  user's jobs together - fit to about 112 at no helpers on 16 KiB pages (113
  doubles the completion queue: 80 KiB a lead), about 128 on 4 KiB pages, and
  about 56 at two helpers on 16 KiB pages; 254 logical nodes need 31.75 MiB on
  the Pi. So on the cluster's own kernel the suite's tier 2 at `-j4` passes or
  fails by which of its many-node tests overlap, and the README's 254 does not
  start without a raised `ulimit -l`. The development VM's 6.12 charges no ring:
  a 254-node job started there (`vm/logs/job6.log`), and every VM run of the
  suite passes.
- **Remedy**: first confirm it - `circulant.70` alone passes, and with the other
  three it fails naming "Cannot allocate memory ... ulimit -l" (the rehearsal's
  ctest needs `--output-on-failure`). Then the README's 254 qualified by kernel
  and limit (the bound per host, and that it counts every job of the user's);
  the many-node tests given a `RESOURCE_LOCK` on a 6.14+ kernel, or the Pi's
  runbook raising `ulimit -l`. The lead holds the examination; this is my
  reading of its log, not a run on the Pi.

### C9-3. A thread cancelled inside a blocking get has its buffer written after it is gone (minor; high confidence; *measured*)

- **Location**: `transport-internode.c:1033-1078` (`remote_get`'s pipelined
  path, the comment at `:1046-1051`), `:839-880` (`make_room` copies out any
  landed piece, `copy_out_any`), `:1132-1186` (`complete_gets` copies only its
  own pending pieces and waits for its block's cumulative counters to agree);
  `metrics.c:576-590` (a thread's place, and so its block, goes back at its
  exit, cancellation included); `base/sweep.h:66-70`;
  `docs/decisions/2026-10-10-blocking-gets.md`, "(b), as built".
- **Failure scenario**: thread A of a `SHMEM_THREAD_MULTIPLE` PE is cancelled in
  a large blocking get with its pieces in flight. Its place goes back; its
  pieces land and stay LANDED, holding the zone. Thread B takes the place. If
  the stale pieces fill the zone, B's first issue copies them out - into A's
  buffer, which may be freed or an unwound stack - and their bytes balance the
  block, so B's get completes. If they leave room for all of B's pieces, B never
  copies them, and its get waits for bytes nobody copies: the block's issued
  count carries A's stale bytes and B's wait copies only its own pieces - until
  some issue of the PE's finds the zone full (*observed in source*; not run).
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `cancel/cancel-get.c`, `vm/logs/job4.log`): PE 0's
  thread 1 starts a 16 MiB get from PE 1 on the other node with asynchronous
  cancellation and is cancelled after 1 to 6 ms (4.7 to 12.6 MB in place); its
  buffer, still allocated, is filled with a canary; thread 2 then gets 1 MiB,
  correct, in 1 to 2 ms. In each of four runs 196,512 to 262,016 bytes of the
  cancelled thread's buffer were written after it was gone - three or four
  pieces of 65,504 bytes, what the zone held.
- **Why minor**: OpenSHMEM defines no cancellation, the library does not
  support it (`base/sweep.h`), and the record says this. With the default
  deferred cancellation the only cancellation point in these waits is
  `count_if_lost`'s `nanosleep` (`transport-internode.c:503`) - the futex waits
  are raw `syscall`s and the sweep calls nothing that cancels once the agent
  runs (`sweep.c:46-65`) - so it takes asynchronous cancellation, or that rare
  path. But the README says nothing of it, and the consequence moved from an
  extent leaked (on `main` before) to a write into memory the program may have
  freed.
- **Remedy**: a sentence in the README's threads limits: a thread must not be
  cancelled, or leave by `longjmp`, inside a call.

### C9-4. The stress tester has no pattern for the newest protocol (after release; high confidence; *measured*)

- **Location**: `tools/pacer/threads.c:46` (`ROW = 16`, `TABLE = 16`: every get
  of `threads` is one piece), `tools/pacer/alltoall.c:63` and `:340-354` (`mix`'s
  largest blocking get, 64 KiB - two pieces - from one thread).
- **What it means**: a blocking get of several pieces from several threads of
  a PE at once - another thread copying a piece into a waiting caller's buffer
  (`make_room`'s `copy_out_any`) - is exercised by `shmem.multiple.*` alone.
- **Evidence** (*measured*, `mutants/mutate.py`, `vm/logs/m-credit_first-*`,
  `tsan-m-credit-*`): the mutant `credit_first` - `copy_out` counting a piece's
  bytes and waking before it copies them, `copy_out_claim_model.py`'s breakage of
  that name - passed 60 s of `mix` (`failures=0`), while 5 of the 12
  `shmem.multiple.*` and `shmem.get-pieces.*` tests failed within ten repeats in
  the release build (15 "a blocking get of several pieces" checks among the
  failures, every cross-node map of `shmem.multiple` failing; the two
  single-threaded `get-pieces` tests passing), and ThreadSanitizer reported 17 to
  22 races in each of six runs of `shmem.multiple.0-1`, at no helpers and at two.
  So the test program holds it well; the pacer would not.
- **Remedy**: `threads` with a blocking get of several pieces into each worker's
  own buffer, checked word by word. After release.

## Round eight's findings in this area

- **Q2-1, keepalive on loopback: fixed, and fixed right.** Keepalive and the
  silent-peer check are off where every node's advertised address is loopback
  and `OSOE_KEEPALIVE` is unset (`progress-host.c:798-822`, `:529-552`). Every
  lead computes it from the same rendezvous table, so a job's leads agree; a
  job that mixes loopback and real addresses keeps keepalive everywhere, and
  can only arise on one host anyway - `osoe_listener_own_address`
  (`listener.c:773-806`) advertises a host's real address when another node's
  is not loopback, and a remote node cannot reach a loopback one. What stays
  exposed is a job on one host whose nodes advertise real addresses
  (containers, several `slurmd` on one machine), not a case the README claims.
  Under eight busy loops at three helpers `circulant.33`, `.64` and `.70`
  passed three runs each, 12 to 26 s, and all 467 internode tests passed (*measured*).
- **Q2-2, the gate: documented, not changed** (C9-1).
- **Q2-3, the detector's helper half held by its model alone: as documented**;
  `deadlock_owners_model.py`'s three breakages fail as stated (below).
- **Q2-4, the grant's head two helpers wrote: fixed, and fixed right.** One
  field a round, written by that round's receiver alone - the receiver of round
  k of the sender is the same lead whichever side counts the round
  (`internode-generic.c:1775-1793`, `:1612-1647`); in the integration's
  ThreadSanitizer logs no report at two or three helpers lies in the circulant
  words (*measured*, `tsan/classify.py`): the helper-against-helper reports left
  are the tree's grant words (C9-1) and `shmem.lanes.rotation`'s lane word.
- **Round eight's unexplained `circulant.16.tree` timeout**: passing at three
  helpers under eight busy loops in 18.6 to 65.8 s, three runs.

## Not findings

- **`shmem.memory-orders` and §3.16**: the check's blind spots are real -
  operators on an `_Atomic` object, `__atomic_` builtins, a protocol word not
  declared atomic, the tools - but none hides a violation today. Every explicit
  call's order argument across `shmem/src`, `base` and `tools`, parsed across
  lines (`static/orders.py`), is `acquire`, `release` or `seq_cst`, with no load
  naming release, no store naming acquire and no compare-and-swap failing with
  release; Clang's `-Watomic-implicit-seq-cst` over the release build's 467
  compile units, `tools` included, gives none - and gives four on a planted file
  (`static/implicit-seq-cst.sh`). No builtins in the tree; the pacer's own
  statistics counters are its only `relaxed`. The plain words the newest work
  leans on are data published by release and acquire, not protocol words: `copy_destination[]`
  before a record's `ISSUED`, the grant fields before their fenced add, the
  unsent request's two flags before its `seq_cst` store.
- **A quiet on the shared default context beside a blocking get's pieces**:
  `OSOE_CHECK_QUIET`'s promise check judges only its context's own block
  (`transport-internode.c:1768-1770`), and the filter is what keeps it right:
  made to judge the thread places' pieces too (`promise_thread_places`),
  `shmem.multiple.checked.*` fired at once on both maps, a blocking get's pieces
  in flight as another thread's quiet returned, while `threads`, which has none,
  stayed clean three runs of three (*measured*, `vm/logs/job7.log`).
- **A thread place reused after its thread exits**: its block's counters are
  cumulative and equal whenever no get of its is in flight, and `copy_out` adds
  only after its copy, so a copier still finishing touches the block's wake word
  and the zone, never the caller's buffer (*observed in source*);
  `shmem.multiple.*`'s second wave takes the first wave's places, 500 runs here.
- **The fallback past block 4,095**: places whose block a token's twelve bits
  cannot carry take the piece-at-a-time path (`transport-internode.c:1071`), and
  the lead's `landed()` bound now admits the thread places' blocks
  (`progress-host.c:162`); `shmem.get-pieces.fallback.0-1` holds it.

## What is sound, and what convinced me

**The models, all of them.** 45 runs - 34 models, `selftest`, both
`shape_timing` checks, `circulant` and its digest, and the six of §4.11 with
`get_completion`'s full cases - exit 0 in two serial streams in 3,851 s, the
largest `reservation` 1,278 s, `get_completion` 1,008, `deadlock_owners` 958,
`abort` 811; the VM's available memory never below 1,877 MiB (*measured*,
`vm/logs/models-*.log`). Read case by case from the `-V` logs
(`vm/model-verdicts.py` for the models that print one line a case, each
model's own summary for the rest - "ALL AS EXPECTED", "fails, as it must",
"REDUCTIONS PRESERVE EVERY VERDICT"), every breakage fails with the verdict
its docstring states - STUCK, LOST WAKE, VIOLATION or TRAPPED on the property
written for it - and every design case holds, the expected residuals of
`abort_exit` (two nodes, two callers) and `ack` (the bound one below what the
design needs) failing as their tables say. The recorded
numbers I could match match: `zc_refused` 13,511, 48,216, 6,236,896 and 142,638
states with liveness and 23,158,348 under x86-TSO without, and
`zc_refused_owes_none` failing on "SEQ: message 2 left in segment 1"
(`2026-10-10-zero-copy-locked-memory.md`); `deadlock_owners`' largest 2.3
million. The six §4.11 models and the engine are unchanged since 0.1.0's tag
(`git diff v0.1.0`), so their verdicts could not have moved; this is their run.

**The blocking get's pieces, as built.** Read against
`docs/decisions/2026-10-10-blocking-gets.md` and the three models it leans on:
the caller's own block, one issuer and one waiter, pair 0 only, counters
cumulative; a piece copied by another thread is copied before it is counted,
the count a `seq_cst` add the waiter's `seq_cst` load acquires; a record's token
written before `ISSUED`, the copier's claim a compare-and-swap from `LANDED`;
the lead's bound covering the places' blocks. The composition no model has
exactly - a thread waiting for room only its own landed pieces can free - is
the wait word's rule (a landing bumps `freed`, `progress-host.c:176`) and is
exercised by `shmem.get-pieces.0-1` (`am.landing_full` at least one). And
broken on purpose it is caught: `credit_first` (C9-4) by `shmem.multiple.*` and
by ThreadSanitizer at no helpers in every run; the promise filter (above).

**The zero-copy send's refusal.** `ring.c:251-310`: a notification decrements
only what a completion's `IORING_CQE_F_MORE` added, so a refusal that says a
notification follows owes it and one that does not owes nothing; the refused
wire is marked free and readable, so the same pass offers the bytes again by
copy, and lends no more; a copied send's `-ENOMEM` stays a failure. On 6.18 a
refusal stops the submission at its entry, and what stays queued is one send
and one re-arm a wire and the futex wait, within the submission bound
(`:90`), submitted by the next reap. The Pi's run of the refused tests at
`e53c9e2` shows each node's notice once (*documented*). `BOTH LENT` is checked
in the refusal cases and holds; a refusal cannot reach it, since a part goes by
reference only while the other buffer owes nothing.
