## Verdict

**Yes, in this question's area.** Nothing blocks the release, and nothing must
precede it; four minors, below.

- **The large collectives' new shapes give the specification's results** (*measured*).
  A probe calling 15 typed reductions - every operator, `long`, `int`, `double`,
  `float`, `unsigned long`, `long double`, `double _Complex`, `unsigned char`,

### 2. The large collectives' shapes, against §9.10.7 and §9.10.9

**By construction** (*observed in source*). `shape_of` (`internode-generic.c:751-764`)
returns 0.1.0's tree under `fixed`, and otherwise `osoe_shape_choose` over the team's
node count, the call's bytes and the job's link - integer arithmetic over values every
node holds alike: the link the root measured and every PE took before `shmem_init`
returned, or the `OSOE_LINK_*` settings, which the rendezvous compares; the setting,
compared; and the call's arguments, which §9.10 has every member pass alike. So every
node runs the same algorithm for the same call, and no message negotiates it. Träff's
cuts the vector into blocks of elements, not bytes (`collective-circulant.c:17-41`), so
no element straddles two leads, and its 16 KiB pieces divide by every element width;
every block of the result is combined at one lead and copied to the others, and a
node's members take their lead's copy, so every member holds the same bits.

member holds the same bits in any one call (above), §9.10.9's Notes leave floating
reductions' rounding open, and the README offers `fixed` as "the setting that makes a
run reproducible" (`README.md:1393`).

### 3. The helpers, against §9.12, §9.8, §3.2 and §9.11

**By construction** (*observed in source*):

- **One wire, one thread, a peer.** A peer's data wire is owner 1 + (s mod H)'s, s its
  peer slot (`owner-map.h:30-41`), and every put, get, atomic, signal and
  acknowledgement between two nodes rides it; one thread parses and applies it in
  arrival order. So the orders §9.12.1 asks for - operations to one PE, delivered in
  issue order across a fence - and put-with-signal's - the put, then the signal fenced
  behind it on the same context and PE (`signal.c:77-107`) - hold whatever H is: no
  operation to one PE can ride two threads. The control wire carries only the
  barrier's, the detector's and the goodbye's messages; the data collectives' pieces,
  grants and releases are puts and fenced adds on the data wires
  (`internode-generic.c:9-24`), and a barrier quiets before its arrival
  (`barrier.c:143-163`), so nothing a program can observe needs an order between the
  two wires (Part 2's adversarial read, "Ordering between the planes", which I re-read
  against the code and which holds).
- **Quiet is remote completion whoever lands the data.** An owner records a segment as
  applied only after every message in it is applied (`progress.c:785-792`), every
  segment it sends carries that count as its acknowledgement (`:1115`, `:1431`), and
  the origin publishes what it receives for its PEs' quiet (`:800-806`). §9.12.2's
  "complete and visible to all PEs" then holds through whichever helper applied the
  bytes; `pe_quiet` (§9.12.3) waits on the same counts, for the PEs it names.
- **Atomicity across helpers** (§3.2, §9.8.1). Every applied atomic and signal update
  is `osoe_rmw_apply` (`rmw.h:20-66`): a C11 seq_cst read-modify-write on the
  target's shared mapping, the same instruction the target's own atomics and every
  other helper's use. Two helpers landing atomics on one word are two CPUs' atomics on
  one address, exclusive by the hardware; what is not exclusive - a put and an atomic,
  or two puts, to one location at once - §3.2 leaves undefined.
- **Wakes.** A helper announces a landing through `osoe_transport_notify` (`watch.c:106`,
  called from `progress-host.c:219`), which every PE of a node already called
  concurrently before step 4; and the deadlock detector leaves the helpers out of a
  PE's threads by the tids they publish (`deadlock.c:98-112`), so a lead with helpers
  is still judged.

**Measured** (`order.c`, `sigadd.c`): six tests, each across nodes, every PE at once:

1. **fence** - a stream of puts of 1,000, 16,385 and 200,000 `long`s to a PE `k`
   ranks on, for every `k`, each followed by `shmem_fence` and a flag put; the target
   waits for the flag and finds every element in place;
2. **quiet** - every PE puts 20,000 `long`s to every other PE with `put_nbi`, quiets,
   and adds one at a checker half the job away, which then gets every target's copy;
3. **put-with-signal** - blocking and nonblocking, `SHMEM_SIGNAL_SET` and `ADD`, 3,000
   and 200,000 elements, the target in `shmem_signal_wait_until`, the data in place
   when the signal is seen, `signal_fetch` agreeing;
4. **atomics** - every PE `fetch_add`s, `add`s, CAS-increments, `fetch_inc`s and
   `inc`s one word on PE 0, which does the same, 200 to 1,200 rounds: the word exact,
   and every `fetch_add`'s old value distinct across the job (an `fcollect` and a
   sort); then `fetch_or`, `xor`, `or` and `and` of each PE's own bit, the word back
   to 0;
5. **wakes** - ping-pong by `atomic_set` and `wait_until` between PEs half a job
   apart, `wait_until_any` over every PE's flag, `signal_wait_until` woken by a
   `put_signal` from the PE before;
6. **pe_quiet** - puts to every other PE, `pe_quiet` on one, then a flag to it, which
   finds that put in place.

And `sigadd.c`: every PE but PE 0 updates PE 0's one signal word 300 times -
`put_signal` and `put_signal_nbi` with `SHMEM_SIGNAL_ADD`, and `put_nbi`, `shmem_fence`
and `shmem_signal_add` - and then every PE adds to every other's word, §9.8.1's
atomicity of signal updates with one another and with the waits: the totals exact,
and every source's last payload in place when PE 0's `signal_wait_until` saw its
total, on 4 × 2 at 0, 2 and 3 helpers, 6 × 2 cyclic at 3 and five nodes at 2, and on
the Pi at 4 × 2 at 0, 2 and 3 (23:32).

- **SOS `spam` failing at two helpers** (the lead's sweep): by every sign the sweep's

  at two helpers, and at three in 24 s (§6).

  buffer's room does, down to about 4 KiB, at a multiple of 8 bytes from the put's start
  (`transport-internode.c:600-636`: the reservation is padded to 8 and the message's
  fixed part is 24 bytes) - so an element of 8 bytes or fewer is never split, but a
  16-byte one, a `long double` or a `double _Complex`, can be, as it could before at a
  chunk's 65,512 bytes. Only a reader racing the put could see half of one, which §3.2
  (items 3 and 4) leaves undefined; and a put of fewer bytes than the least part is
  never cut - it waits for room whole. My probes' every check after a fence, a quiet or
  a signal, the 16-byte types' among them, found whole elements.

  on every member (§2).
- **The deadlock detector with helpers**: they are left out of a PE's threads by tid

  only `wire-settings.h:114-116`'s comment says otherwise - a comment, outside this

## What is sound, and what convinced me

- **The new algorithms compute what §9.10 specifies.** Not because the model holds,
  though it does, but because a probe written from §9.10.9's text - every operator,
  nine types, seven teams, in place and not, sixteen counts, rounding sums compared bit
  for bit across members - passed on twelve maps of 2 to 20 nodes and on the Pi,
  under every shape setting, with Träff's forced and with the link as measured, at 0, 2
  and 3 helpers; and because the same probe fails on every team the moment Träff's
  arithmetic is broken.
- **Every node takes the same shape for the same call**, by integer arithmetic over
  values the rendezvous compares or the root hands every PE before `shmem_init`
  returns; and a divergence between shapes, which only an erroneous program can cause,
  ends the job through the deadlock detector with every PE's wait named, and no PE
  returning with a wrong result.
- **The helpers are invisible to a program.** The ordering rules hold by
  construction - one wire and one thread a peer, acknowledgement after application, one
  seq_cst read-modify-write for every atomic - and the probes found no exception on
  eight maps and the Pi, at three helper counts, under zero-copy, the smallest chunk
  and cut-short sends; the whole-API probe's output did not change by a byte.
- **The public interface did not move**: the headers and the API's files unchanged
  since the first review point, the same 3,595 exports.
- **The previous round's four findings were fixed**, three wholly; the fourth's (C3)
  scope did not grow with the settings.

  TSO changes anything a program sees through the helpers' words is the portability

- **A fast link**, on which helpers would run truly in parallel with real NICs: the
  VM's four cores gave three helpers some parallelism over loopback, and the Pi's
  gigabit runs at 2 and 3 helpers were short (*measured*, about three minutes in all,

  a lowered ceiling - `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_FANOUT=2` leaves Träff's three rows, so nine nodes
  take trees for the world while their smaller teams take Träff's - and the probe passed
  at 9 nodes and two helpers and at 8 and three (*measured*). Twenty nodes was the most
  this round ran.
- **Two threads of one PE in two teams' Träff's reductions at once**
  (`SHMEM_THREAD_MULTIPLE`): the suite's `threads` mode does it; my probes are
  single-threaded but for `observe.c`'s `multiple` runs and `threads.c`'s RMA.
- **The single-node build** was not rebuilt; C1 is from its source.
- The lead's merge-point runs - the presets at 2 and 3 helpers, the 70-PE acceptance,
