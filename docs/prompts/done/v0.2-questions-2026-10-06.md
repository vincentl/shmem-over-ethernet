# v0.2: the open questions after the night of 2026-10-06, explained

Five decisions are yours; each section below stands alone - what the question
is about, what was found and how, the options with what each costs, and one
recommendation. The labels in parentheses are the records' (step 1's record,
`docs/decisions/2026-10-06-single-node-seam.md`; step 2's,
`2026-10-06-tree-shape.md`; step 3's, `2026-10-06-rabenseifner.md`). The sixth
question of the night, the version `main` reports between tags, you answered:
the most recent tag's (`docs/decisions/2026-10-06.md` item 1).

Two of the answers depend on each other, and one on a third: the window
(section 4) sets how far step 3's exchanges can overlap (section 6), and the
algorithm for node counts that are not powers of two (section 5) decides
whether section 6's design applies at all. They are in that order below so
that each can be read alone, but the summary at the end puts them together.

## 1. Step 1: the io_uring start-up check, moved below the seam (Part 1's choice 2)

**What the seam is.** The transport is in two parts since step 1: what every
job uses (`transport-am.c` - the dispatch on locality, the contexts, a one-node
job's fence and wait), and the *internode backend*, which only a job of more
than one node needs - today AM over TCP and io_uring, one day perhaps RDMA, and
in the single-node build a backend that refuses. The SHMEM layer above
(`init.c`, the routines) talks to the transport only through the calls
`transport.h` lists; the first part talks to the backend only through
`internode-backend.h`. That boundary - design §4.10 - is "the seam".

**What changed.** At 0.1.0, `init.c` - the SHMEM layer - made io_uring's
start-up check itself: for a job of more than one node it called
`base/ring-check.c`, and refused by name ("osoe needs io_uring with ... for a
job of 2 nodes") before anything existed in `/dev/shm`, telling no other PE
(every PE has the same kernel, so each refuses alone and no tombstone is left).
So the SHMEM layer knew that the transport is built on io_uring.

Step 1 needed two things from that spot. The single-node build compiles no
`ring-check.c`, so `init.c` could not keep the call without a branch on the
build - which the brief rules out. And the single-node build must refuse a job
of more than one node at that same moment: by name, before anything is made,
telling no one.

What I built adds no call and changes no signature: `init.c` already calls
`osoe_transport_settings_check()` on every PE before its node joins, to check
the transport's settings as values. That call now first asks the backend
"can you run a job of this many nodes here?" (`osoe_internode_backend_check`,
which answers NULL or a sentence) and, if not, refuses telling no one; then it
checks the settings as before. AM's backend answers with the io_uring check -
the same check, the same words; the single-node backend answers "this is
osoe's single-node build ..., and this job spans 2 nodes: the full build runs
it". The rule that such a refusal tells no one lives in `transport-am.c`, the
part every build compiles, so no backend has to remember it.

**Why it is yours.** The step's brief says to stop and ask before any change to
the seam's shape - "a new upcall, a changed signature in `transport.h` or
`internode.h`". No signature changed, but a `transport.h` call's contract grew:
it now also judges the kernel, and refuses in a particular way. The
adversarial read before the merge called that a change of shape in substance.
I built it without waiting because the second part needed exactly this hook,
and recorded it for you.

**What it buys and costs.** The SHMEM layer no longer knows what the
transport is made of: an RDMA backend would answer the same question with its
own facts (a verbs device, say) without touching `init.c`. Nothing a user sees
changed: the full build's io_uring refusal is word for word 0.1.0's (the
review's readiness investigator ran the 43 refusal tests against it), and the
single-node build's refusal is by name, on every PE, before anything is made
(three investigators checked it under every launch path they tried). The cost
is one of naming: a call called *settings* check now also checks the kernel.

**Options.**
- (a) Keep it as built.
- (b) Keep it, and rename the call to say what it does - for instance
  `osoe_transport_join_check`, "what the transport needs before this PE's node
  joins": a rename in a few files and comments, no behaviour.
- (c) Give the kernel check a call of its own in `transport.h`, beside the
  settings check: the contract split in two, one more call every backend's
  first part provides.
- (d) Undo: `init.c` calls the io_uring check again. The single-node build
  then still needs a way to refuse early, which is (c)'s new call anyway - so
  (d) is (c) plus io_uring knowledge back in the SHMEM layer.

**Recommendation: (b)** - the design as built, with a name that matches its
contract. (a) costs nothing and is fine; (c) is a second call where one says
the whole thing.

## 2. Step 2: how good must the cost model's fit be? (step 2's question 1)

**What the model is for.** Step 2 makes each large reduction and broadcast
choose its tree's shape - the fan-out k (how many children a node lead has)
and the window W (how many pieces are in flight) - from the link instead of
fixed settings. The choice comes from a cost model: the time of a pipelined
k-ary tree over N nodes for n bytes, given three numbers about the link - the
hop h (one small message's one-way time, through both progress threads), the
message cost o (what a lead's loop spends per message it sends), and the
bandwidth B. The brief asked that the model first reproduce the Pi's measured
table "every line ... within the noise", or choose nothing.

**What was found** (run in the main session, `tools/modelcheck/shape_timing.py`).
The table is 402 measured lines from 2026-09-26: broadcasts and reductions on
the Pi's four nodes, 4 bytes to 1 MiB, k = 2, 3, 4 and D = 1 to 8. The best fit
- h = 86 µs, o = 7 µs, B = 116 bytes/µs (about 0.93 Gbit/s) - puts 253 lines
(63%) within their noise, median error 1.9%. The misses gather in one band:

| bytes | lines | within the noise | median error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 - 512 | 176 | 146 | 0.7% |
| 1 - 8 KiB | 88 | 17 | 7.2% |
| 16 KiB | 22 | 9 | 3.2% |
| 32 - 128 KiB | 62 | 41 | 6.1% |
| 256 KiB - 1 MiB | 54 | 40 | 2.6% |

Between 2 and 8 KiB the Pi moves bytes faster than its own link speed allows:
a k = 2 broadcast takes 406 µs at 2 KiB, 408 at 4 KiB and 418 at 8 KiB - 4 KiB
more over two hops for 2 µs. At 1 KiB it is slower than the bytes explain. So
the transport has a cost that steps near 1 KiB and then overlaps transmission;
three numbers about a link cannot express it. I tried a fourth constant (part
of the hop overlapping a message's own wire time) and the fit set it to zero:
there is no fourth term I can put to you.

**What the misses cost.** The model's job is the choice, not the time, and the
table can judge choices directly: for every call measured under several
shapes, compare the measured time of the shape the model picks with the
fastest measured shape. The model's pick is within 0.2% of the fastest for
half the calls, within 3.9% for nine in ten, and 9.7% at worst - a 32 KiB
broadcast in a band where the noise itself is 8%.

**Options.**
- (a) Change the bar from "predicts every line within the noise" to "chooses
  within the noise of the best measured shape", which it meets; and fold into
  step 2's Pi session (Part 2, which measures the chosen shape against the
  fixed one at every size anyway) a sweep of 1 to 16 KiB, then refit.
- (b) Hold Part 2 for a Pi session that hunts the missing term first: one put,
  and one put with a quiet, between two leads from 8 bytes to 64 KiB, with the
  progress threads' wakes counted. It may find nothing.
- (c) Fit only the lines where choices are made - the crossover's band and the
  large sizes - and accept larger errors elsewhere: (a) by another route.

**Recommendation: (a).** The misses change no choice by more than the noise,
the claim that protects a program is Part 2's - "no size slower than `fixed`
by more than the noise", measured on the Pi - and a term found later changes
one function in Python and one in C.

## 3. Step 2: where the link is measured (step 2's question 2)

**What the brief asked.** At start-up, "once the leads' mesh is built and before
the start-up release travels down (`rendezvous.c`, `send_down`)", the root's
lead measures h, o and B against its first child, and the three numbers travel
to every node in the release's header, so that every node chooses the same
shapes.

**The problem** (read in source). `send_down` is what tells each node the other
nodes' addresses: the mesh - the TCP connections the data path uses - is built
after it, and the progress threads start after that. Before `send_down` the
only connection between two leads is the rendezvous's own blocking socket. A
measurement there would miss both progress threads, which are most of the Pi's
86 µs hop - and the chooser, believing hops cheap, would build trees too deep
for small messages.

**Options.**
- (a) Measure at the end of `shmem_init`, through the data path: the root's
  lead and its first child's, with both progress threads running - a few round
  trips of small puts for h, bursts of small puts for o, a stream for B - then
  broadcast the three numbers (24 bytes, through the world team's small
  broadcast, which needs no shape) before `shmem_init` returns. Every node then
  holds the same numbers before any collective can choose. Its cost, estimated
  from the fitted link (not measured): about 15 ms of start-up on the Pi
  (16 round trips ≈ 3 ms, bursts ≈ 1 ms, a 1 MiB stream ≈ 9 ms), a millisecond
  or two on one machine's loopback; nothing when the link is set by hand
  (`OSOE_LINK_HOP_NS`, `_MESSAGE_NS`, `_BYTES_PER_SECOND`) or the shape is
  `fixed`.
- (b) Measure over the rendezvous's socket, before `send_down`, as written:
  cheaper to place, but it measures the wrong hop, unless a constant for the
  progress threads is added - a fourth constant by another name.
- (c) Measure lazily, at the first large collective: every node must agree on
  the moment, and the first such call pays for the measurement.

**Two things true of every option.** The root's link stands for every link -
the brief's assumption of a uniform fabric; a cluster whose links differ can
set them by hand. And a measurement at start-up varies from run to run, so
near a crossover two runs of one program may choose differently - at a
crossover the shapes cost about the same, and `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_SHAPE=fixed`
gives 0.1.0's shapes when a run must be reproducible.

**Recommendation: (a).** It measures the hop a collective actually pays, adds no
message to the rendezvous, and costs a start-up about 15 ms on the Pi. Nothing
else in step 2 waits on it: the settings, their agreement and `osoe-stat`'s
display of the link are built.

## 4. Step 2: the defaults' memory (step 2's question 3)

**What the two defaults are.** The *ceiling* is the largest fan-out the chooser
may use (today's `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_FANOUT`, 4, becomes a ceiling); the *capacity*
is the largest window, the pieces of 16 KiB in flight (today's
`OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PIPELINE`, 2). The brief asks for 8 and 4, and to stop if that
costs a PE more than twice the tag's memory for collectives.

**Where the memory is.** Every PE's heap object in `/dev/shm` ends in an internal
region the internode phase of collectives uses: per team number, a row of small
slots for each possible child; per thread, a *lane* of slots for large pieces,
whose size grows with ceiling × capacity. Computed from `internal-region.h`
(256 team numbers, 17 lanes, four nodes):

| ceiling, capacity | a lane | the region, a PE | against 0.1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4, 2 (0.1.0) | 160 KiB | 6.03 MiB | 1 |
| 4, 4 | 320 KiB | 8.69 MiB | 1.44 |
| 8, 2 | 288 KiB | 10.66 MiB | 1.77 |
| 6, 4 | 448 KiB | 12.07 MiB | 2.00 |
| 8, 4 (the brief) | 576 KiB | 15.45 MiB | 2.56 |

**What that memory is, in practice** (read in source). Address space, written
only by collectives that span nodes - pages never touched cost no RAM. But the
start-up check that `/dev/shm` can hold the job counts every page, so the
brief's defaults raise each PE's count from about 263 MiB to about 272 (3.6%):
the README's desktop example of 8 PEs from about 2,100 MiB to 2,175, and a node
of 256 PEs 2.4 GiB more. A desktop - a job of one node, which never runs an internode
collective - pays this in the check alone. (A job of one node could lay out no
lanes at all, which would make the defaults free there; a change to the
layout's rule that both builds share, not checked in detail.)

**What each buys.**
- *Capacity 4* (measured, the Pi, 2026-09-26): a 1 MiB reduction at k = 2 took
  33.5 ms at a window of 2, 23.0 at 4 and 19.4 at 8 - the window is what makes
  step 2's 1 MiB claim. It also sets step 3's overlap (section 6): its
  exchanges keep half the window in flight, and the Pi needs 2 to keep its link
  busy, so a capacity of 2 would leave Rabenseifner's link idle between pieces.
- *Ceiling 8* (computed): nothing on four nodes, where every k ≥ 3 is already a
  star; at 500 nodes an 8-byte reduction climbs 3 levels instead of 5, about
  600 µs instead of 960 at the Pi's link.

**Options.** 8 and 4 (2.56 times); 4 and 4 (1.44 times: the window kept, deep
trees at hundreds of nodes kept too); 6 and 4 (2.00); 8 and 2 (1.77, but the
window the 1 MiB claim needs is gone).

**Recommendation: the brief's 8 and 4.** What grows is address space and the
capacity check's count, by 3.6% of a PE's figure, and each number buys
something measured or computed. If the desktop's count matters to you, add the
one-node layout without lanes rather than lowering the defaults.

## 5. Step 3: node counts that are not powers of two (step 3's question 1)

**The algorithm.** For a large reduction over N node leads, Rabenseifner's
algorithm is a *reduce-scatter* by recursive halving - in each step a lead
swaps half of what it holds with a partner and combines, so after log₂ N steps
each lead holds 1/N of the result - then an *allgather* that spreads the N
pieces to all. Each lead's link carries about 2n each way, against a binary
tree's 3n through an interior node: that is the gain the brief promises.

**The problem** (computed exhaustively for N = 2 to 70, in the model and in C).
Halving needs a power of two. For other N = 2ᵖ + r the brief uses the classic
*fold*: 2r leads pair up first, each pair's even lead takes its partner's half
and, at the end, sends the whole result back - so the fold's even leads carry
the vector about 3.5 times each way:

| N | 4 | 8 | 16 | 7 | 33 | 70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| busiest lead, vectors out / in | 1.50 / 1.50 | 1.75 / 1.75 | 1.88 / 1.88 | 3.00 / 2.50 | 3.44 / 2.94 | 3.47 / 2.97 |

That is no better than the binary tree it is meant to beat, with more steps. The
algorithm's author says the same of it: "For non-power-of-two processes, the
transfer overhead is doubled" (Rabenseifner and Adamidis, *Collective
Reduction Operation on Cray X1 and Other Platforms*, CUG 2004). A real cluster
has a power of two of nodes by accident, so this is the common case, not an
edge. The Pi's four nodes are a power of two: there the fold never runs.

**Options.**
- (a) Build it as briefed: the chooser counts the fold's true cost and takes
  Rabenseifner only where it wins - at powers of two, mostly - and the README
  claims no more.
- (b) *Binary blocks* (the same paper, §3.6): split N into power-of-two blocks
  (13 = 8 + 4 + 1); each block halves on its own, then each smaller block's
  pieces go to the next larger one. It reuses the halving exactly, and is
  identical to it at powers of two; its overhead depends on N's binary form -
  small when the exponents are close (12 = 8 + 4), large when far apart
  (65 = 64 + 1).
- (c) *A circulant reduce-scatter* (Träff, *Optimal, Non-pipelined
  Reduce-scatter and Allreduce Algorithms*, arXiv 2410.14234, 2024): ⌈log₂ N⌉
  rounds and the optimal volume - each lead sends and receives exactly N − 1
  of the N blocks - for every N, no fold and no blocks, by its abstract. By my
  reading it runs on the same kind of circulant pattern as Bruck's allgather,
  which fcollect already runs between leads for any N (design §5.8), so the
  allgather half would be built and model-checked already. But on such a
  pattern each round sends to one lead and receives from another - not a
  pairwise swap - so section 6's swap rule would not apply and every round
  would need credits; and Part 1's arithmetic and model would be redone for
  its schedule. Not yet read beyond its abstract.
- (d) Spread the unfold: each odd fold lead takes its result from every lead
  rather than its partner alone; the fold's even leads drop to about 2.5n -
  better, still over 2n.

**Recommendation: before Part 2 builds the data path, put (c) through the model
beside (a) and (b)** - read Träff's paper, write its schedule into
`rabenseifner_model.py` with the same exhaustive check, add its cost to
`shape_timing.py`, and choose by the numbers. Your rule for v0.2 - performance
and elegance, not the code a choice costs - points at (c) if it holds up: one
algorithm optimal at every N, reusing the allgather that exists. Everything
built so far serves (a) and (b) unchanged; for (c) the cost model, the lanes,
rows and grants carry over, and the halving arithmetic would not. If you prefer
not to spend the time, (a) now with (b) next is the safe order.

## 6. Step 3: flow control within a step (step 3's question 2)

**The gap** (read in the brief). Each step of the reduce-scatter streams a half
vector in pieces through a *row* of W slots in the partner's lane - piece j into
slot j mod W. The brief's rule says when a row may be reused by a later step
(once the earlier step's pieces are all combined, signalled by a grant), but
nothing about slots within a step: a fast sender could put piece W into slot 0
before its partner has combined piece 0, overwriting it. The tree has a rule
for this (a node sends piece k only once it holds release k - D); the brief
gives Rabenseifner's steps none.

**The design proposed.**
- **In a swap step** (every halving and doubling step, and the fold's first),
  no extra message: a lead puts its piece q only after it has combined its
  partner's piece q - d, with d = W/2. Each partner's own pieces then prove how
  far it has got: when its piece q - W + d arrives, it has combined my piece
  q - W, whose slot my piece q will reuse. Neither side can wait on the other
  in a circle, and halves differ by at most one element, so the rule holds to
  the last piece.
- **In a one-way step** (the fold back and the unfold), there is no reverse
  traffic to prove anything, so the receiver adds one to a credit counter at
  the sender per piece it takes - as a broadcast's child already returns empty
  slots to its parent - and the sender puts piece q only once it holds
  q - W + 1 credits.
- **The window it needs**: a swap keeps d = W/2 pieces in flight each way, and
  the link stays busy if d piece-times cover a hop: on the Pi, with 16 KiB
  pieces (about 141 µs each) and an 86 µs hop, d = 2, so W = 4 - section 4's
  capacity.

**How it was checked** (the main session's model, `rabenseifner_model.py`, under
every interleaving): two to five leads, two and three rows, two reductions in a
row; every piece a lead takes is the one it waits for, every put lands in a lane
lent to that collective, and no lead is stuck. Each rule removed fails - the
lag, the credits - as do the brief's own breakages. The model runs a window of
2 (d = 1); the argument above is for any W, and W = 4 is for Part 2's model run
to confirm.

**Options.**
- (a) This design: swaps self-clocked, one-way steps credited.
- (b) Credits everywhere: one mechanism, but a message back for every piece of
  every swap - on the Pi about 7 µs of a lead's loop per 141 µs piece, around
  5%, more on faster links.
- (c) Stop and wait (W = 1): simplest, and the link idles a hop per piece.

**Recommendation: (a)** - but it follows section 5: with the circulant
reduce-scatter there are no swaps, and (b) is the design.
