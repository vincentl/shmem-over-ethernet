**What Part 2 builds** - the design the record and the model hold:

1. **The reduce-scatter's own words** (`internal-region.h`), per team number,
   sized like the gather's by the job's node count, ceil(log2 N) + 4 lines of
   128 bytes, in this order so that every put lands outside the counters a lead
   watches: an arrival counter per row, ceil(log2 N) of them; a credit counter;
   the allgather's grant counter; the reduce-scatter's grant line - its
   counter, then the collective's epoch, identity and bytes and a lane field per
   round; and the senders' lanes line, a lane and an epoch per row. Its own,
   because the allgather ends with no step every lead waits for: a lead that
   leaves can start a small collective whose child adds to its parent's
   counters at once, while another lead still waits on its last pieces (the
   model's `shared_counters` breakage). `osoe_internode_generic_number_taken`
   zeroes them.
2. **The grants.** Entering a large reduction that takes Träff's, a lead grants
   each of its reduce-scatter senders, r - s_k: its own lane into field k of
   that sender's grant line, with the epoch, identity and bytes, then a fenced
   add on its grant counter - before its node's members arrive, as the tree's
   grants go. A lead starts its reduce-scatter once its count shows all
   ceil(log2 N) of its own (one counter cannot say which has come), reads and
   clears the fields, and checks them. As each reduce-scatter round k ends,
   the lead adds one to the allgather grant counter of its round-k receiver,
   r + s_k, which fills its row k in the allgather; a lead starts its allgather
   once that count shows all of its own.
3. **The rounds**, a function beside `in_pieces`: round k's pieces - the
   round's intervals in pieces of a lane's slot - into row k of the receiver's
   lane, piece j in the row's slot j mod the capacity, as the tree's pieces go,
   a put and then a fenced add on the row's counter. **Credits throughout**:
   the receiver adds one at the sender for each piece it takes, and the sender
   puts piece j only once it holds j - W + 1 of the round's, W the call's window
   (one counter a lead, cumulative; a late credit only makes a sender wait
   longer). A reduce-scatter round's first
   piece is preceded by the sender's own lane in the receiver's senders' lane
   field for the row; the receiver reads it as it takes that piece and sends its
   allgather round k there. The receiver checks every piece's header, combines
   (reduce-scatter) or copies (allgather) into the accumulator at the
   interval's offset on the lead's own thread, and the bases advance at the end.
4. **The chooser on**: `shape_of` passes `true`; the chooser already takes
   Träff's only where its ceil(log2 N) rows fit a lane's ceiling + 1 (512 nodes
   at the default ceiling of 8). Publish which algorithm a call took where a
   test can read it - the tree state's line is spent (`node.h`'s static
   assert), so a second line or a counter; decide it in the record.
5. **Tests**, each with the mutation it catches: Part 2's list below (2 to 70
   logical nodes, sizes either side of the crossover, every operation and
   element type at one size, `source == dest`, 3, 5, 6, 7, 12 and 33 nodes by
   name); a Träff's reduction followed at once by small reductions and
   broadcasts on the same team, no barrier between, and alternating with
   trees, as `shmem.shape.back-to-back` does for trees; the model's breakages
   as C mutations - no credit, no grant, a stale grant, an allgather grant at
   its round's start, shared counters, the sender's lane after its first add;
   and `SHMEM_THREAD_MULTIPLE`, two teams' reductions at once.

**Stop and ask**, beside the list below: anything the design above does not
hold in C that the model held - a write into a lead's words or lane it does not
wait for, a counter another collective adds to - and any change to the words
or grants beyond what the author accepted on 2026-10-07.

What this step changes, in one sentence: a large reduction whose vector is
long enough that bandwidth, not hops, sets its time stops sending every
node's whole vector up a tree and the result back down - the busiest link
carrying (k + 1) vectors - and instead halves the vector between pairs of
leads log₂ N times until each holds one N-th of the result, then gathers
the N-ths back, every link carrying under two vectors and no link busier than
another; the chooser of step 2 picks it when the model says it wins, and the
tree when it does not.

The author's decisions: Rabenseifner's algorithm, not a ring, since a ring's
2(N − 1) hops is 170 ms of latency alone at 500 nodes against the project's
axis of algorithms logarithmic in node count; and decisions by performance
and elegance, not by cost in code or tests.

It is written for a session that starts cold, in this repository, with the
VMs and the Pi cluster below. Nothing in it needs the conversation that wrote
it.

The author's goals, the axes to judge the work by:

- **Internode algorithms logarithmic in node count**, in time and in memory.
- **Intranode algorithms linear or better in PEs per node** - unchanged
  here: the lead's combine of its node's members is as it was.
- **A path to RoCE at ~10⁶ PEs that no interface blocks**: the algorithm is
  puts and fenced adds through lanes, which every transport has; a backend
  with its own reduction ignores it.
- **For this step:** no link carries more than twice the vector; the
  crossover from the tree comes from the measured link, not a constant
  (design §10's "Reduction algorithm crossovers" item: "chosen from
  measurement on the §8.4 harness, not fixed in advance"; §5.8 itself says
  the crossover sizes come from measurement).

## The algorithm, as this step builds it

Over the team's N node leads, each holding its node's combined vector of n
bytes in its accumulator after the intranode phase, in pieces of s bytes.

**The fold, when N is not a power of two.** Let p = ⌊log₂ N⌋ and r = N − 2ᵖ.
The first 2r leads pair off, (0, 1), (2, 3), ... (2r − 2, 2r − 1): in each
pair the even lead sends the upper half of its vector to the odd and the odd
sends its lower half to the even, each combines the half it received into
the half it kept, and then the odd sends its combined upper half to the even,
which now holds the pair's whole combined vector. The r odd leads drop out
until the end. The remaining 2ᵖ leads - the r even ones and the N − 2r that
were not paired - are renumbered 0 to 2ᵖ − 1 in order.

**The reduce-scatter, recursive halving.** For i from 1 to p, each of the 2ᵖ
leads pairs with the lead whose renumbered rank differs in bit p − i (a
distance of 2ᵖ⁻ⁱ): the lower of the pair sends the upper half of the segment
it currently holds and keeps the lower, the upper sends the lower half and
keeps the upper, and each combines what it received into what it kept. After
step i a lead holds n / 2ⁱ bytes; after step p, n / 2ᵖ, its N-th of the
result (2ᵖ-th, before the fold's leads are counted back in).

**The allgather, recursive doubling.** For i from p down to 1, the same pairs
in reverse order: each lead sends the segment it holds and receives its
partner's, so each holds twice as much; after the last step every lead holds
the whole result.

**The unfold.** Each even lead of the fold sends the whole result to its odd
partner.

Bytes a lead sends: n(1 − 1/2ᵖ) in the reduce-scatter, the same in the
allgather, plus the fold's n and n / 2 for the leads in it: under 2n on
every link, against the tree's (k + 1) n on an interior node's link. Hops: 2p
plus the fold's 3, against the tree's 2⌈log_k N⌉ - more hops than a star,
which is why the chooser, not this step, decides when it runs.

**The order within a step** is fixed by the pairing: each lead's partner in
step i is known from the team record and the step number alone, so no
message negotiates it. The segment a lead holds after step i is an interval
of the vector known the same way: write the arithmetic once, in a pure
function beside the chooser (`rabenseifner_partner(rank, N, step)`,
`rabenseifner_segment(rank, N, step)`), with a test against a model that
enumerates every N from 2 to 70 and checks that every byte of the result is
combined exactly once from every node and that no lead ever sends a segment
it has not finished combining.

## The pieces, the lanes and the grants

A step's half moves as pieces through the receiver's lane, exactly as a
child's partial moves to its parent today: the sender puts piece j into a
slot of the receiver's lane and does a fenced add on a counter; the receiver
waits for the counter, combines the piece into its own accumulator at the
piece's offset, and the slot is free. Rule 1 holds because every piece lands
in a lane slot and is combined from there into the accumulator by the
receiver's own application thread, which is also rule 2. The window of W
pieces in flight is step 2's; a step's half is streamed, so the scratch is W
slots whatever n is.

**Rows.** A lane has (ceiling + 1) rows. A tree uses one row per child
position and the last for the release; a step of this algorithm has one
partner, so it uses one row, and successive steps use successive rows modulo
the row count. A row is reused only when its earlier step's pieces have all
been combined, which the receiver knows before it starts the later step -
the steps are sequential on each lead - so the rule is: **the grant for step
i is sent when step i − rows has finished, or at entry for the first rows
steps.** The grant carries, as today, the epoch, the identity, the bytes and
the lane; it gains the step, so a stale grant from an earlier step or team
is told from a live one (`lane_model.py`'s `stale_grant` case, extended).

**The fold's leads and the unfold** are two more steps in the same shape:
the fold's exchange is a step with a partner at distance 1 and the unfold a
one-way step, both through lanes and grants.

**Who leads.** Each node's lead thread - the application thread of the team's
first local member, as for the tree - runs every step for its node; the
progress thread only applies the puts and the adds. Nothing runs on the
progress thread that did not before.

**The allgather half.** Build it as the reverse of the reduce-scatter, through
the same lanes - one mechanism, one model, one set of counters - rather than
through `osoe_internode_generic_collect`: Bruck's steps pair differently,
write straight into the peer's buffer under its own READY protocol, and work
per team member rather than per node, so reusing it would mean two protocols
in one collective and a `places` array built to make a node's N-th look like
a member's block. If, building it, the reverse steps prove to be Bruck's in
all but name, say so in the record and reuse; the brief's expectation is that
they are not.

## The chooser

Step 2's `osoe_shape_choose` returns a shape; this step adds the shape "the
reduce-scatter and allgather" with its cost under the same three numbers:
bytes under 2n over B on every link plus (2p + 3) hops and the window's fill
per step, for a reduction only - a broadcast has nothing to scatter. The
chooser takes it when its cost is least; ties go to the tree. `shape_timing.py`
grows the same term, and its fit is checked again against the Pi's table,
which must not move (the table has no Rabenseifner line; the term is checked
by the simulation and then by the Pi measurement below).

On four nodes the arithmetic says: 1 MiB at 111 MiB/s, the tree at k = 2
carries 2n on the root's link, 18.8 ms, and this algorithm 1.5n on every
link, 14.1 ms, plus four hops against two: about 1.3 times faster, which the
Pi can show. The 1.5 times over the tree at k = 2 and 2.5 times over k = 4
that the README's limit paragraph promises come at seven nodes and more,
where the tree has an interior node, and only the simulation and the VM's
logical nodes can show them until a larger cluster exists. **The README
claims only what the Pi measured**, and names the simulation for the rest.

## Not in this step

- Broadcasts: nothing to scatter. Scans: design A's rank tree stands.
- Collect and fcollect: Bruck's stays theirs.
- The intranode phases, the barrier, the transport, any wake.
- A non-commutative operation: the specification defines none; a comment at
  the halving says the halving depends on it.

## Stop and ask

- A write into a lead's lane that the lead does not wait for before it
  returns, or a row reused before its pieces are combined - the rule false
  for some step.
- The chooser preferring this algorithm at a size where the Pi shows it
  slower by more than the noise.
- The allgather half turning out to need Bruck's READY protocol after all:
  put the two-protocol design to the author before building it.
- Any change to the grant beyond the step field, or to the lane's layout.
- The seven-node simulation and the loopback measurement at seven disagreeing
  by more than a factor of two.
