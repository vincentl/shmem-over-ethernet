## 1. Every check has a plant: seven, not four (the author, 2026-09-27)

The brief's four plants each fired their own check and nothing else. Three
checks had none: the sort's range check, the alltoall's canary (the gaps of
the strided dest, and a block past the data), and the sort's capacity
refusal - which the brief's "an oracle cannot be made to fail by a plant"
names as a stop. Asked, the author chose all three:

- `sort-range`: PE 1 sends its first key of round 0 to the PE after the key's
  owner. It is still counted where it lands, so only the range check sees it.
- `alltoall-gap`: between the sync and the strided exchange, PE 1 puts one
  word into a gap of the next PE's dest and quiets. That PE's return from the
  collective needs PE 1 to have entered it, so the word is always there when
  it checks.
- `sort-capacity`: PE 1 reserves too much once (push) or declares one count
  too large (pull). The oracle fires beside it - refused keys are lost - which
  the author accepted.

It brought a fix that stands on its own: **a receiver whose cursor passed its
capacity says so once and leaves its batch unchecked**, where it would have
checked a buffer with holes in it and reported their garbage as range and
order failures. *Undo*: the three plants are an enum entry, a few lines each,
and a test each.

## 2. A plant's test requires every line its checks print (Claude)

The brief's form - one pattern, the failure line then the nonzero count -
asserts one check a plant. The sort-key plant fires three of the oracle's
comparisons, the print plant two checks, the capacity plant three; with one
pattern, every check but the first could go quiet and every test stay green.
And several PEs' lines come in an order that depends on who reserved first. So
`cmake/PacerPlant.cmake` runs the planted job and requires each pattern of a
list on its own, in any order; the alltoall plants also require the summary's
count (96: all eight variants caught the byte, every exchange). A `.` stands
for each `;` in a pattern, which would otherwise split CMake's list - the first
registration was split that way, and still passed, fragment by fragment, until
the test's command line was read. The launcher runs with `-k`: a run that found
a failure exits 1 on every PE by design, and the first to exit must not have
the others killed in their finalize. *Undo*: `PASS_REGULAR_EXPRESSION`, as
the brief had it.

## 3. A hash sum beside the sort's count, sum and xor (Claude)

The oracle review (below) found the brief's three components all blind to one
corruption: a byte swapped between two keys - a torn copy - or a bit set in
one key and cleared in another. Count, sum mod 2^64 and xor are unchanged, and
low in the keys both stay in range and sort as usual, so nothing saw it. The
fourth component is the sum of each key's fmix64, which any change of the set
of keys moves but with odds of 2^-64; the local sort's own self-check takes it
too. Shown by a mutation that swaps the low bytes of two of PE 1's keys after
counting them: every push-mode smoke test fails on the hash line alone. The
sort-key plant fires it too, and its test requires the line. *Undo*: the field
in `struct triple` and one comparison.

## 4. The interface, where it differs from the brief (Claude)

- **`memory()` fits the exercise itself.** The brief has the driver lower R;
  only the exercise knows which of its sizes to lower (the sort's keys a
  round, the alltoall's largest block, the accumulating sort's cycle), so it
  lowers its own and returns the bytes it will use, `UINT64_MAX` with a reason
  when even its smallest does not fit. The driver checks the result against
  the budget and prints the line; the header says `asked=` when R was lowered.
- **`step(units, last)`.** `last` says no step follows, so that the
  accumulating sort closes an open cycle - sorts and checks what it holds -
  before the last checkpoint rather than after it, where a failure would go
  unreported. The driver plans the last step from the slowest PE's time a unit.
- **`checkpoint` may reduce and print.** The alltoall's medians need a
  reduction, and the sort's oracle runs there; the driver calls it on every
  member together, so an exercise's own lines come before the driver's.
- **The budget bounds symmetric and private memory each.** `memory()` returns
  the symmetric bytes, the header prints the private ones, and both must fit.
- The smallest step time is not reduced: every step ends in a collective of the
  team, so it tracks the largest within milliseconds.

*Undo*: each is local to `exercise.h` and the driver's loop.

## 5. The exercises, where the brief left a choice (Claude)

- **Eight variants.** The brief says seven and lists eight; all eight are
  built, and the proposal's "seven ways" counts the two transposing forms as
  one way.
- **The alltoall's unit** is one block size, every variant, on both teams; the
  sizes take turns. Under `--quick` that is twelve units: three sizes, four
  exchanges of every variant each.
- **The alltoall's time** is each PE's own median over the checkpoint's
  exchanges, and a line gives the slowest PE's: one reduction of 40 numbers a
  team a checkpoint rather than one a sample. An untimed sync before each
  exchange makes every dest ready (§9.10), and one after the get form keeps its
  peers from reading a source the next variant refills.
- **The accumulating sort sorts in place** - MSD radix, 8-bit digits - so that
  the budget holds keys rather than a scratch copy of them
  (`local_sort=msd-in-place` in the header). Within a cycle nothing is checked:
  a fast PE's next round may already be reserving on a PE's cursor, so no
  round's own end can be read from it.
- **The pull exchange regenerates the round's stream** for its second pass,
  rather than keeping the keys, so its generate time counts two passes.
- **The oracle runs at every checkpoint**, on what closed since the last - each
  round, in rounds mode - and the xors meet by `shmem_uint64_xor_reduce`: a sum
  of per-PE xors differs between two partitions of one set of keys.
- **A bin carries 16 keys of fixed slack**, not 64: at 10^6 PEs 64 would be
  half a gigabyte of staging a PE; a bin that fills is sent early anyway.
- **The order plant is PE 1's first local sort**, not round 0's, so that it
  fires in accumulate mode, which sorts only when a cycle closes.
- **A plant whose exercise is not named is refused**, and so is any plant on one
  PE: a planted run that could not fire would read as a clean one.

## 6. `parallel_print` (Claude)

- **It syncs at least once a call, a silent one too.** The spec wants a
  collect's dest ready on every member before any member calls (§9.10.8), and
  the next call's fcollect writes the record array this one reads; so each
  member copies what it needs before its first sync, and a sync always
  follows. The common case is still one fcollect, the owners' puts, one sync.
- **Calls on two teams in turn** need a sync of the second team between them
  when it has a member the first lacked: that member waits for nobody, and its
  fcollect may write a record array another member is still reading. osoe
  writes a peer's buffer only once the peer has entered, so it never shows
  here; the spec does not promise it. The header says so, and the print
  exercise syncs the world between its cases.
- **The "hidden option"** for several windows is a setter the print exercise
  alone calls, `parallel_print_window`, beside a capture hook the checks read
  the lines through. The brief's "an arena of 64 bytes, 1 KiB texts" was read as
  a 64-byte window with texts of 25 to 60 bytes, about one window a line: a
  text is never split, so 1 KiB texts cannot go through 64-byte windows.
- **The print test runs `--quick print`** (three rounds) where the brief wrote
  `osoe-pacer print`, which would run 60 seconds; and on 0,1,0,1 as well as the
  brief's two maps, the one layout whose shared labels have gaps.
- A team never named, whose world ranks are not a progression (a split of
  `SHMEM_TEAM_SHARED` can be one), prints as `team(start:?:size)`.

## 10. The `OSOE_CHECK_QUIET` firing: the hook's own race, fixed (the author chose its branch)

The one open item since 2026-09-26 - `shmem.multiple.checked.0-1-2-0-1-0`
fired once in a debug suite, its message lost, unreproduced in three suites
and some 1,300 runs - came back within fifteen seconds of phase 2's `threads`
exercise, on the same map:

```
env OSOE_CHECK_QUIET=1 OSOE_TEST_PERTURB=1 OSOE_GET_LANDING_BYTES=8192 \
  osoe-launch -n 6 -m 0,1,2,0,1,0 -t 120 -- osoe-pacer --seconds 15 --report 5 threads
osoe: PE 4: OSOE_CHECK_QUIET: shmem_quiet returned with a nonblocking get in flight that
  returned before it was called (extent 1, call 134805 of 134807)
```

(`task-stress-phase2` at `9167c81`, `release`, seed 1; four worker threads a
PE on private, serialized, default and team contexts.) Evidence in
`docs/design/evidence/check-quiet-2026-09-27/`:

| on 0,1,2,0,1,0, 15 s a run | fired | wrong data the pacer read |
|---|---|---|
| both hooks | 5 of 11 | none |
| the check alone | 0 of 4 | none |
| the perturbation alone | - | none in 6 |

Always a get whose call ranks just below the wait's, through `shmem_quiet` and
`shmem_pe_quiet` alike, and never a wrong word: the pacer's own row and table
checks (the table's buffer cleared to a canary first, so a get left in flight
would show) stayed clean in every run.

**The diagnosis.** `copy_out` copies a piece, frees its record, then credits
its pair, in that order, so a wait that sees its pair complete cannot be
looking at a record merely slow to free: the break, if real, would be in the
generation-and-pair protocol. But the check's own bookkeeping has a race. A
get call stamps its extents with the call's clock only as it returns, after
sending; if the thread is descheduled between the send and the stamp - easy
with 36 threads on four CPUs, easier under perturbation - the reply can land,
another thread copy it out and free the extent, and a later get take it; the
late stamp then lands on that stranger's get. A wait that began between the
two reads it as a get it had to complete, which it did not.

**The experiment and the fix.** A stamp word now carries its piece's issue
number beside the clock, and the stamp is a compare-and-swap from that issue's
unstamped word, so a late stamp lands nowhere else (`transport-am.c`, the
test hook only; off by default, one flag load a point, as before). With it
the same runs fired **0 of 12**, where 5 of 11 did before - against even odds,
twelve clean runs in a row are under one in a thousand. It hides no real
break: a get still in flight at a wait's return still holds its extent, so
its own stamp always lands. And the four race mutations of step 8 slice 3,
rerun with the fixed hook on the library's threaded test, both hooks, ten runs
each: `noretag` (the get side's, caught by exactly this check), `plain_max`,
`pe_quiet_stores_full` and `bit_first_moved` **10 of 10 each**, the unmutated
control 0 of 10 (`hook-matrix.txt`).

So the shared-context protocol was not at fault; the hook was. The author
chose the fix to land on its own branch, `check-quiet-stamp`, verified and
merged before phase 2 registers the `threads` tests that run with the hooks.
**Verified at `50799df`**, the tree that merged: debug 485, release 507, asan
484 and tsan 365 of as many; and the matrix again against the committed file,
the control 0 of 10 and each of the four mutations 10 of 10.
*Undo*: the previous stamp, a plain store - and the firing with it.

result PE 1's input may not reach, perturbs PE 1's own expectation instead. **A flag several threads read is a data race**, which
tsan reports and which fires the plant on whichever thread wins; each
thread plant is thread 0's alone, the thread's index tested before the flag.

## 12. `mailbox`, where the brief left a choice (Claude)

- **Two signal words a slot.** Six comparisons need a word that grows and one
  that shrinks. The "up" word, set to the round plus one or added one and
  swapped to 0 by its receiver, serves equal, not equal, greater and greater
  or equal; the "down" word, started at 2⁶² and added minus one by every
  message, never reset, serves less and less or equal. *Undo*: one word and
  four comparisons.
- **Whom each PE hears from is arithmetic.** Round t draws K = min(N, 4)
  distinct offsets from the seed, alike everywhere: PE s sends to s + o and
  hears from s − o, so no one is told whom to wait for. A slot takes a new
  message only once its receiver has acknowledged the last, by an
  `atomic_set` its sender waits on - a wait may watch only words updated
  atomically (§9.11). The rounds pipeline with no sync between them, and
  cannot deadlock: a send of round t waits only for acknowledgements every PE
  gave before starting round t. *Undo*: the proposal's random receivers,
  which need their counts exchanged each round.
- **A message proves itself whole** by every word but the first being
  (sender, receiver, round)-determined and checked, rather than by a trailer
  equal to its head; the first word carries the sender's clock.
- **One form a round**, twenty forms; the quick run is twenty rounds, every
  form once. Odd rounds send with `putmem_signal_nbi` and a quiet.
- **The time between node-mates is a message's time in the mailbox**, from
  its send to its reading, while the receiver sends its own round - what an
  application sees, not a bare wake.

## 13. The round trip across nodes, added before the merge (Claude)

The brief, after proposal §4.4, asks `mailbox` for "a ping-pong's round trip
across nodes, since clocks differ there"; the exercise timed node-mates only.
Added at `d87f0a4`. At every checkpoint, after a sync of the team so that no
message of the rounds crosses them, each PE plays ping-pong with its partner,
the PE of its rank on the other node of its pair (nodes 0 and 1, 2 and 3, and
so on): one round trip untimed, so that neither side's lateness to the
checkpoint is timed, then 128, the PE on the even node serving and timing -
`mailbox round_trip_us median= p99= pairs=`, the slowest server's. Messages
are four words put with the typed `shmem_uint64_put_signal`, which the
proposal lists among what `mailbox` covers and which nothing called; the
signal is set to the message's number, and its receiver checks it and every
word.

The receiver takes the signal by a swap to 0, as a slot's up word is taken,
and that is what lets a wrong signal be planted: with a signal only ever
raised, a value one too many would satisfy the next wait early and
desynchronize the pair. `mailbox-round-trip` flips a byte of one message and
signals it one too many; both lines fire, and removing either check fails that
test alone. The partner is the driver's, in `pacer_layout`, so that the
plant is refused before it runs where PE 1 has none (on 0,0,0,1). About 87 µs
between the VM's logical nodes; on the Pi, between `pi-001` and `pi-002` and
between `pi-003` and `pi-004`, a median of 175 µs over the checkpoints (169
to 195) and a 99th percentile of 211 µs - four words each way through the
typed put-with-signal and a wait, against the 153 to 156 µs of a bare
io_uring echo (`docs/pi-cluster-results.md`, the floor). *Undo*: `d87f0a4`.

## 14. `teams`, where the brief left a choice (Claude)

- **The budget of 16 numbers**: a strided team of random start, stride and
  size; one with a negative stride; one PE, by the stride 0 the spec allows;
  a split of the first; and a grid, `split_2d` of the whole team when its
  rows and columns fit what the four leave, else of a strided team of its
  first PEs, one number more. The header says what it spent: 12 at 16 PEs,
  a grid of 4 × 4.
- **Every team is a progression of world ranks** - a split of one is one,
  and so is a grid's row or column - so every answer is arithmetic.
- **Rows and columns at once, out of phase**: the even rows do their row's
  collective first, the odd rows their column's, so rows' and columns'
  collectives run at once and no wait closes a cycle.
- **Fifteen kinds on the grid**: sum, max, min and product of longs; and, or
  and xor of 64-bit words; a sum of doubles; a broadcast from a rotating root;
  a collect of ragged lengths, zero among them; fcollect; alltoall; the
  inclusive and exclusive sum scans; `team_sync`. The strided teams run those
  whose memory is O(1) a member, or O(size) for fcollect. An exclusive scan's
  rank 0 must be 0, as the spec defines it.
- **Two buffers a team, by round parity**, so that no member's next
  collective writes a buffer another is still checking: a collective's dest
  must be ready on every PE before any calls.
- **A put and a get back through each strided team's context** every round.
- **`--teams-exhaust`** splits the whole team until a split returns nonzero,
  compares every PE's count with the most any made (a max reduction), syncs,
  and ends its run: 61 splits at the default `OSOE_MAX_TEAMS`.

## 15. `threads`, where it differs from the brief (Claude)

- **A row where the brief has a mailbox.** The brief has each worker run
  "slices of fetch-and-add accounting and of the mailbox on each context".
  Each slice, on each context: a row of 16 words out to the next PE and the
  previous, a fence between; a quiet, or on odd slices a `pe_quiet` naming
  only the next, which leaves the other put outstanding; and the row read
  back from the next. Then a table: four nonblocking gets under one quiet,
  into a buffer cleared to a canary first. Then four fetch-adds on counters
  anywhere, each remembered. The row checks what the shared path must keep -
  a quiet completes this thread's puts whatever the others issue and quiet
  meanwhile, the promise `OSOE_CHECK_QUIET` checks inside the library - and
  it was the row's slices that brought item 10's firing back. The signal and
  wait forms run single-threaded, in `mailbox`. *Undo*: a mailbox slot a
  thread, each worker waiting on its own.
- **The team context is the world reversed**, `split_strided` from the last
  PE with stride −1, so that every PE number goes through a numbering unlike
  the world's.
- **Sessions**: each slice on the private context runs inside
  `shmem_ctx_session_start(SHMEM_CTX_SESSION_BATCH)`, the slice's operation
  count its hint.
- **The thread limit** (`01d003f`, found by the same check as item 13): the
  brief refuses `--threads T` past `OSOE_MAX_THREADS`; the tool took up to
  1,023 and left it to the library, which ended the job from inside
  `shmem_init_thread`, exit 1. It now refuses before init, exit 2, when T + 1
  - the main thread counts - exceeds the variable (default 16).
- **The hooks in every preset's tests**, not only debug and asan:
  `OSOE_CHECK_QUIET=1 OSOE_TEST_PERTURB=1` and `OSOE_GET_LANDING_BYTES=8192`,
  a landing zone the table's gets overfill - the conditions under which item
  10 fired. On five maps, 0,1,2,0,1,0 among them; under tsan on 0,1.
- **The counters' oracle is O(N) a PE**, the most the tool's rule allows:
  each PE keeps what it added to each PE's eight counters, and one sum
  reduction of those N × 8 words a checkpoint tells every PE what its own
  must hold. *Undo*: counters on the neighbours only, O(1).
- **An exercise may end its run.** `--teams-exhaust` has one step's work,
  which takes no time, and the steps' adaptive growth overflowed a `long`
  (`rounds=-790…` in a summary); an exercise now sets `counters.finished`,
  and a step is capped at 2²⁴ units.

## 17. The mailbox's tail is the core its threads share (Claude, the author asked)

The ten-minute `mailbox` run put a PE's time in the mailbox between
node-mates at a median of 259 µs but a 99th percentile of 8.6 to 11.9 ms -
forty times the median, and about a scheduler's quantum. The guess was the
design's own (runbook §6, the benchmark's bound and unbound pair): Slurm
binds each of a node's four tasks to one core, and a PE's main thread shares
it with the PE's agent thread (design §3.12), so a message can wait for the
scheduler to switch between them. The author asked for the run that tests
it - two minutes of `mailbox` with `--cpu-bind=none`, then two with the
default binding, back to back:

| | messages a second | node-mates, median | node-mates, 99th percentile | round trip, median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bound (Slurm's default) | 39,600 | 262 µs | 11.7 ms | 175 µs |
| unbound | 249,500 | 183 µs | 0.47 ms | 170 µs |

Unbound, the rate is 6.3 times the bound one and the tail 25 times shorter;
the round trip across nodes, measured with every PE's rounds done, is the
same. So the tail is the shared core, not the protocol. The likely
mechanism, not yet shown: one agent a node - the lead's - drives the whole
node's network traffic (design §3.12), and it shares its core with the
lead's main thread, so remote acknowledgements wait while that thread runs;
since every send waits for an earlier acknowledgement, the delay spreads
through the rounds to every message, node-mates' plain memory copies
included. What to do about it - document `--cpu-bind=none` or
`-c2` for osoe jobs, or place the agent differently - is the author's
(For the author, 3). The runbook's §6 already runs `osoe-bench` both ways;
this is the pattern where it matters most.
