## 1. Every check has a plant: seven, not four (the author, 2026-09-27)

The brief's four plants each fired their own check and nothing else. Three
checks had none: the sort's range check, the alltoall's canary (the gaps of
the strided dest, and a block past the data), and the sort's capacity
refusal - which the brief's "an oracle cannot be made to fail by a plant"
names as a stop. Asked, the author chose all three:

- `sort-range`: PE 1 sends its first key of round 0 to the PE after the key's
  owner. It is still counted where it lands, so only the range check sees it.
- `alltoall-gap`: between the sync and the strided exchange, PE 1 puts one
  word into a gap of the next PE's dest and quiets. That PE's return from the
  collective needs PE 1 to have entered it, so the word is always there when
  it checks.
- `sort-capacity`: PE 1 reserves too much once (push) or declares one count
  too large (pull). The oracle fires beside it - refused keys are lost - which
  the author accepted.

It brought a fix that stands on its own: **a receiver whose cursor passed its
capacity says so once and leaves its batch unchecked**, where it would have
checked a buffer with holes in it and reported their garbage as range and
order failures. *Undo*: the three plants are an enum entry, a few lines each,
and a test each.

## 2. A plant's test requires every line its checks print (Claude)

The brief's form - one pattern, the failure line then the nonzero count -
asserts one check a plant. The sort-key plant fires three of the oracle's
comparisons, the print plant two checks, the capacity plant three; with one
pattern, every check but the first could go quiet and every test stay green.
And several PEs' lines come in an order that depends on who reserved first. So
`cmake/PacerPlant.cmake` runs the planted job and requires each pattern of a
list on its own, in any order; the alltoall plants also require the summary's
count (96: all eight variants caught the byte, every exchange). A `.` stands
for each `;` in a pattern, which would otherwise split CMake's list - the first
registration was split that way, and still passed, fragment by fragment, until
the test's command line was read. The launcher runs with `-k`: a run that found
a failure exits 1 on every PE by design, and the first to exit must not have
the others killed in their finalize. *Undo*: `PASS_REGULAR_EXPRESSION`, as
the brief had it.

## 3. A hash sum beside the sort's count, sum and xor (Claude)

The oracle review (below) found the brief's three components all blind to one
corruption: a byte swapped between two keys - a torn copy - or a bit set in
one key and cleared in another. Count, sum mod 2^64 and xor are unchanged, and
low in the keys both stay in range and sort as usual, so nothing saw it. The
fourth component is the sum of each key's fmix64, which any change of the set
of keys moves but with odds of 2^-64; the local sort's own self-check takes it
too. Shown by a mutation that swaps the low bytes of two of PE 1's keys after
counting them: every push-mode smoke test fails on the hash line alone. The
sort-key plant fires it too, and its test requires the line. *Undo*: the field
in `struct triple` and one comparison.

## 4. The interface, where it differs from the brief (Claude)

- **`memory()` fits the exercise itself.** The brief has the driver lower R;
  only the exercise knows which of its sizes to lower (the sort's keys a
  round, the alltoall's largest block, the accumulating sort's cycle), so it
  lowers its own and returns the bytes it will use, `UINT64_MAX` with a reason
  when even its smallest does not fit. The driver checks the result against
  the budget and prints the line; the header says `asked=` when R was lowered.
- **`step(units, last)`.** `last` says no step follows, so that the
  accumulating sort closes an open cycle - sorts and checks what it holds -
  before the last checkpoint rather than after it, where a failure would go
  unreported. The driver plans the last step from the slowest PE's time a unit.
- **`checkpoint` may reduce and print.** The alltoall's medians need a
  reduction, and the sort's oracle runs there; the driver calls it on every
  member together, so an exercise's own lines come before the driver's.
- **The budget bounds symmetric and private memory each.** `memory()` returns
  the symmetric bytes, the header prints the private ones, and both must fit.
- The smallest step time is not reduced: every step ends in a collective of the
  team, so it tracks the largest within milliseconds.

*Undo*: each is local to `exercise.h` and the driver's loop.

## 5. The exercises, where the brief left a choice (Claude)

- **Eight variants.** The brief says seven and lists eight; all eight are
  built, and the proposal's "seven ways" counts the two transposing forms as
  one way.
- **The alltoall's unit** is one block size, every variant, on both teams; the
  sizes take turns. Under `--quick` that is twelve units: three sizes, four
  exchanges of every variant each.
- **The alltoall's time** is each PE's own median over the checkpoint's
  exchanges, and a line gives the slowest PE's: one reduction of 40 numbers a
  team a checkpoint rather than one a sample. An untimed sync before each
  exchange makes every dest ready (§9.10), and one after the get form keeps its
  peers from reading a source the next variant refills.
- **The accumulating sort sorts in place** - MSD radix, 8-bit digits - so that
  the budget holds keys rather than a scratch copy of them
  (`local_sort=msd-in-place` in the header). Within a cycle nothing is checked:
  a fast PE's next round may already be reserving on a PE's cursor, so no
  round's own end can be read from it.
- **The pull exchange regenerates the round's stream** for its second pass,
  rather than keeping the keys, so its generate time counts two passes.
- **The oracle runs at every checkpoint**, on what closed since the last - each
  round, in rounds mode - and the xors meet by `shmem_uint64_xor_reduce`: a sum
  of per-PE xors differs between two partitions of one set of keys.
- **A bin carries 16 keys of fixed slack**, not 64: at 10^6 PEs 64 would be
  half a gigabyte of staging a PE; a bin that fills is sent early anyway.
- **The order plant is PE 1's first local sort**, not round 0's, so that it
  fires in accumulate mode, which sorts only when a cycle closes.
- **A plant whose exercise is not named is refused**, and so is any plant on one
  PE: a planted run that could not fire would read as a clean one.

## 6. `parallel_print` (Claude)

- **It syncs at least once a call, a silent one too.** The spec wants a
  collect's dest ready on every member before any member calls (§9.10.8), and
  the next call's fcollect writes the record array this one reads; so each
  member copies what it needs before its first sync, and a sync always
  follows. The common case is still one fcollect, the owners' puts, one sync.
- **Calls on two teams in turn** need a sync of the second team between them
  when it has a member the first lacked: that member waits for nobody, and its
  fcollect may write a record array another member is still reading. osoe
  writes a peer's buffer only once the peer has entered, so it never shows
  here; the spec does not promise it. The header says so, and the print
  exercise syncs the world between its cases.
- **The "hidden option"** for several windows is a setter the print exercise
  alone calls, `parallel_print_window`, beside a capture hook the checks read
  the lines through. The brief's "an arena of 64 bytes, 1 KiB texts" was read as
  a 64-byte window with texts of 25 to 60 bytes, about one window a line: a
  text is never split, so 1 KiB texts cannot go through 64-byte windows.
- **The print test runs `--quick print`** (three rounds) where the brief wrote
  `osoe-pacer print`, which would run 60 seconds; and on 0,1,0,1 as well as the
  brief's two maps, the one layout whose shared labels have gaps.
- A team never named, whose world ranks are not a progression (a split of
  `SHMEM_TEAM_SHARED` can be one), prints as `team(start:?:size)`.

## 7. Small things (Claude)

`base/size.c` is compiled into the tool, so `--memory` and the heap size parse
exactly as the library parses `SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE`. The header prints sizes
in that spelling, exactly when whole and to a tenth otherwise (`72.1m`). The
library's name has its spaces made underscores, so a field stays one word. The
checkpoint line adds `step_s`, the slowest PE's step. A trailing newline in a
`parallel_print` text is dropped: every line has its own.

## 8. The author's first run on the Pi: failure lines by content, plants announced (Claude, asked)

The author's first run on the cluster was a planted one, `--quick --plant
print-hash print` at 4 x 4 (`docs/design/evidence/pi-cluster/2026-09-27/pacer/print-hash-plant-79d59d2.txt`),
and read as a fault. It was the plant working - 56 failures a round, exactly
as designed, and every case PE 1 cannot disturb right on 16 PEs over four
nodes - but the screenful showed two things wrong with the tool:

- **A failure split over several lines.** `[4-15] ... long: returned 2 lines,
  expected 1` and `[2-3] ... long: ...` should be one line: each PE's k-th
  failure went to call k, so a failure recorded after different numbers of
  earlier ones landed in different calls. Now one fcollect of the lines'
  hashes (32 bytes a PE, sized for the world), then one `parallel_print` a
  distinct line, in the order of the lowest rank holding it, every holder
  offering it; at most 16 a checkpoint, the rest counted. On the VM at 16
  PEs the same run prints `[2-15] ... long` once.
- **Nothing said the failures were wanted.** `plant=` sat at the end of the
  header. A planted run now prints, after the header, `osoe-pacer planted
  print-hash: PE 1 hashes other text than it prints; checks must fail, and the
  exit status is 1`, and names the plant on its last line.

The plant tests assert both lines, and print-hash the line grouped across
positions (`[0,2-3] ... parity`, which PE 0 records after two other failures
and PEs 2 and 3 after one); each assertion was seen to fail with its code
mutated away. Commit `4a96354`, the tree verified again and measured on the
Pi. The author's accumulating run of the night stopped at 127 s of its 600
with every check clean and no error from `srun` - a closed session, not a
crash; the measurements now run under `nohup`. *Undo*: `flush_failures` and
two lines of `main`.

## 9. `OSOE_MAX_TEAMS`, documented as it behaves (the author asked)

Asked whether a variable raises the team limit, and wanting it documented
well: `OSOE_MAX_TEAMS`, 4 to 1,024, default 64. The README's row and §5.7 said
it counts "teams in existence job-wide at once"; since a destroyed team keeps
its number (§5.7), it bounds the teams a session creates in its life - 61 at
the default. The row now says so, what each split spends - one number for
`split_strided`, one a row and a column for `split_2d`, 2√N for a square grid,
so no grid of a whole job above about 930 PEs at the default or 2.6 × 10⁵ at
1,024 - and what a number costs a PE, about 160 KiB of internal region.
Nothing else changes: the colouring that would cut a grid's numbers to O(PEs a
node) stays after the release, as decided on 2026-09-26 (design §10).
Commit `def6fa8`.

## The oracles reviewed: what wrong output would each still accept?

| Check | Accepts, still |
|---|---|
| sort oracle: count, sum, xor, hash sum over the team | only changes that keep the set of keys - a key at the wrong PE (the range check's) or in another round of one checkpoint. Before the hash sum: any change that keeps sums and xors, a byte swapped between two keys among them (item 3) |
| sort range | nothing at the wrong PE; a key corrupted within its owner's range is the oracle's |
| sort order | a sorted batch the sort itself lost keys from - which the local sort's self-check, with the hash sum, catches |
| sort capacity | a fetch-add returning a wrong offset below the capacity: two senders then overlap, and the oracle sees the lost keys and the stale ones in their place |
| alltoall words | nothing wrong in the data it reads - every word is compared - but a word that arrives after the completion point and before the check is accepted: premature completion is caught only when the data is still missing when looked for, which the canary makes likely at large blocks and across nodes |
| alltoall canary | writes past one block beyond dest, or before its start, or into another object: unchecked. A block sent to the wrong PE lands in that PE's dest and is caught there, traced to its sender |
| print, text and count | lines through the capture path only: the stdout branch of the same function is one `fwrite` of the same buffer, not compared. Never exercised: a team whose world ranks are no progression. A window-ignoring mutant survives while every text fits the 64 KiB arena, which on four PEs they always do |
| the driver's count and exit status | a failure recorded after an exercise's last checkpoint - none is: teardowns record nothing |

## The Pi

At `4a96354`, `release`, 4 × 4, about 45 minutes of cluster time; the numbers
and their readings are in `docs/pi-cluster-results.md`, "The pacer's first
runs", and the outputs in `docs/design/evidence/pi-cluster/2026-09-27/pacer/`.
Every run ended `failures=0` and exit 0: the default (sort, then alltoall, five
minutes each), the accumulating sort at 1 GiB a PE, the pull exchange, and
`--no-ptr`, ten minutes each. What they found, for the author:

- **The library's alltoall buys nothing measurable at 4 × 4** over puts, a
  quiet and a sync (within 4% at 8 B to 64 KiB); put-with-signal beats both.
- **Bulk nonblocking gets run at a quarter of the puts' rate**: the pull
  exchange moves the round's 512 MiB in 3.3 to 6.0 s where the push takes 1.1.
- **The sort's keys a second is the tool's own sort as much as the
  library**: 2.8 s of every 4-second round is the 16-bit LSD sort on one Pi
  core; digits of 8 or 11 bits would suit the Pi's caches better, a choice for
  the author since the brief fixed 16.
- **Retransmission timeouts** came back under the accumulating sort's load,
  22 of 38 on `pi-002`, with segmentation offload off on every node; nothing
  stalled a checkpoint.
