## The locked-memory limit (P9-1, C9-2)

- **What it fixes.** From Linux 6.14 io_uring charges every ring to one count
  of locked memory for all the user's processes on a host, held to each
  process's own soft limit. One machine's logical nodes count together: on a
  Raspberry Pi 5 (6.18, 16 KiB pages) at the 8 MiB a shell has, 112 logical
  nodes with no helpers, 56 with two and 42 with three were the most that
  started, and the release's tier 2 on a Pi node, four tests at once as the
  runbook runs it, failed four runs of four, where the Slurm step it ran in
  had no hard limit at all.
- **Built** in `session_start` (`init.c`), beside the open files' raise and
  before any ring is made: the soft limit raised to the hard one, the change's
  reason in its comment. No other limit is changed; the README's new section,
  "What `shmem_init` changes in the process", says both, why, and what a
  program inherits from them, and its `osoe-launch` paragraph, its limits on
  launching and the zero-copy row say that the hard limit is what counts.
  Design §6.2's item says it too.
- **Tested** by `shmem.limits.0-0` and `shmem.limits.0-1`, in every preset
  (the first in the single-node build too): the program lowers both soft limits
  below their hard ones before `shmem_init`, and finds them raised after - and,
  on a kernel that charges rings, a job of two nodes would be refused its rings
  at the locked-memory limit of nothing the program set, were it not raised.
  `RLIMIT_NOFILE`'s raise, since phase 1, had no test before.

## ThreadSanitizer's verdict (C9-1, with round eight's Q2-2)

- **What it fixes.** ThreadSanitizer replaces a process's exit status with its
  own, 66, once it has reported anything - on a nonzero return from `main` and
  in its handling of a deadly signal alike - and the gate the verifications ran
  at a helper count, `tsan-gate.py`, read `ctest`'s log and took any 66 for
  the tool's reports alone: a planted failing check, a crash and a wrong byte
  each passed it. At no helpers `ctest` ignores the exit status of the 333
  tests of 606 judged by a pass expression, so a race in one of them was never
  read. And round eight's Q2-2 stood: the gate passed any race in a heap
  mapping, the library's own words for its collectives at the mapping's tail
  among them, with no helper's frame asked for.
- **Built: the verdict inside `ctest`** (`cmake/OsoeThreadSanitizer.cmake`). In
  a ThreadSanitizer build every test runs with the tool's `exitcode=0`, which
  leaves each process its own status, `abort_on_error=1`, which makes a crash
  the tool catches end by SIGABRT where `exitcode=0` alone ends it with 0, and
  a `log_path` of the test's own name, appended to whatever `TSAN_OPTIONS` the
  shell has. Every test is judged as in the other presets. The reports are
  judged by `cmake/thread-sanitizer-gate.py` as the cleanup of a fixture every
  test requires, `tsan.reports`, after every other test and at any subset
  `ctest` is asked for; its setup, `tsan.reports.setup`, empties the directory
  and runs a canary with one race, whose report the gate must find - a gate
  reading a directory the tool never wrote to would pass anything. CMake 3.28
  at least, in that build only.
- **What passes**: a data race, in a heap mapping, every access in the heaps'
  range, naming a progress helper's thread; in a program's heap, below the
  test's page-rounded `SHMEM_SYMMETRIC_SIZE` - read from `ctest`'s own list of
  the tests - or in the internal region only at the places, and against the
  accesses, the gate names with the reason each is ordered: a team number's
  words against their zeroing when a split took the number (the split's sync
  comes between), and a parent's grant words or a child's lane word against
  the collective before's (that collective's end comes between). The places
  are named by `cmake/thread-sanitizer-place.c`, built from the library's own
  layout functions and defaults. Everything else fails: an ERROR, the tool's
  word for a crash; a thread leak or any report of another kind; a race no
  helper made; a race outside the heaps; a race in the internal region
  elsewhere, Q2-2's Träff row among them.
- **No helper left finished and unjoined on a death path.** The first runs so failed on
  thread leaks in four tests - `shmem.helpers.deaf.0-1`,
  `shmem.reply-pool.overflow`, `shmem.kill-after-init.TERM` and
  `shmem.dead-pe.lead.0-0-1-1` - which no run had shown: a helper that had
  stopped, or seen the node's abort, returned at once, and where the process
  then ended by a fatal's `_exit` on a thread the agent was not leading, it was
  a finished thread never joined. Now a done helper's thread returns only into
  the agent's join, which releases it first (`progress-host.c`,
  `wait_released`): a process that ends around it finds it running, and the
  agent's own endings - the orderly one, and the abort's, which joins the
  helpers that are done within its grace and detaches the rest - release each
  just before they join it.
- **`shmem.stopped-bulk.0-0-1`**, which reads back 64 MiB a helper landed and
  runs past its minute in the tool's slow path at a helper count, is given five
  minutes in that build (`osoe_tier2_map_test`'s new `SECONDS`), so that
  `ctest` passes it rather than a gate excusing its timeout.
- **Checked** in the development VM on the fixes' tree:
  - the whole preset at no helpers 612 of 612, no report at all - where the

## A blocking get's wait for room (Q5-2)

- **What it fixes.** Since the blocking gets' read fixes (`c06ac75`), a
  blocking get waiting for room in the landing zone named its own target: with
  another node's stalled gets holding the zone, "no progress toward node 1
  (PE 2)" blamed a node that owed it nothing - and a get of one piece at a time
  named its target there always, its own pieces never in the zone while it
  waits for room.
- **Built** in `make_room` (`transport-internode.c`): the target is named only
  while the get's own pieces - its context's bytes issued and not yet copied
  out - hold part of the zone; otherwise the wait is the PE's, toward no node,
  its record the bytes the zone holds, as a nonblocking get's is. A wait begun
  naming the target is begun again naming none once its pieces are out, which
  happens at most once, since none of its pieces is issued while it waits.
- **Tested** by `shmem.get-no-progress.zone-held.8.0-1-2` and `.150000.0-1-2`:
  PE 0's zone, set to 64 KiB, filled by a nonblocking get from PE 2, which
  stops, and PE 0's blocking get from PE 1, live, of one piece and of three;
  the wait must end naming no node, under the blocking get's own routine.
