### A2: the progress helpers' default, and a count rule

*What it is.* Each node's lead process has one progress thread, its agent, that sends and
receives every message between nodes. `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS` adds threads that take over
the data wires, divided among them by peer node; the agent keeps the control wires (the
barrier's). The default is none until a fast link was measured (`docs/decisions/2026-10-07.md`,
items 4 to 6).

*The evidence* (H4: eight `c4-standard-16`, unbound, three rounds; the provider caps a node's
sending at 2,718 MiB/s, eight nodes at 21,744):

- **Streams**: no helper or one - the same, since one thread still holds every data wire - 67
  to 80% of the ceiling, less the more PEs a node; two helpers 97 to 99% at 8, 16 and 32 PEs a
  node; four, 1 to 2% more. Gets 31%, 35% and 64% faster at two helpers.
- **CPU**: the progress threads' seconds a GiB 30% more at two helpers, 70% more at four.
- **Collectives**: none slower; the alltoalls 15 to 27% faster at two helpers, 24 to 40% at
  four; a 64 KiB reduction 8 to 11% faster; the barrier unchanged.
- **The mailbox** (small messages with signals): 36% faster at two helpers with 8 PEs a node
  (one a core), 5% at 16 (one a vCPU), **55% slower at 32** (two a vCPU); four helpers already
  19% slower at 16.
- On the Pi's gigabit (round eight, S4): two helpers made a 64 KiB broadcast and a 4 KiB
  alltoall 6 to 35% slower under Slurm's binding, 2 to 9% unbound; the mailbox about a fifth
  faster.
- Not yet measured: the same under a site's binding, where a helper shares its PE's core unless
  the job reserves one (H26) - the case most sites run.

*Options.*
(a) None by default, as today; the README tells a fast link's users to set two.
(b) Two by default on a job of three nodes or more - two nodes have one peer, which one helper
already holds alone.
(c) As (b), but only where a node runs no more PEs than it has CPUs online (known at start-up,
before the count shapes the node's connection area), none otherwise: the oversubscribed
mailbox kept, the streams' gain there (46% at 32 PEs a node) given up.
(d) A rule from the measured link - helpers only where it outruns one thread: the link is
measured after the connection area is laid out, and its bandwidth read 1.9 to 5.1 GB/s from
job to job here (H14).

*Recommendation*: **(c)**, built in the VM now behind the decision and confirmed under a site's
binding (H26) at the third session before it becomes the default - every measured workload at
one PE a vCPU or fewer gains or holds, the Pi's losses at four PEs a node are small unbound,
and the one large loss, the mailbox oversubscribed, is the case it excludes.

### A3: the zero-copy send's default, and a third buffer

*What it is.* `OSOE_WIRE_ZERO_COPY=on` lends each send's buffer to the kernel
(`IORING_OP_SEND_ZC`) instead of copying it, until the kernel's notification; a connection
has two buffers, so one may be lent while the other copies. Off by default: over loopback it
lost 40%, and on the Pi it saved nothing.

*The evidence* (H8, 8 PEs a node, three rounds): with no helpers, where one thread holds the
rate back, it raised the stream 7 to 12% and took 6 to 9% off the progress CPU a GiB; with two
helpers, at the ceiling already, it changed neither rate nor CPU (1 to 3% more CPU). Every send
by reference had its notification, none was copied after all, and no case waited behind a
notification, so nothing asks for a third buffer. Locked memory is unlimited on these nodes; on
most systems it is 8 MiB, and a send the limit refuses goes again by copy, the connection
copying from then on, with a notice.

*Options.* (a) Off by default, two buffers, as today. (b) On by default for jobs of more than
one node. (c) On wherever helpers are off. And for the buffers: (i) two, as today; (ii) a third
while zero-copy is on.

*Recommendation*: **(a) and (i)**: it pays only where helpers do not run, which A2 is to make
rare on a fast link, it depends on a limit most systems keep low, and nothing showed a third
buffer would be used.

### A4: the ladder's defaults - the unsent limit and its rule, and the buffers

*What it is.* Each data wire limits the bytes queued in the kernel unsent, so that a small
operation is not stuck behind a stream: by default a rule from the link measured at start-up
(its bandwidth times a turnaround of 128 µs, a guess), or `OSOE_WIRE_UNSENT_BYTES`; beside it
the connection buffer (`OSOE_AM_BUFFER`, 256 KiB) and the receive buffers (`OSOE_WIRE_BYTES` ×
`OSOE_WIRE_BUFFERS`, 16 KiB × 64).

*The evidence.* The first session's ladder (H5): a fetch-add's round trip behind a stream at
the link's rate - 1,960 µs at the system's limit, 618 by the rule (about 417 KB there), 582 at
256 KiB and 524 at 64 KiB, the stream at the same rate in every case; a 128 KiB connection
buffer 516 µs, 1 MiB 747; 256 receive buffers 586, 64 KiB ones 567.
Tonight, at the image's settings, the rule's case again 605 µs (H27). And H14: the rule's input,
the bandwidth measured at start-up, read 1.9 to 5.1 GB/s from job to job, so the limit it sets
varied from about 220 to 530 KB between jobs. H6 - the ladder's code rungs, measured on the
trees before them - did not run; it would say what rungs already merged gave, and changes no
default.

*Options.* (a) Keep the rule and the buffers. (b) Halve the rule's turnaround, to about 200 KB
on this link, nearer the best measured. (c) A fixed limit, 256 KiB, in place of the rule.

*Recommendation*: **(a) for 0.2**: the rule takes two thirds off the system's delay at no cost
to the stream; a lower limit took about a sixth more off on one link, and the rule's input is
to be steadied (N2 below) before its target is moved.

### A6: the receive pool at many peers

*What it is.* Each owner of a node's wires receives into a pool of provided buffers (64 of 16
KiB); when the pool runs dry the kernel completes a receive with `-ENOBUFS` and the owner
re-arms it. The decision waited for H11, the cluster at size, which did not run.

*The evidence.* The first session's fan-in (seven nodes into one PE): 256 buffers cut the
re-arms about five times and raised no rate. Tonight's zero-copy runs, every node sending to
every other at 8 PEs a node: 12,000 to 26,000 re-arms in ten seconds on PE 0's node with no
helpers; with two, 1,000 to 2,000 in 1 MiB blocks and a few dozen in 64 KiB ones, the rate at
the ceiling.

*Options.* (a) Keep 64. (b) 256. (c) Wait for H11 at the third session.

*Recommendation*: **(a)**, now: eight nodes are this cluster's most, so H11 adds no peers to
what was measured, and more buffers have not raised a rate anywhere; a cluster of many more
nodes (the off-network one, N4) is where the pool should be measured again.

### Batch 1: P5 and P6

**P5, a team handle past 2^32 generations.** A created team's handle is its descriptor's
address plus its generation - how many times the PE has taken the number - times the size of
the PE's array of descriptors (design §5.7, D6), and the generation was 32 bits: the 2^32nd
take of one number ended the job, which a program splitting and destroying in a loop reaches
in hours. It is 64 bits now (`team.h`), private to the PE, bounded by what the sum can carry
below 2^64 - about 2^45 takes at the most numbers, computed when the array is made - with the
job's end named past that. A testing hook, `OSOE_TEST_TEAM_GENERATION`, starts every created
number at a generation (refused by name past the bound); `shmem.team-split.generations.L2`
takes one number eight times from 2^32 - 4, each team working and every handle another, and
the first, destroyed, still ends the job by name. With the field 32 bits again the job ends at
the fourth split and the test fails. Wrapping, the item's other way, would have given a
destroyed team's handle to a live one.

**P6, a split whose PEs pass different arguments.** §9.4.6 and §9.4.7 require every parent PE
to pass the same start, stride and size, or `xrange`; one that did not made the others wait,
or split apart, silently on a node. The split's reduction now carries a word mixing the
arguments and the word's inverse beside the free masks and the marks (19 words of the two
lines' 32 at the most numbers): a bit some PE has and another has not is 0 in both ANDs, every
parent PE sees the same, and each ends the job by name with what it passed.
`shmem.team-split.divergent.*` and `.divergent-2d.*`, on one node and on two: PE 1 passing a
size one less, or an `xrange` of 1 against 2, every argument valid on its own PE. The check
removed, all four fail; compared without its inverse, they pass too - the inverse is what makes
every PE's verdict the same when one PE's word is a bit-subset of the others'. No layout or
wire change; the split's model (`split_numbers_model.py`) is about the numbers, which are as
they were.

### T46: tier 3's one-second tail at 128 logical nodes

Measured by a Sonnet agent from the lead's brief (`docs/design/evidence/v0.2-step4/2026-10-20-t46/`,
its README the full account): 44 runs at 128 logical nodes on the Pi-like link, around each
every namespace's counters, the bridge's ports, the host's `softnet_stat` and `arp_cache`, and
in eight runs every node's ARP and TCP handshake frames captured with their times. At the
kernel's default backlog 12 of 30 runs had late leads - 45 of them, 410 to 968 ms behind the
run's median, with nothing between +190 and +410 ms. In the captured runs each of the 14 late
leads is one handshake frame lost - a SYN (4) or a SYN-ACK (13) sent and never received,
17 of the runs' 4,654 dropped frames - and the caller dialling again from a new port about a
second later (999 to 1,010 ms): osoe's dial deadline (`PROBE_NS`, 1 s, `listener.c`), which
fires just before TCP's own initial timeout would. The frames are dropped by the kernel's
per-CPU backlog (`net.core.netdev_max_backlog`, 1,000) on the bridge-to-node hop, which every
logical node shares - 128 nodes' ARP broadcasts arrive as 1.46 million frames a run for the
0.38 million sent. Raised to 65,536: no drop and no late lead in six runs of six, against four
of six at the default. ARP lost nothing that cost a second here, though a lost ARP frame can
(loss injected on two ports: 1.01 to 1.03 s in 3 of 39 pairs). Not shown: the drop site is
read from counters, not traced; what fills the queue beyond ARP's copies; and one run slow
throughout with no drop.

So the tail is the rehearsal's - one kernel's backlog serving 128 nodes - and not a defect of
osoe's, as the author's answer of 2026-10-19 expected. 65 items. Two choices it leaves, below.

### Batch 1: P38, the link's measurement steadied

**The bandwidth.** The root timed three streams of 128 and 512 KiB to its first child and took
the slope of the medians; on Google Cloud's link the 384 KiB between them took about 140 µs,
which a few tens of microseconds at either end of a stream moved by half (H14: 1.9 to 5.1 GB/s
of 2.85). Now the first try, at those lengths, says how much longer the streams must be for the
long one's bytes beyond the short one's to take a millisecond - from the long stream's time
less the round trip just measured - and the tries are made at that length, up to 64 times (8
and 32 MiB); a try at other lengths is not counted. A link where they take a millisecond
already - the Pi's gigabit, whose 384 KiB take 3.4 ms - keeps its first try and its cost.

**The message cost.** The root timed one put to each of its first children against one put to
the first alone, the pairs back to back, so that every child but the first came to the fan from
two round trips idle, the first from one. Where a thread sleeps deeper the longer it idles - a
virtual machine's idle CPU - the others' wake was counted as the message's: on three nodes of
one PE each, three jobs read 2 to 4 µs and two 112 and 159 µs (H14), and H13's eight nodes
read 3, 16, 20, 20, 21 and 28 µs in its six measured jobs. Each try now makes a fan not timed
first, so that every child comes to the timed fan, and the first to its put alone just after
it, from one round trip idle. A round trip more a try: fifteen on three nodes or more.

### P37, for the author: what the cluster's table says of the chooser

Before a refit, the model against H13's table (`docs/fast-link-results.md`;
`docs/design/evidence/v0.2-step4/2026-10-20-fast-link/kit/window/`), and the table's rounds one
by one. Eight nodes, 8 PEs a node, microseconds.

**The chooser's three jobs were two kinds.** Each measured its own link at start-up, and the
rounds' median hid that they chose differently:

| The chooser's job | Message cost measured | Reduce 256 KiB / 1 MiB / 4 MiB | Its shape, by the model |
|---|---|---|---|
| round 1 | 3.2 µs | 1,015 / 3,182 / 12,293 | Träff's, a window of 12 |
| round 2 | 20.0 µs | 1,640 / 6,256 / 25,125 | the tree of 7 children, window 4 |
| round 3 | 27.8 µs | 1,626 / 6,722 / 25,898 | the same |
| the best fixed tree | - | 1,343 / 4,788 / 19,081 | 4 children, window 8 |

Where the message cost read low, the chooser took Träff's reduction, and it ran 24 to 36% faster
than the best fixed tree, and twice as fast at 1 and 4 MiB as the tree it took where the cost
read high.
So for reductions the table's 17 to 26% is, first, P38's unsteady message cost, which turned
Träff's off in two jobs of three; the third session says whether P38 steadies it there.

**The fan-out is chosen right at each window.** The chooser's window is the capacity, the
pipeline depth (`OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PIPELINE`, 4 by default, 8 at the most). At a window of 4 the
measured best tree is the one it takes - at 1 MiB, 8 children 6,212 and 4,321 (reduce, broadcast)
against 4 children 7,417 and 5,643 and 2 children 9,362 and 7,358 - and at a window of 8 a
fan-out of 4 is best, 4,788 and 3,359 against 8 children's 6,244 and 3,861 and 2's 5,568 and
4,220. The closed form orders them the same way at both windows (`shape_timing.py`'s `cost`): the
star at 4, a fan-out of 3 to 6 at 8 by the message cost. So the costs' order needs no refit, and
none is made. Their scale is off: the closed form and the simulation are 1.5 to 2.2 times short
of the trees' measured times and 1.9 to 3.6 times of Träff's, growing with the bytes - most
likely the work within a node at 8 PEs, which the model leaves out - and the choice between
Träff's and a tree holds through it here.

**What is left is the window**, for broadcasts and every tree: 8 pieces in flight ran the best
tree 18 to 28% faster than the best at 4, from 256 KiB, and the chooser cannot go past the
capacity, which sizes every lane's slots. Options:

- (a) `OSOE_COLLECTIVE_PIPELINE`'s default from 4 to 8. Each lane then holds (ceiling + 1) × 8
  slots, about 1.1 MiB in place of 577 KiB at the ceiling of 8, so 9.6 MiB of address space a
  PE at `OSOE_MAX_THREADS=16` becomes about 19, touched only by threads that lead large
  collectives; the release slots in every team number's block double too; every node must
  agree, as now. In the model's two simulations more pieces in flight never cost a large
  collective time, on the Pi's link or this one. Timed first at the third session - the chooser
  at depth 8 against depth 4 and the fixed (4, 8), on P38's steadier link - as A2 was.
- (b) Keep 4, and the README tells a fast link's users to set 8.
- (c) A window apart from the capacity for large collectives on a fast link: a layout change.

*Recommendation*: **(a)**, once the third session's timing confirms it: the gain is the
measured best tree's, and the cost is address space. P37 stays open on this choice and that
timing.

### Batch 2: P36, the helper count rule behind `auto`

`OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS=auto` takes the author's rule (A2): two progress helpers on a job of three
nodes or more where the node runs no more PEs than its machine has CPUs online, none otherwise.
Every PE of a node works it out in its bootstrap from the job's nodes, the node's PEs and
`sysconf`'s CPUs online, before anything is laid out by the count, so the node's PEs agree, and
the node's own rendezvous still checks that they do; nodes may differ, as they may now. Under
`SHMEM_DEBUG` each node's lead says the count its connection area was laid out for and why -
"the job's nodes 2 ... two need three nodes or more", "this node's PEs 3 and CPUs online 2 ...
two need no more PEs than CPUs". The default stays `0` until H26 measures the helpers under a
site's binding and the author confirms it: the switch to `auto` is then one line, `bootstrap.c`'s.

`OSOE_TEST_ONLINE_CPUS` stands in for the machine's count, so that the tests hold on any machine:
`shmem.helpers.auto.three` (0,1,2 on 4 CPUs: two on every node), `.two` (0,0,1,1: none on two
nodes) and `.crowded` (0,0,0,1,2 on 2 CPUs: none on node 0, whose three PEs outnumber its CPUs,
and two on nodes 1 and 2), each moving data between every pair of PEs through the internode
suite's program; and `shmem.start-up.test-online-cpus.invalid`. The mutants, each of which
failed what it should and nothing else: the node count's condition removed, `.two`; the CPUs',
`.crowded`; the count worked out but not applied, `.three` and `.crowded`; the hook ignored,
`.crowded` and the refusal.

Two things for the author when `auto` becomes the default. A job whose cpuset gives it fewer of
a node's CPUs than the machine has online is judged by the machine's, as the decision's "CPUs
online" says; under a site's binding H26 shows whether that matters. And `osoe-launch`'s logical

### Batch 3: P20 and P18, the listeners' addresses and ports

**P20, the rendezvous on every address.** A lead listened for the rendezvous on the one address

### Batch 4: P23 and P24, calls the PEs must make alike

**P23, a symmetric-heap call compared, always.** `shmem_malloc`, `shmem_calloc`, `shmem_align`,
`shmem_malloc_with_hints`, `shmem_realloc` and `shmem_free`, wherever they do something, first AND
a word of their kind and arguments, and its inverse, over every PE (P6's word, now
`collectives.h`'s `osoe_arguments_word`, its values unchanged), and every PE ends alike where the
AND says they differ, each naming what it passed (the release review's conformance C5: a PE that
asked for another size went on with its heap apart, and puts to its objects landed in others').
Through the finalize team's split lines, which no application collective uses: a split of the
world on another thread meanwhile shares nothing with it, and the world's epochs `osoe-stat` shows
stay the program's own. `shmem_realloc` of a null pointer is checked as `shmem_malloc`, and to size
0 as `shmem_free`, as the specification makes them; a call that does nothing - a size of 0 - takes
no collective, so one PE's 0 against another's 8 leaves the others in the check until the
deadlock detector names them. `shmem.malloc.divergent.*` (PE 1 another size, hints, count and size
of the same product, alignment, realloc size, or block to free; every routine on one node, malloc
and free on two) and `shmem.malloc.equivalent.*`; mutants: no check, all eight fail; the freed
block left out of the word, the two `free` ones.

It costs what the author's "one small reduction a call" said, and in the VM that is not small: a
`shmem_malloc` and `shmem_free` pair at 4 PEs, the median of 7 runs of 500, from 35 to 102 µs on
one node and from 137 to 366 µs on two logical nodes - about 33 µs a call on one node and 115 on
two, a reduction being two meetings of the node's PEs where the barrier each call already takes is
one. A program that allocates in a loop pays it. Folding the arguments into the call's barrier
would make the barrier carry data, a protocol change; not taken.

**P24, the calls of a node's PEs compared, under `OSOE_CHECK_COLLECTIVES=1`.** Off by default until
it is timed at many PEs a node (H12). Each PE says its call - kind, a reduction's or scan's
operation, element and width, its bytes where every member must pass the same, and a broadcast's
root, an alltoall's strides or a scan's inclusiveness - in three spare words of its own internal
region's release-counter line for the team's number (`internal-region.h`, beside the collect's
two: words of an allocated line, no layout change), with plain stores before its arrival; the
node-mate that meets every arrival compares them all with its own before anything is released: a
data collective's lead after its wait, a sync's last arriver after its bump. Each member's arrival
is a release read-modify-write of the arrival counter, and the comparer's acquire of its final
count - or its own read-modify-write, the last arriver's - reads its release sequence, so the
stores are seen (§3.16), as the lead reads the members' sources. Not covered: an alltoall is
compared at its closing sync, after its puts, so named rather than prevented; a scan over a team
spanning nodes has no meeting within a node; a PE passing 0 elements returns before arriving, a
hang for the detector. A PE without the setting says nothing, and the one comparing names it.
`shmem.collective-check.{kind,gather,root,count,sync,alltoall}` on one node and on two - PE 1 a
non-lead of node 0 there, whose difference no lead's check sees - `.off` (the default unchanged),
`.unsaid`, and `.clean.*`, the collectives' own programs with the check on. Mutants: the compare
off, every mismatch and `.unsaid` fail; the say off, every test with the setting on; the sync's
compare removed, `sync` on one node and `alltoall` on both (on two nodes the reduction's lead
still catches `sync`); the bytes ignored, `gather` and `count`; the detail ignored, `root`. A
false alarm the clean runs found before the merge: a collect's members but its lead hold no
places, and said "fcollect" - the gather is now told its kind by its caller.
