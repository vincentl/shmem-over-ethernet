## The author's decisions

1. **`shmem_global_exit`: only the caller carries the status** (D1). Every PE
   other than the caller leaves a global exit as it did - at once, standard
   I/O flushed, `_exit` - but with status 0, so that a launcher which ends the
   step at the first bad exit (`srun -K1`, `KillOnBadExit`) sees none before
   the caller's own, after its handlers and its flush; `srun` returns the
   highest exit code of all tasks, the caller's. Decided on the plan's §3.1 C,
   measured on a patched tree under the VM's Slurm (handlers of 0 to 5 s all
   finished, `srun -K1` returned 7; the unpatched tree returned 143 at
   200 ms). The alternative the plan first recommended - holding the other
   PEs until the caller's process has ended - was dropped: it races Slurm's
   kill after the caller's exit. The limit to state: a site with `WaitTime`
   or `srun -W n` ends the rest n seconds after the first task leaves,
   whatever its code. The author's words: "yes to global exit - its a solid
   plan." *Undo*: `_exit(0)` back to `_exit(status)` and the grace thread's
   status likewise in `abort.c`; `abort_exit_model.py`'s STATUS property as
   it was.
2. **Teams: numbers held per PE, then counters per team with the buffers per
   PE - both before the tag** (D3). The author asked how limiting the
   job-wide, never-returned team number really is, and for alternatives that
   set aside no large memory up front; then why "counters per team, buffers
   per PE" was not the recommendation, saying "I am fine accepting significant
   code rewrite to achieve a better design." The design is
   `docs/design/team-resources-proposal.md`: Step D, a number is each member
   PE's resource - a free mask a PE, a split ANDs the members' masks in a
   reduction over the parent and takes the lowest common bit, destroy frees
   it (what Sandia's library and MPI context ids do); Step E, a team keeps
   counters and one small slot a position, and the large slots are a PE's,
   one lane a thread, lent to a collective by a grant. The limit becomes the
   live teams one PE is in; a destroyed team costs nothing; a number costs
   about 6 KiB a PE in place of 200. Four slices, about four weeks. What the
   proposal still asks the author is its §8. *Undo*: the proposal is a
   document; nothing is built.
3. **The start-up fixes before the tag are the plan's middle option** (D2):
   every PE validates every setting; the control segment exists before the
   `/dev/shm` refusal and a PE refusing its own environment tells its node; a
   30 s notice from every start-up wait naming what it waits for; the
   rendezvous proves the secret before it acts, unconfirmed callers served
   side by side; the refusal tests run with `-k`; the sentences. A refusing
   node telling its neighbours in the rendezvous tree is after the tag. "D2
   seems fine to me."
4. **Merge into `main` at green checkpoints.** The standing direction of
   2026-09-23, reaffirmed for the release fixes and the team slices: a part
   merges when its verification is green and its record written.
5. **The release checklist moves into the tree**, as `docs/release-checklist.md`,
   from the author's status page, which the repository scrub stops pointing
   at. "Yes, move checklist."
6. **The README is true of the tree at every commit.** Each change carries
   the README sentences for what it changes, in the same branch, so that a
   merge describes itself; a sentence that later work will replace - the team
   row until the redesign lands, a limit a later slice lifts - may say so
   ("temporary", "work in progress"). The author's words: "README should be
   updated before each commit to reflect commit reality. It is okay to
   include 'work in progress' or 'temporary' or something like that for items
   that will be replaced."
7. **D4 to D9 stand as the plan recommends**: the floor test's pins only when
   asked; `OSOE_AGENT_SLICE_US` stays 100 with both sides documented;
   `OSOE_MAX_THREADS` stays 16 and is compared PE by PE; the models under a
   label of their own; the version 0.1.0; the evidence trees scrubbed to
   placeholders and SPDX lines added. The author asked whether the team plan
   changes D3, D7 or D8: D3 *is* the team plan; D7 and D8 are untouched by it
   - the team work adds models under the same label, and the version stays
   0.1.0 until the tag, which follows the teams.

8. **The team proposal's six questions (§8), answered the same evening.**
   (1) *Node blocks*: a pool with an index, "smart for scaling", asked
   whether it has performance concerns - none on a hot path: a collective's
   arrive and release go through a pointer the descriptor holds, the engine
   reads the map once per team it learns, a split pays a bitmap claim and a
   map store beside its reduction and sync; the one new thing is a limit on
   the teams a node holds at once, `OSOE_TEAM_BLOCKS`, default 1,024 (under
   1 MiB of blocks and 256 KiB of map at 256 PEs and 256 numbers, against the
   table's 56 MiB), free to differ between nodes since the wire carries a
   team's name - its lead's local id and number - not a pool index.
   (2) *The small slot*: a setting, not baked in, `OSOE_SMALL_COLLECTIVE_BYTES`,
   default 1 KiB ("tuning would be interesting"), 128 to 16,384, agreed at the
   rendezvous since every lead must classify a collective alike; a number
   then costs about 15 KiB a PE. (3) *Threads*: the recommendation, a lane
   per thread record. (4) *`OSOE_MAX_TEAMS`*: 256 after E "is more than
   sufficient". (5) *The names* - lane, grant, small slot, block index,
   holders, generation - all fine. (6) *D before E*: "yes, make after D a
   merge point then E". *Undo*: each is a line in the proposal (D3, E2, E9,
   E10, §5) and, once built, a setting's default or a layout.

9. **A global exit's caller leaves last** (put to the author that night,
   during the release fixes' Part 2). Building item 1 found that `srun -K1`
   still returned 143 when the caller's process ended before another PE's:
   a PE of another node still being told (2 and 7 runs of 30 for a caller
   that is not its node's lead), a PE whose flush waited on a lock or had
   megabytes to write, a second caller inside its handlers. Asked where 143
   comes from - Slurm kills every task still running at the first nonzero
   exit, and a task it kills counts 128 + 15 - and what the specification
   requires (§9.1.6: the status returned to the environment is one of the
   values passed), the author chose both halves of the fix: the PE carrying
   the status waits for its own node's PEs, and for the other nodes', before
   its own end. *Undo*: the waits in `abort.c` and `progress-host.c`, and the
   carrier rule back to `called` (`abort_exit_model.py`'s `called_carries`).
   Built and recorded in `2026-10-02-release-fixes.md`, Part 2.
