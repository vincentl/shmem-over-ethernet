# 2026-10-27: T38 measured - the CPU a wake costs, as built and with the spin

T38 (`docs/prompts/v0.2-open-items.md`; round nine's scale S4): `shmem.helper-wake` cannot see an
owner - a progress helper, or the lead's agent - that spins before it raises the flag it raises
before it sleeps. The flag rises late, the next put still finds a sleeper and wakes it, and every
count is perfect, while each idle pass costs the spin. On 2026-10-18 a bound on the CPU of an idle
job failed as a rate: the spin was three times a baseline that itself varied threefold
(`2026-10-18.md`, "T38 tried, and left open"). The author's answer of 2026-10-25
(`2026-10-24.md`, "The author's answers", item 2): measure the CPU a wake costs under a trickle of
puts, with the spin and without; a test of it if the spin costs more than five times, otherwise a
count of passes for each wake in the layout-42 slice.

**It does not, robustly: two and a half times on an idle VM, eleven to thirty-one times beside busy
loops, because the as-built cost moves sevenfold to twentyfold with the machine's load and the
spin's does not. So no test is built, and T38's guard is the count, in the layout-42 slice - of an
owner's idle passes whose flag rose late, not of its passes, which the spin leaves at five a wake.**

## What was measured

In the `osoe` VM, alone on it, `main` at `94cd6e0b` built twice in the release preset, once with
round nine's mutant (`preflag-mutant.py`: a helper, and the agent, that moved nothing in a pass
polls its ring's completions, its wake word and its dirty bits for up to 200 µs before it raises
its flag). A program of four logical nodes of one PE each: PE 0, alone on node 0, puts 8 bytes to
nodes 1, 2 and 3 in turn, 300 puts 2 ms apart, so that every owner is asleep at each put; every
node's lead reads each owner's thread CPU (`/proc/self/task/<tid>/schedstat`, user and system,
the scheduler's own count of its time on a CPU), its wakes (`transport.am.wakes`) and its passes
(`transport.am.passes`) around the trickle. Node 0's owners are the ones PE 0's puts wake, so
node 0's CPU over its wakes is the CPU a wake. At no helpers and at two, the deadlock detector off,
the trees alternating; alone, and beside one, four and eight busy loops. The program, the scripts
and every run are in `docs/design/evidence/v0.2-step4/2026-10-27-t38/`, whose README says what each
file is; the program is kept there as a patch, not in the suite, since it bounds nothing.

## What it found

Node 0's CPU a wake, in µs - the median and the range over the rounds - and the spin's cost over
the as-built, of the medians and of the worst pair (the spin's lowest over the as-built's highest):

| | Helpers | As built | With the spin | Times | Worst pair |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alone, 2 ms apart, 6 rounds | 0 | 143 (90 to 157) | 347 (283 to 369) | 2.4 | 1.8 |
| | 2 | 137 (107 to 176) | 361 (352 to 384) | 2.6 | 2.0 |
| Alone, 10 ms apart, 2 rounds of 100 puts | 0 | 158 (150 to 166) | 421 (413 to 429) | 2.7 | 2.5 |
| | 2 | 163 (147 to 178) | 412 (388 to 436) | 2.5 | 2.2 |
| Beside a busy loop, 3 rounds | 0 | 15 (14 to 17) | 216 (212 to 217) | 14.5 | 12.8 |
| | 2 | 14 (13 to 16) | 221 (219 to 223) | 15.7 | 13.7 |
| Beside four, 3 rounds | 0 | 19 (13 to 29) | 212 (212 to 213) | 11.3 | 7.3 |
| | 2 | 18 (13 to 37) | 231 (218 to 263) | 12.7 | 5.9 |
| Beside eight, 3 rounds | 0 | 7 (7 to 17) | 212 (204 to 225) | 31.1 | 12.1 |
| | 2 | 10 (9 to 17) | 217 (211 to 240) | 22.9 | 12.2 |

- **The spin adds 193 to 263 µs a wake at every load**: its poll runs to its time limit, by the
  clock, and a faster core does not shorten it. **The as-built cost is the passes' own, five a
  wake, and moves with the machine**: 18 to 35 µs a pass on an idle VM, 1.4 to 7 beside busy
  loops. The other nodes, woken by their sockets, show the same: a put received cost 45 to 164 µs
  as built alone and 6 to 59 beside busy loops, and with the spin 245 to 460 and 202 to 381.
- **Passes a wake do not move**: 5.0 in both trees in every series 2 ms apart, 5.2 in both 10 ms
  apart at two helpers. The mutant polls between a pass and the flag without passing.
- **Part of the swing is the host's.** A fixed burst of arithmetic, the library not involved, cost
  31 to 48 µs 2 ms apart on the idle VM and 17 to 20 beside one or four busy loops - the host (an
  Apple M3, on mains power) gives a lightly loaded VM slower cores. That is a factor of about two
  of the library's seven to twenty; the rest is not explained here (the wakes between the VM's
  idle vCPUs are the likeliest, and are not measured).

## What follows

- **No test.** A test runs alone (`ctest -R`), beside others in the suite at `-j4`, and beside
  eight busy loops at the tag (T1), so its bound must sit above the highest as-built cost a wake
  and below the lowest with the spin: 178 and 204 µs, 1.15 times apart - less room than the idle
  rate of 2026-10-18 left. The rule's five times holds beside load and fails alone. T38 stays
  open.
- **The count, in the layout-42 slice** (the author's alternative), **must be of an owner's passes
  that moved nothing and were not followed by its flag within a few microseconds** - the 2026-10-18
  record's words - **not of its passes**: under the spin, passes a wake stayed at five. As built,
  nothing but a store or two lies between such a pass's end and the flag (`raise_flag_and_look`,
  `shmem/src/progress-host.c`), so a late flag needs the owner preempted in that window; the spin
  makes one at each idle pass it polls to its end, at least one a wake. Counted with the fine clock
  read at both points - a few tens of nanoseconds an idle pass - into a field of the owner's record,
  the slice's test can bound it a wake under the same trickle, a count that no core's speed enters.
  The same test can bound passes a wake, which held at 5.0 to 5.2 in every series and needs no new
  field: it would catch the other shape of spin, a poll made of passes.

**Not tried**, for the author's weighing: a test that keeps a core busy while it measures would sit
in the second regime, eleven to thirty-one times, but would rest on how this host treats an idle VM
and would take a core from the tests beside it; and the review's other form of the guard, the time
from a round's acknowledgement to the flag's rise, which the spin makes 200 µs by its own clock.
