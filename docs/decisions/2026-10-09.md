# 2026-10-09: the author's answers on v0.2's scope before its tag

On the evening of 2026-10-09, with step 4's Part 3 under its last verification,
Claude put six points to the author: the two questions step 4 had left open,
two documents owed, and two things found on the way that 0.2 could take. The
author answered each in a word; what follows says what each answer settles.

1. **Question 4, a blocking get a piece at a time, gets a short exploration
   before a decision** (`2026-10-07-one-thread-profile.md`, "Question 4": a
   blocking get waits a round trip for each piece of 64 KiB less a reply's
   header, where a put streams; Claude recommended issuing its pieces as a
   nonblocking get does, models first). The author: schedule a
   short exploration to gather information, and experimental data if it
   helps, before deciding. The first number is already in: on the Pi a 1 MiB
   blocking get took 11,559 µs at the median against a put's 9,080, the
   difference about one round trip for each of its seventeen pieces
   (`2026-10-08-helpers.md`, "On the Pi"). The exploration runs after Part 3's
   merge.
2. **The control wire's acknowledgements stay** (Part 2's first adversarial
   read, F1, `2026-10-08-control-wire.md`): the control wire's segments are
   acknowledged as the data wire's are, which the build already does; the
   alternative, the deadlock detector's reports carrying counts of control
   messages sent and applied, is not taken. The author: "yes".
3. **Part 4, a fast link, is in 0.2** (the brief's Part 4, which waits on a
   link above a gigabit): the author puts it in 0.2's scope, and 0.2 may ship
   without it if the hardware is not there in time. Helpers stay off by
   default either way. To that end the author asked what renting four or eight
   cloud nodes would cost; Claude's answer, from the providers' published
   prices: on Google Cloud, `c4-standard-16` - x86-64, 16 vCPUs, 23 Gbps
   inside a network, and no limit a connection that Google documents, where
   AWS holds one connection to 5 Gbps, or 10 in a cluster placement group,
   which matters to osoe's one data wire a node pair - at about $0.79 a
   node-hour on demand, about $0.32 to $0.47 spot; a campaign of three or
   four sessions of four hours on eight nodes about $80 to $100 on demand.
   Beside Part 4's numbers it would give osoe its first x86-64 kernel, real
   node counts past the Pi's four, and question 4's data on a fast link.
   Not yet decided: the account would be the author's, and every launch is
   put to the author first.
4. **The release notes say that v0.1's `osoe-pacer mix` could hang**: a run
   given `--seconds` decided at each period's top, each PE by its own clock,
   whether its time was up, and a run whose periods ended near the deadline
   could split - about one run in forty at 60 s on the default seed
   (`2026-10-08-helpers.md`, "The mix that hung"; fixed at `bc0751d`). The
   author: "fine".
5. **A test that every `OSOE_` setting the code reads is in the README** - a
   row for `OSOE_TEST_ABORT_DELAY_MS` was missing from the settings table
   until Part 3's documents, and no review had caught it. The author: "yes".
   Before the tag.
6. **Design §3.12 rewritten as built** at step 4's merge point, as the brief's
   record section asks. The author: "yes".
