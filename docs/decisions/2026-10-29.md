# 2026-10-29: P2's hand-on wake and the room count, built behind a setting and timed

## The brief

The author, that morning: "Yes: build P2's hand-on wake behind a setting, time it in the VM
against waking every writer - the node's rate and the writers' sleeps at 4 to 64 writers - and
bring me the numbers before it becomes the default." The model was built the night before

## What was built

On the branch `hand-on-wake`, from `main` at `eacb39c2`, `all` - waking every writer, as osoe
always has - staying the default:

- **`OSOE_TURNOVER_WAKE=all|hand-on`**, read by every PE (`bootstrap.c`, refused by name
  otherwise), in each connection's view (`hand_on`): the lead's decides its agent's wake at an
  open, each writer's its own hand-on. A node's own; nodes may differ.
- **The wait word's two steps of one** (`base/wait-word.h`): `osoe_wait_word_wake_one`, the
  agent's at an open and a writer's after a reservation, and `osoe_wait_word_pass_on`, a
  registered waiter's wake of one other - both the two-sided protocol's mover, waking one.
- **The reservation's room** (`struct osoe_reservation`): what the look found, after the
  reservation or short of it, which the hand-on decides by.
- **The writer's half** (`transport-internode.c`, `reserve_waiting`): after every reservation
  whose room left is a put's smallest part or more - 4,136 bytes at the defaults, the floor
  below which a put already waits for the next buffer - wake one waiter; and woken to room that
  large but too little for its own message, pass the wake on, once a turnover, then look again.
  Room below a put's part wakes no one, which the model did not have: its `floor` cases, added
  for it, are live (`docs/design/evidence/hand-on-wake/`, every case of the night before again,
  the largest 11.6 million states, 3 minutes 10 seconds).
- **Two counters**, `am.turnover_hand_ons` and `am.turnover_passes`; four tests -
  `shmem.turnover-wake.{default,all,hand-on}`, eight PEs of node 0 putting 16 MiB each to node
  1's one PE, every byte checked and the counters saying which wake ran, and
  `shmem.start-up.turnover-wake.invalid`; the README's row and its Limits sentence; design
  §3.11's as-built paragraph.

**Checked**, in the VM: the release build without a warning and the static checks 14 of 14 on
the tree as committed but its records; the targeted tests; the internode suite whole with
`OSOE_TURNOVER_WAKE=hand-on`, 621 of 621. The test counts by configure: release and debug 1,201,
asan 1,161, tsan 833, the single-node build 579 of the full build's and its five, 621 labelled
`internode`, of which tsan runs 278. *Not run*: the whole verification - debug, asan, tsan, the

The agent's CPU a GiB is within a fifth either way to 32 writers - the hand-on's the higher, its
segments the more - and at 64 0.16 s under all against 0.12 under the hand-on. Every table, both block sizes whole, is in the
evidence's README; the raw lines `herd-2026-10-29.txt`.

So the hand-on does what P2 asked of it - the herd H12 measured on the cluster is gone, the
sleeps flat from 16 writers where all's grow as a quarter of the writers, every writer within
4% of the mean, no put past 27 ms where all's reach 0.5 to 2 s at 64 - and at 64 writers, 16 a
vCPU, it moves 1.36 to 1.56 times the bytes. But below 64 it moves 5 to 35% fewer. **Why**:
its chain fills a buffer one writer at a time, and the agent, which seals the open buffer as
soon as it has bytes and nothing is in flight (§3.9), sends it emptier - 136 to 168 KiB a
segment against all's 179 to 256 - and every link is a writer's `futex_wake` on its own path
and the next writer's scheduling delay: §3.11's own warning of the chain.

**An experiment, not in the branch** (`experiment.diff`, a diagnostic copy with two switches):
the open waking four writers at once - as many as a reopened buffer holds messages of a chunk
at the defaults - and no hand-on. Against all, alternated with it and the hand-on, two series
of 4 s: 0.98 to 1.00 of all's rate at 4 and 8 writers, 0.91 to 0.98 at 16, 1.04 to 1.11 at 32
and 1.79 at 64, at both block sizes; its sleeps 0.86 to 1.62 a 64 KiB, flat; its slowest writer
0.86 to 0.96 of the mean; its longest put 4 to 33 ms. The herd's cure is the count, not the
chain. Four together with the hand-on fell between, 0.76 to 0.99 below 64. What a count of the
room's chunks does not cover is a room of small messages, which holds more writers than it
counts - a node's PEs' atomics toward one node - where the hand-on's chain wakes as many as
fit; untested.

**Not explained**: about one hand-on run in eighteen had every writer's longest put at 45 to 98
ms at once, where all's and the experiment's shared waits reached about 20 ms. Not the
turnover's wake: the one caught by the diagnostic copy had node 0's two buffers sealed, full and
committed, its agent asleep 36 ms on the send in flight, and node 1's agent asleep the same 36
ms with nothing to receive - the stream held between the two nodes, on the loopback's TCP as
far as can be told; the kernel's counters do not single it out (the evidence's last section).

## The room count

**The design**, as the author gave it, and two calls of the session's:

- **The open wakes up to ceil(room / chunk)** writers at once, `osoe_wait_word_wake_up_to` - the
  room the open made, after the pre-open drain's replies, in `OSOE_AM_CHUNK`'s bytes, the largest
  message: four at the defaults, three whole messages and a part. *The session's*: counted from
  that room and at least one - from the full buffer it reopens the count is none, which the
  model's `room_count_before_open` shows is a lost wake.
- **A reservation smaller than a chunk hands on**, by the hand-on's floor. *The session's*: only a
  writer's that waited. The open counted a chunk for each writer it woke, so a woken one that
  took less left room the count did not see - a room of small messages wakes as many as fit -
  while a writer that reserves without waiting, a put's next part or a 64 KiB put's 40-byte
  tail, takes room the count gave others, and a hand-on there wakes a writer to find none. The
  pass is the hand-on's.

**The model first** (`turnover_wake_model.py`'s `room`): five cases, a chunk of two units in
buffers of two to four, writers needing more than the room in all, live, the largest 749,336
states; the breakage `room_count_before_open` fails, LOST WAKE, as it must; every earlier case
keeps its state count.

**Built** on the branch as `OSOE_TURNOVER_WAKE=room`: `enum osoe_turnover_wake` for the setting,
each connection's view its wake and the chunk; the README's row and Limits sentence and design
§3.11 say it. Three more tests and a mode: `shmem.turnover-wake.room`, and the same programs with
eight-byte puts under all, hand-on and room (`small.*`), the last asking for hand-ons as the
hand-on's does. **Checked**: the release build without a warning, the static checks 14 of 14,
the targeted tests 17 of 17 and the internode suite whole under room, 625 of 625; the turnover
and connection tests under ThreadSanitizer, 16 of 16 with its gate's setup and judgement, and
under AddressSanitizer, 14 of 14, each preset built without a warning; the counts by
configure, release and debug 1,205, asan 1,165, tsan 837, 625 labelled `internode`, of which tsan
runs 282, the single-node build 579 of the full build's and its five. *Not run*: the whole
verification.

**Timed** (`herd-room-2026-10-29.txt`; the evidence's README has every table): all, the hand-on
and the room count rotated, three series of 5 s, 4 to 64 writers, blocks of 1 MiB, 64 KiB and the
small messages 4 KiB and 64 B. Each one's rate over all's, the medians:

| | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 writers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 MiB, room | 1.00 | 1.08 | 1.00 | 1.18 | 1.94 |
| 64 KiB, room | 0.97 | 0.98 | 0.95 | 1.18 | 1.90 |
| 4 KiB, room | 0.99 | 1.09 | 0.96 | 0.91 | 1.44 |
| 64 B, room | 1.00 | 1.15 | 1.15 | 1.09 | 1.13 |
| 1 MiB, hand-on | 0.80 | 0.83 | 0.79 | 0.97 | 1.56 |
| 64 B, hand-on | 1.30 | 0.73 | 0.67 | 0.60 | 0.61 |

The room count keeps all's rate where the writers are few - 0.91 at its least, 4 KiB puts at 32
writers - and passes it where they are many, 1.13 to 1.94 at 64 writers at every size, with the
herd's other costs gone: its sleeps a 64 KiB flat from 16 writers (1.39 to 1.47 at 1 MiB, 2.57 to
2.63 at 4 KiB, where all's reach 14.3 and 14.8), its slowest writer within 5% of the mean from
16 writers, its longest put's median under 28 ms where all's is 0.6 to 0.9 s at 64 writers of
the three larger sizes, and the agent's CPU a GiB a third less at 64 writers of 1 MiB and 64 KiB. The hand-on loses most with small messages: it hands on at
nearly every small put while anyone waits - 1,008 times a 64 KiB of 64-byte puts, against the room
count's 2 to 20. At 64 B the room count's agent spends more CPU a GiB than all's at 32 and 64
writers, 0.39 to 0.41 s against 0.25, its segments the smaller. The rare shared stall came once in
its 60 runs and in none of the hand-on's this time: not the hand-on's own.
