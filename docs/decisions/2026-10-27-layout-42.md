## D26: a node's PEs in one PID namespace

**What it does.** Each PE reads `/proc/self/ns/pid`'s device and inode (`base/pid-namespace.c`,
zeros where `/proc` cannot say) and publishes them in its slot before it joins; once the node
has joined, every PE compares the known ones with the first PE's that is known
(`check_agreement`) and a node whose PEs differ is refused, every PE that gets there naming the
same two: "the node's PEs are in more than one PID namespace: local PE 0 (rank 0) is in
pid:[4026531836] and local PE 1 (rank 1) in pid:[4026532385]; ...".

**Until the join, no pid from another namespace is judged.** A node-mate judges its lead by
pid twice before `check_agreement` - while the lead finishes the segment
(`lead_ended_before_ready`) and while the node joins (`wait_count`) - and from another namespace
the lead's pid names another process or none, so a lead at work was taken for one that had
ended: a refusal naming a cause that is not the cause. The lead's namespace is now beside its
pid in the header, a plain field stored before `launch`'s release as the pid is, and
`lead_ended` judges only where the two agree. The cost: a PE in another namespace whose lead
dies before the node has joined waits until its launcher ends it, as every node-mate did before
the adversarial read of 2026-10-03 gave it `lead_ended` - the misconfiguration being what the
join then refuses.

**Round six's shifted pids.** The control segment's leftover check (`osoe_shm_create`) reads
the creator's namespace beside its pid: a leftover made in another namespace is refused as
such, by name, and never reclaimed - its creator may be running there - whatever process its
pid names here. Until now a pid another process held here was named as that process's:
"is still running: is another job using these job and step ids?". The old "its creator's pid
... this process's own" message stays for a leftover whose namespace is unknown. And
`osoe-stat --gc` leaves a segment of another namespace alone and says why (the source's sketch,
`phase1-step7-slice3-portability.md` Q2); the job tables and the connection area record no
namespace, their layouts their own, and are judged by their creators' pids as before.

**Tests**, one node, both builds, skipped where unshare is refused (exit 77):
`shmem.start-up.pid-namespaces`, three PEs, local PE 1 put in a PID namespace of its own by
`unshare --user --map-root-user --pid --fork` and local PE 2 a second late, so that PE 1 waits
for the join and looks at its lead meanwhile; and `shmem.start-up.pid-namespace-leftover`, a job
of one PE in a namespace of its own killed after init with its segment kept - its creator pid
2 there, a live kernel thread here - refused as another namespace's by a start by its ids, and
left alone by `--gc`. The VM allows unprivileged user namespaces (Debian's default).

## P7: a team of one claims no block

A team of one touches nothing of the node's - its sync and collectives return at once - and
until now it still claimed, named, mapped and left a block of the pool, a kilobyte of
`/dev/shm` that held nothing it used: a `split_2d` with an `xrange` of 1 made one a PE, and four
emptied the default pool at 256 PEs a node. Now `claims_block` claims for a team of more than
one PE alone; `shape` builds no node set for a team of one; `take_number` gives it no block,
`block_index` UINT32_MAX, so its epoch word's high half is 0 (`OSOE_EPOCH_WORD`, the wrap written
out), which `osoe-stat` already read as none; and its destroy leaves nothing.

**The model.** `pool_dry_model.py` gains `one`: a pool of two, one free and one a live team's,
and a lead whose split makes a team of one beside a team of more - a split_2d's row of one PE
and its column - with a property, SPARE, that a split whose teams of more than one PE find their
blocks does not fail dry. The design holds it (5 and 7 states, SC and TSO), and
`singleton_claims`, the claim as it was, fails it. The other cases keep their verdicts.
`team_block_model.py` has no case for it - a team of one never enters a block's life - and says
so.

**The test.** `test-team-split.c`'s `dry` mode asserted the opposite - a team of one taking the
fifth block and another failing dry - and is rewritten on three PEs, since the regression it
also holds, a lead that needs two blocks and finds one, needs a lead of two teams of more than
one PE: five teams fill the pool's five blocks; six teams of one then split, two a PE, more than
the pool has, each working and its epoch word naming no block
(`osoe_test_teams_without_block`); a sixth team of three fails dry; a split_2d by rows of two,
whose PE 0 leads two of more than one, fails holding the one it claimed; and a team two PEs
destroy late serves PE 0's split at once. `shmem.team-split.dry.L3`, where `.L2` was. The first
whole verification found one more test of the old meaning: `test-team-storage.c` read, through
`osoe-stat -d`, a record for its team of each node's PEs reversed, which is a team of one on a
node of one PE (`0-1-2-0-1-0`, `local-order.internode`) and on every PE under force-remote, where
SHMEM_TEAM_SHARED is the PE alone - three tests failed on it in the release preset. It now
requires that record only of a team of more than one PE, and of a team of one that it has none.

## D1: the summary walks a data collective

**What is published.** The tree state gains the data collective the team's local lead is in
and what it waits for: the epoch, opened by `collectives.c` before the lead's wait for its
node's members and closed before it releases them, and at every wait of the generic phase the
kind, the node whose word ends it and the piece, round or step - a child's piece or the
parent's release and grant in the tree, a round's sender or receiver in Träff's, a step's peer
in Bruck's and the gather by ranges, a child's total or the parent's down in the leads' scan of
collect's totals. `osoe-stat --summary` prints a line under the team's while the lead is in one:
`node 0 ... world  data  4/0   waits for node 1's piece 0   next 1`; and a created team that has
run only data collectives has its line now, where it had none.

**Published at every look, not once.** The first build published each wait as it began, and the
walk test found a cycle at once: a tree's root waits for every child in one wait, named the
first missing child when it began, and kept naming it after it came - node 0 named node 1,
node 1 named its parent. The wait's condition, which `osoe_region_wait_condition` evaluates after
every wake, now publishes whenever it finds itself still waiting, so the node named is one
missing now (`say_awaited`, `node_totals_in`, `count_in`).

**Where a count cannot say whose.** Träff's grants - each round's receiver adds to one counter -
and the exchange of collect's totals wait on a count several leads add to, and reading the
fields beside it before the count says they are there would race with the puts that write them:
those waits publish how many are missing and name no node. The walk ends there, and the line
with nothing open is the node to look at; with eight nodes and node 6 missing, Träff's walk from
node 0 ends at node 4, one grant short. A grant field each round with a word of its own would
name the receiver - a protocol change, modelled first, which is not this slice's. A scan's
members are a tree of PEs, not leads; each names the PE it waits on in its blocked record
already, which the PE lines show.

**Tests.** `test-hang-walk.c` gains modes, eight logical nodes, node 6 never calling the
collective and PE 0, in it, walking from a thread of its own: `shmem.hang-walk.reduce`,
`.reduce-large` (lanes, the shape fixed), `.broadcast`, `.fcollect`, `.collect-scan`,
`.collect-exchange` and `.circulant` (Träff's, the Pi's link set by hand), each requiring the
walk to end at node 6 - 0 to 2 to 6 for the trees, 0 to 6 for Bruck's, 0 to 5 to 6 for the scan
of totals - or, for the two counts, at a count one short with every other lead in the collective;
and `.summary`, two nodes, the launcher's timeout printing the summary's data line.

- **A walk through Träff's or the exchange stops at a count.** A grant word a round, beside

  the exchange's slots likewise. Not built: the line that has nothing open names the node.
- **A PE in another namespace whose lead dies before the join** waits for its launcher, as
  every node-mate did before `lead_ended`: it can judge its lead by nothing until the join

- **The job tables and the connection area record no namespace**: their layouts are their
  own, and `--gc` judges their leftovers by their creators' pids, as before.
- **`shmem.team-split.dry.L2` is now `.L3`**: the mode needs a lead of two teams of more than
  one PE, which two PEs cannot make once a team of one claims nothing.
- **The VM's `/tmp` holds `batch2`**, 1.5 GiB of tmpfs, the second batch's tree of 2026-10-25,
  merged since: not removed here, the removal being yours to say.

  'killed process'`, runs as the VM's user, and `kernel.dmesg_restrict` is 1: `dmesg` fails, the
  grep finds nothing either way, and a record's "no process killed for memory" that rests on it
  says nothing. `journalctl -k` reads the kernel's journal as that user, in the
  `systemd-journal` group, which is where this record's claim is from.
