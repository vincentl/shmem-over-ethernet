# 2026-11-01: the large-cluster playbook, its adversarial read, and the morning's discussion points

## The ask

The author, the evening after the 0.2.0 tag, going to bed: scan the repository,
code and documentation, and write a playbook for another AI agent to follow if
the tree is cloned to a large HPC cluster - which tests to run and in what
order, what code might need updating, changing or rewriting, where new
algorithms might be considered; correctness always the goal, performance
chased; a first proposal, then an adversarial review, a refutation and a
refinement; then a list of discussion points for the two of us to work through
in the morning.

## What was written

Three files, none committed - the author reads first, as with every proposal:

- **`docs/prompts/large-cluster-playbook.md`**, the playbook. Phase A: eight
  gates in order, each with a pass condition and a stop rule, from the site's
  facts through the eight-node yardstick to the stress tester at size and the
  corpus. Phase B: the ladder - node counts doubling to the whole cluster and
  PEs a node to the cores - with a battery at each rung, framed around the
  off-network brief's seven questions, which it does not repeat. Phase C:
  twenty-one places the code is likely to bend, ranked by how soon the ladder
  meets each, with the class of change each would call for; and a table of
  where a different algorithm might be considered, each row with why it might
  win, why it might not, and how the question is decided. Four grants, G1 to
  G4, name where the playbook asks more than the brief's rules allow, each the
  author's to give or withhold below.
- **`docs/reviews/archive/large-cluster-playbook-adversarial-read.md`**, the
  read's record: 25 findings, 22 accepted whole and 2 narrowed, none refuted
  outright; the two blocking ones would each have cost the agent a day. The
  lead's first draft had offered a remedy (`OSOE_NODELIST`) that does not exist
  and asked for a change (the least-try message cost) built on 2026-10-28.
- **This record**, with the discussion points.

`docs/prompts/README.md` indexes the playbook.

## How it was made

Four Sonnet agents surveyed the code in parallel - the transport and start-up,
the collectives, the data path and memory, the tests and the kit - and reported
findings with citations; the lead spot-checked the concrete claims it cites
(`shmem_pe_quiet`'s allocation a call, `OSOE_AM_HUGEPAGES` present and without
effect, the 16 KiB piece and the window of 8, the README's words on the
progress threads' core under a binding) and read the design notes' §5.8 and
§10, the README's scaling section, the ceilings, the brief, the release note
and the third cluster session's results itself. The draft, written from
those; then a fresh agent on the lead's model read it adversarially by the
project's method; the lead verified the five findings that would change the
most against the source before accepting any, and rewrote.

## What the surveys established, and the lead checked

The facts the playbook's phase C rests on, each in the tree today:

- **The suite cannot run across separate machines**: every test of a job
  starts it with `osoe-launch` (239 registrations) and nothing can put `srun`
  in its place; the tier-3 registrations (about 70, each a map, a program and
  an environment) are the unit a harness would take.
- **The lead's progress threads share PE 0's core under a task-per-core
  binding** (`OSOE_AGENT_CPUS=task` the default; the README's row says it), and
  `auto`'s two helpers apply on a node whose every core has a PE.
- **A large collective's piece is a compile-time 16 KiB and at most 8 are in
  flight an edge**: 128 KiB in flight a round trip, one to two gigabytes a
  second an edge - a tenth of a 100 Gbit/s link.
- **The node's lead combines its P members' vectors serially** and releases
  them with one wake of all; **a node pair's 64 credits are shared by every PE
  of the node** toward that peer, for gets and fetching atomics.
- **Node names of more than one bracketed range have no remedy under `srun`**:
  the `OSOE_*` variables are read as a whole set only where `OSOE_PROCID` is
  set; and **no setting chooses the interface** - a peer is dialled at its
  name's first IPv4 address.
- **`shmem_pe_quiet` allocates n - 1 flags a call**; the silent-peer check is a
  `getsockopt` on each of the lead's agent's wires every 10 s; the deadlock
  detector is a star from node 0.
- **The kit** is unbound by default (`KIT_SRUN='-K1 --cpu-bind=none
  --overcommit'`), makes an allocation a job, and runs `osoe-stat --gc` only
  after a kill of its own, by an `srun` outside the killed job's allocation.
- **`OSOE_AM_HUGEPAGES` is still in the tree** - `am-configuration.c`,
  `connection-area.c`, `init.c`'s info line, `unknown-settings.c`, the README's
  row - documented as having no effect, though the author retired it on
  2026-10-11 (A10, "its removal now an implement item"); no open item carried
  the removal, and the list closed at zero code items on 2026-10-30.

## For the author: the morning's discussion points

Each with what it is about, the evidence, the options and one recommendation.

1. **The fabric (C21).** If the cluster has InfiniBand or RoCE NICs, osoe's TCP
   runs over IPoIB or the NIC's Ethernet side, at the kernel stack's latency
   and a fraction of the fabric's bandwidth; the RDMA backend is designed behind
   `transport.h` and `internode.h` and not built, by the decision in force
   ("RoCE is a constraint, not work"). (a) TCP as it is: the floor measured
   beside `ib_write_lat` and `_bw`, reported, nothing more; (b) the Soft-RoCE
   functional backend the design foresaw as the post-release road, built here
   first; (c) a verbs backend developed on the cluster itself, where the
   hardware is. *Recommended*: (a) for the first campaign - a backend is months,
   and the first session's job is to find out what the TCP design does at scale,
   which no fabric changes; (b) or (c) decided from the floor's numbers after.
2. **What the cluster is.** Every gate depends on facts the author may already
   have: the node count, cores and sockets a node, memory and `/dev/shm`, the
   NIC and the fabric, the kernel and whether `io_uring_disabled` is set, Slurm's
   task plugin and default binding, the allocation sizes and time limits a user
   gets, whether the file system is shared, whether `ssh` to nodes is allowed,
   whether the cluster is off the network. *Recommended*: the author fills one
   table before the session; A0 becomes a check against it, and the arithmetic
   of §2.1 is run on paper first, so that the ladder's top rung is known to be
   reachable or not before any job runs.
3. **The four grants, G1 to G4.** The brief says measure first, change code
   only where a run cannot go on, model nothing there. The playbook asks: G1 the
   suite's harness under `srun` (C1); G2 the small changes - the hostlist
   parser, an interface setting, the neighbour-table check, `pe_quiet`'s
   allocation, the kit's `--gc` and its `SHMEM_DEBUG` on the leads only; G3
   timing simulations and models written on the cluster for §5's rows the
   numbers favour, nothing built from them; G4 the one-allocation `common.sh`
   change before the ladder. *Recommended*: G2 and G4 granted; G3 granted; G1
   granted but done here first (point 4).
4. **Build C1's harness here, before the cluster?** The development VM has a
   one-node Slurm of four CPUs in which the kit's `launch`, `on` and `spawn`
   have run; a `ctest` registration that emits `srun` for the tier-3
   programs' block and cyclic maps can be built and tried there, and the suite
   that checks results would then run across real nodes from the cluster
   session's first day instead of its last. The multi-node Slurm fixtures,
   written from the documentation, could be captured on the cluster and
   replayed here after. *Recommended*: yes, here, as the first VM work after the
   tag; about a day.
5. **The progress threads' core (C3).** Under a task-per-core binding the
   lead's agent and both helpers share PE 0's core, and `auto` gives two helpers
   on a node whose every core has a PE. (a) The README's "Running a program with
   `srun`" says to set `OSOE_AGENT_CPUS=node`, or leave a core without a PE, at
   many PEs a node - words only; (b) `node` the default; (c) `auto` counting a
   node's spare CPUs - none where every CPU has a PE - which revisits A2 and
   P36. *Recommended*: (a) now; (b) and (c) after the PEs-a-node ladder's
   numbers, which H26 at 8 PEs of 16 CPUs did not give.
6. **Which design changes to model first, here.** Phase C names four protocols
   worth a model before any cluster: the parallel intranode combine (C5), the
   two-level alltoall (C8), the hierarchical scan (C9), a control tree in place
   of the control mesh (C17). *Recommended*: C5 and C8 - the two the
   PEs-a-node ladder meets first and the two with the largest arithmetic gain
   at 256 PEs a node - modelled in the VM, timed at 32 PEs on four cores; C9
   and C17 after the ladder says whether their costs show.
7. **The pipeline's cap (C6).** The piece is a compile-time 16 KiB and the
   window at most 8: 128 KiB in flight an edge, a tenth of a 100 Gbit/s link by
   arithmetic. Making either settable is a layout change (the piece sizes every
   lane). *Recommended*: measure first - the kit's §6 ladder and `window.sh`'s
   fixed shapes at 8 nodes on the fast link - and decide after; no change
   before the cluster.
8. **The credits at many PEs a node (C11).** 64 a node pair, shared by every
   PE of the node toward that peer: a quarter of an outstanding get a PE at
   256. *Recommended*: a setting in the timed runs (`OSOE_AM_CREDITS=256`,
   every node alike); a default by the node's PEs only after `gets.sh`'s
   numbers.
9. **`OSOE_AM_HUGEPAGES`, retired on 2026-10-11 and still in the tree.** The
   decision says retired; the open-items list made its removal an implement
   item that no item id carried, and the list closed at zero code items.
   (a) Remove it - the setting's read, the connection area's `MAP_HUGETLB`
   branch, the info line, the unknown-settings entry, the README's row, with
   the settings-documented test holding; (b) keep it as documented, "no
   effect", and amend the decisions index. *Recommended*: (a), a small batch in
   the VM before the cluster, and a line in this record on how the item was
   lost, so the next list carries an id for every "now an implement item".
10. **A bound on `shmem_finalize`'s wait (C19)?** A hung node holds every
    other's finalize until `OSOE_NO_PROGRESS_TIMEOUT` or the allocation ends;
    the project has refused timeouts that end a job (design §3.15). *Recommended*:
    no bound; the README's "How a job ends" says so in one sentence, and the
    user's `OSOE_NO_PROGRESS_TIMEOUT` is the bound.
11. **The no-spin rule.** Nothing in the library spins (`test-no-spin.c`
    holds it); the data-path survey notes a bounded spin in the owner after a
    productive pass, or in `wait_until`, as the untouched way to cut the two
    thread wakes on a small message's path where a node has spare cores.
    *Recommended*: keep the rule; not a question for the cluster, and only
    worth reopening with `OSOE_AGENT_CPUS=node` numbers at many PEs a node.
12. **The return contract at this size.** The brief's one Markdown file, with
    about 36 tables of nine rows added by the ladder; and `SHMEM_DEBUG=1` on
    every PE of a 128,000-PE job is hundreds of thousands of lines a job.
    *Recommended*: one file still, the rungs' medians in one table a measurement
    and the spreads in an appendix (§7); `SHMEM_DEBUG` on PE 0 and the leads
    only, a kit wrapper (G2).
13. **Two documents or one.** The brief (phase B) and the playbook (phases A
    and C around it) overlap in purpose; the playbook keeps the brief's branch
    and return file. *Recommended*: two files - the brief is frozen as the tag's
    last item and the playbook changes with the answers here - and the brief's
    "What this session is for" gets one sentence pointing at the playbook.
14. **One session or several.** The gates, the ladder's lower rungs (8 to 64
    nodes) and the whole-cluster rungs need allocations of very different
    sizes, and the small changes of G2 are better in the tree before the top
    rungs run. *Recommended*: two sessions at least - the gates and the lower
    rungs first, their findings and G2's changes merged here, then the top
    rungs and the whole-cluster questions.
15. **A small-collective cost model.** §5's new rows - the dissemination
    barrier, the binomial tree, recursive doubling, the tree at fan-out 16 - are
    decided by a model of hops and message costs that does not exist:
    `shape_timing.py` models the large collectives only. *Recommended*: a small
    model in the VM, a day, before the cluster, so that the ladder's barrier and
    small-reduction times have a prediction to stand beside.
16. **Naming post-0.2 reviews.** The archive's recent names carry `v0.2-`; this
    read is named for its work alone, as `CLAUDE.md` asks. *Recommended*: names
    by the work from here on, and a sentence in `docs/reviews/README.md`.

## The author's answers, the morning of 2026-11-02

   and sets osoe's floor beside `ib_write_lat` and `ib_write_bw`; a backend is

   before any cluster session - `ctest` registrations that emit `srun` for the

   *Made the same morning.*
