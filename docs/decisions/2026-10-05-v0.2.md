# 2026-10-05: what v0.2 is

The author's decisions for the work after the 0.1.0 tag, taken the evening of
the day the talk's questions came in. They set the scope and order of v0.2,
whose briefs are `docs/prompts/task-v0.2.md` and the step briefs it names.
Documents only: no code changed.

The questions behind them came from the talk's "What comes next" slide and
its audience: three performance items (progress threads sharded by peer node,
a bandwidth-optimal large reduction, the tree's shape chosen per link), a
suggestion that `process_vm_readv` and `process_vm_writev` could support
symmetric statics, and the audience's own ask - `osoe-launch` and the library
on one beefy desktop, with no io_uring and so on older kernels, for a faster
development cycle on small PE counts.

1. **v0.2 is four steps, in this order: the single-node build, the tree's
   shape per message size, Rabenseifner's reduction, then progress helpers.**
   Each goes to its own session with its own brief; each merges at green
   checkpoints with the README true at every commit. *Undo:* reorder the
   steps in `task-v0.2.md`; nothing in one step's brief depends on a later one.

2. **The single-node build is an opt-in, `OSOE_SINGLE_NODE`, default `OFF`.**
   The author's words: "we want the builder to opt in to this, not out of the
   full build". On, the library serves jobs of one node only: no liburing at
   configure time, no io_uring, no TCP, a job of more than one node refused at
   `shmem_init` by name. The full build is unchanged, and keeps every
   requirement it has today: Linux 6.7's futex2 calls and liburing 2.6. *Undo:*
   flip the default, or remove the option and its backend.

3. **Rocky Linux 9 is the single-node build's goal.** Red Hat 9 and its
   rebuilds: a 5.14 kernel with backports, glibc 2.34, GCC 11, binutils 2.35.
   Ubuntu 24.04 and Debian 12 fall out of the same work. The multi-node build
   stays on its io_uring requirements - the author: "For multi-node, stick
   with the io-uring requirements." *Undo:* drop the futex fallback (item 4)
   and the single-node build reaches only kernels of 6.7 and later.

4. **The classic `futex(2)` is used only by the single-node build**, in place
   of the futex2 calls, which do not exist before 6.7: `FUTEX_WAIT_BITSET` and
   `FUTEX_WAKE_BITSET` with the private flag and an absolute monotonic
   deadline, which is every futex2 feature the library uses on one node. One
   wait path a build, chosen when configuring, never at run time. The full
   build keeps futex2 alone, since its ring's `IORING_OP_FUTEX_WAIT` needs 6.7
   anyway. Recommended by Claude; the author: "only fall back to futex for the
   desktop build". *Undo:* a runtime choice, or futex2 in both builds.

5. **Development and testing stay aarch64-first.** The Lima VMs and the Pi
   cluster; a Rocky 9 aarch64 VM for the single-node build. No x86-64 hardware
   is promised: "we might be able to get a bit of time on some cloud-based x86
   pairs of servers", and the author cannot guarantee it now. So the x86-64
   items - the first native x86-64 run of the four presets, the x86-64 network
   path, the pipeline's window on links faster than gigabit, and the helpers'
   performance tuning - are **in the brief as an option gated on hardware**,
   not in the main order. *Undo:* promote them into the order when a machine
   exists.

6. **Rabenseifner's algorithm, not a ring**, for large reductions: recursive
   halving reduce-scatter over the node leads, then the internode allgather
   that fcollect already has, with the crossover from the tree chosen from
   measurement as design §5.8 requires. A ring's 2(n - 1) hops is 170 ms of
   latency alone at 500 nodes, against the project's axis of internode
   algorithms logarithmic in node count. *Undo:* a ring, for a fabric where
   latency does not matter.

7. **Decisions in v0.2 go by performance and elegance, not by the code or
   the tests they cost.** The author: "we are trying to be our best - so don't
   skimp because of code or testing complexity". For the tree's shape this
   means the measured form: the link's hop latency and bandwidth measured at
   start-up, the fan-out and pipeline depth chosen from them per message size,
   not a fixed threshold. *Undo:* none needed; a brief that finds the cheap
   form faster or clearer argues it in its record.

8. **Progress helpers: a complex of the lead's thread and io_uring helpers.**
   The author's premise, stated as settled: one thread is not enough to drive
   an HPC link - "full stop". Claude's design, accepted for the proposal: the
   lead's agent keeps the control plane - the barrier tree and team state, the
   control queue, the deadlock detector, team publication, abort, close and
   the silent-peer check - and helpers own disjoint sets of peer connections
   with a ring each and move the bytes: receive, parse, apply, reply, seal and
   send. Every queue between them is one writer and one reader. The helpers are
   built last, after everything with a measurable result on the hardware the
   project has; correctness is tested locally with oversubscribed logical
   nodes (more than four PEs a node, several logical nodes a host), and
   performance when x86-64 pairs with a fast link are available. A count of one
   helper must be today's code path. The proposal is
   `docs/design/progress-helpers-proposal.md`; the brief waits on it. *Undo:*
   leave `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS` at zero; the control plane and data plane
   split stays, since it is what makes the count free to grow.

9. **`process_vm_readv` and `process_vm_writev` are not a route to symmetric
   statics**, and the README's statics limits will say why in two sentences:
   they copy between address spaces and map nothing, so a put or a get becomes
   a syscall, an atomic cannot be done through a copy at all, a wait would
   never be woken, and Yama's `ptrace_scope` 1 refuses siblings. What osoe does
   - gather the statics by linker script and remap them as shared memory at
   init - is the only way Linux offers to *map* another process's private
   memory. Design §4.3 rejected the copy route in 2026-09 as "phase 1.5"; the
   talk's question is the reason to write the answer down where a reader looks.
   Claude's recommendation, not struck by the author. *Undo:* strike the
   sentences.

10. **The shape of the briefs.** One arc brief, `docs/prompts/task-v0.2.md`,
    in the form of `task-first-release.md`; one self-contained brief a step,
    in the form of step E's (`docs/prompts/done/phase1-step7-slice4-prompt.md`),
    since each step goes to its own Opus session that starts cold; the helpers
    step built from a design proposal first, the way step E was built from the
    team-resources proposal. An adversarial read by a fresh agent before every
    merge, and the full review at two points: after the single-node build and
    after the helpers. Author: "default" and "yes".

11. **The demo's zero injection bandwidth is bale's.** `histo` and `ig` print
    per-node volume divided by time, and the per-node volume multiplies the
    per-PE updates by `cores_per_node`, which defaults to 0 and is set only by
    `-c` (`apps/histo_src/histo.upc:153`, `std_options.upc:40`). Pass `-c 4`
    on the Pi cluster. Not a change to this tree: the demo kit lives on the
    cluster's login node, and its cheat sheet takes the flag.
