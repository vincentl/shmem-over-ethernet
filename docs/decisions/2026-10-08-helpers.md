## What was built

1. **The setting** (`bootstrap.[ch]`). `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS`, 0 to 64, read by
   every PE; a malformed or larger value is refused by name before anything is
   laid out by it (Part 1's N5). It sizes the node segment's owner records and
   the connection area, so the node's own rendezvous compares it between a
   node's PEs, in a job of more than one node, naming both counts (N3) - before
   the connection area, which would have refused such a PE in the name of
   `OSOE_AM_*`. `SHMEM_INFO` lists it.
2. **The threads** (`progress-host.c`). The agent starts the helpers at its
   first pass - or at its exit or its abort, if it never passed - so each begins
   with the agent's signal mask and CPUs; each asks the agent's slice
   (`osoe_agent_place_this_thread`, `agent.c`), names itself `osoe-helper-N`,
   publishes its tid in its owner record, enables its own ring - its only
   submitter from then on - and runs the agent's pass over its own wires with
   Part 1's per-owner sleep protocol. A helper counts its thread-scope counters
   into a block nobody reads (`osoe_metrics_unpooled`), so it takes none of its
   PE's `OSOE_MAX_THREADS`. Each owner's connections, busy words and reply
   descriptors on lines of their own (`aligned_alloc`), since with helpers
   adjacent owners are different threads writing every pass; the wires, laid
   out by ring, and each ring's small arrays may share a line at a ring's edge
   (the second read's N2, below).
3. **Requests and answers** (`helper_request_model.py`). A request word the
   agent alone writes - a sequence above its kind: idle, quiet, stop - stored
   seq_cst, then the helper's wake word bumped and woken; the helper loads its
   wake word, then the request and the abort word, at the top of every turn.
   A quiet or idle request is answered after the pass of the turn that read it,
   in one word: the request it answers and its verdict. A stop is acknowledged
   at once; the helper then flushes and closes its own wires
   (`flush_before_close`, `close_connections`, which on a helper return at a
   sweep that finds the node's abort, rather than end the process as the
   agent's sweep does), says done, and returns; the agent joins it.
4. **A helper that never answers**: the agent reads the helper's count of
   turns after its seq_cst store of the stop, nudges it once for each count it
   sees, and ends the job naming a helper that has turned twice since without
   acknowledging; one that does not turn at all is waited for, sweeping, under
   the no-progress rule, with a notice after 30 s. `OSOE_TEST_DEAF_HELPER` is
   the defect for the test.
5. **The abort.** The agent, on the node's abort, starts any helper not yet
   started, nudges each, aborts owner 0's wires within the grace, waits for the
   helpers' done words within the same grace - and joins each that is done,
   leaving one that is not to the process's end, detached - then, a global
   exit carried here, for its peers' ends on the control wires alone
   (`carrier_model.py`'s `control_only`). A helper reads the abort word at
   every turn and at every sweep of its close, aborts its own wires within its
   grace, says done and returns. A reset or an end of stream without its goodbye
   is excused while the node's abort is declared (`progress.h`'s host
   `aborting`), at every helper count, and nothing more is sent on a connection
   that failed (`flush`, `flush_control`; the second read's S1, below).
6. **The detector across owners** (`deadlock_owners_model.py`). Each call of the
   agent's idle or quiet view takes each helper's answer to the request the
   previous call posted - none, or a stale one, counts as not quiet - and posts
   a new one; activity sums each owner's application count, which each owner
   publishes per message (`application_applied`, a single-writer word). The
   detector's `/proc` count of a PE's threads leaves the lead's helpers out by
   the tids in their owner records.
7. **Counters** (node layout 36). The AM counters a record an owner, in a
   section of the node segment's own, the seventh metric scope
   (`OSOE_MSCOPE_OWNER`): the node record they were until layout 35 moved there
   as owner 0's. Each record says its owner's tid, data and control wires, and
   a new count, `transport.am.wakes`: the owner's waits that its wake word ended
   (`ring.c`). `osoe-stat`'s default view has a line an owner - who it is, its
   tid, its wires, passes, wakes, segments and messages - and a node line
   summing them; the dump a section an owner; the JSON an array of them; the
   threads view a row for each helper, which has no counter block. Readers that
   want the node's count sum the owners: the tests' `osoe_test_node_metric`,
   `osoe-pacer`'s totals.
8. **The rest.** The unsent limit taken by each owner for its own wires, the
   node's line said once every owner has (`say_unsent`); the silent-peer check
   the agent's over the control wires; `OSOE_WIRE_SPLIT`'s notice once a node.
9. **Tests.** `shmem.helper-wake.0-1-2-3`: PE 0 puts to each peer node in turn
   at two helpers, and the helper the map names for that peer must be woken -
   its `transport.am.wakes` - at least a tenth of the 100 puts, and the other
   helper at most twice. `shmem.helpers.deaf.0-1`: `OSOE_TEST_DEAF_HELPER=1`
   ends the job at `shmem_finalize` naming helper 1. Refusals: the count
   malformed, above 64, and different on a node's PEs. The suite runs at any
   count by the environment `ctest` passes on.

## The mix that hung: the pacer's own defect

**What was seen.** On 2026-10-08 a minute's `osoe-pacer mix` at no helpers -
8 PEs on four logical nodes, in a verification of the C just before `64f48c4` -
was ended by the deadlock detector: PEs 0, 3, 4 and 7 in `shmem_sync_all` at
the world's epoch 457, PEs 1 and 6 in `shmem_team_sync` at 457 too, PE 2 in
`shmem_team_sync` at 126941 and PE 5 at 127789. Teams `mix1` = {1, 5} and
`mix2` = {2, 6} each had one member at a world sync and the other at its
team's, and neither had printed a line of period 5. Nothing reproduced it at
first: 20 runs of `main` and 20 of the branch alternating on the VM, and on
the Pi cluster 10 and 10 at no helpers and 5 of the branch at two, all clean.

**The cause.** The mix decided at the top of each period whether `--seconds`
was up, each PE by its own clock, read from a start of its own at its own
moment after the last period's world sync. The default seed fixes the periods'
lengths, nominally 61.97 s for the first five's slowest teams, and in the hung
run they took 13.7 + 10.0 + 13.1 + 10.0 + 13.2 = 60.0 s, each exercise's steps
ending its period a little early or late (the second read's N5): so a 60 s run
makes that decision just at the deadline: of the 40 clean runs on
the VM, 36 went on to period 5 and 4 stopped after period 4; of the Pi's 25, 18
and 7. In the hung run
PEs 1 and 6 read the time as up and left for the teardown, whose first rebind -
the mailbox's, onto the world - syncs the world: the `shmem_team_sync` at 457.
The other six read it as not up and began period 5: PEs 2 and 5 waited in their
rebinds' syncs for PEs 6 and 1, and PEs 0, 4, 3 and 7 ran their exercises to
the period's `shmem_sync_all`, also at 457 - the routine an odd period ends
with. Every routine and epoch in the report fits, and the detector named a real
deadlock. The defect is the pacer's, from the day the mix was written
(`42bd9b9`, 2026-09-27): `main` and v0.1 have it, and nothing of this part is
on its path.

**The fix.** The time is up when the PE that has run longest says so: a
max-reduction over the world at the top of each period (`mix_time_up`), as each
exercise's steps already agree their elapsed time. **The test**,
`tools.pacer.mix-end.0-0-0-0`: four PEs on one node, 4 s of 0.5 s periods, with
PE 1's clock started an hour late by `OSOE_TEST_PACER_LATE_START`, so that by
its own clock its time is never up and PEs each deciding for itself split at
the first period's end past 4 s, however long the build's periods take. It
passes with the fix; with the old reading restored it fails every run, the
detector finding PEs 0, 2 and 3 in the teardown's world sync and PE 1 in its
team's (3 of 3; as first written, PE 1 two seconds late, it split them only
where a period ended within two seconds of the deadline - the second read's
S3, below). On one node, so that every preset and the single-node build run
it.

## Where the build departs from the proposal and the brief

- **Helpers start at the agent's first pass**, not in `shmem_init` after the
  agent (the proposal's §3.7, the brief's item 1): so that each begins with the
  agent's signal mask and CPUs and the agent alone creates and joins them. So
  `shmem_init` can return before any helper exists - a writer's work waits in
  the helper's bitmap, which its first pass takes - and a failed
  `pthread_create` is a fatal on the agent then, not in `shmem_init`.
- **No abort request word** (the proposal's §3.6): every owner reads the node's
  abort word, as every agent does, and the agent nudges the helpers; the
  agent's wait for their done words is the proposal's. A third request kind,
  idle, beside quiet and stop, for the detector's cheap look.
- **A helper that never answers is found by its turns** (the proposal's "a
  deadline, as every wait has", plan item 3).
- **The silent-peer check stays the agent's**, over the control wires, as the
  proposal's §3.6 has it, where the plan as first written gave every owner its
  own.
- **The owner's per-connection state is a line-aligned slice of an array the
  node allocates once**, not a member of `struct osoe_progress_owner` (§3.2).
- **The stop-and-abort model is two**: `helper_request_model.py` for the stop
  and `abort_owners_model.py` for the abort.
- **`shmem.reply-pool.overflow` at a helper count** overflows the data wire's
  helper's pool, one descriptor a connection, at its second get, not owner 0's,
  whose arithmetic the test was written for; it passes at two and three helpers
  and checks the helper's pool, not the forwards' term.

## The models

- `helper_request_model.py`: the lead's requests to a helper - the request
  stored, then the helper's wake word bumped and woken; the helper loading the
  word before the request, so it never sleeps through one; a quiet request
  answered after a whole pass, naming the request it answers; a stop
  acknowledged at once, then done after the close, and only then the ring
  freed; a helper that never answers found by its turns, not by a time (plan
  item 3). The design holds under SC and TSO, with liveness; seven breakages
  fail - the request loaded first, a wake without the bump, done before the
  close's last ring use, a deaf helper whose turns nobody counts, an answer
  taken for the wrong request, the count of turns read before the stop is
  posted, and the count a plain store, which holds under SC and fails under
  TSO.
- `carrier_model.py`'s `control_only`: the carrier rule with helpers (plan item
  5) - the lead judges each peer by the control wire it holds alone - holds,
  where judging each wire alone (`two_wires`) fails.
- `deadlock_owners_model.py`: the deadlock detector with a helper a node -
  the agent the control plane, the helper the data wires - sampled through
  quiet requests, the activity summing both owners' applied counts. All seven
  of `deadlock_model.py`'s scenarios hold with liveness (`get_far` the largest,
  2,320,876 states); three breakages fail - the helper's count left out of the
  activity, an answer without a pass after the request, the second half not
  waiting for the helper's answer.

## The plan

1. **The setting.** `OSOE_PROGRESS_HELPERS`, 0 to 64, default 0, read by every
   PE at start-up and agreed among a node's PEs - it shapes the connection area,
   whose header has carried the count since layout 5 - and free to differ
   between nodes. A count past 64 is refused before the area is laid out (Part
   1's read, N5), and a PE whose count differs from its lead's is refused naming
   both counts, read from the header first (N3).
2. **The threads.** On the node's lead, after its agent: H helper threads, each
   created with every signal blocked, placed by `OSOE_AGENT_CPUS`'s rule and
   asking `OSOE_AGENT_SLICE_US`'s slice, as the agent is. Helper h is owner h:
   it enables its ring on itself, the ring's only submitter (invariant 19), and
   runs the pass the agent runs, on its own wires, with Part 1's per-owner sleep
   protocol (`lower_flag`, `raise_flag_and_look`, the claimed wake). With H > 0
   owner 0 holds the control wires alone.
3. **Requests, answers and the wake.** Each helper has a request word, which
   the lead's agent alone writes, and an answer word, which the helper alone
   writes, in the lead process's memory - helpers are threads of the lead's
   process, so no other process sees them. The lead stores a request, bumps the
   helper's wake word and wakes it - unconditionally, requests being rare; the
   helper loads its wake word before it loads the request, as every wait loads
   its word before its pass (§4.9), so a request is never slept through. Three
   kinds:
   - **quiet**, the deadlock detector's: the helper answers after its next whole
     pass with whether it is idle and quiet (§3.15), so an answer for request q
     reflects a pass begun after q was posted - the "pass between the halves" a
     sample needs, owner by owner;
   - **stop**: the helper acknowledges at once, then flushes and closes its
     wires (`flush_before_close`, `close_connections`), then answers done and
     returns. The lead waits for done under the close's own rule, the
     no-progress watch, since a close waits as long as the peers take to
     finalize; and for the acknowledgement by the helper's turns, not by a
     time - a helper that never answers is a defect, and ends the job naming
     it. The helper counts its turns, a seq_cst store at each turn's end; the
     lead reads the count after its seq_cst store of the stop, and nudges the
     helper - a bump of its wake word and a wake - once for each count it
     sees. A helper that has turned twice since without acknowledging has read
     the stop and ignored it: the turn in progress at the post may have loaded
     the request before it, and the next loads it after, a store-buffering
     pair that seq_cst on both sides forbids. One that does not turn at all is
     waited for as every wait is, sweeping, under the no-progress rule, with a
     notice; the plan as first written had a fixed deadline here, which is a
     wait that gives up because time has passed (§3.15), and the proposal's
     "a deadline, as every wait has" is the sweep's (§3.10);
   - **abort**: no request - every owner reads the node's abort word at every
     turn, as every agent does, and aborts its own wires with the disposition
     the word carries, bounded by the grace; the lead waits for the helpers'
     done words, under the same grace, before its process ends.
4. **The detector across owners.** `osoe_progress_host_quiet` posts a quiet
   request to every helper not already answering one, and is quiet only when
   owner 0 is and every helper has answered the latest request quiet; the
   sample's second half already retries until its patience ends. Activity
   sums the owners' application counts, each published by its owner alone and
   read with acquire loads (§3.5); counters only grow, so an unchanged sum is
   every count unchanged.
5. **A peer's abort across owners.** With no helpers owner 0 holds both of a
   peer's wires and treats an abort on either as the peer's (Part 2). With
   helpers an abort read on any wire declares the node's abort - as owner 0
   does now - and every owner reads the node's word, so the excuse of a reset
   consults the node's word as well as the wire's: a node that is ending has
   nothing to fear from a peer that is ending too. The carrier rule, which
   only the lead's wait uses, is judged on the control wires alone, the wires
   the lead holds: a process's end ends all its streams at once, so a peer's
   control wire ending says what its data wire's would, and of two carriers
   the first to begin tells the other on the control wire
   (`carrier_model.py`'s `control_only`). What the excuse cannot cover, as
   owner 0 holding both wires did, is a helper reaping a peer's reset before
   owner 0 has applied the abortive goodbye the peer sent on its control
   wire. A goodbye that arrived is read before the reset behind it, so the
   case is a data wire whose goodbye never arrived, and a peer ends with one
   of its goodbyes unacknowledged only once its grace has run out: ten seconds
   in which owner 0 had the control wire's goodbye in its socket. It takes an
   owner 0 that ran no pass for that long, and it ends this node by a fatal
   naming the reset rather than with the peer's status. *Amended* (the first
   read's N1, the second's N1): the window also takes a peer whose agent ends
   from a sweep of its own close at `shmem_finalize` (`sweep.c`), which waits
   for no helper and tells no peer, as it did with no helpers - that peer's
   end is read as its death.
6. **Counters and osoe-stat.** Each owner's counters in a record of its own in
   the node segment (layout 36), and readers - the metrics, `osoe-stat`, the
   detector - sum them; `osoe-stat`'s node header lists the owners, each with
   its wires, passes and counters (the brief's item 4). *Amended:* as first
   written, owner 0's counters stayed the node record's in the header and only
   the helpers' had records; as built, every owner's is in the owner section,
   owner 0's among them, so that a reader finds them all one way.
7. **The rest.** The unsent limit taken by every owner for its own wires (the
   request word the lead's application thread writes, each owner keeping what
   it last took and publishing what it reached, and the lead's agent saying
   the node's line once every owner has taken it); the silent-peer check the
   lead's, over the control wires, as the proposal's §3.6 has it - every peer
   answers on its control wire, whose keepalive probes it while it is idle and
   whose check watches it while a control message is in flight, so a vanished
   peer is found there within the same budget whatever its data wire holds
   (the plan as first written gave every owner the check over its own wires);
   each ring's `OSOE_WIRE_SPLIT` notice once a node (Part 1's N7); the reply
   pool sized per owner, as Part 1 laid it out. Each helper's tid in its owner
   record, so that the deadlock detector, which counts a PE's threads through
   `/proc`, leaves the lead's helpers out as it leaves its agent; and a helper
   counts its thread-scope counters into a block of its own that nobody reads,
   so that it takes none of its PE's `OSOE_MAX_THREADS`: its work is its owner
   record's.
8. **Models first.** The request protocol - post, wake, answer; the stop's
   acknowledgement and deadline; a helper that never answers; the quiet
   answer's pass - as a model with its breakages; the detector with owners, a
   node's engine being several; then the C.
9. **Tests.** The suite at 2 and 3 helpers on 3, 4, 8 and 16 logical nodes, in
   every preset, TSan and ASan among them; `osoe-pacer` mix and mailbox at 0 and
   2 helpers; a test that a PE's wake reaches its wire's owner; a helper that
   never answers a stop word, ended by the deadline by name; the mutation each
   catches. Then the README: the Limits' "one thread a node" paragraph, the
   memory rows, the settings table.
