4. **A control wire: a second connection a node pair, the lead's, for control
   messages alone** (§8 item 1), not a mailbox from the helper that receives a
   control message to the lead. The author: "absolutely - mixing control with
   data can lead to deadlocks". This goes past item 8 of
   `2026-10-05-v0.2.md`, whose sketch put a queue with one writer and one
   reader between threads: nothing between threads is a queue now. Checked
   before the answer: no control message needs ordering against data. The
   deadlock detector's proof counts acknowledged, applied messages
   (`deadlock.h`), not a probe that trails data on one stream; a barrier's
   quiet precedes its arrival; the goodbye goes on every wire. *Undo:* one
   wire a pair and a mailbox to the lead, the alternative of the proposal's
   §3.1.
5. **Two wires a pair always, at every helper count** (§8 item 2). The author:

   undo already kept the control and data split at zero. The brief's Part 2
   measures a barrier during a put stream on the Pi before and after the
   control wire, and stops if the Pi's barrier pays anything beyond the noise.
   *Undo:* a second wire only when helpers exist.
6. **No helpers by default, and a count only from the environment** (§8 item

   fast link has been measured. The author: "agree, default to no helpers, set

   *Undo:* a rule, once a fast link's numbers give one.
7. **The zero-copy send is built in step 4, behind a setting off by default**
   (§8 item 4). The proposal offered the rung or the hardware-gated items, and
   Claude recommended the gated items; the author pushed back - "will we lose
   any performance? if no, we should just do it to debug and get it working".
   Off, nothing changes. On everywhere, it would lose in three places: over
   loopback the kernel copies anyway, as it does every zero-copy send looped
   to a local socket, and adds a notification completion a send; below about
   10 KB a send, pinning the pages costs more than the copy it saves; and on a
   fast link a sent buffer stays the kernel's until the peer's TCP has
   acknowledged it, so a connection's two buffers cap one peer's stream at
   about 256 KiB a round trip - some 50 Gb/s at 40 µs. So:
   `IORING_OP_SEND_ZC` behind a setting, off by default, for segments above a
   threshold; a buffer released on the notification completion rather than
   the send's, modelled first (`wait_word_model.py`, the §3.5 reopen); its
   correctness tested in the VM, where loopback runs the notification path all
   the same; on the Pi, a real NIC, whether the kernel copied anyway and what
   it saves the agent; its default, and whether a connection needs more than
   two buffers, decided on a fast link. Design §3.3's "Not used" line says so
   until it is built. *Undo:* leave the setting off, or remove it.
8. **Träff's credits are for the pieces a sender's window waits on alone, as

   the first P - W of a round of P, so that no credit lands in a lead's words
   once it has returned from its last collective, which a team number's zeroing
