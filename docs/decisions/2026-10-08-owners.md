# 2026-10-08: owners, with no helpers (v0.2 step 4, Part 1)

The second part of v0.2's fourth step, on `docs/prompts/v0.2-step4-progress-helpers-prompt.md`
(the brief), its "Part 1": the lead's agent becomes owner 0, holding every
wire, and the code takes the owner as a parameter - with no thread added. The
design is `docs/design/progress-helpers-proposal.md` §3.2 to §3.5.

**Where it stands.** Built on the branch `owners`, from `main` at `44f24bb`
(Part 0 done): the model at `1dd7bf8`, the C at `d7001c1`, the design notes at
`5264aba`. Its verification and its adversarial read are below; the Pi's lines
are compared with `44f24bb` itself, the tree Part 1 starts from, since Part 0
moved them from step 3's merge point on purpose.

## What was built

1. **The map** (`shmem/src/owner-map.h`). An *owner* is a thread with a ring and
   the wires attached to it. Peer slot *s*'s wire is owner 0's as its index *s*
   when the node runs no helpers, and owner 1 + (*s* mod *H*)'s as its index
   *s* div *H* when it runs *H*; an array the node keeps a wire is grouped by
   owner, which with no helpers is peer-slot order. The index numbers
   everything an owner keeps a wire: the wire, its connection's bookkeeping,
   its busy bit, its dirty bit, its ring's completion tag; the peer slot keeps
   numbering what is node-wide - the area's connections, `connection_metrics`,
   osoe-stat's view, a context's dirty set.
2. **The connection area, layout 5** (`connection-area.[ch]`). The header
   records the helper count, which every PE of the node must open the area with,
   after `ready` and `opened`, which stay where every layout since 3 has had
   them whatever the line's size (asserted); then a line an owner holding its
   `sleeping` flag and its wake word; then a dirty bitmap an owner, each from a
   line of its own, since each is taken by another thread. A PE of another
   layout is refused naming the layout. `osoe_connection_area_mark` sets the
   bit of the connection's index in its owner's bitmap. Owner 0 still sleeps on its PE slot's
   `agent_wake`, which stop, abort and the posted arrivals bump too; a helper
   will sleep on its word in the area.
3. **The engine an owner at a time** (`progress.[ch]`). `struct
   osoe_progress_owner` holds an owner's ring, wires, connections, busy bits,
   reply pool, counters, and its close and abort state; `struct osoe_progress`
   is the engine, whose control plane - the internode tree's state and the
   deadlock detector's messages - is owner 0's: `forward`, `release_local` and
   `osoe_progress_deadlock_send` write owner 0's connections only
   (`control_index`), and a collective or detector message arriving on a
   helper's wire is refused by name. The pass, idle, quiet, close, closed,
   abort, aborted, the peers' end and the silent check are functions of an
   owner. `osoe_am.ring` is gone.
4. **The host** (`progress-host.c`, `wire.c`). A ring an owner, created disabled
   by the mesh - which attaches each wire to its owner's ring at its index - and
   enabled by its owner on its first pass. The sleep protocol is an owner's
   (`lower_flag`, `raise_flag_and_look`); `osoe_progress_host_poll` is owner 0's
   pass with the lead's own work beside it (the unsent request, the silent
   check, the posted arrivals). A pool an owner (`descriptors_of`): the
   replies' term over the connections it holds, and the forwards' and the
   detector's, the control plane's, owner 0's - with no helpers, the node-wide
   pool as before. Close and abort run on an owner's wires.
5. **The PE side's wake addresses the owner** (`transport-internode.c`). Each
   call that woke the lead now wakes the owner of the connection it marked
   (`osoe_progress_host_wake(peer)`), claiming that owner's flag - the owner
   named once, for the mark and the wake alike (`osoe_connection_area_owner`). A wake
   `OSOE_TRANSPORT_MORE` holds back is remembered by the thread, an owner a bit
   (`osoe_progress_host_defer`), and made with its next wake whichever owner
   that is for, or by `osoe_progress_host_wake_deferred` after asking several
   peers to acknowledge; a wait for what the PE held back toward any number of
   peers - the landing zone full, a context's gets to land - wakes every owner
   of a data wire (`osoe_progress_host_wake_all`), each only if it sleeps.
6. **The model and the tests.** `lead_wake_model.py` takes the owner as a
   parameter (`1dd7bf8`): its earlier cases are unchanged, one and two helpers
   hold, and two breakages lose a wake - `wrong_owner`, a writer whose map is
   wrong for its mark and its wake alike, and `mark_wake_apart`, one that marks
   its connection's owner and wakes another (the adversarial read's N2, whose
   variant it is). `shmem.owner-map` checks the map both ways at every helper
   count to 64 and up to 300 peers, and the area at 0 to 4 helpers on 9 nodes
   and at 2 and 3 on 300 - more than 64 wires an owner: each mark in its
   owner's bitmap alone, no two owners' flags on one line and no two bitmaps
   starting on one. `shmem.owners` runs the engine at 2 and 3 helpers on 5 and
   9 nodes, every owner of a node turned by its one thread: a put, a blocking
   get and a fetching AMO between every two nodes, each checked where its
   sender wrote, and each connection's record, by peer slot, counting its own
   peer's traffic (the read's S1). Every existing test runs as before, at no
   helpers.

## What waits for the helpers, and why

The brief's Part 1 lists two items whose substance exists only once a helper
thread does; both move to Part 3, where something exercises them. The design is
unchanged - only the part each lands in.

- **Counters summed for readers** (the brief's item 4). Owner 0's counters are
  the node record `am`, as before; a helper's need a record of their own in the
  node segment, laid out for the helper count, and the readers' sums - metrics,
  osoe-stat, the detector - need those records. They come with osoe-stat's
  per-owner view, Part 3's item 4.
- **Stop and abort through request and done words** (the brief's item 5).
  Close and abort are functions of an owner now, the code a helper will run on
  its own wires; the lead's request word, the owner's done word, the lead's
  wait for it with a deadline, and their model come with the threads that
  answer them. Built now, the words would be written and read by one thread
  alone, and a model of them would check a protocol no test runs.

So does the brief's verification "again at 2 and 3 helpers on 3 to 16 logical
nodes under TSan and ASan", as far as it needs the threads: with no helper
thread, nothing runs two owners at once. What one thread can check at a helper
count is checked - the engine at 2 and 3 helpers (`shmem.owners`), the map at
every count to 64 and the area at 0 to 4 (`shmem.owner-map`) - in every preset,
TSan and ASan among them.

Part 3 also takes three of the adversarial read's notes: a PE whose helper
count differs from its lead's and whose area is larger is refused by the size
check naming `OSOE_AM_*` rather than the count (N3: read the lead's count from
the header first, or name the count there); the count must be refused past 64
before the area is laid out, since the PE's mask of owners held back is a word
(N5); and each owner's ring opens with its own provided buffers and announces
`OSOE_WIRE_SPLIT`, so the memory rows grow with the count and the notice is
printed an owner at a time (N7).

The unsent limit and the silent-peer check are owner 0's, over owner 0's wires -
every wire, with no helpers. Part 3 decides whether each owner limits its own
(the request is a word the lead's application thread writes once a session) and
the silent check goes to the control wires (Part 2), as the proposal's §3.6 has
it.

## The adversarial read

By a fresh agent given the diff of `d7001c1` and the design notes of `5264aba`,
the owner rule, and eight properties to attack - first that an owner's index
and a peer slot are never confused, since with no helpers they are one number
and every test passes whichever a line uses:
`docs/reviews/archive/v0.2-step4-part1-adversarial-read.md`. Its verdict: with
no helpers every pass, wake, pool and counter is `44f24bb`'s, and the map is
right everywhere it is used - checked exhaustively by a program of its own to
64 helpers and 1,000 peers, and through the engine's arithmetic at 1 to 8
helpers on 2 to 40 nodes; but nothing could fail on an index taken for a slot
outside the map itself. No fatal finding, one should-fix, seven notes, each
answered on the branch:

- **S1, should-fix: no test could tell an index from a slot.** The engine needs
  no thread to run at a helper count, so `shmem.owners` runs it at 2 and 3 on
  5 and 9 nodes from one thread a node, and `shmem.owner-map`'s area check runs
  at 300 nodes, where an owner holds more than 64 wires. The three mutations
  the read named, and a fourth, are run against them below.
- **N1: on x86-64, layout 5 moved the area's `ready` word** from byte 64 to
  128, so a PE of the earlier layout waited on a field moved under it rather
  than being refused. The new fields follow `opened` now, as layout 3's did; a
  static assertion pins `ready` and `opened` to the second and third lines at
  either line size; and a PE of another layout is refused naming the layout,
  where the message spoke of AM sizing.
- **N2: the notes named a breakage the model did not run**, a writer marking
  one owner and waking another - the C's own risk, since the mark and the wake
  each computed the owner. They name it once now
  (`osoe_connection_area_owner`), the model runs the case
  (`mark_wake_apart`, lost wakes under SC and TSO in 33 states), and the notes
  say what each breakage is.
- **N4: `release_local` indexed owner 0's connections without a check** of its
  own, safe only because `forward` refuses first; it refuses too now.
- **N6: `slot` named two things**: a peer's place in node order, and a wire's
  place on its ring. The ring's is its `index` now (`wire.[ch]`, `ring.[ch]`).
- **N3, N5 and two of N7's items** are Part 3's, above; N7's comment in
  `ring.h` is brought up to owners, and its note on a message older than this
  change is N1's.
