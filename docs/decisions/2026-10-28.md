Each from the numbers alone, with its options and one recommendation.

1. **The reductions' choice of Träff's (P37, P38).** The window of 8 decided on 2026-10-25 is
   timed: from 256 KiB it makes every broadcast 17 to 27% faster and brings the chooser's to
   the fixed shape's, and it is the lead's to build. What it leaves is the reductions. On eight
   nodes the closed form takes Träff's from 256 KiB only while the link's message cost reads
   under 5.5 to 10.5 µs (at the link's 2.85 GB/s, by the size and the hop), and the
   measurement read 23 to 45 µs: nine of the ten chooser's jobs took the tree of 7 children,
   and the one that read 21 µs and 0.55 GB/s took Träff's and ran its reductions of 256 KiB,
   1 MiB and 4 MiB 1.4, 2.4 and 2.6 times faster - faster than every fixed tree, as the second
   session's one job that read 3.2 µs. A put's fastest round trip here is 41 µs, so a message
   cost of tens of microseconds is mostly the idle CPUs' wakes that P38's untimed fan was to
   take out and on this cluster did not; nor did its longer streams steady the bandwidth, 0.88
   to 5.68 GB/s from job to job, which sets every data wire's unsent limit.
   - (a) The message cost from the least of the measurement's tries, not their median - a
     wake lengthens a try and never shortens one - the model's tests and the link's held to
     it: VM work, timed on no cluster, none being left.
   - (b) Träff's for every reduction past the small class whose rows fit a lane, the closed
     form choosing only among the trees: faster in every job that took it, here and on the
     Pi at 1 MiB (step 3), but at many nodes and middling sizes the model's trees win, and no
     cluster can show where.
   - (c) The choice left to the measurement; the README says a fast link's large reductions
     may run at half their speed, and that `OSOE_LINK_MESSAGE_NS` set to a few microseconds
     takes Träff's.
   - *Recommended*: (a), and (c)'s sentence in the README until it is built.
2. **The helpers' default (P36).** `auto`, built on 2026-10-20, takes two helpers on a job of
   three nodes or more where a node runs no more PEs than it has CPUs online - so two in both
   of H26's bindings, at 8 PEs a node of 16 CPUs. Bound to one CPU a PE - four cores'
   hyperthreads, the other four idle - two helpers bought the streams 1 to 5% and the mailbox
   a quarter and cost the 4 KiB alltoall 15%, the mailbox a tenth of its unbound rate either
   way; with a core a PE they bought the streams 21 to 22% and the 64 KiB reduction and the
   alltoalls 13 to 23%, and halved the mailbox. Unbound (H4) they bought 23 to 31% and the
   mailbox 36%.
   - (a) `auto` the default as built; the helpers' row says what a binding does - little
     either way at one CPU a PE, the mailbox halved at a core a PE, `0` for a job that lives
     by its message rate.
   - (b) `auto` counting the CPUs a PE may run on, its affinity, not those online: none at one
     CPU a PE, which costs nothing measured, and two at a core a PE, as (a).
   - (c) `0` the default, `auto` a setting.
   - *Recommended*: (a). Where the helpers buy nothing they cost little, where they buy they
     buy a fifth to a third of a stream, and the mailbox's loss at a core a PE is the one H4
     found past one PE a vCPU, which the row already says.
3. **The hand-on wake (P2)**, decided on 2026-10-25 to wait for H12's numbers. Every writer
   sleeps once a turnover: the puts' sleeps a 64 KiB moved are a quarter of the PEs a node at

   - *Recommended*: (a): the herd is measured and grows with the writers, and the change can be
     shown by a count with no cluster.
4. **`OSOE_CHECK_COLLECTIVES` by default (P24's)**, off until timed at many PEs a node. H12:
