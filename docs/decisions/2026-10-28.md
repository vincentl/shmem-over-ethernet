## P2's model

The hand-on wake, modelled first as the author answered (`docs/design/evidence/hand-on-wake/`):
a buffer reopened wakes one writer waiting for room; a writer that reserves and leaves room wakes
the next - its CAS, then its load of the waiters' count, the other side of a waiter's
registration and then its look at the room; and a woken writer that finds too little passes the
wake on once a generation, loading the word afresh before it sleeps, its own bump having changed
it. Live in every case run, two and three writers needing one or two units of a buffer of two,
the largest 11.6 million states with the engine's finished-state check run whole; at most one pass
a writer a generation, and without that bound two writers too large for the room pass the wake
back and forth (`pass_always`, PASSES). A first version's agent, held to a count of opens and
opening on a writer's request as well, reported a lost wake that was the bound's; it opens on a
reservation alone now.

What the model cannot say is the reason for P2: every design in it is live, a bare wake-one too,
since the agent opens again after every reservation; the hand-on is a change of speed, and the
design notes measured a bare wake-one 10 to 25% slower than waking every writer at 4 and 16
writers in the VM, a scheduling delay a link of its chain - which the hand-on is too, a shorter
one where the room runs out. Nor can it see a writer starved of every generation's wake, which
waking every writer cannot do. So the build waits to be timed: the node's rate at 4 to 64 writers,
beside the writers' sleeps, before it replaces waking every writer - for the author, with the
VM's numbers.
